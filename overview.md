## Current Focus

The overlap is **AI in the enterprise**: how organizations adopt agents without losing reliability, accountability, or trust. Liles supplies the engineering depth — gates, orchestration, evaluation, retrieval. O'Hara supplies the executive frame — governance economics, the manager layer, workforce restructuring, and the regional build-out that supplies the capacity. The Improving corpus adds the **delivery-practice layer**: how a services firm packages that thinking for clients — platform engineering, data streaming, nearshore capacity, partner selection, and change management.

## Key Themes

### Compounding reliability is the engineering spine

Per-step accuracy compounds multiplicatively, so long chains fail far more than any step suggests ([Stacking Errors](/concepts/stacking-errors.md)); the remedy is a [quality gate](/concepts/quality-gates.md) at every transition, [human-in-the-loop](/concepts/human-in-the-loop.md) approval at irreversible steps, and [Saga Pattern](/concepts/saga-pattern.md) compensation where rollback is impossible. The Improving corpus restates this for the enterprise as [pipeline reliability](/concepts/end-to-end-success-rate.md) and [AI monitoring](/concepts/ai-monitoring.md) with a real compute budget.

### Governance: uniform rules fail, reversibility governs

All three corpora argue [AI Governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md) is runtime infrastructure, not a policy binder, built on [Data Governance](/concepts/data-governance.md), [agent identity](/concepts/agent-identity.md), and an [AI inventory](/concepts/ai-inventory.md). O'Hara sharpens it: [uniform governance](/concepts/uniform-governance.md) is how agents die, and [reversibility](/concepts/reversibility.md) should be the governing criterion. The Improving corpus operationalizes it as an [AI operating model](/concepts/ai-operating-model.md), an [AI Center of Excellence](/concepts/ai-center-of-excellence.md), and a [governed AI gateway](/concepts/governed-ai-gateway.md), framed against [NIST AI RMF](/entities/nist-ai-rmf.md), [ISO/IEC 42001](/entities/iso-42001.md), and the [EU AI Act](/entities/eu-ai-act.md).

### The AI adoption gap: from pilot to production

Improving's most consistent recent theme is why AI stalls: [pilot purgatory](/concepts/pilot-purgatory.md), [AI readiness](/concepts/ai-readiness.md), [data readiness](/concepts/data-readiness.md), and [AI change management](/concepts/ai-change-management.md) that sequences trust before tooling. The remedies are an [AI strategy](/concepts/ai-strategy.md) and [business case](/concepts/ai-business-case.md), [prompt](/concepts/prompt-evaluation.md) and [LLM evaluation](/concepts/llm-evaluation.md) habits, [AI literacy](/concepts/ai-literacy.md), and [responsible AI](/concepts/responsible-ai.md) enforced continuously.

## Open Questions

- Whether [outcome-based pricing](/concepts/outcome-based-pricing.md) becomes real, or stays a fixed fee with a KPI attached.
- Whether the DFW AI build-out's infrastructure lead survives the grid-cost and talent questions the corpus raises.
- Whether [nearshore](/concepts/nearshore-outsourcing.md) delivery keeps its cost-and-talent advantage as AI compresses the work it staffs.
- Whether governance frameworks ([NIST AI RMF](/entities/nist-ai-rmf.md), [ISO/IEC 42001](/entities/iso-42001.md)) add explicit data-governance guidance.
