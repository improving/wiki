Two fields. Same August. Same drought.

One green. One the color of a paper bag. You can see the line where they meet, running along the hedge like someone drew it with a ruler.

The only difference between them is a plough.

I've been following [Bart Simmons](https://x.com/simmonsbart?ref=driventodevelop.com), who runs 2S Ranch out here. We pick up their beef at [Provision House](https://theprovisionhouse.com/?ref=driventodevelop.com) in Dallas, and I know how this sounds, but it's just different. The fat, the flavor, the whole thing. You can tell something was done right even if you can't fully explain what. Fortunately, HE can explain why, and I’ve been enjoying learning more and more about it.

[Sama Hoole](https://x.com/samahoole/status/2087485131971875190?ref=driventodevelop.com) writes about a field in Herefordshire that hasn't had a plough on it since sometime in the eighteenth century. Forty different plants growing together, roots deep enough to chase water below where August can reach them. The field across the lane was reseeded four years ago. More controlled. More intentional by the modern measure. It's brown.

That's the thing about regenerative farming that doesn't show up in the marketing language: it's not a method. It's a relationship with time. You're not managing soil. You're trusting a system that's been developing longer than you've been alive. The two hundred years of dunging and grazing and rainfall are the feature, not the starting condition you improve on. We're very good at the plough. At turning over, starting fresh, reseeding with something we can measure. We like to see “progress” or feel like we’re moving things forward because we are pushing them. And then we wonder why things don't hold.

Bart gets this. The beef tastes like the evidence. Sama gets this. The fields are the evidence.

Here's what I keep sitting with: in your work, in your organization, in your own life, how many of the things you're doing are optimizing for this season at the expense of the foundation beneath it? The hedge doesn't lie.
