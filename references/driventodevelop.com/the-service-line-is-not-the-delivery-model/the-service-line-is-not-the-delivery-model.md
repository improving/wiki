70% of consulting firms now have an AI service line. Graduate hiring is down 30%. Organizational structures are, according to multiple analyst reports, "remarkably stable." Those three facts belong in the same paragraph. The tension between them is the most honest signal in the market right now.

I've sat in enough planning sessions to know what an AI service line launch looks like from the inside. New branding. New practice lead. New deck. Sometimes new pricing. Rarely a restructured delivery model. The firms winning the next phase aren't the ones that slapped AI onto an existing practice. They're the ones that redesigned how work actually gets done: who does what, how teams are staffed, what the output looks like, what they're willing to guarantee.

At Improving, we've been doing this work. Not the branding layer. The delivery layer. Where the rubber meets the road and where, if you don't align and then actually deliver, you are going to hear about it (and not in a good way). I've seen what it takes to restructure a delivery model, and it is not a quarterly initiative. It is an operational overhaul that most firms are not willing to make while revenue is still flowing from the old model; think [Innovator's Dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Innovator%27s_Dilemma?ref=driventodevelop.com).

There's a distinction worth making clearly. A firm that has an AI service line is not the same as a firm that has AI-structured delivery. The first is a marketing decision. The second is an operational commitment. One of these is way harder than the other (trust me). The market is not yet good at telling them apart.

Watch the staffing patterns, not the press releases. Graduate hiring down 30% is not a cost-cutting story. It is a signal about what entry-level work looks like in an AI-native delivery model right now. The composition of teams is changing because the composition of the work is changing. A company that hasn't rethought how it staffs and delivers cannot hide that in a rebrand.

If you're evaluating a consulting partner right now, ask them to walk you through a delivery model change they've made in the last 12 months. Not a new offering. A structural change to how work actually gets done. The answer will tell you everything.
