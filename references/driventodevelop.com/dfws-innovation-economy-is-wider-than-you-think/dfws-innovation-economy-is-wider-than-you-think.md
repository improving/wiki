![DFW's Innovation Economy Is Wider Than You Think](images/photo-1743893545857-f2bab2285947)

This week, more than 15 Texas cities are hosting simultaneous innovation events. Founders, investors, and entrepreneur support organizations gathering for a statewide week of programming. Meanwhile in Fort Worth, TechFW and UNT Health launched a new Innovation Forum linking startups directly with Cowtown Angels investors and campus lab resources. If your mental model of the DFW tech economy is still about data centers and semiconductor plants, you're reading a chapter that closed before this one started.

I've watched the DFW story for a while. The version that dominates the news is a capex story: big companies relocating, hyperscale data centers going up in the suburbs, chip fabs in Sherman. All real, all significant. But capex is about what large organizations are building here. The story I find more interesting is what's being created here.

DFW ranked third nationally for economic development project volume. The metro added 37,230 tech workers between 2022 and 2025, one of the largest single-market gains tracked. AI-specific workers grew 45% year over year to nearly 30,000. That's not a relocation story. That's talent formation. The cities that build durable innovation economies are not the ones that win the most facility announcements. They're the ones that build the infrastructure for people to start, fund, and grow companies. DFW has had the anchor-tenant layer for years. What's new is the startup infrastructure is catching up.

TechFW, Dallas Innovates, the statewide innovation week are not PR. They're mechanisms that create conditions for the next generation of companies to start here, get funded here, and grow here rather than relocate to Austin or the Bay Area. That's the piece of the DFW story that doesn't make the data center press releases.

For our work at Improving, it's also the segment where CodeLaunch lives, connecting growth-stage tech firms with the capital and resources they need to scale. The market for that work is broader and younger than it was three years ago. Most people operating here haven't caught up to that yet.

Where do you see the DFW founder economy three years from now?
