[Arthur Brooks went to Italy on vacation](https://www.thefp.com/p/arthur-brooks-vacation-americans-europeans?ref=driventodevelop.com) and spent half of it mentally writing an article. His wife was frustrated. He wasn't sure he'd done anything wrong.

Neither am I.

I told Susie when we were dating that I never planned to retire. That I wanted to drop dead at work at the age of 95. She knew...she married me anyway, and I love her for it. That doesn't mean I don't enjoy vacations. I love the food, the wine, the slow mornings, the unhurried time with my family (the wine!). But I've always gotten restless around day three. Not miserable, just itchy. We made a deal early on: one big trip, 10 days+, every five years. On those trips, as long as I'm present for what we've planned (I sneak in a little work when everyone naps), it works.

Brooks gave me language for something I've felt but never quite named. His research shows the brain's response to boredom isn't universal. About half of people experience it as generative fuel. Quiet time switches them on, not off. The shower, the journal, the long drive with nothing on the stereo (yeah, I'm one of those guys). For this group, what looks like "not working" is actually where the real work happens. The other half experience boredom as aversive, and they reach for stimulation to escape it. Neither is broken. They're just different operating systems.

The mistake we keep making is treating rest as one-size-fits-all. The two-week unplug to the coast isn't a prescription; it's a preference. For some people, meaning is the mechanism. Work is the restoration. Claiming otherwise doesn't make them workaholics. It makes them honest.

I've tried to cultivate a taste for long, unstructured quiet. I find it. I just don't need 10 days of it to get there. An hour in the morning journaling and I'm recharged. The rest is bonus.

Before you book the two-week trip to decompress, answer one honest question: are you actually the kind of person who decompresses that way? Or are you chasing someone else's idea of what rest is supposed to look like?
