[Brookings](https://dallasinnovates.com/dallas-fort-worth-metro-named-ai-star-hub-in-new-brookings-report-mapping-americas-ai-economy/?ref=driventodevelop.com) named Dallas-Fort Worth one of 28 AI Star Hubs driving two-thirds of the country's AI job market. The interesting part is not the designation. It is what kind of AI economy DFW is actually building, because it does not look like Austin or Seattle.

Celestica just announced an 876M dollar AI manufacturing campus in Fort Worth. Hillwood AllianceTexas zone is running AI-powered logistics. Dallas Innovates published its [2026 AI 75 list](https://dallasinnovates.com/2026-ai75/?ref=driventodevelop.com), and it reads like a cross-section of the industries that actually run the region's economy: healthcare, advanced manufacturing, financial services, logistics. 36% of local jobs now have meaningful AI exposure. These are not speculative deployments. They are production systems embedded in the workflows of industries with long procurement cycles, regulatory oversight, and genuine tolerance for complexity.

That matters to me because our teams work in exactly those industries. We are not primarily in the AI-native startup ecosystem. We are in the places where vertical AI (domain-trained, industry-specific systems built for real operational environments) is taking hold fastest. And the advisory work in those environments requires something different than what the hyperscaler narrative suggests. It requires technical credibility and operational depth, in roughly equal measure.

DFW AI economy is focused on the hard stuff. The places where a bad model has regulatory consequences. Where an unreliable system does not just slow things down, it creates liability. Where the question is not can we deploy AI but can we trust this system enough to run our operations on it. Those are the environments where the real work is happening. And those are the environments where Improving has been building.

If your mental model of DFW AI economy is still data centers and hyperscaler real estate, the [Brookings report](https://dallasinnovates.com/dallas-fort-worth-metro-named-ai-star-hub-in-new-brookings-report-mapping-americas-ai-economy/?ref=driventodevelop.com) is worth your time. The picture it paints is more interesting than the headline.
