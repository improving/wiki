# corporate currency

Corporate currency is the informal social economy inside organizations: attention, favors, and commitments behave like priced goods that people earn, spend, and lose. It matters because the framework is descriptive rather than cynical — the behaviors it prescribes are generous and reliable, and failing to keep commitments is the fastest way to go bankrupt.

## Key Claims

- Attention is priced by organizational level, making executive time the most expensive currency.
- Giving public credit earns currency, while self-promotion carries a look-at-me tax.
- Failing to keep commitments depreciates currency faster than anything else.
- The framework prescribes generous, reliable behavior, not cynical manipulation.
- Self-referential claims are discounted because the listener can see the motive behind them.
- Asked-for context and written records are taxed far less, and reputation built by others compounds while self-promotional reputation needs constant maintenance.
