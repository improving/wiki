# AI readiness

AI readiness is whether data, process, talent, and governance are in place before autonomous AI can be deployed. The sources split it into a technical dimension — clean, connected, governed data — and a psychological one: the dominant blocking belief is that using AI well means trusting it implicitly, when the workable posture treats AI as a capable collaborator whose output needs skilled review.

## Key Claims

- Three prerequisites precede experiments: a data strategy and source of truth, right-sized governance (neither lockdown nor Wild West), and a defined business value case.
- 95% of enterprise AI solutions fail on data issues, so data quality, governance, and scalable infrastructure are the backbone of any AI initiative.
- AI readiness is mostly a psychology problem: the pervasive blocker is the assumption that using AI well means trusting it implicitly, producing an all-or-nothing posture.
- Three mental-model shifts matter: "AI replaces" → "AI compresses," "I need to understand it" → "I need to manage it," and "autonomy is risk" → "governance makes autonomy safe."
- Organizations stuck at Stage 2 of the [AI trust evolution](/concepts/ai-trust-evolution.md) cannot reach Stages 5–8 without that shift.
- A pilot gets a clean dataset, motivated users, and a personally invested sponsor; production offers none of those, so a pilot was never a real readiness test.
- Validate readiness via a problem-selection workshop and a data-readiness check before modeling, and assign a business owner to the outcome.
