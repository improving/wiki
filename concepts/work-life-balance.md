# work-life balance

Work-life balance is the common framing for dividing time between career and personal life. In this corpus it appears mainly as a target that is rejected: the argument is that work and family are not equal quantities, so the goal is not balance but choosing deliberately where time goes.

## Key Claims

- Time cannot be replaced, given back, or returned once consumed, which makes it the scarce resource rather than money.
- We steal time from families who forgive us, not from employers, so the tradeoff is misdescribed when aimed at work.
- Freely given family time is not stealing — the framing only applies to time taken under pressure.
- You cannot balance two fundamentally unequal things, so balance itself is not the goal.
- A promotion to company president meant late-night coding sessions would become less frequent and focus would be prioritized differently.
