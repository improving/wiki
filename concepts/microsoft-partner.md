# Microsoft Partner

A Microsoft Partner is a firm officially recognized by Microsoft to offer Microsoft-related products and services — application development, SharePoint consulting, intranet development, and data analytics among them. Partners keep pace with a fast-moving platform and help clients adopt better processes while meeting compliance needs. In this corpus the model appears both as a delivery credential and as a case study in how buyers actually choose a services firm, where the badge matters less than what the partner can do with the technology.

## Key Claims

- Becoming a partner is selective, and partners gain access to services available only within the partner network.
- Microsoft's "find a partner" directory lists partners and lets buyers filter by country, products, services, and size, or search a known name directly.
- Microsoft rebranded the Microsoft Partner Network and launched the Microsoft Cloud Partner Program (MCPP) in October 2022, affecting 400,000 global partners.
- Buyers weight a partner's competencies low, partly because it is hard to differentiate one Microsoft gold partner from another; if the technology is identical across partners, the differentiator is what the partner can do with it.
- The top four reasons partners win deals are trust (already delivered for a client), transferable trust (referrals), industry expertise, and size/breadth of capability — location and price matter less.
- Improving is a Microsoft Managed Partner supporting M365, Azure, and D365 across account segments and industries; it created a corporate Microsoft Services Team led by Lowell Messner to maximize the value of the partnership.
- A representative engagement: a Fortune 500 health insurer's enterprise data strategy rebuilt on Azure, using Kafka to speed ingestion into Snowflake as the centralized data hub.
