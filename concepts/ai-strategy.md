# AI strategy

An AI strategy is a prioritized plan connecting AI use cases to business outcomes, data readiness, and governance. The sources frame failure as an execution problem — 88% of proofs of concept never reach production — so a credible strategy evaluates business priorities, data, technology foundations, and governance maturity, then produces a phased, executable roadmap. Strategy-only advice that cannot build is a recurring red flag.

## Key Claims

- 88% of AI proofs of concept never reach production and 95% of enterprise AI solutions fail on data issues; 77% of companies explore AI but only 20% achieve significant ROI.
- Use-case selection criteria are strategic alignment, business impact, feasibility, ROI potential, and time to value.
- A three-stage maturity roadmap runs Ad Hoc → Strategic → Transformational, with ethics/model-review boards and continuous training pipelines appearing only at the top.
- Three prerequisites precede experiments: a data strategy and source of truth, right-sized governance (neither lockdown nor Wild West), and a defined business value case with a real "R" in ROI.
- Two questions separate real partners: what percentage of your recommended solutions reach production, and can you share a case where you recommended against AI.
- Best-practice targets include time-to-production of 6–9 months, payback under 12 months for operational AI, and ROI above 150% within 18–24 months.
- The diagnostic question is whether AI is a feature added to the organization or the foundation it is built on; the foundation model is deliberately model-agnostic.
