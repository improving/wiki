# Labor Compression

Labor compression is the claim that AI's economic effect is best modeled as fewer hours required per unit of output rather than the elimination of roles. It reframes the workforce conversation from headcount to capacity, and it connects to how precisely teams name what AI is doing.

## Key Claims

- Automation historically compresses hours per output rather than eliminating roles; the ATM and spreadsheet cases show compression, not replacement.
- Two mechanisms drive compression: productivity amplification and task automation.
- Value capture depends on whether demand or execution capacity is the limiting factor, splitting work into demand-limited and collapsable tasks.
- When AI raises everyone's execution floor, equal gains compress prices and reset expectations, shifting advantage to context quality, integration speed, and judgment.
- AI readiness is mostly a psychology problem: practitioners wrongly equate using AI well with trusting it implicitly, and reframing from "replacing jobs" to "compressing hours" is part of the fix.
- Imprecise vocabulary ("AI adoption," "integration") blocks shared understanding, so naming the mechanisms is itself a force multiplier.
