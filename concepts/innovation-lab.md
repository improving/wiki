# corporate innovation lab

A dedicated unit set up to incubate new products and capabilities separately from the maintenance work that dominates most IT budgets. The corpus argues a lab signals a growth priority, but only works if it runs a genuinely different execution model — new-product focus, cross-discipline teams, and accountability measured in revenue rather than activity.

## Key Claims

- For North American companies, 70% of IT budgets go to maintaining existing infrastructure and operations, leaving only 30% for innovation, while 88% of CIOs say they are increasingly innovation-focused.
- Innovation is a discipline that works through a method, not through suspended processes and measurements.
- Labs should focus on new product development across idea generation, implementation, and validation, run by cross-discipline teams organized around a product.
- Labs need tension with the parent organization: shared vision, purpose, and strategy, but flexible "how" and "what."
- The practical accountability metrics are new-product sales as a percentage of revenue and the gross margins on those sales.
- Improving's own AI Innovation Lab is cited as where the firm works on AI pilots — and the same source notes 90% of senior executives report no measurable AI productivity gains over three years, so labs must attack compressible, demand-unlimited work rather than the most urgent pain.
- Lab-adjacent community programs extend the model outward: Improving co-ran "Ion Startup University" with Houston's Ion, a free six-month curriculum on product thinking, pitching, and networking, and produces CodeLaunch, a zero-equity accelerator where volunteer teams build MVPs.
