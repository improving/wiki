# data readiness

Data readiness is whether an organization's data is clean, governed, consistent, and connected enough for AI to work — a prerequisite, not a data-volume question. Because AI agents act across systems end to end and follow the standards of the data they are fed, messy, siloed, or untrustworthy data produces messy, siloed, or untrustworthy AI. Readiness means clean governed data, integrated systems, clear process logic, and accessible secure infrastructure, established before deployment rather than discovered during it.

## Key Claims

- "If your data is messy, your AI will be messy" — agents retrieve, update, validate, and pass work along, so they inherit the quality of their inputs.
- Data readiness does not mean having lots of data; it means clean, governed, consistent data, integrated systems, clear process logic, and accessible secure infrastructure.
- Three prerequisites to AI deployment: a data strategy that establishes the source of truth, governance calibrated between lockdown and Wild West, and a defined business value case.
- "You can't put the toothpaste back in the tube" — settle governance before letting data roam, watching PII, compliance, and sensitive IP.
- Run a data readiness assessment for coverage, timeliness, consistency, lineage, and bias before committing to scope; where data is weak, limit exposure with RAG, source citation, or constrained use cases.
- A recommendation system degraded not because the model was weak but because an upstream service redefined "active users" while a feature pipeline served stale values for nearly a month.
- Two contrasting postures: Company A treats AI as a feature and bolts tools onto broken processes; Company B treats AI as an operating model and redesigns data flows, systems, and governance.
