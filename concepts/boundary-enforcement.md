# Boundary enforcement

Boundary enforcement is the practice of automatically holding agents and systems inside declared architectural and permission limits. It appears across this corpus as both an architecture concern — agents crossing module boundaries invisibly at machine speed — and a governance concern, where it is one of the five components that make autonomous AI trustworthy.

## Key Claims

- AI agents take the shortest path to working code, crossing architectural boundaries invisibly.
- Parallel agents without isolation create merge conflicts and entanglement; isolation plus automated boundary enforcement preserves architecture at AI speed.
- Uncontrolled AI generation can cost 25 to 50 percent of delivery capacity.
- Boundary enforcement is one of five governance components, alongside audit trails, quality gates, human approval, and access control.
- Observability, reversibility, and boundary enforcement are preconditions for extending autonomy.
- Agentic AI adoption stalls on the same trust questions that stalled DevOps, not on capability; most organizations sit at Stage 2 or 3 of the 8-Stage AI Trust Evolution.
