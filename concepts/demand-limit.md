# Demand Limit

Demand limit is the ceiling that available demand — not production capability — places on the value AI can create. Across this corpus it is the second axis, alongside time impact, that decides whether faster work becomes more output, more headcount reduction, or neither. Capacity built ahead of confirmed demand, and productivity gains that land in demand-limited work, both fail to convert into value.

## Key Claims

- IBM's Together AI inference cluster will not go live until early 2027, capacity built ahead of confirmed enterprise demand; more AI capability does not create demand for what it produces.
- Nvidia posted an earnings beat and was still sold off: capital markets no longer fund AI buildout on forecasts alone.
- The 2x2 AI impact matrix pairs time impact with demand limit; high impact plus high demand means redeploying freed capacity into demand generation, while high impact plus low demand forces honest headcount or role-growth decisions.
- Labor compression splits into demand-limited and collapsable tasks; when AI raises everyone's execution floor, prices compress and advantage shifts to context access, integration speed, and judgment.
- Across 100,000+ developers, commits rose 180% but released software only 30% and consumer usage stayed flat — the bottleneck moved from writing to shipping, then to value.
- Quadrant assignments expire as AI capability rises and cost falls.
