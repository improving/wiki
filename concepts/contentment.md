# contentment

Contentment is a settled satisfaction with who you are, distinct from the pursuit of happiness. Across this corpus it is presented as an achievement rather than a gift: learned, practiced, and grounded in [gratitude](/concepts/gratitude.md) rather than acquisition.

## Key Claims

- Contentment is not bestowed by other people or things — it cannot be given to you.
- It is learned through the experience of lacking it, which is why discontentment is instructive rather than merely painful.
- Practicing gratitude is the practical path to finding contentment.
- People are typically wrong when guessing what will make them happy, so chasing happiness misfires.
- Chasing happiness drives materialism and [comparison](/concepts/comparison-trap.md) with others.
- We are happiest when growing toward our potential, so growth — not happiness — should be the goal, with contentment as its companion.
