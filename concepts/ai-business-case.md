# AI business case

An AI business case frames an investment around evidence, risk-adjusted return, and the sponsor's actual priorities rather than enthusiasm for the technology. The sources converge on a one-page value hypothesis — problem, target metric, owner, timeline — anchored to a dollar-figured outcome, because most projects die when no one defined what "working" meant. Governance and data costs belong in the case, not added after it.

## Key Claims

- Begin every initiative with a one-sentence problem statement ("improve X by Y% for Z users") tied to a dollar sign — saved time, increased revenue, or reduced errors.
- Require a one-page value hypothesis documenting problem, target metric, owner, and timeline; if it does not fit, it is not ready to build.
- A stalled healthcare chatbot reframed around reducing review cycle time in one workflow achieved 27% labor reduction within 90 days.
- Gartner projects 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end-2027; the cited causes — escalating costs, unclear business value, governance gaps — are all pre-launch questions.
- The pre-launch test: who owns the kill criteria? If no one can say it in a sentence, it is a project-design problem, not merely a governance one.
- Business value needs a real "R" in ROI: define what decisions a result would inform and how success is measured, and scope governance before data roams.
- Best-practice targets include payback under 12 months for operational AI and ROI above 150% within 18–24 months.
