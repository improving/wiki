# stakeholder orientation

Stakeholder orientation is the Conscious Capitalism pillar that treats a business as existing within an ecosystem — employees, vendors, customers, industry peers, and local community — rather than serving shareholders alone. It is driven by higher purpose and, applied personally, is how a person activates that purpose through the groups they choose to serve.

## Key Claims

- Stakeholders include employees, vendors, customers, industry members, and local government; the model widens focus beyond shareholders to partners, the community, and the industry.
- The stakeholder model rejects the "zero-sum" view that helping competitors necessarily hurts you.
- Personally, stakeholder orientation is how you activate your higher purpose, and stakeholder models evolve across life stages even as purpose stays more permanent.
- Four attributes of a personal model: specificity in groups, measurable impact, impactfulness, and actionability.
- Impact should be measured — for example, through intentional time spent with friends and family — and relationships should be reciprocal; letting go of relationships that consistently diminish you is not selfish.
- Improving's nearshore expansion is framed as serving the local ecosystem, not just extracting cost savings.
- Improving's CEO joined the Conscious Capitalism board, tying the pillar to the company's operating philosophy.
