# Intentional Focus

The deliberate choice to carve out time and attention for slow, connective thinking rather than defaulting to skim-reading and reflexive distraction. Across this corpus it is treated as a habit that must be scheduled and defended, not a mood that arrives on its own.

## Key Claims

- The author's default is to skim rather than read deeply; deep thinking only happens when it is made intentional.
- A note-taking tool such as [Obsidian](/entities/obsidian.md) provides a vehicle to capture and integrate ideas so they compound instead of evaporating.
- Idle moments that once produced reflection are now filled by phones; most people reach for a device the instant they are bored.
- Deep thinking is genuinely hard and requires both time and focus, so it competes directly with the reflex to stay busy.
- One post commits to sitting quietly with boredom instead of reaching for a phone — a small, repeatable practice for reclaiming focus.
