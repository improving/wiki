# customer success

The discipline of ensuring clients actually achieve the outcomes they bought, rather than only delivering the work they asked for. The corpus frames it as a strategic growth driver for consultancies — proactive support and frequent check-ins raise retention and open expansion — and as something measured, with NPS, CSAT, and CLTV as the tracking metrics.

## Key Claims

- Customer success is not just a support role; proactive support and frequent check-ins build trust and keep clients committed.
- Success teams drive retention and expansion by anticipating changing client needs and handling renewals before they are due.
- The metrics to track are NPS, CSAT, and CLTV; CRM systems and data reveal trends and predict client needs.
- Improving reported an NPS of 78% and an average market study score of 9.1 out of 10 from 32 detailed client interviews, with 81% of interviewees scoring 9 or higher.
- That same feedback surfaced opportunities around pricing, resource management, and strategic partnerships — success measurement as a source of critical input, not only praise.
- Onboarding is where customer success begins: it is the client's first experience of whether the provider delivers on its promises, and clients who feel heard and supported from the start are far more likely to stay and expand.
- A customer-success culture requires training and alignment across success, sales, and product, and continually refreshed service offerings.
- Happy clients bring new business through word-of-mouth, case studies, and communities.
