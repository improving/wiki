# Scrum mastery

Scrum mastery is the depth of Scrum practice that goes beyond a certificate: understanding why the framework works and applying it in real teams. This corpus argues the field has a proficiency problem — certification is emphasized over application — and that the real drivers of success are the five Scrum values and the leader-who-serves stance of the 2020 Scrum Guide, not ceremony mechanics.

## Key Claims

- ScrumMatch data cited by Don McGreal holds that 38% of Scrum Masters have little to no understanding of Scrum's core principles, often never having read the Scrum Guide ([Raising the Bar for Scrum Mastery](/sources/raising-the-bar-for-scrum-mastery.md)).
- Improving's counter is scenario-based assessment, immersive training (case studies, simulations, peer learning), and mentorship in real projects ([Raising the Bar](/sources/raising-the-bar-for-scrum-mastery.md)).
- The five Scrum Values — commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage — were the real predictor of success across Scrum, XP, and Kanban teams; openness is the hardest to live ([What Really Makes Scrum Work?](/sources/what-really-makes-scrum-work.md)).
- The 2020 Scrum Guide reframes the Scrum Master as a leader who serves — coach, blocker-remover, facilitator, and change agent — and AI is now a tactical edge for that role ([The AI-Enhanced Scrum Master](/sources/the-ai-enhanced-scrum-master.md)).
- The Scrum Master owns the process while leaders own the goals; conflating them produces micromanagement and confused teams ([Managing the Process vs Managing the Goals](/sources/difference-between-managing-process-and-managing-goals.md)).
- The process-adoption trap shows up as a Scrum Master acting as process cop or project manager, estimates used only to signal readiness, and retrospectives producing no meaningful action ([The Unseen Impact, Part II](/sources/the-unseen-impact-to-your-agile-transformation-part-ii.md)).
- Earning Scrum.org's Professional Scrum Trainer certification is an 18-month path; the assessments are published as among the most difficult in Agile ([My 18-Month Journey to PST Certification](/sources/my-18-month-journey-to-pst-certification.md)).
