# agile leadership

Agile leadership means leading with coaching, trust, and self-managing teams rather than enforcing practices and tracking individuals. This corpus frames it as the leadership posture the Agile values demand: leaders set the goal and remove obstacles, teams own how the work gets done, and psychological safety is treated as the most important ingredient of agility.

## Key Claims

- Bringing in Agile coaches to "fix" teams or enforce practices rarely works; teams complete activities mechanically without committing, so coaches should coach continuous improvement instead of enforcing ([Agile Leadership Starts With You](/sources/agile-leadership-starts-with-you.md)).
- Psychological safety is the most important aspect of Agile — teams cannot improve without it — and management itself needs coaching ([Agile Leadership Starts With You](/sources/agile-leadership-starts-with-you.md)).
- Micromanagement disguises itself as Agile leadership through individual velocity tracking and task-level check-ins, which incentivize ticket hoarding and erode psychological safety ([Micromanagement Disguised as Agile Leadership](/sources/micromanagement-disguised-as-agile-leadership.md)).
- The servant-leadership alternative: measure the team, remove impediments, and trust by default, tracking team velocity, cycle time, deployment frequency, and satisfaction rather than individual story points ([Micromanagement Disguised as Agile Leadership](/sources/micromanagement-disguised-as-agile-leadership.md)).
- Leaders should own the goal and hand it to the team without caveat; Scrum Masters should own the process that facilitates it ([Managing the Process vs Managing the Goals](/sources/difference-between-managing-process-and-managing-goals.md)).
- The Agile Manifesto's values read as a plea to empower the people who own the work, each demanding a different leadership posture ([What is the Core of Agile?](/sources/what-is-the-core-of-agile.md)).
- Alignment on delighting the customer produces autonomy, and autonomy produces agility at scale ([Agility: to Scale or Not to Scale](/sources/agile-to-scale-or-not-to-scale.md)).
