# learning strategy

A learning strategy aligns training and development with business objectives rather than delivering training for its own sake. In this corpus the test of a learning program is whether it changes behavior and moves an organizational outcome — not how many people were trained or how many certificates were earned — and the most effective approaches blend classroom awareness, hands-on labs, sustained programs, and coaching.

## Key Claims

- Training programs should directly contribute to company goals; ROI should be measured against organizational results rather than headcount trained or pass/fail rates.
- Planning should begin with a needs assessment and blend hands-on activities, e-learning modules, and collaborative projects to fit different learning styles.
- Measurement climbs a three-tier stack: learning activity (completion, knowledge retention) to performance (behavior change, engagement) to business outcomes (ROI, client satisfaction, project success).
- Traditional instructor-led training has "four D's" — disruptive, dense, detached, and disregarded — with little post-training accountability.
- Certifications create a knowledge baseline and motivate study, but passing an assessment is not mastery: "a driver's license doesn't make you a good driver."
- Effective mixes include Deeper Learning Programs spreading 30–50 objectives across six to ten weeks, labs that tackle real business problems, and coaching that anchors behavior.
