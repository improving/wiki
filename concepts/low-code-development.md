# low-code development

Low-code development builds applications with minimal hand-written code, moving work from IT to power users while still reading and writing enterprise data. In this corpus the pattern is almost always Microsoft Power Platform — Power Apps, Power Pages, Power Automate, and Copilot Studio — combined with Microsoft 365 services like SharePoint and Dataverse. It matters because it delivers working software at a fraction of custom-build cost, provided teams invest in design, architecture, and governance rather than shipping the bare-bones result.

## Key Claims

- Power Apps builds canvas apps for free-form design or model-driven apps from Dataverse entities, uses an Excel-familiar formula language, offers hundreds of connectors, and can embed in Teams, SharePoint, and Dynamics 365.
- Authentication and access control ride on Microsoft 365, with the Power Apps Admin Center managing deployment and lifecycle.
- Limitations: weak for pixel-perfect UI, light source control that complicates parallel work, a continuing need for developer-minded resources, and dependence on browser quality and caching.
- Design pays off: of 60+ available controls a quality app should use fewer than ten; one client's 8-week traditional build became a one-week bare-bones Power App, extended to three weeks with design resources for a far better product.
- Power Pages extends Power Apps to external websites with a drag-and-drop designer plus direct HTML/CSS/JavaScript editing, custom domains with SSL, and Copilot assistance.
- Copilot Studio lets power users build AI copilots over corporate knowledge from templates or scratch, deployable in Teams and SharePoint without deep technical skills.
- The Microsoft 365 "configure out of the box" middle ground delivered a contract lifecycle management system in two months and 275 hours for just under $30,000, against costly and inflexible pre-built tools.
