# Data Governance

Data governance is the system of policy, ownership, and controls that makes data trustworthy across its lifecycle. This corpus argues it has moved from a compliance artifact to a runtime function: active metadata, embedded enforcement, and catalogs that act as the control plane.

## Key Claims

- Gartner predicts 80% of data and analytics governance initiatives will fail by 2027; the failure patterns are the compliance trap, the accountability gap, and tool-first failure.
- Only 8% of organizations rated their governance programs highly successful in 2020, and the top barriers were behavioral — policy adherence and usability — not budget.
- Gartner retired its Metadata Management Magic Quadrant in favor of Active Metadata Management (2021) and later a governance platforms quadrant, signaling consolidation.
- Active metadata is captured automatically as a byproduct of system operation; passive catalogs go stale because curation is manual.
- Governance makes the knowledge stage of the knowledge–insight–action chain trustworthy, not merely compliant.
- Only 42.3% of organizations have well-established data and AI ethics policies.
