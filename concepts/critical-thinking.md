# Critical Thinking

Critical thinking is the capacity to hold an idea, examine its evidence, and reason to your own conclusion rather than accepting or outsourcing one. Across this corpus it is treated as the scarcest and most durable professional skill — the thing writing sharpens, college should build, and AI can quietly erode.

## Key Claims

- Reading opens new perspectives and cultivates critical thinking; writing forces ideas into coherence and exposes where what you thought diverges from what you wrote.
- Ideas held only in your head get artificially filled in, so writing is the test of whether the thinking is real.
- Outsourcing thinking to AI causes cognitive atrophy, which Sam Altman calls one of AI's most overlooked risks.
- Executives who delegate reasoning become editors of AI output rather than engines of thinking; AI can scaffold what to think about, but the thinking must stay yours.
- Universities traded asking what is true and good for asking how things make you feel.
- Critical thinking paired with civil engagement is rarer than any technical skill, and serious rooms turn to the person who can think, not the loudest one.
