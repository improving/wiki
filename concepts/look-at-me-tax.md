# look-at-me tax

The look-at-me tax is the reputational discount applied to self-promotion because the listener can see the motive behind it. It sits inside the [corporate currency](/concepts/corporate-currency.md) framework as the cost side of self-referential claims.

## Key Claims

- Self-referential claims are discounted because the listener can see the motive; building others' reputations compounds better than announcing your own wins.
- The tax hits hardest on credit-claiming, unsolicited wins, and comparative positioning.
- Asked-for context and written records are taxed far less than volunteered self-promotion.
- Reputation built by others compounds, while self-promotional reputation needs constant maintenance.
- Giving public credit earns currency; failing to keep commitments depreciates it faster than anything else.
- Announcing AI transformation before earning a track record applies the look-at-me tax to a system's [social capital](/concepts/social-capital.md).
