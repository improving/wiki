# Kanban

Kanban is a method for managing the flow of value through a system, using visibility and explicit limits on [work in progress](/concepts/work-in-progress.md) rather than committing to fixed iterations. In this corpus it appears mainly through certification guidance and as evidence that flow practices have spread well beyond software — the emphasis is on minimal, context-fit practices, not prescriptions.

## Key Claims

- Two certification paths exist: Applying Professional Kanban (APK), sourced from ProKanban.org's *The Kanban Guide*, and Professional Scrum with Kanban (PSK), sourced from Scrum.org's *The Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams* ([A Guide to Kanban Certification Training](/sources/guide-to-kanban.md)).
- Neither course makes someone an expert, but both build a foundational understanding through discussion, discovery, and shared experience ([Guide to Kanban](/sources/guide-to-kanban.md)).
- Both bodies reject prescriptive approaches because team context varies, offering minimal practices teams build on; PSK assumes existing Professional Scrum knowledge while APK requires no Scrum prerequisite ([Guide to Kanban](/sources/guide-to-kanban.md)).
- PK I and PSK I certifications never expire and carry no ongoing fee, and both bodies offer free open assessments ([Guide to Kanban](/sources/guide-to-kanban.md)).
- Kanban principles have spread beyond software — the corpus cites Kanban applied to hiring as evidence that agile ideas are now ubiquitous infrastructure ([Agile Mythbusting](/sources/agile-mythbusting-debunking-the-agile-is-dead-narrative.md)).
- The framework matters less than lived values: teams using Kanban, Scrum, or XP succeeded when they shared commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage ([What Really Makes Scrum Work?](/sources/what-really-makes-scrum-work.md)).
