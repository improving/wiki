# consulting talent model

The consulting talent model describes how firms staff and grow: a broad base of junior analysts doing document-heavy work, leveraged upward into a narrow senior layer. This corpus argues that model is already being rebuilt around AI, and that the accountability for reskilling has moved from theory to public consequence.

## Key Claims

- [Accenture](/entities/accenture.md) is exiting 11,000 staff who cannot be reskilled on AI; the CEO's phrase "compressed timeline" signals the clock already ran out.
- The same firm's restructuring carried an $865 million price tag, tying the talent shift to a concrete financial commitment.
- [Deloitte](/entities/deloitte.md) is restructuring practices and KPMG cut 400 US advisory roles — the pattern is sector-wide, not one firm's problem.
- AI absorbs the document-heavy work first- and second-year analysts performed, hollowing out the traditional pyramid's base.
- The replacement shape is fewer senior people augmented by AI, with growth concentrated in AI-aligned roles.
- Leaders must know who is making the AI transition and who is not; firms that fix reskilling now gain a hard-to-replicate talent advantage.
