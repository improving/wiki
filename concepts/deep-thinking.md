# Deep Thinking

Deep thinking is the deliberate practice of giving an idea sustained attention — reading it properly, connecting it to what you already know, and letting it sit. The corpus frames it as effortful and increasingly rare, squeezed between a default habit of skimming and a phone that fills every idle moment.

## Key Claims

- The author's default is skimming rather than reading deeply, a pattern he treats as the norm rather than a personal failing.
- Intentional time plus the right materials enables depth; tools alone do not.
- [Obsidian](/entities/obsidian.md) provides a vehicle to capture and integrate notes, making connections durable instead of fleeting.
- Boredom used to create the space for thinking, but devices now fill that space and rob us of reflection.
- Most people reach for their phone the moment they are bored.
- Deep thinking is hard and requires time and focus; the author commits to sitting quietly instead of reaching for his phone.
