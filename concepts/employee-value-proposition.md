# Employee Value Proposition

An employee value proposition (EVP) is the honest account of what people get in return for their work — growth, total compensation, technology, culture, and more — used to attract, screen, and retain talent. Improving's writing argues it belongs to recruiting and retention, not just marketing: a clear EVP lets candidates self-identify their fit and makes the people who join likelier to stay. Awards, inclusive policies, and performance-linked rewards all become evidence for the proposition.

## Key Claims

- A clear EVP screens out candidates who won't work and is organically attractive to those who share your language, saving recruiter time.
- EVPs mix tangible and intangible elements — growth, total compensation, technology, culture — and reduce the effort candidates spend evaluating fit.
- Employees who "get" the EVP make similar referrals, pose less retention risk, and onboard more smoothly into culture, values, and systems.
- Building an EVP draws on engagement, candidate, and new-hire surveys; People & Culture data (demographics, promotions, benefits, retention, compensation); a talent-marketing asset inventory; and direct conversations with employees.
- Improving shared national tax-savings legislation as one-time bonuses of up to $1,000 for many US employees, starting with the most consistently profitable offices — framed as appreciation for the "Involvement" core value.
- Being named #1 Best Company to Work for in Texas (12th year on the list, survey-based) is used as external evidence of the proposition.
- Inclusive, family-friendly policies — flexible hours, remote options, mentorship, and visible role models — are presented as retention levers, backed by cited statistics on women in tech leadership.
