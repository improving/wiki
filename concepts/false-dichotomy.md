# false dichotomy

A false dichotomy is an either/or framing that presents two options as the only ones available, when the real choice is broader or the two sides can be integrated. Across this corpus it is a recurring thinking error — applied to parenting, to truth and division, and most explicitly to the phrase "work-life balance."

## Key Claims

- Work-life balance is itself a false dichotomy: you cannot balance something that is always in flux, and the framing creates anxiety and feelings of inadequacy.
- Work is part of life, so the task is integration and finding the good in it, not walling the two off.
- Linking fitting in to happiness creates a false either/or for gifted kids, who think differently and often sit outside friend groups; gifted means two standard deviations above the mean, not merely high-achieving.
- Divisiveness is not the issue; what you divide into is — dividing by differences breeds conflict, while dividing by shared values drives change.
- A lack of division means no boundaries, which harms relationships.
- Keep things simple, but avoid false dichotomies that block compromise.
