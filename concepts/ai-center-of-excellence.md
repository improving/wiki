# AI Center of Excellence

An AI Center of Excellence is a cross-functional body that centralizes AI standards, use-case intake, and governance without becoming a bottleneck. Its first job is a three-tier split of practices; its second is participating during the work through [quality gates](/concepts/quality-gates.md) rather than auditing afterward. The sources warn a CoE can easily become internal compliance police, so it must define success by business outcomes rather than the services it provides.

## Key Claims

- The minimum viable AI CoE is two people with clear ownership of the blockers and standing authority to say no to an unready pilot.
- The three tiers are "always true, centrally managed" (e.g., a PCI compliance check no team should customize), "shared definition framework" teams may adjust with reason, and "only true for one team, fully democratized."
- Without a CoE, organizations get either fragmented practice (five teams building five versions of the same compliance check) or a governance binder nobody reads.
- Governance must check output as it is produced: a quarterly audit of a 200-outputs-per-week agent accumulates roughly 2,600 documents before review.
- Roles include an engagement/program lead, a data steward/compliance owner scoped to the mandatory tier, an embedded technical role working real backlog, and a change/enablement lead (the most understaffed).
- Agile precedent: a center of excellence should be a consultative helper, not the internal compliance police, and should be measured by business outcomes such as customer satisfaction, cost, and time to market.
- First 90 days: two to three weeks mapping readiness, a documented three-tier split, one pilot cohort on real backlog, and an identity conversation run by the change lead — starting with four people.
