# career development

Career development is the deliberate growth of skills, judgment, and roles over the course of a career — treated in this corpus as compounding wealth rather than a ladder. The recurring advice is to organize growth into rhythms, own your goals with an accountability partner, and weight investment toward durable assets like relationships, reputation, and judgment.

## Key Claims

- The individual-contributor-to-leader path runs through deep technical mastery in one area, a strong involved mentor, a brand of keeping commitments and delivering results, personal accountability, health, and eventually servant leadership.
- Improving's PATH program uses three meetings — StrengthsFinder/DISC results and work history, then goal-setting — followed by repeating 90-day cycles with a coach.
- PATH's three stated success factors are writing goals down, owning your own goals, and having an accountability partner; leaders describe careers as a rock wall, not a ladder.
- The time-horizon model organizes compounding behaviors into daily habits, weekly planning behaviors, and quarterly investments; skills depreciate while relationships, reputation, and judgment do not.
- 94 percent of employees said they would stay longer at a company that invests in their growth (LinkedIn), and growth plans should be customized per person.
- Imposter syndrome is part of the journey; a common symptom is not recognizing your own accomplishments, so acknowledging them deliberately matters.
- Autonomy, recognition, fair pay, and regular feedback are the levers leaders use to retain people.
