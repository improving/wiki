# Design Maturity

Design maturity is the extent to which design is integrated into an organization's culture, processes, and decision-making — treating design as a problem-solving and innovation tool rather than aesthetics or usability polish. Improving's design leaders describe it as a deliberate, ongoing capability that bridges vision and execution, and that grows through outcomes focus, collaboration, and design thinking. Raising it takes leadership commitment and organization-wide change, not just hiring designers.

## Key Claims

- Design maturity means aligning design practices with user needs and business goals, not just producing visual polish.
- The Nielsen Norman Group UX maturity model frames the path from "Absent" to "Integrated and Optimizing," where design is a core strategic driver.
- An InVision study found only a small percentage of companies reach the highest design-maturity levels.
- Reaching maturity requires leadership advocacy plus user research, collaboration, experimentation, and integration into the product lifecycle.
- Design distills complex problems into actionable solutions and should be embedded deeply across the product lifecycle to surface opportunities and anticipate challenges.
- Design leadership orchestrates, mentors, and inspires rather than only creating; success comes from collaboration with clients and partners on co-created solutions.
- A design-led approach prioritizes outcomes over outputs and defines success metrics with clients up front.
- Maturity does not happen overnight: leaders should assess current gaps, invest in teams, and make design part of strategy — empowering teams with design thinking's empathy, ideation, and purposeful iteration.
