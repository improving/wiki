# shadow AI

Shadow AI is the use of AI tools adopted outside IT approval — the shadow IT story repeating at far greater speed and scale. Approval queues do not stop adoption so much as push it underground, and because the governance gap is a design problem rather than a discipline problem, the winning response is to make the sanctioned option competitive with the unsanctioned one and to govern by reversibility rather than restriction.

## Key Claims

- 78% of AI users at work bring their own tools outside IT approval; one CIO calls shadow AI "shadow IT at 10x."
- IBM's breach research found unsanctioned AI tools inside 43% of security incidents, more than doubling in one year.
- 65% of AI users fear falling behind if they do not adopt AI now, which drives adoption ahead of approval.
- Restriction-based programs only make the AI inventory harder to find, and you cannot govern what you cannot see.
- Shadow AI discovery is layer two of a four-layer runtime governance stack — policy, AI inventory and shadow AI discovery, model monitoring and drift detection, and runtime policy enforcement — and most enterprises have not done a real inventory.
- In healthcare, shadow AI is flagged the #1 provider risk for 2026; one framework addresses it with four pillars: policy, technology, training, and monitoring.
- When the compliant tool is also the best tool, shadow adoption declines naturally, so enablement beats restriction.
