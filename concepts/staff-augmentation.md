# staff augmentation

A sourcing model that adds individual external specialists into an existing team, filling capacity or skill gaps while the client retains architecture, prioritization, and delivery ownership. It sits at the flexible, low-commitment end of the engagement spectrum — the mirror image of [managed services](/concepts/managed-services.md), which transfers operational responsibility to the provider.

## Key Claims

- Staff augmentation adds specialists into existing teams while the client keeps architecture and delivery ownership, at the cost of limited delivery accountability.
- It is distinguished from managed services, which carries a contractual SLA, and from break-fix support, which is reactive and unpredictable.
- It is one of the four common offshore engagement shapes, alongside dedicated teams/PODs, managed services, and end-to-end delivery.
- The model suits short-term, scarce expertise: for a company needing a specific capability briefly, partnering for capacity and capability is the pragmatic choice.
- Selection should weigh time zone, cost, scale, language, culture, and team stability, since augmented staff work inside the client's own process.
- The nearshore context makes it workable: a 6–8 hour overlap with US business hours supports the live collaboration embedded staff require.
- Because the client owns delivery, augmentation offers flexibility but no guaranteed outcome — the risk that remains with the buyer is the reason outcome-based models exist.
