# comparison trap

The comparison trap is the habit of measuring what you have, do, or earn against other people rather than against your own potential. Across this corpus it is named as the real enemy of contentment: comparison and jealousy live inside us, and the gap they create is adjustable rather than fixed.

## Key Claims

- Comparison with others robs you of gratitude and joy; compare yourself to your own potential instead.
- Different strengths, abilities, and experiences justify different rules — forcing homogeny in the name of fairness is destructive.
- The distance between what you have and what you want is adjustable, not fixed, yet most people quietly raise their expectations every year without deciding to.
- Kids across income levels share more than the gap between their families' means suggests.
- Children who receive everything come to treat it as a baseline.
- Wanting a better life for your kids does not guarantee it, and people get so wrapped up in improving life that they forget to appreciate it; appreciation is presence with the present, not laziness.
