# Software Craftsmanship

Software craftsmanship is an identity defined by the standards a developer refuses to abandon under pressure, not by a job title or a toolset. Across this corpus it is treated as a behavioral discipline: it determines whether teams accumulate or avoid [Technical Debt](/concepts/technical-debt.md), and it is sustained by peers rather than by individual willpower.

## Key Claims

- A craftsman is defined by the standards they refuse to abandon; shipping below your standard under deadline is a moral choice, not neutral pragmatism.
- [Peer accountability](/concepts/peer-accountability.md) turns personal standards into a durable team culture.
- Automation and [automated testing](/concepts/automated-testing.md) are professional obligations, not conveniences.
- Technical debt is the compounding result of continually trading craftsmanship for velocity, and AI accelerates accumulation when standards are absent.
- A must-read canon starts with *Head First OOAD* and *Clean Code*, spans *Design Patterns* and *Enterprise Integration Patterns*, and includes *The Phoenix Project*.
