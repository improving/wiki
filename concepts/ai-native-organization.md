# AI-native organization

An AI-native organization is one that redesigns its processes and architecture around AI as a foundation, rather than adding AI capabilities onto workflows that were designed without it. This corpus argues the distinction determines whether an organization captures incremental gains or transformational advantage.

The feature model treats AI as an add-on and caps improvement at 10 to 30 percent on existing processes. The foundation model instead redesigns processes around where human judgment actually matters, making AI a designed node in the system rather than a productivity accessory. Drawing on Hutchins' distributed cognition, one post holds that intelligence lives across people, tools, and protocols, so AI's value depends on how well it is integrated, not how capable the model is.

## Key Claims

- Foundation layers are model-agnostic and survive model churn, while feature integrations are tied to a specific model's lifetime.
- Most digital transformation is renovation mislabeled as transformation — new tools on unchanged processes.
- Treating AI as a productivity add-on speeds up individual nodes without improving the system as a whole.
- The useful question is how well the system is designed, not how smart the model is.
