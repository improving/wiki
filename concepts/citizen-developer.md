# citizen developer

A citizen developer is a non-professional developer — a power user, analyst, or subject-matter expert — who builds applications and automations with low-code tools. In this corpus the model is Microsoft-centric: Power Apps shifts application work from IT to citizen developers who build apps that read and write enterprise data, and Copilot Studio lets them assemble AI copilots without deep technical skills. The model matters because it widens who can deliver software, but bare-bones results are ugly, awkward, and brittle unless design and governance are added.

## Key Claims

- Power Apps shifts application work from IT to citizen developers, letting power users build low-code apps that read and write enterprise data.
- Power Pages extends that reach to external websites and portals with a low barrier to entry for non-specialists.
- Copilot Studio builds on Power Virtual Agents and lets power users create copilots from templates or from scratch, deployable where employees already work.
- Citizen-built apps risk poor UX and brittle architecture; adding UX and architectural design resources is what turns a bare-bones app into a maintainable product.
- The UX designer and the Power App developer should collaborate from the start — joint stakeholder interviews, low-fidelity wireframes, and shared user flows.
- Governance is still required: Power Pages uses web roles and table and page permissions, and Power Apps rides on Microsoft 365 authentication and an admin center for lifecycle.
