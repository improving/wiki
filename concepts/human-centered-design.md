# human-centered design

Human-centered design starts from what users actually need and the context they work in, rather than from the technology or the deliverable. Improving's UX writers trace the idea back to the Bauhaus (1919), which framed design as a method of problem-solving, and to Don Norman, who coined "user experience" at Apple in the early 1990s. It matters because products that miss human expectations fail their organizations — and because UX is now treated as part of business strategy alongside service design and customer experience.

## Key Claims

- The common misconception reduces UX to aesthetics or interfaces, ignoring its effect on functionality, user satisfaction, and business success.
- Empathy reveals what clients need, not just what they ask for; two stories show a system that "worked" but could not deliver the trust users valued, and an impersonal car-maintenance experience.
- A human-centered project kickoff aligns client goals, user needs, and technical feasibility through stakeholder interviews, user research, competitive analysis, journey maps, and design workshops.
- Design leadership orchestrates and aligns rather than only producing interfaces; one engagement lifted user interaction 30% after goals were tied to engagement metrics.
- AI accelerates research synthesis, ideation, and prototyping, but conversations with users and the judgment calls that make design meaningful stay human — "what AI produces is raw material, what you choose to pursue is the design."
