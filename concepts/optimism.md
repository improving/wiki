# Optimism

Optimism in this corpus is not cheerfulness but a working premise: the future can be improved, so it is worth acting on. It appears both as a personal precondition for taking responsibility and as the stance that receives truer feedback, because creators are judged by market action rather than by commentary.

## Key Claims

- Without belief that the future can be better, people will not take responsibility for improving it; optimism is a requirement for living, not an optional attitude.
- Pessimism is an easy, intellectually lazy trap, and most pessimistic predictions have turned out to be false.
- Intellectuals are rewarded for pessimism while entrepreneurs are rewarded for optimism — an incentive bias that skews public discourse.
- Creators get feedback from real action, which aligns more closely with reality than commentary does.
- The practical instruction is to lean into change regardless of the pain or effort involved.
