# strategic demand management

Strategic demand management is the practice of balancing what the market wants against the fixed capacity of the teams delivering it. It works two directions at once — aligning products to the market and aligning team capacity to the work — and uses systems thinking, early viability tests, and explicit [work-in-progress](/concepts/work-in-progress.md) limits to keep investment matched to real value.

## Key Claims

- Strategic planning gives the whole organization a sense of purpose and direction, not just leadership, and demand management must balance both product-market fit and capacity ([Strategic Demand Management: What's the Rush?](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Fixed headcount forces "fussy" prioritization, simplifying decisions about what to focus on and when ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Validate "big bets" with small experiments and MVP-style viability checks before spending heavily ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Managing WIP improves quality, morale, trust, and forecasting, and prevents burnout ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- The restaurant analogy: a restaurant balances demand to capacity live, putting customers on a waitlist when the dinner rush exceeds servers ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Applied to mid-sprint "urgent" requests: make trade-offs explicit, let the people doing the work decide what gives, and record the change so recurring patterns can drive a policy tweak ([Struggling to Deliver Done Work, Part 2](/sources/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-2-stakeholder.md)).
