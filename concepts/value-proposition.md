# value proposition

The specific value an offering delivers to a defined customer, stated from that customer's perspective rather than the provider's. In this corpus the concept appears in two guises: as a positioning tool that lets the right customers (or job candidates) self-select, and as the thing under pressure when AI compresses production work and clients stop paying for hours saved.

## Key Claims

- A clear value proposition screens out the wrong audience and is organically attractive to those who share the provider's language — the "Cookie Monsters," "Hard-No's," and "Cookie Curious" of the bakery metaphor.
- The same logic applies to the employee value proposition: tangible and intangible elements such as growth, compensation, technology, and culture let candidates self-identify, reducing recruiter effort and retention risk.
- For consultancies, demonstrating ROI is part of the value proposition, and the tracking metrics are NPS, CSAT, and CLTV.
- AI is breaking the time-and-materials frame the value proposition used to rest on: McKinsey is moving 25% of its global fees to outcome-based pricing and Bain says 30% of its work is already AI-enabled, heading to 50%.
- Clients watching a 40-hour analysis compress into 15 minutes refuse to pay for the hours saved, so value has to be re-expressed as outcomes rather than effort.
- The value then shifts to judgment rather than production — deciding what to produce, how to frame results, and what to ask next — work models cannot compress.
- Improving's 78% NPS and 9.1/10 market study score are presented as evidence that clients perceive the value, while critical feedback on pricing and partnerships shows the proposition is continuously renegotiated.
