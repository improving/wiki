# emotional regulation

Emotional regulation is the discipline of letting feelings inform you without letting them steer. The corpus's recurring formulation is that emotions are "great servants but poor masters" — they color reasoning and cloud judgment precisely while feeling justified, so the work is to notice them early and respond rather than react.

## Key Claims

- Nothing can be done with anger that cannot be done better without it, yet the emotion feels justified while it distorts judgment.
- Physical cues like a dry mouth and sweating signal rising emotion; absolutes ("always," "never") and interrupting others are verbal tip-offs.
- You cannot reason someone out of fear, because emotion subsumes reasoning — the opposite of fear is compassion, not fearlessness.
- Acknowledging unrecognized emotions helps people process them, and shared experience helps them elevate out of an emotional response.
- What spills out when you are bumped into reflects what has been brewing inside; exhaustion raises emotional volume and exposes fragility.
- Rest, stillness, and connection shape what comes out of you, and responding instead of reacting is a practice, not a trait.
