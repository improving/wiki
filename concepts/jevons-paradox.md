# Jevons paradox

The observation that making a resource cheaper to use tends to increase total consumption of it rather than reduce it. In this corpus it is the mechanism that keeps AI's economic effect ambiguous: automating a task can expand demand for the work rather than shrink the labor around it.

## Key Claims

- Jobs are bundles of tasks that technology hits unevenly; [demand-limited tasks](/concepts/demand-limited-vs-collapsable-tasks.md) need role evolution while collapsable tasks need role redefinition.
- Jevons-style rebound can turn a demand-limited task into an execution-limited one, so freed time gets absorbed by new demand.
- The diagnostic is what you would do with the other half of the freed time.
- Every major tech wave since 1950 brought confident, quantified, wrong economic predictions.
- [Daron Acemoglu](/entities/daron-acemoglu.md) estimates a 1.1–1.6% GDP gain over a decade, about one-fifth of Goldman's projection; the [productivity paradox](/concepts/productivity-paradox.md) rhyme is hard to escape.
- MIT NANDA found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact.
