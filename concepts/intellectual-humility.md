# Intellectual Humility

Intellectual humility is the willingness to say "I don't know" and to ask questions before concluding that someone else is wrong. This corpus treats it as both an honesty practice and a reasoning practice: it protects the accuracy of what you claim and the quality of what you learn from disagreement.

## Key Claims

- Saying "I don't know" is hard but necessary; fabricating an answer compounds a knowledge gap with a lie.
- Knowledge gaps can be remedied, but dishonesty is harder to fix — the asymmetry favors admitting ignorance.
- Listeners usually detect the bluff even when they are too polite to say so.
- Different backgrounds and experiences create different filters, so people reach different conclusions from different contexts.
- Understanding another's context deepens both the relationship and your self-knowledge.
- Ask questions before arriving at conclusions about others; disagreement usually reflects difference, not error.
