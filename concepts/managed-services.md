# managed services

Handing responsibility for running systems to a provider who operates them continuously against agreed service levels, rather than delivering a project or supplying staff. The corpus frames the model's value as the shift from reactive break-fix work to proactive monitoring, maintenance, and planning — with contractual SLAs as the thing that distinguishes it from staff augmentation and support.

## Key Claims

- Managed services differs from staff augmentation (no SLA guarantee) and break-fix support (reactive, unpredictable) by combining proactive operation with contractual SLAs.
- The global managed services market is projected to grow from $437.3B in 2026 to $847.4B by 2033; managed security services are growing at a 16.4% CAGR.
- Delivery models include fully managed IT, managed security (MSSP/MDR), CSP and licensing management, co-managed IT, and application/platform production support.
- Proactive services include 24/7 monitoring, patching and maintenance, multi-layered security, strategic planning, and automated backup and disaster recovery.
- Improving's Canada Post Track and Trace case produced a 95% reduction in unscheduled downtime and cut Mean Time to Acknowledgment from 10.3 to 5.11 minutes.
- AI has become an operating layer inside managed services: predictive maintenance, real-time threat response, automation of routine tasks, and AI-assisted support.
- Selection criteria include defined SLAs and escalation paths, true 24/7 coverage, security and compliance alignment, rigorous onboarding, and readable reporting.
- Common pricing models are per user, per device, fixed monthly fee, and hourly for out-of-scope work; buyers should scrutinize guaranteed response times and uptime.
