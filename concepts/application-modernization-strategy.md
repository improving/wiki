# 7 R strategies

The 7 R strategies are the menu of migration options used to plan application modernization: retain, retire, repurchase, rehost, replatform, refactor/rearchitect, and rebuild. Choosing among them is a matching exercise — each strategy fits a different objective, risk appetite, and system — and the choice is preceded by stakeholder alignment and an audit of business criticality and technical debt.

## Key Claims

- The seven strategies are retain, retire, repurchase, rehost, replatform, refactor/rearchitect, and rebuild.
- Approaches range from rehosting (lift-and-shift) through replatforming (managed services) and refactoring, with a full rebuild reserved for systems too brittle to migrate incrementally.
- Signals to modernize include end-of-life support, licensing costs outpacing business value, week- or month-long deployment cycles, retiring legacy talent, and audit findings.
- Start by engaging stakeholders, evaluating personnel, budget, and process constraints, then auditing applications by business criticality and technical debt.
- Modernization often demands a cultural shift, eased by training and involving employees rather than imposed on them.
- A modernized base enables AI, streaming data, IoT, and analytics, and improves security posture and regulatory compliance.
- Legacy systems are accumulated answers to real problems: treat migration as archaeology protected by automated tests, not demolition.
- Case evidence: Berkshire Hathaway Energy moved a legacy Java Swing app to React and Java microservices on Azure with Kafka; NCLH rebuilt its reservation platform with domain-driven design and reactive architecture.
