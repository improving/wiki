# Web 3.0

Web 3.0 is framed in this corpus as the shift from Web 2.0's asynchronous likes-and-comments to synchronous, real-time live streaming, and the creator community rising with it. The business tension is that employees and customers create content whether or not the company sanctions it, so firms must decide how to engage creators rather than control them. The decentralized ledger technology often named alongside it is [blockchain](/concepts/blockchain.md).

## Key Claims

- Web 2.0 was an asynchronous global conversation; Web 3.0 shifts it synchronous and real-time.
- Live streams with audience engagement provide an immersive experience for creators and consumers.
- Employees will engage their own audiences regardless of company policy, becoming enablers or inhibitors of the brand.
- Businesses should consider platforms including Twitch, LinkedIn Live, YouTube Live, Discord, and Steam.
- Suggested moves: brand your own stream, join employee streams, sponsor or co-brand with followed creators, and use streaming in marketing.
- Aggregation tools StreamYard and Restream let you stream once to multiple platforms.
- Heavy-handed content policies may backfire; hosting panels on ethical content creation and blurring work/public/private personas is more productive.
- The underlying ledger is often confused with currency and anonymity; it is a distributed, verifiable record rather than digital money.
