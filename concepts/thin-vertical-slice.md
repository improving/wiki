# thin vertical slice

A thin vertical slice is a narrow, end-to-end piece of a product that delivers real, measurable value early — one job to be done, one user group, one quantifiable outcome. It is the antidote to overambitious scope: instead of building every layer of an "enterprise brain" at once, teams ship a small working path through the whole system, learn from it, and let each phase fund the next.

## Key Claims

- Define a thin vertical slice as one job to be done, one user group, and one quantifiable outcome, scoped for a 12-week win ([Overambitious Scope](/sources/overambitious-scope.md)).
- Ship behind feature flags, A/B test impact on KPIs, establish kill criteria, and review decisions every two to three sprints ([Overambitious Scope](/sources/overambitious-scope.md)).
- Example: an underwriting assistant that summarized three documents cut review time 25%; later phases added policy lookups and exception handling ([Overambitious Scope](/sources/overambitious-scope.md), [Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail](/sources/top-10-reasons-ai-projects-fail.md)).
- Improving's ten AI failure patterns include overambitious scope, and their fixes call for 12-week thin vertical slices and cross-functional squads ([Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail](/sources/top-10-reasons-ai-projects-fail.md)).
- A healthcare case reframed a stalled chatbot around one workflow, handling 80% of repetitive cases and cutting labor hours 27% ([Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail](/sources/top-10-reasons-ai-projects-fail.md)).
- In Scrum, each Sprint should ship an end-to-end, usable slice; splitting a feature by layer delivers no usable value in the first Sprint ([Transformative Insights](/sources/transformative-insights-enhancing-development-effectiveness-through.md)).
