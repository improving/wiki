# offshore outsourcing

Contracting delivery to distant, lower-cost regions — historically Asia Pacific, Eastern Europe, and the Middle East & Africa. The corpus describes the model as having moved from cost arbitrage to a cornerstone of global digital strategy, trading real-time collaboration for scale, niche skills, and follow-the-sun coverage, while introducing latency, miscommunication, and jurisdictional risk.

## Key Claims

- The global offshore software development market is valued at over $150 billion, with thousands of vendors and widely varying quality.
- Asia Pacific offers unmatched scale — India alone produces over 1.5 million engineering graduates a year — plus a 10–12 hour offset for follow-the-sun work, at the cost of real-time collaboration and higher attrition.
- Eastern Europe suits complex R&D, FinTech, and cybersecurity with GDPR compliance, but carries geopolitical risk and rising costs; the Middle East & Africa is a value-scaling option with variable infrastructure maturity.
- Around 70% of companies cite access to specialized skills as a top reason to offshore; about 53% name data security as a top concern, and roughly 54% report cultural or communication challenges.
- Common failure modes include "ghost firms" that vanish after deposits, vendors holding IP hostage, and teams secretly subcontracting to unqualified third parties.
- Hidden costs include onboarding, knowledge transfer, security tooling, and vendor transition.
- Hybrid or onshore leadership models reduce the "thrown over the wall" problem and improve alignment and feedback loops.
- Credibility signals include partner status (Microsoft Gold, AWS Advanced), certifications (ISO 9001, CMMI, SOC 2, ISO 27001), and a turnover rate below 15%.
