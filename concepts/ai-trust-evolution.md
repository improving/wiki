# AI Trust Evolution

AI trust evolution describes how organizations earn their way from assisted to autonomous AI. It treats trust as something extended incrementally, the way social capital is built between people, and it argues the shift from permission to autonomy is only safe when a governance engine backs it.

## Key Claims

- Most organizations sit at Stage 2 or 3 of the 8-Stage AI Trust Evolution, stalling on trust questions rather than capability questions.
- Organizations extend too much trust to AI too fast, skipping a track record; broken commitments deplete an AI system's social capital faster than they build it.
- Audit trails, boundary enforcement, and gates keep the trust balance from depleting.
- AI readiness is mostly a psychology problem, not a tooling problem; practitioners wrongly equate using AI well with trusting it implicitly.
- Announcing AI transformation before earning it applies the look-at-me tax.
- AI dissolves Agile's scarcity-driven trade-offs: the manifesto's "over" becomes multiplication between both sides under governance, across the Constraint, Synergy, and Intent eras.
- Career capital shifts from execution speed and credentials toward judgment, mentorship, and defining work.
