# digital transformation

Digital transformation is the work of reorienting an organization around digital capability so technology actually improves the business, rather than adopting tools to check a box. In this corpus it is framed as a strategy-and-execution problem spanning [AI adoption](/concepts/ai-adoption.md), [application modernization](/concepts/application-modernization.md), [data platform modernization](/concepts/data-platform-modernization.md), and [platform engineering](/concepts/platform-engineering.md) — and it fails most often on alignment, measurement, and maintenance rather than technology.

## Key Claims

- For most organizations the data is not the problem — actionable insight is; technology should meaningfully improve the business, not just be adopted ([Powering Modern Enterprises](/sources/powering-modern-enterprises-an-overview-of-improvings-expertise.md)).
- 70% of North American IT budgets go to maintaining existing infrastructure and operations, leaving only 30% for innovation, while 88% of CIOs say they are increasingly innovation-focused ([The Benefit of a Corporate Innovation Lab](/sources/innovation-lab-benefits.md)).
- The "technology paradox": rushed, trend-driven adoption (e.g. building a custom ERP instead of buying scalable SaaS) creates cost and risk, and unmaintained systems become a "technology maintenance overhang" that forces later overhaul ([How Technology is Holding Back Business Value](/sources/how-technology-is-holding-back-business-value.md)).
- Durable modernization depends on trust and perseverance, not hype — mRNA vaccine work traces to 1990s experiments long before precision-medicine excitement ([Medical Modernization](/sources/medical-modernization.md)).
- Unmanaged complexity raises costs and lowers quality; PMI data cited holds that organizations with a consistent project-management approach report 38% more projects meeting their original goals ([Empowering IT Projects with Modern Delivery Frameworks](/sources/empowering-it-projects-with-modern-delivery-frameworks.md)).
- UX is part of human-centered transformation, not confined to designers: design evolved from the Bauhaus through Don Norman's coining of "user experience" and is now treated as business strategy ([UX by Everyone Pt 1](/sources/ux-by-everyone-pt-1-the-ux-mindset.md)).
