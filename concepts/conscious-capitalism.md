# conscious capitalism

Conscious capitalism is a business philosophy that assumes profit rather than apologizing for it, and then focuses on how that profit is earned and spent. Across this corpus it appears as both an operating stance — the stakeholder model extended to employees, suppliers, community, and customers — and a teaching subject for emerging entrepreneurs.

## Key Claims

- Business requires profits to keep providing opportunity; the question is what the profit is for, not whether it should exist.
- The stakeholder model extends value beyond shareholders to partners, employees, suppliers, customers, and the community.
- Culture happens by default or by decision, so it needs deliberate attention rather than hope.
- Growing yourself is the best way to grow your business.
- [Improving](/entities/improving.md) was built on conscious capitalism ideas from the start, making the philosophy an operating identity rather than a campaign.
- Speaking to Romanian business students, one author argues conscious capitalism and strong culture can guide emerging entrepreneurs through a post-communist transition that shapes their mindset toward capitalism.
