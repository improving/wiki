# gratitude

Gratitude in this corpus is not a feeling that arrives but a practice that is chosen — the mechanism by which contentment is learned. The recurring claim is that humans are wired to see the lack before the plenty, so appreciation has to be deliberately trained against that default.

## Key Claims

- We notice absence before abundance; pattern-matching for absence blinds us to what we already have, and naming gratitude starts a virtuous cycle.
- Contentment cannot be bestowed by other people or things; it is learned through the experience of lacking it, then practicing gratitude.
- Chasing happiness directly drives materialism and comparison, whereas people are happiest growing toward their potential — so growth, not happiness, is the better goal.
- The gap between what you have and what you want is adjustable, not fixed; most people quietly raise their expectations every year without deciding to.
- Comparison and jealousy are the real enemy and live within us, so the better comparison is against your own potential.
- Children who receive everything treat it as a baseline; they learn restlessness or appreciation from what you model.
