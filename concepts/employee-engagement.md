# employee engagement

Employee engagement is the discretionary effort and commitment people bring to work beyond the minimum their job description requires. In this corpus it is treated as an output of culture, recognition, and connection rather than a program you install: leaders living stated values, programs that keep a distributed company connected, and recognition that lands all shape whether people give more than the floor.

## Key Claims

- Improving was named the #1 Best Company to Work for in Texas, its 12th year on the list, in a ranking based on employee surveys; a leader credits guiding principles being lived daily rather than stated on a website.
- Quiet quitting — doing the bare minimum — is read as a symptom of disengagement or a work-life-balance problem; a Gallup poll found at least half of American workers admit to doing the bare minimum.
- Viva Engage aims to recreate spontaneous office interactions and foster belonging across locations, claimed to strengthen culture, knowledge sharing, decisions, and retention.
- Improving's four connection programs — Come Together, PATH, ImprovingU, and the annual Las Vegas retreat — are argued to pay back in engagement and retention; 1,356 Improvers participated in Come Together at least once in 2023.
- Growth investment matters: 94 percent of employees said they would stay longer at a company that invests in their growth (LinkedIn Workforce Learning Report).
- A learning strategy measures engagement through performance and satisfaction surveys, not just completion rates.
- Manager support is a lever: employees whose managers actively support AI use are 8.7 times more likely to say AI transformed their work.
