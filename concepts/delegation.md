# delegation

Delegation is the deliberate handoff of work, decisions, and expertise to others so that a leader's attention is freed for the problems only they can see. Across this corpus it is the dividing line between leading and merely working: leaders who cannot delegate become firefighters, and leaders who delegate badly just abstract responsibility without shrinking it.

## Key Claims

- The best leaders anticipate problems before they happen; strategy is long-term while tactics are short-term and reactive.
- Delegation and trusting the team free leaders to identify danger zones and mitigate them before crises start.
- You must share responsibility and give up expertise to lead well — the biggest growth is what you foster in others, not yourself.
- Leaders should set success criteria and follow up regularly; the Peter Principle results when managers will not let people go.
- One post pushes back: delegation only abstracts responsibility rather than shrinking it, so feeling busy comes from excess surface area, not lack of time.
- The happiest people keep their surface area small and focus on a few things rather than spreading across many.
