# cross-functional teams

A cross-functional team carries every skill needed to take an idea from spark to ship — design, development, and testing together — so work does not queue behind another team's priorities. In this corpus the pattern is the antidote to dependency-driven delivery, whether the work is a product increment or an AI initiative, and the failure mode is a team that stays busy without producing finished, feedback-ready work.

## Key Claims

- Scrum expects cross-functional teams with all the skills to take an idea from spark to ship — designers, developers, and testers together — which yields both speed and ownership.
- Non-cross-functional teams create delays waiting on others, dependencies tied to other teams' priorities, coordination overhead, clashing priorities, and rising complexity.
- Dependencies are a holdover from traditional project management (Analyze, Design, Build, Test, Deploy), which rotated specialists to avoid idle time and optimized staying busy over delivering value.
- The recommended first move is small: invite a UX expert part-time or embed an infrastructure/DevOps specialist for a few sprints — and grow the capability in the team rather than merely borrowing it.
- In AI work, the "AI wizard in the corner" builds brittle, unowned solutions; lasting delivery needs a squad of data engineers, ML/GenAI engineers, domain SMEs, and application developers, plus a named business owner.
- UX integrates into product teams through shared principles and a collaborative, cross-functional mindset rather than a fixed rulebook.
- Modernization advice is to reorganize into small cross-functional "two-pizza teams," each with its own CI/CD pipeline.
