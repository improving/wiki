# quiet quitting

Quiet quitting is the trend of doing only the bare minimum your job description requires — withdrawing the discretionary effort that engagement normally supplies. This corpus treats it as ambiguous rather than simply lazy: it can be a healthy boundary against overwork, or a symptom of disengagement rooted in poor management and broken engagement, and the practical response depends on which it is.

## Key Claims

- A Gallup poll found at least half of American workers admit to doing the bare minimum to meet their job description.
- Quiet quitting reads two ways: a boundary against overwork, or a symptom of disengagement — and remote work and poor management are cited as drivers.
- Balance tactics include setting boundaries, limiting hours, building relationships, taking vacations, and practicing self-care.
- Career-protecting tactics include weekly one-on-ones, clarifying how performance is measured and putting it in writing, and connecting your role to the organization's purpose.
- "Quiet firing" is emerging as a passive-aggressive way to pressure employees out, making a financial cushion important.
- Disengagement is not only an individual choice: manager engagement has declined at nearly three times the rate of employee engagement over three years, and employees with supportive managers are far more likely to be transformed by new tools.
