# nearshore outsourcing

Hiring delivery teams in geographically close, time-zone-aligned countries to combine the reliability of onsite talent with the affordability of outsourcing. The corpus treats Latin America as the archetypal nearshore region and argues the real advantage is not proximity itself but the synchronous collaboration and cultural alignment that proximity makes possible.

## Key Claims

- Proximity matters measurably: a one-hour increase in time-zone distance reduces synchronous communication by about 11%, which hurts productivity.
- Nearshore teams keep a 6–8 hour overlap with US business hours, enabling live stand-ups, sprint reviews, and faster feedback loops.
- Cost savings are typically 20–40% versus onshore (some sources cite 40–70%), while avoiding full-time benefits, training, and office-space costs.
- Latin America supplies the talent: 437 universities produce over 220,000 STEM graduates a year, feeding a pool of more than 2 million developers.
- Mexico, Chile, Guatemala, and Argentina have strong IP laws similar to the US and take data protection seriously.
- 87% of IT organizations have considered nearshore outsourcing to cut costs (Deloitte), and the nearshore market is projected to grow at a CAGR above 12% over five years (Statista).
- The harder work is culture and communication, not proximity: remote-first teams need structured standups, one-to-ones, async tools, and data-driven KPIs, and surveillance tools push talent away.
- The regional comparison warns that LATAM rates are higher than Asia or Africa and some economies are volatile, so a hybrid model can blend a collaboration-friendly region with a scale-friendly one.
