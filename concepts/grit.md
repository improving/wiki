# Grit

Grit is the willingness to go through hard things rather than around them. Across this corpus it is presented less as a personality trait than as a learned posture: people who came up easy read difficulty as a wrong turn, while people who have worked at hard things handle difficulty better.

## Key Claims

- There is no silver bullet; chasing an easier way is itself the wrong turn, and blaming externals avoids facing that life is simply hard.
- Those who worked at things handle difficulty better than those who did not — difficulty is a signal of value, not evidence of a wrong choice.
- Hard work and determination, not luck, carried a walk-on who earned his scholarship after two coaching changes to an undrafted NFL rookie and then a Super Bowl and three Pro Bowls.
- Fulfillment is made through hard work, not found; every job has its "bedpans," and the hard parts do not make it the wrong job.
- Passion does not equate to fun, easy, or always rewarding work, and research shows people are poor at affective forecasting about what will make them happy.
- Stop trying to find fulfillment and make it instead.
