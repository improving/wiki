# buy, build, or partner

The recurring strategic choice of how to acquire a capability: purchase it, build it in-house, or partner with someone who has it. The corpus argues the decision should be driven by need and horizon rather than ideology — and that in modern software the binary has largely dissolved into a blend, where you buy the baseline and build only where it defends value.

## Key Claims

- The answer depends on the need and situation: for short-term, scarce expertise, partner for capacity and capability; for long-term, larger-scale needs, building your own operation is viable given name recognition and financial stability.
- Buying an IT services company is rare for end-user companies, because the acquired firm's talent is already committed to its own clients and would need recruiting anyway.
- A BOT (Build Operate and Transfer) agreement lets a company invest in a trained team that transfers to it after two to three years for a one-time fee — sometimes the transfer never happens.
- For a services company needing scale, building is slower and uneven, buying adds clients, revenue, and an operations base but costs more with scarce targets, and partnering risks inconsistent quality across two cultures and time zones.
- Architecture evolution — objects to SOA to microservices — plus cloud adoption dissolved the buy-versus-build binary; mature organizations adopt a buy-and-build strategy, integrating third-party capabilities while reserving custom code for true differentiators.
- The failure mode is reinventing the wheel: teams "spend a year reproducing baseline capabilities of a foundation model or a SaaS API that could be delivered today," driven by innovation bias, not-invented-here syndrome, and poor time-to-value awareness.
- A buy-build-blend model applies the test directly — buy when time-to-value matters, build when defensibility requires it, blend when integration wins — and customize only domain grounding, proprietary tools, or unique datasets.
- Focus on what a third-party solution provides, not how it provides it; if the "how" is your secret sauce, that is exactly the part worth building.
