# cognitive scaffolding

Cognitive scaffolding is any external structure that holds and extends a team's thinking so it does not depend on a single person's memory. This corpus treats [shared vocabulary](/concepts/shared-vocabulary.md) and delivery tooling as the same phenomenon: both preserve knowledge beyond individuals, and good scaffolding produces compounding organizational capability rather than mere efficiency.

## Key Claims

- Teams cannot think efficiently about ideas they cannot name; false dichotomies persist when the synthesis has no name.
- Naming a recurring pattern makes it discussable and actionable, and shared vocabulary compounds over years, speeding architectural discussion.
- Precise vocabulary includes people while jargon excludes them; leaders should name important unnamed patterns and use the names consistently.
- Version control, tests, and CI/CD externalize and preserve team knowledge; they are already cognitive scaffolding, not just automation.
- Inconsistent AI terminology blocks shared understanding of [AI maturity](/concepts/ai-maturity-model.md), the same naming failure at organizational scale.
- [AI governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md) is cognitive scaffolding for the AI era; deploying agents without it repeats the mistake of CI without [quality gates](/concepts/quality-gates.md).
