# PI planning

PI (Product Increment) planning is the scaled-agile event where multiple teams and stakeholders align on a shared increment — typically a quarter's theme — before work begins. In this corpus it appears through a single detailed account: inviting UX into the session shapes the experience early and aligns the team with decision-makers. The corpus covers the ceremony lightly, so treat this page as a thin but honest summary.

## Key Claims

- Being present in PI Planning let UX hear requirements from Product, constraints from Development, and priorities early, keeping designs both intuitive and realistic ([UX at the Table](/sources/ux-at-the-table-elevating-pi-planning-with-service-design.md)).
- In one session, a Service Design Blueprint around the quarterly theme "Full Install Workflow" mapped everything from pre-installation tasks to early in-app steps, exposing workflow gaps and cross-system dependencies the team had not seen ([UX at the Table](/sources/ux-at-the-table-elevating-pi-planning-with-service-design.md)).
- The session brought Development, Product, UX, and client stakeholders together, each perspective adding a layer; a client stakeholder shared the blueprint with leadership as a quarterly highlight ([UX at the Table](/sources/ux-at-the-table-elevating-pi-planning-with-service-design.md)).
- Advice for practitioners new to PI Planning: connect with team leads beforehand, arrive with early concepts to spark discussion, and advocate for UX activities in the session ([UX at the Table](/sources/ux-at-the-table-elevating-pi-planning-with-service-design.md)).
- PI Planning is a signature ceremony of scaling frameworks; the corpus names Nexus, LeSS, SAFe, and Disciplined Agile and argues there is no best framework — fit depends on size, culture, and team maturity ([Agility: to Scale or Not to Scale](/sources/agile-to-scale-or-not-to-scale.md)).
