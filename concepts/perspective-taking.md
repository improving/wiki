# perspective-taking

Perspective-taking is the practice of reconstructing how a situation looks from inside someone else's context — their background, experience, and incentives — before judging their conclusion. The corpus frames it as the growth move in conflict: most disagreement reflects different filters rather than someone being wrong, and the inability to step outside your own story is what makes you the villain in everyone else's.

## Key Claims

- No one believes they are the villain of their own story; we excuse our own mistakes rather than face the wreckage we caused.
- Different backgrounds and experiences create different filters, so people reach different conclusions from the same facts.
- Understanding another's context deepens both the relationship and self-knowledge; disagreement usually reflects difference, not error.
- Ask questions before arriving at conclusions about others.
- Stepping into another's point of view is the real growth — and sometimes the answer is simply to say "I'm sorry."
