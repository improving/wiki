# Audit Trail

An audit trail is the persisted, inspectable record of what an AI system did, at what gate, and with what evidence. Across this corpus it is treated as one of the core components of governance — the thing that lets an organization answer "who approved this, and on what basis?" after the fact. Source control's auditability model maps directly onto traceable AI decisions.

## Key Claims

- Audit trails are one of five governance components, alongside boundary enforcement, quality gates, human approval, and access control.
- A written policy is not governance: agentic chains execute in milliseconds, so post-hoc review is impossible and evidence must be captured at runtime.
- Persisting per-gate traces rather than one collapsed status dropped gate-attribution loss from 100% to zero on wrongly funded loans.
- The Intelligence Operating System's Layer 2, the Governance Engine, supplies audit trails, boundary enforcement, and approval points.
- Audit trails, gates, and boundaries keep an AI system's trust balance from depleting; broken commitments drain social capital faster than they build it.
- Stage 3 autonomous agents require audit trails and error handling before production; scaling first damages confidence in autonomous AI.
- Interpretability findings do not transfer across models the way an audit log does, making trails the portable governance evidence.
