# mentorship

Mentorship is guided development through a more experienced partner — but in this corpus it is treated as a designed system rather than a series of helpful conversations. Done well it has a specific ask, a recurring cadence, and a mentee-owned agenda, and it works best as a two-way relationship that develops both people.

## Key Claims

- Accidental mentorship fails three ways: reactive engagement, vague asks, and undefined cadence; "can you mentor me?" is unactionable.
- A specific ask (thirty minutes on a concrete challenge) forces the mentee to do the thinking and makes the request answerable.
- Monthly is the minimum effective cadence, quarterly is better than nothing, and the mentee owns the agenda.
- The mentor's primary obligation is honest, specific, sometimes uncomfortable feedback rather than encouragement or validation.
- AI can supply knowledge and frameworks but not the specific context or social navigation a mentor who knows you provides.
- Improving's PATH program pairs employees with a coach over repeating 90-day goal cycles and is described as bigger than mentorship, since it can create new roles.
- Extending trust — giving someone opportunity, support, and honest feedback — is framed as the leadership form of mentorship, and new mentors often face imposter syndrome.
