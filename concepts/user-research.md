# user research

User research is how teams learn what users actually need — through interviews, observation, testing, and synthesis — instead of assuming. Improving writers treat it as the evidence base for human-centered design: it surfaces the "why" beneath surface requests, and it must be done often and early rather than once. As AI speeds up synthesis and prototyping, the conversations and judgment calls remain the human part of the work.

## Key Claims

- If you wholeheartedly disagree with user feedback, you are either asking the wrong questions or need to ask more; avoid leading and close-ended questions and resist putting your own biases on users.
- Dig past surface requests to the underlying "why" — the Henry Ford "faster horses" anecdote shows the danger of staying superficial.
- Observation counts as research: watch users do the task, and treat everyday surroundings — apps, kiosks, public spaces — as fieldwork.
- A structured kickoff runs stakeholder interviews, user research, and competitive analysis to uncover needs and opportunities, then grounds designs in feasibility with early developer collaboration.
- For data products, users arrive with predefined questions; discovery via interviews, empathy mapping, and user stories surfaces them, and task-based framing (Jobs to Be Done) is favored over personality personas.
- AI accelerates research synthesis and prototyping but rarely surfaced something the designer had not noticed — the gain was confidence rather than time saved.
- Developer experience research needs distinct playbooks for external developers who choose your tools and internal developers who have tools chosen for them.
