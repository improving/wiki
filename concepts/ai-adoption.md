# AI adoption

AI adoption is the organizational work of moving AI from scattered experiments into measured production workflows, and the sources agree the hard part is not the model but the surrounding system. The term itself is nearly information-free — "AI adoption" spans one employee using ChatGPT to fully autonomous systems — so practitioners stage it and tie it to evidence. Adoption stalls when it is treated as a tooling rollout rather than a redesign of workflows, data, trust, and ownership.

## Key Claims

- "AI adoption" is nearly information-free because it spans everything from a single ChatGPT user to Stage 7 autonomous systems; stage numbers (via the [AI trust evolution](/concepts/ai-trust-evolution.md)) restore precision.
- AI coding tools raised throughput (epics closed 66% faster, task throughput +34%) but bugs per developer rose 54% and code churn 861%, so adoption must be measured post-merge rather than by generation metrics.
- The feature model of adoption yields only 10–30% improvement because it is bounded by existing process efficiency; the foundation model redesigns processes around what AI dissolves.
- Eight in ten AI projects miss expected value, mostly from starting with technology instead of a defined problem and measurable outcome.
- Realistic productivity gains of 30–40% appear on text, research, pattern recognition, and summarization tasks — and losses appear when AI is applied elsewhere.
- Gen Z adoption is broad but paired with concern about ethics, privacy, and job displacement, making [AI literacy](/concepts/ai-literacy.md) and critical thinking part of the adoption story.
