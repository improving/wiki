# Capability Forecasting

Capability forecasting is the attempt to predict what AI systems will be able to do next, and to plan investment and safety work around that prediction. Across this corpus the exercise is treated as genuinely uncertain: scaling laws made it look predictable, while emergent capabilities and measurement artifacts make confident forecasts unsafe.

## Key Claims

- Scaling laws made AI look like normal science with predictable returns.
- Emergent capabilities and persistent anomalies challenge the scaling framework.
- Normal science frames extrapolation; a paradigm shift demands scenario planning — so the two frames imply different planning methods.
- Build evaluation and safety structures that work under both frames rather than betting on one.
- Emergent abilities appeared as phase transitions at critical model scale thresholds.
- Schaeffer et al. argue apparent emergence is often an artifact of discontinuous metrics.
- A non-ergodic framework says aggregate capability can mask per-use-case variability.
- Safety evals on current models may not predict future capability jumps, so pilots with smaller models are a lower bound, not a forecast.
