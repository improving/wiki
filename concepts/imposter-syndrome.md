# imposter syndrome

Imposter syndrome is the experience of doubting your own competence despite evidence to the contrary — feeling like a fraud even when you are meeting expectations. In this corpus it appears among new consultants, new mentors, and women in tech, and the recurring remedies are feedback-seeking, accepting not-knowing as a growth signal, and deliberately acknowledging your own accomplishments.

## Key Claims

- A new consultant describes imposter syndrome growing from self-doubt into questions of self-worth on his first client engagement.
- The fix he found was continuous feedback-seeking from peers and his Delivery Lead; "silence and guessing are your worst enemy."
- Not having an immediate answer does not make you an imposter — it is an opportunity to grow, since learning only happens when you do not already know.
- A common symptom is failing to recognize your accomplishments, so self-promotion and admiring your own work are treated as critical, not vain.
- New mentors can feel it too; a mentee's success (a Scrum Master offer) helped validate the mentor's value.
- Cynthia Turpin (International Women's Day 2022) notes imposter syndrome is common among women in IT — "you are not hired because you know all the things."
- Women of Improving devotes events to confidence and self-advocacy in emotionally safe spaces, addressing it at the group level.
