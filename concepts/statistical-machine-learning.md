# statistical machine learning

Statistical machine learning is the family of classical methods — linear and logistic regression, decision trees, and support vector machines — that predate and outlast deep learning's prominence. This corpus argues they remain valuable for their simplicity, interpretability, and efficiency, and that they serve as the rigor layer that validates and legitimizes advanced and generative AI.

## Key Claims

- Regression, decision trees, and SVMs are effective wherever transparency and speed matter, despite deep learning's prominence.
- Interpretability is their core advantage: healthcare, finance, and manufacturing need to understand the "why" behind a prediction, not just its accuracy.
- They provide a framework for testing hypotheses, evaluating model assumptions, and quantifying uncertainty — evidence for accountability and regulatory compliance.
- In generative AI they help identify biases, ensure data-distribution integrity, and supply quantitative metrics for output quality.
- They act as a first line of defense — robustness checks, bias assessment, and assumption validation before complex models are deployed, guarding against spurious correlations.
- Machine learning generally is defined by choosing an algorithm, setting hyperparameters, defining an accuracy metric, and training on many examples to produce a model.
