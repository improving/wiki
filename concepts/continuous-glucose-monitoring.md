# continuous glucose monitoring

Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) uses a wearable sensor to sample blood glucose around the clock instead of at isolated finger pricks. Across this corpus it is the instrument that turned vague dietary advice into measurable, personal feedback — and the clearest evidence that identical meals can produce different responses in different bodies.

## Key Claims

- [Levels](/entities/levels.md) paired with a Dexcom G6 revealed glucose responses to meals, and the Dexcom G6 syncs continuously unlike the Freestyle Libre.
- Eating vegetables, then protein, then starch reduces glucose spikes — an ordering effect visible only with continuous data.
- Creative work caused a 90-point glucose spike that a walk brought back down, linking mental effort to metabolic response.
- Switching from Liquid IV to [IDLife](/entities/idlife.md) Hydrate stopped CGM-visible glucose spikes, showing that product composition matters more than category.
- CGM showed different glucose responses to identical meals, undermining one-size-fits-all diet advice.
- One post pairs periodic [biomarker testing](/concepts/biomarker-testing.md) baselines with CGM's continuous stream, arguing data should guide health decisions over subjective feeling.
