# AI bias

AI bias is systematic unfairness in model outputs, traced to the data, labeling, and objectives that shaped the model rather than to a fixable content defect. Because models learn from material generated by people, they inherit and can amplify the epistemic hierarchy of their training corpus — and marginalized groups are most often affected. Treating bias as a content problem that filters or fine-tuning can patch misses where it actually lives: encoded in the model's probability distributions before it ever generates output.

## Key Claims

- Bias enters during data collection and labeling, in algorithm design, and when predictions are interpreted and applied; AI is entirely reliant on humans and cannot itself discern biased input.
- A leading question steers the model toward the answer the user seems to want — many users asking ChatGPT whether Edmonton would win the Stanley Cup got statistics skewed in Edmonton's favor; Edmonton did not win.
- AI-generated images of animals in organizational roles depicted only male animals in top positions and erased women from the workforce entirely.
- Miranda Fricker's testimonial injustice describes a speaker's credibility being systematically deflated because of identity, and internet-scale training data encodes exactly this hierarchy.
- Common Crawl's language mix is roughly 44% English, and Llama 2's training data was reported as 89.7% English after preprocessing.
- Kotek et al. (ACM FACCt 2023) found LLMs 3 to 6 times more likely to pick gender-stereotypically aligned occupations in pronoun resolution, more extreme than actual Bureau of Labor Statistics figures.
- High-stakes contexts — hiring, lending, medical triage, legal analysis, performance evaluation — are most dangerous, and mitigation means characterizing affected populations then applying stronger human review, representative [targeted fine-tuning](/concepts/targeted-fine-tuning.md), or both.
