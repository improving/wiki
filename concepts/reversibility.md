# reversibility

Reversibility is the property that determines how much oversight an AI agent action deserves: the question is not how risky an agent is but whether its mistakes can be undone. The corpus argues this is a better organizing principle than the usual autonomous-versus-supervised buckets, because most frameworks currently apply the lightest controls exactly where consequences are permanent.

## Key Claims

- Ask not how risky an agent is, but whether its mistakes can be undone.
- Reversible and irreversible are more useful buckets than autonomous and supervised.
- Governance burden should scale with reversibility, not with autonomy level.
- Uniform governance scrutinizes every action regardless of its cost, and agents die from governance design rather than technical failure.
- Gartner projects 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission agents by 2027.
