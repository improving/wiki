# employee recognition

Employee recognition is the deliberate acknowledgment of contributions in a way the recipient actually values. In this corpus the flagship example is Improving 100 — a peer-nominated program whose reward is an all-expenses-paid trip rather than a certificate — and the recurring lesson is that recognition works when it creates memories and connection, and when it is tailored to how each person wants to be appreciated.

## Key Claims

- Improving 100 recognizes top performers across 22+ offices in four categories: Community Impact, Business Growth, Delivery Excellence, and Technical Innovation.
- Employees nominate their peers — anyone can nominate anyone — and leadership makes the final selection after due diligence, which recipients say makes the recognition feel more special.
- The reward is an all-inclusive trip for the recipient and a plus-one: 62 honorees attended the 2023 Cancún trip, and the 2024 trip ran June 27–29 at Maroma Beach Resort.
- Recognition is framed as community — feeling seen and appreciated — rather than trophies; recipients argue it builds cross-border connection in a multi-country, largely remote company.
- A Great Place to Work survey found recognition is the most important driver of great work, but it is not one-size-fits-all: some want private praise and others public acknowledgment.
- Recognition sits alongside professional growth, autonomy, financial fairness, and feedback as one of five "leadership languages" that retain people.
