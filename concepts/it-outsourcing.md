# IT outsourcing

Contracting a third party to deliver IT capability — software development, operations, security, or a whole function — rather than hiring and managing it internally. The corpus covers the model's geography (onshore, nearshore, offshore), its commercial shapes, and the trends reshaping it, with the pandemic-era shift toward nearshore and the rise of AI as recurring themes.

## Key Claims

- IT outsourcing spending was predicted at $519 billion in 2023, a 22% increase over 2019.
- The pandemic exposed the traditional outsourcing model's weaknesses and accelerated a shift toward nearshoring in Latin America and the Caribbean; the IDB expected nearshoring to add $78 billion to LAC exports after 2023, and Bloomberg reported 80% of US companies were actively considering it.
- No single hub wins on every dimension, so location choice trades cost, talent depth, communication, time-zone overlap, and stability against the project's needs.
- A hybrid model blends a collaboration-friendly region (Latin America) with a scale-friendly one (India) to balance talent mix, near-24-hour coverage, cost, and risk diversification.
- The decision to outsource sits inside a broader buy-build-partner choice, where short-term scarce expertise favors partnering and long-term scale can favor building or buying.
- Other 2023 trends cited were AI/ML and automation, blockchain, IoT, and hybrid work — the AI/ML outsourcing market alone was $93.5 billion in 2021 with a forecast 38.1% CAGR.
- Outsourcing's named challenges are loss of direct control, service suitability, communication, and dependency, mitigated by frequent engagement and transparency.
