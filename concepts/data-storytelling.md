# data storytelling

Data storytelling combines narrative with well-chosen visualization to turn analysis into understanding and action. Improving writers argue it connects the two halves of business intelligence — static reporting systems and interactive self-service analytics — and helps people collaborate around the same insight. It matters because raw dashboards rarely persuade on their own: chart choice, perception limits, and plain-language framing decide whether an insight lands.

## Key Claims

- BI splits into reporting systems and interactive, graphical self-service analytics; data storytelling connects the two and helps people collaborate.
- Humans estimate length and position in two dimensions well but angles, area, and color poorly — so pie charts, which encode values three ways, compare badly.
- Dashboards compound the problem: many charts on one page, changing rapidly, make information harder to discern.
- Power BI's Smart Narratives uses natural-language generation to turn insights into editable text for a whole page, a single visual, or a hover icon, while Q&A queries data in natural language and Key Influencers ranks drivers (in one example, age 47–90 made high income 11.96 times more likely).
- SAP Analytics Cloud's Analytics Designer lets analysts build interactive planning stories with cascading dropdowns and background data actions, not just static charts.
- As AI drafts analysis, the business side's value shifts toward judgment, framing, and storytelling — turning data into a narrative anchored to outcomes.
