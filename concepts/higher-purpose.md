# higher purpose

Higher purpose is the Conscious Capitalism pillar holding that a business exists for a reason beyond profit, while still assuming profit is required to survive. It acts as a North Star that shapes an organization's stakeholder model, culture, and leadership — and, in this corpus, it is treated as something an individual can articulate for themselves as well as a company.

## Key Claims

- Conscious capitalism holds that business exists for a higher purpose while still assuming profit is required to survive; the question becomes how profit is spent and where value is delivered.
- A personal higher purpose determines one's stakeholder model, culture, and how leadership is applied.
- Purpose statements should be distinct and authentic, not vague jargon — examples cited are Oprah Winfrey's wish to inspire students and Richard Branson's desire to have fun and learn from mistakes.
- Feeling adrift at work often signals a mismatch between personal purpose and the job.
- Work/life integration through aligned purpose and values is what people actually seek, not balance for its own sake.
- Purpose anchors conscious culture; individuals can adopt a focused few of Conscious Capitalism's ten culture qualities and align them with their purpose.
- Corporate innovation labs are anchored to a higher purpose, and Improving's CEO framed the company as purpose-driven and thinking beyond profit.
