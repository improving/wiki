# AI change management

AI change management is the sequencing discipline of building trust and evolving roles before deploying AI tooling, because transformations fail on people. The standard levers — leadership alignment, communication, role evolution, resistance management — are treated as four appearances of one sequencing error: deploying the tool before the trust conditions exist. Resistance is rational until proven otherwise, so the fix is visible proof that early adopters were not harmed.

## Key Claims

- Prosci's 2026 research (1,100+ professionals) puts human factors at 56–64% of AI implementation challenges, with user proficiency alone at 38%.
- A top-ten global technology company trained roughly 80 engineering teams; usage fell below 5% within weeks of training, and recovery took about a year of team-by-team work.
- Leadership alignment is often treated as an event (a briefing plus a status update), after which a manual sign-off is quietly reintroduced "just for now."
- Framing matters more than distribution: "co-pilot, not autopilot" works because it is falsifiable and names which decisions still require a human.
- Identity is the mechanism: a tester whose identity is "I run these scripts" experiences an AI test-generation agent as a threat, while one who "owns quality" experiences it as leverage.
- Every change has two parts — the change, which produces fear, and the reason, which is where courage comes from; communicating only the first is not a healthy change plan.
- Organizations should reskill before replacing, since replacing a role often costs 1.5–2 times salary once recruiting, onboarding, ramp-up, and lost institutional knowledge are counted.
