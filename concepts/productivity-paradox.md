# productivity paradox

The productivity paradox describes what happens when AI lifts output across an entire market at once: prices and expectations reset, and the gain that looked like an edge becomes table stakes. It matters because it explains why measured economy-wide returns lag confident forecasts, and why advantage then shifts to context, integration speed, and judgment.

## Key Claims

- Equal productivity gains across a market compress prices and reset expectations.
- Every major tech wave since 1950 brought confident, quantified, wrong economic predictions.
- Goldman, McKinsey, PwC, and IMF forecasts extrapolate task exposure rather than measured adoption; Acemoglu estimates a 1.1–1.6% GDP gain over a decade, about one-fifth of Goldman's.
- MIT NANDA found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact.
- AI narrows the gap between top and average performers, so advantage concentrates in context access, integration speed, and judgment at the margin.
