# Open-Weight Models

Open-weight models are models whose trained weights are released for download and self-hosting, as distinct from closed models reachable only through an API. Across this corpus they are treated as an economic and regulatory battleground: cheap open weights reshaped pricing and adoption, but the advantage was a subsidy, and safety standards that require post-release monitoring sort by architecture.

## Key Claims

- DeepSeek warned developers on August 6, 2026 that API prices would rise significantly, revealing eighteen months of falling open-weight prices as a market-share subsidy rather than durable cost advantage.
- A safety standard requiring post-release monitoring and recall effectively bans open weights without using the word ban, because only centrally hosted models can be monitored and recalled.
- Equal application of such a standard produces unequal outcomes by architecture, shifting compliance burden to deployers.
- Two dueling July 2026 letters show every signatory's stated position tracking its own business model: Nvidia's pro-open-weight letter drew roughly fifty signatories, while OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google sat out.
- Meta's five-day gap between closed Muse Spark 1.2 (API-only, 12–20x tier spread) and open Glimmer (Apache 2.0) is deliberate segmentation.
- API buyers pay for a relationship; self-hosters were never in the closed tier's market, so the open release chases developer mindshare Meta cannot invoice directly.
