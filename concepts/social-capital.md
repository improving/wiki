# social capital

Social capital is the store of trust, credibility, and standing a person — or an AI system — accumulates through reliable behavior in an organization. The corpus frames it as an economy: attention, favors, and commitments are priced and exchanged, and the balance can be built or depleted. Extending trust to autonomous agents follows the same logic as trusting people: earn authority incrementally and back it with governance infrastructure.

## Key Claims

- Attention is priced by organizational level, so executive time is the most expensive currency.
- Giving public credit earns currency; self-promotion carries a look-at-me tax.
- Failing to keep commitments depreciates currency faster than anything else.
- Organizations extend too much trust to AI too fast, skipping a track record.
- Broken commitments deplete an AI system's social capital faster than they build it.
- Audit trails, boundary enforcement, and gates keep the trust balance from depleting.
