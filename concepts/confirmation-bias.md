# confirmation bias

Confirmation bias is the tendency to process information so that it supports what you already believe — and people are especially prone to it when the issue is important or self-relevant. In this corpus it is the quiet engine behind failed agile transformations: coaches and organizations apply the framework they know, not the one the situation needs, and then read every signal as validation.

## Key Claims

- Confirmation bias is processing information to support existing beliefs, and it is strongest when the issue matters personally or professionally.
- In transformations it leads coaches and organizations to force familiar frameworks — Scrum, Disciplined Agile, SAFe — regardless of fit.
- Common sources: an organization believing one framework will fix all impediments, experts deeply trained in a single framework, and sunk cost in one certification path.
- A poor fit is applying Scrum where experimentation and inspection are not needed — such as a help desk resolving urgent tickets with known solutions.
- Five countermeasures: gather evidence-based current-state data, keep focus on client needs, consider factors outside the team, research other frameworks, and return to Agile and lean values and principles.
- It is also a named cause of the process-adoption trap, where teams go through the motions of a process without understanding the "why."
- The remedy is to coach mindset first and confirm assessments and changes incrementally, keeping outcomes rather than solutions in view.
