# leading by example

Leading by example means influence flows from what you actually do, not what you say others should do. Across this corpus it is the load-bearing mechanism for culture: people are described as too intuitive to be fooled by values that leaders do not live, so modeled behavior — not announced behavior — is what spreads.

## Key Claims

- Modeling a behavior makes others want to imitate it, and passion for the work spreads through a virtuous cycle.
- Speaking of your own effort, even to inform, lessens its inspirational effect; announcing a sacrifice invites suspicion that you seek recognition.
- People are too intuitive to be fooled by faked values — living values cannot be faked convincingly, so a culture is only alive when leaders internalize it.
- Leadership is measured by how far you bring others: managers bottleneck on their own cognitive load, while leaders distribute it so people can think and grow.
- Children learn restlessness or appreciation from what you model, which extends the principle past the workplace into the home.
- Courage is a prerequisite: it is not innate but a virtue cultivated through small steps, self-awareness, and shared vulnerability.
