# empathy mapping

Empathy mapping is a lightweight research synthesis tool that captures what users see, do, hear, feel, and think, turning scattered observations into a shared picture of their experience. It is one of the practical techniques Improving writers recommend for building empathy — alongside role play and direct observation — because it reveals what clients need rather than only what they ask for. It appears in discovery for both conventional UX and data products, where users arrive with predefined questions that research must surface.

## Key Claims

- Empathy mapping is one of three techniques for building empathy in design, together with role play (feeling the user's emotions) and observation (watching users do the task).
- The map captures what users see, do, hear, feel, and think, making their context explicit to the whole team.
- It is useful when the client is not the end user, since clients often hold wrong preconceptions about what users value.
- In analytics and data-visualization discovery, empathy mapping is used alongside user interviews and user stories to surface the questions users bring to the data.
- The same empathetic lens applies to services, not just software.
