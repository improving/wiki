# servant leadership

Servant leadership, coined by Robert K. Greenleaf in 1970, makes serving the team, removing obstacles, and growing people the leader's primary responsibility. In this corpus it is the practical alternative to micromanagement: leaders measure the team rather than individuals, trust by default, and measure their own success by the autonomy and growth they create.

## Key Claims

- The servant-leadership stance is to serve the team, remove impediments, ask questions, and trust by default; leaders' involvement should be invited, not imposed ([Micromanagement Disguised as Agile Leadership](/sources/micromanagement-disguised-as-agile-leadership.md)).
- Delegation is a core servant-leadership skill, aided by three tools: reversibility (one-way vs two-way doors), risk level ("scraped knees vs broken bones"), and Management 3.0's delegation poker rated one to seven ([Lead with Purpose: A Guide to Delegation](/sources/lead-with-purpose-a-guide-to-delegation.md)).
- Delegation yields autonomy, mastery, and purpose — the three motivators in Dan Pink's *Drive* — and trains future leaders ([Lead with Purpose](/sources/lead-with-purpose-a-guide-to-delegation.md)).
- In Conscious Capitalism, awareness plus empowerment plus enablement produce servant leadership and a stakeholder model of shared success ([Navigating Conscious Leadership](/sources/navigating-conscious-leadership-in-your-everyday-life.md)).
- Product Owner effectiveness — producing change through others — is servant leadership applied to product work ([Drucker's Blueprint Pt. 2](/sources/druckers-blueprint-product-owner-to-effective-executive-pt-2.md)).
- Agile at scale rests on servant leadership enabling engaged teams who receive direct customer feedback ([Agility: to Scale or Not to Scale](/sources/agile-to-scale-or-not-to-scale.md)).
