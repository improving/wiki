# platform as a product

Platform as a product means running an internal platform with the practices of a product team — real users, research, a roadmap, feedback loops, and adoption and outcome metrics — instead of treating it as infrastructure plumbing. The corpus credits Thoughtworks with originating the framing in 2017 and returns to it as the antidote to nearly every platform failure mode.

## Key Claims

- Thoughtworks originated the "platform as a product" framing in 2017 ([platform engineering companies](/sources/platform-engineering-companies.md)).
- The six recurring platform failure modes — product misfit, overly complex design, "Swiss knife" bloat, insufficient documentation, siloed development, and stagnation — all trace to ignoring the developers the platform serves ([how to fail](/sources/how-to-fail-at-platform-engineering.md)).
- Product misfit happens when developers are not involved in building the platform, producing features they never use; the fix is early surveys and interviews ([how to fail](/sources/how-to-fail-at-platform-engineering.md)).
- Maersk's IDP applied user-centric methodologies — clear vision, executive backing, journey mapping, user testing, continuous feedback — and acknowledged the platform cannot cover every use case ([Maersk IDP](/sources/elevating-developer-experience-maersks-path-to-a-more-user-centric.md)).
- Reaching Level 3 self-service means owning the interface as the product rather than building every instance, with parameterized golden paths and instrumented requests before automation ([maturity model](/sources/platform-engineering-maturity-model.md)).
- Platform-as-a-product with organizational backing is one of the patterns that makes a portal actually reduce cognitive load; a platform team measured on availability rather than developer pain builds for the wrong priorities ([IDP cognitive load](/sources/internal-developer-portal-what-reduces-cognitive-load.md)).
