# UX Maturity

UX maturity describes how far an organization has embedded design into its culture, processes, and decision-making — from design as an afterthought to design as a core strategic driver. Improving's writers use the Nielsen Norman Group's maturity model to contrast reactive organizations, where UX "just happens later," with proactive ones that research early and identify the right problem before building. Raising maturity is a leadership and cultural problem, not a matter of hiring more designers or adding visual polish.

## Key Claims

- The Nielsen Norman Group UX maturity model has six stages, from "Absent" (design as afterthought) to "Integrated and Optimizing" (design as core strategic driver).
- Lower-maturity organizations make decisions without user feedback; higher-maturity ones are user-centric, data-driven, and integral to strategy.
- An InVision study found only a small percentage of companies reach the highest design-maturity levels — a significant opportunity.
- Many companies sit at the emergent or reactive stages, where UX is an afterthought and design "just happens later"; the goal is proactive UX integrated into early research and strategy.
- Changing an organizational mindset requires a cultural shift that starts top-down; toolsets and skillsets are easier to change than mindset.
- Strategic UX lets teams define value, calculate effort, evaluate confidence, and prioritize — giving leaders reduced risk, higher confidence, cost savings, and more empathetic products.
- Outcome focus elevates design from production service to strategic partner, aligning teams and building executive trust.
- It is never too late: even mid-project, teams can revisit whether the problem and desired outcome are correctly understood.
