# work in progress

Work in progress (WIP) is the partially finished work a team is carrying at any moment — work that has been started but not delivered. In this corpus WIP is treated as the hidden tax on flow: too much of it worsens quality, forecasting, and morale, while explicit limits and smaller batch sizes restore predictability and reduce burnout.

## Key Claims

- Managing WIP improves quality, morale, trust, and forecasting, and prevents burnout ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Lean's Muda (waste), Mura (imbalance), and Muri (overload) describe WIP symptoms; refining backlog items more than one to two sprints ahead is a form of over-planning waste ([Go Lean or Go Home](/sources/go-lean-or-go-home.md)).
- Dependencies keep teams busy rather than producing done work — rotating specialists to avoid idle time optimizes utilization over value delivered ([Struggling to Deliver Done Work, Part 1](/sources/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-1-dependencies.md)).
- Non-cross-functional teams accumulate delays and unfinished work; the fix is to start small by embedding the missing skills ([What You Don't Change, You Choose](/sources/what-you-dont-change-you-choose.md)).
- Fixed headcount combined with WIP limits forces explicit trade-offs about what gets started and what waits ([Strategic Demand Management](/sources/strategic-demand-management.md)).
- Shorter sprints, incremental releases, and continuous delivery reduce the risk of a "bug inventory" — work that is started but not safely releasable ([Go Lean or Go Home](/sources/go-lean-or-go-home.md)).
