# Active Listening

Active listening is the practice of working to uncover what someone actually needs, as distinct from what they first say. This corpus frames it as a trust behavior rather than a courtesy: it is how real issues surface, how empathy becomes visible, and how communication failures get caught before they become resentment.

## Key Claims

- Genuinely uncovering client needs underpins a client-centric approach; active listening and feedback loops surface the real issues behind stated needs.
- Proactive communication creates transparency and prevents headaches, and consistently exceeding expectations builds a reputation that retains clients.
- Empathy is not a buzzword but the force behind meaningful connection; active listening, naming feelings, and genuine concern are how trust is built.
- Informal gatherings and wellness rituals create belonging and psychological safety, so empathy ripples through every facet of an organization.
- People are not compilers: fixing a mistake is not enough — you must communicate, apologize, and make amends.
- Unheard change communication leaves people in "NIMSU" (making things up), so owning mistakes and saying sorry is required to move forward.
