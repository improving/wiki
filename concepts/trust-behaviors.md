# trust behaviors

Trust behaviors are the specific, learnable actions from Stephen M.R. Covey's *The Speed of Trust* that build or tax credibility in a relationship. Improving writers work through them one by one — Keep Commitments, Create Transparency, Listen First, Deliver Results, Right Wrongs, Clarify Expectations, Confront Reality, Extend Trust — treating trust as a set of practices rather than a personality trait, and running "Trust Pods" to study the full set of thirteen.

## Key Claims

- Keep Commitments is the behavior Covey calls the "Big Kahuna": if you have any doubt you can keep a promise, do not make it — a "maybe" or "no" is better than a cancelled promise, and a broken word does lasting damage.
- Create Transparency means telling the truth in ways people can verify for themselves; timing matters (raise a blocker on Tuesday, not the Friday it is due), and failing to own failures is the quickest way to lose trust.
- Listen First is a deliberate practice of seeking understanding while withholding judgment; it makes people feel heard, deepens understanding, and supports the other behaviors.
- Deliver Results is consistency over years rather than a single peak: how a result is achieved matters, effort alone is not enough, and the need to deliver never ends.
- Right Wrongs means restoring a relationship by taking responsibility; ego and pride cause adults to stop doing what they learned as children, and unresolved wrongs become landmines.
- Extend Trust turns trust from a noun into a verb: a trusted person becomes a trusted leader by giving others opportunity, support, and honest feedback.
- A Tiny Pulse survey found transparency was the top factor in employee happiness, and an HBR study found 70 percent of employees felt most engaged when leaders communicated goals and strategy.
