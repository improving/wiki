# self-awareness

Self-awareness is the practice of seeing your own ego, triggers, and internal state accurately — and it recurs across this corpus as the precondition for nearly every other kind of growth. The recurring argument is that we can only observe our outsides, while "nearly everything happens on the inside," so the limiting beliefs we experience are largely self-created.

## Key Claims

- Seeing yourself objectively is hard and honesty about it is painful; the payoff is knowing what to focus on.
- Frustration is rooted internally, not in externalities — externals are merely triggers for inner challenges, and performance journaling exposes the pattern.
- Our responses shape how others treat us, so changing a reaction can break a negative interaction cycle; unaddressed behavior signals that it is acceptable.
- What spills out when someone bumps into you reflects what has been brewing inside; exhaustion raises the volume and exposes underlying fragility.
- The stories we invent about strangers reveal our own values, biases, and fears more than they describe anyone else.
- Writing exposes gaps that thinking hides: ideas held only in your head get artificially filled in.
