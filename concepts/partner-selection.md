# partner selection

The work of vetting a services partner before committing work to them — a decision the corpus treats as strategic rather than procurement, because the market is large and quality varies drastically. The recurring message is that the best partners behave as strategic contributors rather than order-takers, and that delivery maturity, governance, and security should weigh at least as heavily as raw technical skill.

## Key Claims

- Around 70% of companies cite access to specialized skills as a top reason to offshore, while about 53% name data security as a top concern and roughly 54% report cultural or communication challenges — all three shape selection.
- Delivery maturity and governance matter more than raw coding ability; mature partners offer direct access to project tracking tools such as Jira or Azure DevOps.
- Security and compliance criteria include SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, and HIPAA where relevant, plus real-time monitoring, threat detection, incident response, and vulnerability assessment.
- Contracts should assign IP on creation, include indemnification, and specify dispute jurisdiction; a turnover rate below 15% is considered excellent.
- Common failure modes to screen out include "ghost firms" that vanish after deposits, vendors holding IP hostage, and teams secretly subcontracting to unqualified third parties.
- Credibility signals include partner status (Microsoft Gold, AWS Advanced, Google Cloud), awards such as the Inc. 5000 and Best Places to Work, and certifications like ISO 9001 and CMMI.
- Evaluation areas span domain and industry experience, cultural fit, communication, scalability, pricing flexibility, and delivery methodology.
- For managed services specifically, buyers should check defined SLAs and escalation paths, true 24/7 coverage, rigorous onboarding, and readable reporting.
