# multitasking myth

The idea that "multitasking" is not parallel work but rapid context switching, each switch carrying a real cognitive cost. Across this corpus it grounds two arguments: that monitoring idle AI agents recreates the multitasking trap, and that chasing efficiency everywhere costs depth.

## Key Claims

- Multitasking is a myth; every context switch costs roughly twenty-five minutes of refocus.
- Idle AI agents feel like waste even though they cost nothing meaningful, triggering "agent anxiety."
- Constantly monitoring agents creates the same self-reinforcing attention loop as multitasking.
- Compute is now on demand, but human attention remains the scarce resource.
- Optimizing every minute makes us miss important details; focus on value provided rather than schedule kept.
- Deliberately choosing one thing to do less efficiently can make it more thorough.
