# deliberate practice

Deliberate practice is effort designed specifically to improve performance, as opposed to repetition that merely passes the time. It matters across this corpus because it is how skills that look innate — courage, judgment, expertise — are actually built, and because the corpus argues those skills erode when the repetitive work underneath them is automated away.

## Key Claims

- Deliberate practice is designed to improve performance, not just repeat it; sessions should run 60–90 minutes and at most 3–4 hours daily.
- Constant feedback and measurement drive improvement, and rest and spacing are part of the method rather than indulgences.
- Intrinsic motivation sustains mastery; 10,000 hours alone is insufficient.
- Reading builds knowledge while writing builds clarity, and writing exposes where what you thought diverges from what you wrote — a cheap feedback loop on your own thinking.
- Courage is a virtue cultivated through deliberate practice, small steps, and shared vulnerability rather than an innate trait.
- The Amplifier Paradox argues the stakes are rising: a cancer-specialist study found AI improved output while eroding judgment ("intuition rust"), 11 million gateway jobs disappeared in 18 months, and a 2026 coding study found heavy AI reliance cut manual-mastery scores by 17%.
