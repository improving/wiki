# AI augmentation

AI augmentation uses AI to expand what a worker can do rather than replace the worker, and the sources argue it requires clear human intent about which decisions stay human. The winning pattern is consistent: AI gathers, summarizes, and proposes; humans review, contextualize, and decide. The distinction that makes it work is complexity (many steps, a knowable recipe) versus difficulty (no rulebook, requiring judgment).

## Key Claims

- AI handles complexity — data collection, number-crunching, large data sets — while humans handle difficulty: weighing evidence, intuition, and the impact of decisions.
- The winning pattern: AI gathers, summarizes, and proposes; humans review, contextualize, and decide.
- Realistic productivity gains of 30–40% appear on text, research, pattern recognition, and summarization tasks, with losses when applied elsewhere.
- A client case lifted lead identification from $1B to over $14B in potential leads within months; humans were not displaced but moved into supervisory roles.
- An accounts-payable automation processed roughly 100,000 invoices a year through a prebuilt model with 13 validation checks and a 70% confidence gate, routing exceptions to clerks for an estimated $75K annual saving.
- AI changes tasks, not talent: you can automate payroll but not customer conversations, generate code faster but not replace creativity, trust-building, or empathy.
- Non-developers can use agentic tools to expand their own capability — a product person built small apps to feel scope, cost, and trade-offs firsthand — without becoming a developer.
