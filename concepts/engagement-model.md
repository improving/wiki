# IT engagement model

The commercial and operating shape of an outsourcing relationship — how ownership, pricing, communication, delivery accountability, and risk are distributed between client and provider. The corpus argues that a mismatch between a client's expectations and the chosen model is itself a driver of cost overruns, delays, and quality problems, so the model should be picked to fit the project type, budget pattern, and organizational maturity.

## Key Claims

- Seven models recur: fixed price, dedicated development team, time & material, staff augmentation, managed services, SLA/milestone-based, and hybrid.
- Fixed price suits small, well-defined projects but offers low flexibility and risks compromised quality if scope is underestimated.
- A dedicated development team fits long-term product work and complex domains but demands higher commitment and active client-side product ownership.
- Time & material suits agile and exploratory work with fast iteration but challenges budget predictability and requires strong governance.
- Staff augmentation embeds specialists while the client retains delivery ownership, at the cost of limited delivery accountability.
- Managed services shifts operational risk to the provider against SLAs and KPIs, but limits rapid change and risks vendor dependency.
- SLA/milestone-based models tie payment to measurable checkpoints, fitting regulated industries at the cost of flexibility and heavy upfront planning; hybrid models blend structures per workstream.
- Selection depends on project purpose, technical expertise, budget constraints, scope stability, team size, and development stage — and buyers should read the SLA for guaranteed response times and uptime.
