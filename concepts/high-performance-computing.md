# high-performance computing

High-performance computing (HPC) is large-scale compute for research, simulation, and increasingly AI training. This corpus's argument is that traditional on-premises HPC was designed for predictable batch workloads, while today's research is bursty and unpredictable — so the model is shifting to elastic, governed cloud capacity delivered as a self-service [platform engineering](/concepts/platform-engineering.md) product.

## Key Claims

- Traditional on-prem HPC suffers high CapEx, underutilization, limited elasticity, and lengthy provisioning cycles.
- Today's AI training, geospatial analysis, and simulation are bursty and unpredictable, breaking the assumptions on-prem clusters were built for.
- A defense research agency's Research Accelerator Portal provisions secure cloud environments often within an hour of approval, with budget estimation, approval workflows, and spend alerts at 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, and 100%.
- CAMH extended its on-prem cluster with a governed cloud portal rather than replacing it, cutting estimated costs by about 40%.
- Portals are built on Google Cloud with Vertex AI, Jupyter, Gemini, and Terraform inside PBMM-compliant architectures.
- Leading institutions are framed as hybrid by design: on-prem for steady-state workloads, cloud for elasticity and AI acceleration.
