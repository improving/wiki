# Limiting Beliefs

Beliefs about our own limits that constrain what we attempt, usually installed from outside or invented internally and then left unexamined. Across this corpus the recurring point is that these limits are largely self-created and therefore contestable.

## Key Claims

- "We can only see our outsides, but nearly everything happens on the inside" — the constraint usually lives in the interpretation, not the situation.
- All limitations in you are created by you; they are not fixed features of the world.
- The acronym LIE — "Limiting Idea Entertained" — makes the mechanism explicit: a limiting idea only limits you once you entertain it.
- We all believe lies others feed us or that we tell ourselves; the work is catching them.
- One practice: journaling daily and performance-journaling weekly, with an added slot to reflect on current limitations.
