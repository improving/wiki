# Peer accountability

Peer accountability is the mechanism by which shared standards are upheld by a cohort of equals rather than enforced top-down. In this corpus it appears twice: as the social glue that makes structured learning cohorts compound, and as the force that turns an individual's craftsmanship standards into a durable team culture.

## Key Claims

- Values without structure produce uneven, non-compounding learning across an organization.
- Structured cohorts build shared vocabulary and peer accountability without relying on managerial hierarchy.
- ImprovingU combined semester-based global courses with quarterly local cohorts and journaling, and used dot-voting to prioritize topics.
- A craftsman is defined by the standards they refuse to abandon.
- Shipping below your standard under deadline is a moral choice, not neutral pragmatism.
- Peer accountability converts personal standards into team culture; automation and testing are professional obligations, not conveniences.
