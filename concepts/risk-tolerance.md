# Risk Tolerance

Risk tolerance is the amount of uncertainty an individual or plan can carry before the downside becomes unacceptable. In this corpus it appears in two settings: matching an investment portfolio to a young person's goals and temperament, and the leader's judgment about when to stop gathering options and act — since a leader's style shows in how they manage risk amid unknown variables.

## Key Claims

- Understand each person's risk tolerance and long-term goals before investing, rather than applying one default allocation.
- Index and mutual funds lower risk through broad portfolio exposure; ETFs track sector indexes at lower fees than traditional mutual funds.
- Early retirement saving compounds as earnings are reinvested over time.
- Good leaders are intuitively better at the optimal stopping problem — deciding when to act instead of endlessly analyzing.
- Decisions rarely carry the fatal significance we attach to them; balance being adequately informed against exhaustively considering every option.
