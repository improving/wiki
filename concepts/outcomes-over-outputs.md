# outcomes over outputs

"Outcomes over outputs" is the discipline of measuring design and delivery by the change they produce — conversion, retention, reduced support volume — rather than by the artifacts shipped. Improving's UX writers argue design teams are too often celebrated for wireframes, prototypes, and design systems that show what was made, not what changed. The shift aligns teams, reduces risk, builds executive trust, and elevates design from production service to strategic partner.

## Key Claims

- Outputs are tangible deliverables — journey maps, interaction models, prototypes, usability findings, design-system components — that represent progress but not success.
- Outcomes reflect real-world change: higher conversion, reduced support volume, increased task completion, faster onboarding, better retention.
- "Outputs say, 'Here's what we made.' Outcomes say, 'Here's why it mattered.'"
- A cart-abandonment example: an output-focused team redesigns checkout; an outcome-driven team targets a 20% reduction and guides decisions with research, experimentation, and A/B tests.
- Becoming outcome-driven means defining success via user and business metrics, linking every artifact to an intended outcome, instrumenting experiences, and shifting governance from feature roadmaps to outcome roadmaps.
- Moving from a feature-driven to an outcome-driven mindset is a cultural shift that starts top-down; toolsets and skillsets are easier to change than mindset.
- Over-engineering fails the same test in reverse: speed gets measured (lines of code per hour, story points) while direction does not, so teams cannot tell whether they are building the right thing.
