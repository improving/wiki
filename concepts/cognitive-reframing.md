# cognitive reframing

Cognitive reframing is the deliberate choice to reinterpret an event, an emotion, or a mental replay so it serves you instead of steering you. The corpus treats it as the practical bridge between [emotional regulation](/concepts/emotional-regulation.md) and [self-awareness](/concepts/self-awareness.md).

## Key Claims

- Emotion is always a reaction and cannot itself be intentional; what can be chosen is the mindset and the action that follow the emotion.
- Mindsets can be reactionary or intentional, and the intentional mode is usually better.
- Priming before entering a situation can shape which emotions arise in the first place.
- Mental reruns of past conversations rarely produce anything useful on their own — the rerun is not the learning.
- Asking "what did I miss?" converts rumination into learning; reflection matters less than how you choose to use it.
- Forgiveness is framed as giving up the right to a better past, which frees attention for the forward-looking question.
