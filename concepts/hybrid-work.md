# Hybrid Work

Hybrid work blends remote and in-office work, assigning each setting the tasks it does best: home days for focused deep work, office days for collaboration, ceremonies, and bonding. Improving's writing treats it as the emerging default rather than a compromise, with the practical question being how to keep distributed employees connected and culturally included. The evidence base is a mix of first-person experience, survey figures, and tooling.

## Key Claims

- A team that began all-remote in July 2020 chose to return to the office for sprint ceremonies four months in; in-person presence improved communication with stakeholders and helped earn client trust.
- A client-site visit let the team gather UX observations firsthand and synthesize them on sticky notes at a whiteboard — something virtual tools still cannot match.
- Office days produce hallway conversations (one led to a tennis group running two and a half years); home days support focused deep work, and the author is productive in both.
- Improving's Come Together Initiative kept hybrid teams connected through monthly themes: January 2022's wellbeing theme ran company-wide virtual talks in English and Spanish plus a distinct in-person event at each office.
- Microsoft survey data cited for 2023: 70% of employees want hybrid work, 46% are considering switching jobs over remote options, and 66% of businesses plan to redesign office space.
- Microsoft Teams Premium adds security, meeting, and webinar features for hybrid collaboration — watermarking, end-to-end encryption, translated captions, and Town Hall capacity — at $10 per person per month.
- Viva Engage is positioned for the community and culture layer of hybrid work, reaching employees not tied to specific projects.
