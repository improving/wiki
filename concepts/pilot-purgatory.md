# pilot purgatory

Pilot purgatory is the state where AI proofs of concept never reach production, stalling on integration, governance, and ownership. The sources put the share of enterprises stuck there at 56–74%, wasting 18–24 months and $500K to $3M+ per failed pilot, and they agree the cause sits in the organization around the model. Escaping it is a jump from [woodshop to factory](/concepts/woodshop-to-factory-transition.md): the five blockers — governance, integration debt, talent, budget politics, and change management — must move together.

## Key Claims

- BCG puts the share of enterprises struggling to scale AI past the pilot at 74%; another estimate finds 56% of organizations stuck in "pilot purgatory."
- The average enterprise wastes 18–24 months on failed pilots costing $500K to over $3M each; 88% of AI proofs of concept never reach production.
- A pilot gets a clean dataset, motivated users, and a personally invested sponsor; production offers none of those, so a pilot was never a real test.
- Integration is a top killer: demos that dazzle in a sandbox meet authentication, rate limits, PII, and workflows and become too cumbersome to adopt.
- Overambitious scope ("deliver an enterprise brain in phase one") means the pilot never ships because it is never quite perfect; scope instead for a 12-week thin vertical slice.
- The five-stage sequence — Discover, Design, Develop, Demonstrate, Deploy — forces the organizational work to happen in order; use case one may take 12 weeks while use case ten takes two.
- A minimum viable [AI Center of Excellence](/concepts/ai-center-of-excellence.md) — two people with standing authority to say no to an unready pilot — is the standing owner that prevents the stall.
