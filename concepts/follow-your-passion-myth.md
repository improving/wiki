# follow your passion myth

The "follow your passion" myth holds that feelings should point the way to a career and that the right pursuit will simply feel right. This corpus argues the causal arrow runs the other way: passion is the reward for competence, not the starting line.

## Key Claims

- Emotions are great servants but poor masters — feelings are not who we are and should not direct our actions.
- "Follow your passion" fails because it puts emotions before actions, inverting cause and effect.
- Working hard and becoming genuinely good at something is what creates passion.
- Passion is a byproduct of getting really good at something, not an input you can select in advance.
- The market pays for competence, reliability, and craft — not for feelings.
- We are bad at predicting what will make us happy, so choosing a path by anticipated feeling is unreliable.
- Colleges should teach communication, resilience, and discomfort tolerance rather than chase feelings.
