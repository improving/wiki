# Regional AI Economy

The regional AI economy is this corpus's account of how Dallas–Fort Worth became a load-bearing AI hub. The argument is structural rather than promotional: talent, demand, infrastructure, and capital converge in one metro, and the work that follows is vertical and industry-specific rather than AI-native startup churn.

## Key Claims

- Site Selection ranked Dallas the number one tech hub in North America, and DFW added more tech jobs than any other U.S. metro from 2022 to 2024.
- Wistron is investing $761 million in AI supercomputers in Fort Worth, and 248 data centers are planned across Texas.
- Celestica announced an $876 million AI manufacturing campus in Fort Worth; Brookings named DFW one of 28 AI Star Hubs.
- DFW's AI work targets regulated, liability-sensitive industries, and 36% of local jobs now have meaningful AI exposure.
- Institutional gravity has replaced hype: 700 people filled a Dallas Regional Chamber AI awards event, and the AI 75 list is led by operators and institutions rather than startups.
- Proximity of infrastructure, talent, and clients removes excuses to delay — client questions shifted from whether to do AI to catching up.
