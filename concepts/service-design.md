# service design

Service design looks past the screen to the whole service a customer experiences across systems, hardware, people, and time. Improving practitioners use service design blueprints to map those end-to-end journeys and make hidden dependencies visible to everyone who owns a piece of them. It matters because many experiences fail long before the user opens the application — in pre-installation tasks, other systems, and human touchpoints that no single team sees.

## Key Claims

- In a PI Planning session, a Service Design Blueprint mapped the full "Full Install Workflow," from pre-installation tasks to early in-app steps, exposing gaps the team had not seen.
- The install journey spanned other systems, hardware, installation steps, and human touchpoints long before a user opened the application, so a blueprint was the right tool to visualize the complexity.
- The session brought Development, Product, UX, and client stakeholders together, with each perspective adding a layer to the blueprint.
- Mapping the full journey revealed workflow gaps and cross-system dependencies, and showed how much time the pre-installation tasks required.
- A client stakeholder shared the blueprint with company leadership as a quarterly highlight, opening the door to mapping the pre-planning phase.
- Service design now sits alongside customer experience and user experience as a component of business strategy, and blueprints help align teams around outcomes.
