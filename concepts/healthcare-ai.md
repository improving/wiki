# healthcare AI

Healthcare AI spans payers, providers, and research institutions, and this corpus treats governance — not capability — as the binding constraint. Health plans face a trust crisis over AI-driven claim denials, providers face burnout and thin margins, and regulators are moving fast, so the winning pattern is AI paired with observability, bias testing, and human review rather than raw automation.

## Key Claims

- The AMA reports 60% of physicians believe AI tools cause unnecessary claim denials, and 80% of prior authorization denials are overturned on review; UnitedHealthcare, Cigna, and Humana face lawsuits.
- Governance is the foundation: observability, bias testing, formal risk analyses, acceptable-risk thresholds, and human-in-the-loop reviews for gray-area decisions.
- Texas, Arizona, and Maryland prohibit fully automated medical necessity denials; Colorado mandates annual AI impact assessments.
- CMS interoperability by 2026 requires 80% real-time prior authorization decisions using FHIR, with denial reasons made public.
- Provider-side, initial claim denials hit 11.8% in 2024 — nearly half from missing or inaccurate data — and AI error rates can fall from 46% to 32%, then 20%, then 10% as it learns an organization's workflows.
- An emergency room that deployed 30 disconnected AI systems saw clinician performance drop 30%; integration matters more than tool count.
- Shadow AI is flagged as the top provider risk for 2026, and about 96,000 physicians are projected to exit the workforce that year.
- CAMH, Canada's largest mental-health teaching hospital, used a governed cloud portal for AI/ML research, cutting costs about 40%.
