# DevOps

DevOps is the synthesis of software development and IT operations into a shared practice with shared accountability. Across this corpus it functions less as a toolchain than as a case study in resolving false dichotomies: it took an apparent opposition — dev versus ops — and showed the tradeoff was context-dependent, not universal.

## Key Claims

- DevOps synthesized an apparent opposition between development and operations into one practice.
- False dichotomies present context-dependent tradeoffs as universal either/or choices and block progress.
- Tribal identity drives practitioners to overextend norms beyond the contexts where they fit.
- The Phoenix Project helps developers and operations work together; Enterprise Integration Patterns is the bible for distributed messaging.
- AI-era dichotomies such as speed versus safety repeat the mistake — governance enables scale rather than opposing it.
- Rogers' curve is a diagnostic for where an organization sits relative to the market; applying an Innovator strategy as a Late Majority organization is the common mistake.
