# ImprovingU: What It Looks Like to Institutionalize Learning — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/improvingu-what-it-looks-like-to-institutionalize-learning/improvingu-what-it-looks-like-to-institutionalize-learning.md`

## Summary

There is a difference between a company that values learning and one that learns. The first relies on individuals' intrinsic motivation, producing uneven and non-compounding capability growth. The second operationalizes learning as a design decision. Drawing on his experience building ImprovingU as President, Devlin Liles argues that values without structure are wishes: consistency at organizational scale requires cadence, topic-selection process, and reflection mechanisms, not exhortation.

## Key Claims

- Relying on intrinsic motivation grows self-directed learners but leaves capability on the table for everyone else.
- ImprovingU had two tiers: a semester-based global tier (4–12 meetings) for structure across geography, and a slower quarterly local tier with journaling between sessions.
- Local topics were chosen by participants via dot-voting from a submitted backlog, applying Agile prioritization to learning culture.
- Structured learning produces shared vocabulary, peer accountability without hierarchy, and compound depth rather than surface breadth.
- The observed outcome: teams with consistent structure grew capability more evenly and adapted to change more smoothly.
