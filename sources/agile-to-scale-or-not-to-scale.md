# Agility: to Scale or Not to Scale is NOT the Right Question — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/agile-to-scale-or-not-to-scale/agile-to-scale-or-not-to-scale.md`

## Summary

Kris Wickett argues the agile community asks the wrong question when it debates scaling. Drawing on years in the restaurant industry, she distinguishes "scaling Agile" (spreading Scrum across teams) from "Agile at scale" (making the whole organization able to sense, respond, and adapt). The latter rests on systems thinking, lean thinking, and a growth mindset, not on any single scaling framework.

## Key Claims

- "Scaling" means growing while staying effective and efficient, but the goal should be bringing agility to every part of the company, not just teams.
- Agility is the ability to move quickly and easily and to think and understand quickly — something every business should want in a fast-changing digital age.
- Restaurants are the author's model of Agile at scale: engaged employees, servant leadership, direct customer feedback, and continuous inspect-and-adapt.
- "Agile at scale" means the organization itself is agile; systems thinking and lean continuously refine how work flows from concept to cash.
- Alignment on delighting the customer produces autonomy, and autonomy produces agility at scale.
- The four most popular scaling frameworks are Nexus, LeSS, SAFe, and Disciplined Agile — and there is no best framework across the board; fit depends on size, culture, and team maturity.
- A growth mindset (per Carol Dweck) is the differentiator between thriving and surviving companies.
