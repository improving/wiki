# Bridging the Gap: How a Product Person Learned to Build With Agentic AI — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/bridging-the-gap-how-a-product-person-learned-to-build-with-agentic-ai/bridging-the-gap-how-a-product-person-learned-to-build-with-agentic-ai.md`

## Summary

A product owner and Scrum Master argues that staying purely on the "business side" is becoming a liability now that agentic AI removes the build bottleneck. Using BMAD, a framework that wraps work in a cast of agent personas (product manager, analyst, architect, developer, tester, Scrum Master), he built a Cynefin facilitation app in an afternoon and had an analyst agent tear apart his own PRD. The point is not to become a developer but to feel scope, cost, and architectural trade-offs firsthand, which makes him a more honest and useful product partner.

## Key Claims

- The slow part of delivery is no longer typing but thinking: whether the problem is real, whether constraints are real, and what dependencies and models actually cost.
- BMAD's "party mode" agents ask sharper scoping questions than many real meetings, exposing fuzzy users, scope, and value propositions.
- A throwaway Cynefin web app with a 72-hour data-retention backend went from idea to working front end in a single afternoon.
- An analyst agent rewrote his PRD with clearer problem statements, explicit hardware and training targets, and a challenge to a premature dependency.
- The Scrum-stances app experiment reframed his "first mover" language as "first to operationalize stance development" and pushed him toward open source and crowdfunding over license revenue.
- Suggested playbook: pick a tiny selfish problem, commit one weekend, lean into agent conversations rather than code, and bring one artifact back to the team.
