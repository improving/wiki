# The Competitive Axis Moved to Governance — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-competitive-axis-moved-to-governance/the-competitive-axis-moved-to-governance.md`

## Summary

Top-of-curve benchmark scores have compressed to the point that a half-point win no longer sells, so labs with the deepest enterprise and government exposure are competing on governance credentials instead. The post pairs two August decisions: Anthropic naming a career jurist as its first Chief Global Affairs Officer, and AWS turning on zero data retention by default in Bedrock for Claude models. Meanwhile the rest of the industry competed on price and packaging. The thesis is that frontier quality is plateauing and [trust infrastructure](/concepts/trust-infrastructure.md) is the new differentiation.

## Key Claims

- On August 4 Anthropic named Tino Cuéllar, a former California Supreme Court justice, as its first Chief Global Affairs Officer.
- AWS made zero data retention the Bedrock default for Claude Opus 5 with no contract addendum required, framed explicitly as a governance and privacy selling point.
- On SWE-bench Verified, Claude Opus 5 sits near 96%, Mythos 5 at 95.5%, and Fable 5 at 95% — three flagships within one point; Terminal-Bench 2.1 gaps are measured in tenths.
- The same week, AWS cut Bedrock pricing on GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% to $0.20 per million input tokens, while DeepSeek held near $0.14 per million — moves on the price/packaging axis.
- Early commercial aviation is the analogy: once engines and airframes matured, safety record and certification decided survival, not top speed.
