# Passion Is the Reward, Not the Starting Line — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/passion-is-the-reward-not-the-starting-line/passion-is-the-reward-not-the-starting-line.md`

## Summary

Responding to Dartmouth's renaming of its Center for Professional Development to the Center for Career Design — with a stated shift toward helping students find their "passion" rather than a profession — O'Hara argues the move doubles down on bad advice. Passion, he contends, is not an input you discover but an output that arrives as a byproduct of getting good at something. He calls for colleges to teach communication, resilience in discomfort, and self-knowledge through real work instead.

## Key Claims

- Dartmouth renamed its Center for Professional Development to the Center for Career Design and now focuses on passion over profession.
- The research is "remarkably bad at predicting what will make us happy"; passion is largely a feeling tied to novelty and early success.
- When work gets hard, people conclude they picked the wrong passion and start over, meandering and blaming.
- The market pays for "competence, reliability, and craft," not feelings.
- Employers' real complaint — graduates who can't communicate or handle difficult conversations — is solvable through teaching.
