# Know Your Developer: DX Strategies for Internal vs External Developers — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/know-your-developer/know-your-developer.md`

## Summary

Atulpriya Sharma argues that developer experience (DX) is the new UX, but that companies wrongly apply identical strategies to two very different audiences: external developers who choose your tools and internal developers who have tools chosen for them. Drawing on his KubeCon India keynote, he frames a shared Find-Investigate-Run-Ship-Transform (FIRST) journey and then contrasts discovery, evaluation, migration, and success metrics for each group. The "Know Your Developer" principle, he says, should be the foundation of every DX strategy.

## Key Claims

- DX is the sum of all developer interactions with a tool, covering both external developers (product, docs, website) and internal developers using your APIs and platform.
- External developers discover tools via Google, ChatGPT, Reddit, GitHub issues, and conference talks rather than vendor websites.
- External evaluation demands working examples, architecture diagrams, benchmarks, sandboxes, and one-command installs; anything gated behind "contact sales" is a disqualifier.
- External success is measured in mind share: GitHub stars and forks, Docker pulls, conference talks, and community contributions.
- Internal developers adopt a company-standardized platform, discovering tools via service catalogs, internal developer portals, Teams/Slack channels, and lunch-and-learns.
- Internal migration needs step-by-step guides, side-by-side comparisons, rollback plans, and golden paths that work with existing security and monitoring standards.
- Internal success is measured in developer velocity, platform adoption, time-to-first-deployment, support-ticket reduction, and infrastructure cost per service.
