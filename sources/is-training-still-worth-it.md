# Is Training Still Worth It? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/is-training-still-worth-it/is-training-still-worth-it.md`

## Summary

Don McGreal argues that training is worth it only when it is done with intention — when it changes behavior or supports growth rather than checking a compliance box. He diagnoses traditional instructor-led training's "four D's" and proposes a mix of alternatives that connect learning to real work. The real ROI, he says, is measured in applied outcomes, not survey scores or certificates earned.

## Key Claims

- Training serves two purposes: to change something (skills, ways of working, behavior) or to support growth; everything else rolls up to these.
- Traditional instructor-led training's four D's are that it is disruptive, dense, detached (generic case studies), and disregarded (little post-training accountability).
- Certifications create a knowledge baseline and motivate study, but passing an assessment is not mastery — "a driver's license doesn't make you a good driver."
- Self-guided learning is flexible and cheap but fails without discipline or a peer group.
- Deeper Learning Programs spread 30–50 objectives across six to ten weeks with real-world assignments, turning abstract knowledge into lived experience.
- Labs tackle real business problems in time-boxed sessions and yield tangible work products; fieldwork and coaching build habits that stick but scale narrowly.
- The most effective strategies combine classroom awareness, labs for outcomes, DLPs to sustain practice, and coaching to anchor behavior.
