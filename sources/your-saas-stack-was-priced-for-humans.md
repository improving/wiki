# Your SaaS Stack Was Priced for Humans. Now What? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/your-saas-stack-was-priced-for-humans/your-saas-stack-was-priced-for-humans.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that per-seat SaaS pricing rests on an assumption agents invalidate: that a human will touch the product. When an agent completes work by calling APIs directly and never loading the UI, the link between active users and software revenue breaks. He cites Gartner's "agentic arbitrage" framing to show billions in enterprise application spend at risk, and suggests enterprises treat redundant or renegotiable subscriptions as a real line item in the AI business case.

## Key Claims

- Gartner says $234 billion in enterprise SaaS spending is at risk by 2030 because the software assumed human users
- Gartner calls the dynamic "agentic arbitrage"; the core logic is that agents do not need seats
- The SaaS invoice assumes usage, not a human touching the product 40 hours a week
- Vendors that do not adapt their pricing face difficult renewals with procurement teams who have done the math
- Enterprises should add one question to every agent project brief: which subscriptions does this make redundant, reduce, or renegotiate?
