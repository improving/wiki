# Corporate Currency: The Hidden Social Economy of Organizations — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/corporate-currency-the-hidden-social-economy-of-organizations/corporate-currency-the-hidden-social-economy-of-organizations.md`

## Summary

The post argues that organizations run on an informal social economy — "Corporate Currency" — in which every person carries a balance that rises when they earn credit and falls when they spend it. Drawing on Peter Blau's exchange theory and Bourdieu's social capital, it presents a working operator's model: denominations scale with organizational level (CEO time ≈ $100/15 min, VP ≈ $50/30 min, Manager ≈ $20/hr), currency can be earned, given, or claimed, and spending is progressive.

## Key Claims

- Attention is priced by scarcity: the CEO's 15 minutes costs roughly 5x a VP's 30 minutes and far more than a manager's hour.
- Giving credit publicly creates currency; claiming your own wins is taxed by the look-at-me tax.
- Repeated asks to the same person cost progressively more, so earn between asks.
- Failing a commitment is the most expensive action, depreciating trust at a rate that is hard to reverse.
- Naming the ledger risks corrupting it (Gneezy and Rustichini's "A Fine Is a Price"), but the prescribed behaviors are the opposite of cynical.
