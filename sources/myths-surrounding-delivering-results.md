# Deliver Results: Myths Surrounding This Simple Sounding Trust Behavior — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/myths-surrounding-delivering-results/myths-surrounding-delivering-results.md`

## Summary

Delivering results looks like common sense but is really about consistency over long periods, not a single outcome. The post works through four myths about the behavior, arguing that effort, the result alone, a one-time peak, and a finite horizon all misread what actually builds trust and career capital.

## Key Claims

- Myth 1: working hard is not enough — results with the least effort are better, and being busy with symptoms rather than root issues eventually fails.
- Myth 2: results are not all that matter; a result delivered by lying, cheating, or stealing is tainted, so how you achieve it matters.
- Myth 3: hitting a peak does not earn a promotion; the level of a role must be sustained for a while before it is earned, and premature promotions often have to be corrected.
- Myth 4: the need to deliver never ends — higher positions typically demand more results, and a role can be outgrown by a fast-growing company.
- Consistency in delivering results over years is what builds trust and career capital.
