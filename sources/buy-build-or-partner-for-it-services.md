# The Great Debate: Buy, Build, or Partner for IT Services? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/buy-build-or-partner-for-it-services/buy-build-or-partner-for-it-services.md`

## Summary

Anurag Kumar, Improving's Nearshore Region Managing Director, answers the recurring question of whether companies should buy an IT services firm, build their own offshore operation, or partner with one. His answer is that it depends on the need and situation. He splits the decision by company type — an end-user company needing talent versus a services company needing to scale — and by short- versus long-term horizon.

## Key Claims

- Kumar's own path is the product of IT outsourcing: contract developer in the 80s, India-based outsourcing leadership from 2002–2009, founding iTexico in 2011, and iTexico's 2020 merger with Improving.
- For a company needing short-term, scarce expertise, partner for capacity and capability; weigh time zone, costs, scale, language, culture, and team stability.
- For long-term, larger-scale needs, building your own operations is viable, especially with name recognition and financial stability.
- Buying an IT services company is rare for end-user companies because the acquired firm's talent is already committed to its own clients and would need to recruit anyway.
- A BOT (Build Operate and Transfer) agreement lets a company invest in a trained team that transfers to it after two to three years for a one-time fee; sometimes the transfer never happens.
- For a services company that needs to scale, building is slower and uneven; buying adds clients, revenue, and an operations base but costs more and suitable targets are scarce; partnering risks inconsistent quality across two cultures and time zones.
