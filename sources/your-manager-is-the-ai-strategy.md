# Your Manager Is the AI Strategy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/your-manager-is-the-ai-strategy/your-manager-is-the-ai-strategy.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that AI adoption programs treat a management problem as a tool problem. Citing Gallup's State of the Global Workplace 2026, he notes employees whose managers actively support AI use are 8.7 times more likely to say AI transformed their work—echoing Improving's own observation that AI use tracks the upline manager's use. The disconnect sits in the middle of the organization, and it is a leadership failure rather than a training failure.

## Key Claims

- Employees with actively supportive managers are 8.7 times more likely to say AI transformed their work
- Manager engagement has declined at nearly three times the rate of employee engagement over three years
- McKinsey's State of Organizations 2026: 86% of executives feel unprepared to embed AI in daily operations, while only 25% of the workforce uses AI regularly
- 86% of CEOs say employees already have the skills; the gap is not at the top or the floor but in the middle
- 50% of CHROs are not very confident in managers' ability to guide AI use
- The blocker is permission structure—psychological safety, à la Google's Project Aristotle—not tooling

## Relevance

Links AI adoption to [psychological safety](/concepts/psychological-safety.md) and [change management](/concepts/change-management.md); argues AI strategy belongs in manager development, not IT.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Strong on named survey statistics (Gallup, McKinsey); the 8.7x figure is correlational and the Improving claim is anecdotal.
