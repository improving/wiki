# Deep Thinking (Part 2) — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/deep-thinking-2/deep-thinking-2.md`

## Summary

A follow-up to his earlier post on deep thinking, this piece locates the obstacle in how we handle boredom. O'Hara observes that most people, himself included, reach for a phone the moment they are bored, filling the space that was once used for reflection. He argues deep thinking is simply hard and time-hungry, and commits to a month of not jumping to his phone.

## Key Claims

- Many people, teens included, rarely wrestle with deep questions of meaning, purpose, and value.
- Boredom is quickly numbed with phones — social feeds, games, news, Discord — unwittingly robbing us of thinking.
- Idle moments once spent reflecting (waiting rooms, commutes, recovering after a workout) are now device time.
- Deep thinking is hard because it demands time and focus, which are in short supply depending on the day.
- He commits to one month (February) of sitting quietly and thinking rather than reaching for his phone.
