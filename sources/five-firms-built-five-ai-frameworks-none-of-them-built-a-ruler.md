# Five Firms Built Five AI Frameworks. None of Them Built a Ruler. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/five-firms-built-five-ai-frameworks-none-of-them-built-a-ruler/five-firms-built-five-ai-frameworks-none-of-them-built-a-ruler.md`

## Summary

Overlaying BCG, Gartner, Bain, McKinsey, and NIST AI frameworks, Liles finds five institutions converging on the same shape — an ambition ladder, a targeting map, an operating model, a scorecard, and a governance loop — yet none tells a leader which part of that shape they are standing on. He argues the missing piece is a ruler: an honest maturity assessment. Improving's eight-stage model supplies it, with Stage 4 ("the wall") as the first stage requiring team and IT together.

## Key Claims

- The five frameworks each describe one part of the elephant; BCG's 10-20-70 ratio is directional, not the product of a study.
- The wall exists because of math: chain independent steps and end-to-end success is per-step reliability raised to the number of steps — twenty steps at 95% each yield roughly 36% clean runs.
- Stages 1-3 are individual competency; Stage 4 is the wall, where deterministic validation and enforced review points require shared agreement.
- Stages 7-8 are named aspirational because nobody runs them in production today.
- What is missing from most boardrooms is the ruler, not the model.
