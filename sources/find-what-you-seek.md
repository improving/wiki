# Find What You Seek — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/find-what-you-seek/find-what-you-seek.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that fulfillment does not arrive passively; it is found in effort aligned with purpose. A day of pure ease leaves people anxious and hollow because humans are wired for effort that matters and for competence and progress. Drawing on Viktor Frankl and research studies, he contends that purpose is most often revealed through work, and that connection and contribution — not ease or escape — are what sustain us.

## Key Claims

- "You can't find what you don't seek" — God, knowledge, growth, and a meaningful life are active pursuits.
- A McKinsey study found that 70% of people define their purpose through work, and nearly 90% of younger workers.
- The Harvard Study of Adult Development (85+ years) shows connection and contribution sustain us, not ease.
- People are wired for competence; small wins create momentum, and the climb feels better than the finish line.
- "Fulfillment is not found in ease; it's found in effort aligned with purpose."

## Relevance

Informs personal-development themes — the claim that purpose and fulfillment are produced by deliberate effort and contribution rather than waited for. Draws on a [McKinsey & Company](/entities/mckinsey-company.md) statistic about purpose and work.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Persuasive and motivating, but the cited studies are summarized secondhand and the McKinsey and Harvard figures are not linked to sources.
