# The Leadership Gap Just Got a Number — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-leadership-gap-just-got-a-number/the-leadership-gap-just-got-a-number.md`

## Summary

McKinsey and Microsoft published major AI research reports within weeks of each other and reached the same conclusion from different directions: technology is not the rate limiter — leadership is. O'Hara cites figures showing most organizations experiment with AI while reporting no meaningful bottom-line impact. Microsoft's Work Trend Index, drawing on trillions of Microsoft 365 signals across 20,000 workers, attributes most of AI's impact to culture, manager support, and talent practices rather than individual mindset. The separation between leaders and laggards is about how leaders set intent and build conditions for experimentation.

## Key Claims

- 88% of organizations are experimenting with AI; 81% report no meaningful bottom-line impact.
- McKinsey: 86% of leaders say their organization isn't ready to adopt AI in day-to-day operations, and one in six has no C-level AI owner.
- Microsoft's Work Trend Index: culture, manager support, and talent practices account for 67% of AI impact; individual mindset and behavior 32%.
- Microsoft's "Frontier zone" (16% of AI users) has 80% producing work that wasn't possible twelve months ago — separation is about conditions, not model access.
- Psychological safety and an environment for experimentation matter more than mandates.

Informs [psychological safety](/concepts/psychological-safety.md) and [change management](/concepts/change-management.md) as the levers of AI value, reinforcing the argument that managers, not platforms, are the AI strategy.
