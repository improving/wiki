# The Engineer's Evolution, Stage 2: The Developer With an Assistant — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-engineers-evolution-stage-2-the-developer-with-an-assistant/the-engineers-evolution-stage-2-the-developer-with-an-assistant.md`

## Summary

The first post in the Engineer's Evolution series frames how the software engineer's role changes as teams climb an eight-stage AI maturity curve, then details Stage 2, where most of the industry sits. At Stage 2 an engineer uses an off-the-shelf assistant inside the IDE while the workflow barely changes; gains are real but personal and ephemeral because nothing is encoded or shared. The role expands rather than shrinks, with design and judgment growing on top of coding.

## Key Claims

- Only about a third of technology workers use AI daily, and a much smaller fraction use it on work they'd be held accountable for.
- Stage 2 gains are personal and ephemeral: nothing is captured, so teams cannot compound learning and two developers reach different quality levels.
- The assistant absorbs output variance with human attention, so going faster means spending more attention catching mistakes.
- The main Stage 2 risk is quiet: inconsistent output that looks fine in the moment and accumulates into drift over time.
- The move that matters is the metacognitive shift from doing the work to designing how the work gets done; a better assistant won't buy that shift.
