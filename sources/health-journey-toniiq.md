# Health Journey: Toniiq — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/health-journey-toniiq/health-journey-toniiq.md`

## Summary

O'Hara documents sourcing lesser-known supplements — PQQ, Berberine, NMN, Resveratrol and others — and choosing [Toniiq](/entities/toniiq.md) over anonymous Amazon sellers to avoid quality risk. He stacked Resveratrol+ 98%, PQQ, NAC 98%, FŪEL 99%, and Berberine 1500, noting that research gave him little to measure their effect. After two weeks, with diet and exercise unchanged, he lost 10 lbs. An update at the top retracts the recommendation because Toniiq became unreliable at shipping.

## Key Claims

- He avoided Amazon supplements, treating unknown quality as a gamble.
- His initial stack: Resveratrol+ 98%, PQQ, NAC 98%, FŪEL 99%, and Berberine 1500.
- He dropped 10 lbs in two weeks with no other diet or exercise change — which he calls interesting but not direct evidence.
- He wanted to reduce pill count while watching for further changes over a 90-day period.
- UPDATE: he can no longer recommend Toniiq due to unreliable supply and poor communication.

## Relevance

A data point on supplement sourcing and self-experimentation within the Health Journey series; touches the difficulty of measuring supplement effects without clear biomarkers.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Short n=1 anecdote with an explicit retraction; the weight-loss figure is unattributed and he flags it as not direct evidence.
