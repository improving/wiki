# Writing & Thinking; why writing is what's saving us from GPT — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/writing-and-thinking/writing-and-thinking.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that the point of writing is not to produce prose but to force slower, deeper thinking. Writing exposes gaps in understanding: if you cannot express an idea, you probably do not know it as well as you assumed. He worries that fragmented attention now prefers reacting to reasoning, and that leaning on GPT for writing shortcuts the very process that sharpens thought.

## Key Claims

- Writing exists to make you cogitate and craft a clear explanation, not merely to generate words
- You often discover you cannot communicate something as well as you thought you understood it
- Fragmented attention defaults to responding rather than reasoning, so intentional space for reasoning must be created
- Some things should be made *less* efficient so they can be *more* effective
- AI can augment work, but should not be relied on for what we think

## Relevance

Connects AI-assisted work to [deep thinking](/concepts/deep-thinking.md) and [critical thinking](/concepts/critical-thinking.md), warning against optimizing away the effort that produces insight.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. A brief opinion piece grounded in a Mortimer Adler quote and personal experience; persuasive rather than empirical, with no data.
