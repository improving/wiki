# The Hard Work — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-hard-work-of-fulfillment/the-hard-work-of-fulfillment.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that having to do the hard, unglamorous work is evidence you are in the right job rather than the wrong one, because it is the work that makes you grow. He acknowledges every job has its "bedpans," but says the attitude, not the work, makes a job wrong — especially when complaining becomes consistent rather than occasional. Citing research on affective forecasting, he contends people are poor at predicting what will make them happy, so fulfillment should be made rather than found, and that process is hard, patient work.

## Key Claims

- Doing hard, real work means you are in the right job; it is the means by which you grow.
- Every job has its "bedpans," and consistent complaining — not occasional frustration — is the signal that the problem is your attitude, not the work.
- Research on affective forecasting shows people are terrible at predicting what would make them happy.
- Stop trying to find fulfillment and make it instead; you cannot predict the future well but you can make it happen.
- Passion does not equate to fun, easy, enjoyable, or always rewarding — buying that belief is a dangerous spot, so give yourself grace and "Just. Keep. Working."
