# The Premium You Charged Was for Knowing Things — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-premium-you-charged-was-for-knowing-things/the-premium-you-charged-was-for-knowing-things.md`

## Summary

McKinsey, Bain, and BCG are all cutting jobs in the same quarter, which O'Hara reads as a market signal rather than a business decision. He quotes an IE Business School professor arguing that AI is replacing the technical expertise that made these firms privileged intermediaries of knowledge. The premium consulting charged was for knowing scarce things — credentials, methodology, proprietary benchmarks — and AI now supplies knowledge instantly and cheaply. But judgment, relationships, and accountability remain human and are not being automated, so the repricing hits knowledge-sellers first.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey is cutting 3,000–4,000 jobs; Bain and BCG are cutting in the same quarter.
- The premium firms charged was for scarce specialized knowledge; AI now provides knowledge instantly, cheaply, and at scale.
- Consulting that sells knowing things is dying; consulting that sells judgment, relationships, and accountability is not.
- "We are buying intelligence with models, but we are not buying wisdom with them."
- Cuts target junior research and back-office roles first, but repricing will move up faster than expected.
