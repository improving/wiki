# Being Disruptive — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/being-disruptive/being-disruptive.md`

## Summary

The author explains why he stops listening when a startup pitch calls itself "disruptive." Disruption, he argues, can only be applied in hindsight through the lens of success: Twitter and Amazon did not set out to be disruptive, they set out to solve a problem. He also rejects pitches built from buzzwords and acronyms — "startups don't buzz, flys do; and it's usually around horseshit" — and dismisses "I think" claims, asking instead for actual data, even a rough estimate, because numbers show the idea has been thought through. His advice is to show how you deliver value to someone with a pain, then re-pitch without a single buzzword.

## Key Claims

- "Disruptive" can only be judged in hindsight, through the lens of success.
- Twitter did not set out to be disruptive, and Amazon was not bankrolled for being disruptive; both set out to solve a problem.
- Buzzwords, gamification, or making something "social" cannot rescue an idea that does not work; fix the idea first.
- Pitches should show actual data, even a rough estimate (a "SWAG"), rather than unsupported "I think" claims.
- The pitch should demonstrate how value reaches people who have the pain.
