# Two Kinds of Public Servant — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/two-kinds-of-public-servant/two-kinds-of-public-servant.md`

## Summary

O'Hara sorts public officials into two kinds. The first came from somewhere: they made payroll, signed the front of the checks, and understand what a lease, a permit delay, or a city's friction actually costs a business. The second never left the track — school, staff, campaign, office — and their incentive structure rewards the frame that protects constituents from business. He illustrates with two cases: Governor Pritzker's refusal to give "taxpayer dollars to a billionaire-owned team," after which the Illinois House never voted, Indiana said yes, and the Bears left Chicago; and Samsung moving its U.S. headquarters from New Jersey to Plano while New Jersey fell from 22 Fortune 500 companies in 2018 to 15 in 2025, which site-selection experts call a "five-alarm fire." He argues the founders designed public service as a rotation, not a career, and that winning regions treat business as a partner in building a community.

## Key Claims

- Pritzker's principled-sounding stand preceded the Bears leaving Chicago; Illinois never voted and Indiana said yes.
- Samsung moved its U.S. headquarters from New Jersey to Plano.
- New Jersey went from 22 Fortune 500 companies in 2018 to 15 in 2025, a "five-alarm fire" by site-selection standards.
- Career-office incentives reward the "protect constituents from business" frame; calling a stadium deal "billionaire welfare" plays well on a mailer.
- The founders intended public service as a rotation of people who had built things, and winning cities and states treat business as a partner.

## Relevance

A regional-economy argument about how officials' framing of business affects corporate site selection and relocation.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Opinionated political commentary; the two cases are vivid but selected, and causal attribution of relocations to gubernatorial rhetoric is asserted rather than demonstrated.
