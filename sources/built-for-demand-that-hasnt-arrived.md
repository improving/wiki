# Built for Demand That Hasn't Arrived — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/built-for-demand-that-hasnt-arrived/built-for-demand-that-hasnt-arrived.md`

## Summary

IBM's multi-year deal with Together AI to build a Nvidia-powered inference cluster on IBM Cloud, live in early 2027, is a bet on demand eighteen months away. Liles reads it as the demand limit at infrastructure scale: more AI capability does not automatically create more demand for what it produces. He argues the market has begun pricing exactly that risk.

## Key Claims

- IBM is building capacity ahead of any workload sitting in front of it today, contracted well before demand appears anywhere outside a forecast.
- The "demand limit": AI output volume does not automatically grow the number of people who need that output.
- Teams pointed at well-scoped, high-stakes problems converted speed into stakeholder value; poorly scoped work produced volume nobody asked for plus cleanup cost.
- Nvidia posted an earnings beat and still sold off; SpaceX's debut showed revenue overshadowed by capital spending growing faster than the revenue it should produce.
- Nobody outside IBM has publicly run the arithmetic on whether the cluster's size matches 2027 enterprise demand.

## Relevance

Extends the [demand limit](/concepts/demand-limit.md) argument to infrastructure spending, and argues for measuring [token cost](/concepts/token-cost.md) and downstream need before scaling.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by Devlin Liles. Strong analogical argument backed by his own delivery data, but the cluster-size critique is an open question he explicitly says lacks public numbers.
