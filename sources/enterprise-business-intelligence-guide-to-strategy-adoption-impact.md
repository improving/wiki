# Enterprise Business Intelligence: A Guide to Strategy, Adoption, and Impact — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/enterprise-business-intelligence-guide-to-strategy-adoption-impact/enterprise-business-intelligence-guide-to-strategy-adoption-impact.md`

## Summary

Executives are buried in dashboards yet still debate which numbers are correct. This guide argues that business intelligence succeeds or fails on trust in the data and a governed semantic layer, not on tools or visualizations. It marshals failure statistics, a five-factor success model, and a four-phase adoption lifecycle, warning that AI applied to weak foundations only accelerates failure.

## Key Claims

- 60–70% of BI initiatives fail to deliver business value (Gartner); 90% of companies use AI in BI but only 39% see profit impact.
- Poor data quality costs the U.S. economy $3.1 trillion annually (IBM).
- The semantic layer keeps "revenue" meaning the same thing across Power BI, CRM, and AI agents; Snowflake's Open Semantic Interchange pushes a vendor-neutral standard.
- Five success factors: executive sponsorship, data governance, analytics aligned to business KPIs, user-friendly tools, and agile iteration.
- A 5% error in historical data can amplify into millions in forecasting mistakes; predictive projects without data quality see 4.2x higher model failure and 67% longer time-to-production.
- Four-phase lifecycle: foundations and metric alignment, reporting enablement, adoption and decision integration, then scaling.
- AI amplifies foundations: weak data yields faster failure, wasting an average of $1.2 million within 6 to 18 months.
- Recommends 10–15 metrics to start, business owners for metrics (CFO owns revenue), and tool-agnostic definitions.
