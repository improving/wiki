# The Audit Nobody Wanted — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-audit-nobody-wanted/the-audit-nobody-wanted.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara opens with a consultant's text about a client's Anthropic bill that nobody in the room wanted to explain, framing it as a pattern he has watched build for months. A Wall Street Journal piece confirms the inside view: companies that said yes to everything AI-related are now receiving invoices that don't match their business cases. Uber burned its annual AI coding budget in four months, and Microsoft canceled most of its internal Claude Code licenses and told engineers to find a cheaper alternative. O'Hara insists this is not a story about AI failing but about adoption outrunning accountability — companies maxed out usage before defining the problem, and the use cases that hold up are those with measurable output, code being the clearest example.

## Key Claims

- Clients are receiving AI bills (such as Anthropic invoices) that nobody wants to explain and that don't match the original business cases.
- Uber burned through its annual AI coding budget in four months.
- Microsoft canceled most of its internal Claude Code licenses and told engineers to find a cheaper alternative.
- The pattern is adoption outrunning accountability: companies maxed usage before deciding what problem they were solving, sometimes laying people off citing AI efficiency at the same time.
- Use cases with measurable output — code most clearly — survive scrutiny; many others do not.
- The advice: find where AI delivers, build the business case before adding the seat, and tie spend to a specific justifying outcome.
