# What Really Makes Scrum Work? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/what-really-makes-scrum-work/what-really-makes-scrum-work.md`

## Summary

Michael Wallace, after twenty years as a developer and twenty coaching teams, observes that his successful teams shared something beyond any framework: they worked well together. He argues that the five Scrum Values from the Scrum Guide — commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage — were the real drivers of success, whether teams used XP, Scrum, or Kanban, and that trainers should cultivate those values before worrying about mechanics.

## Key Claims

- Students are most interested in the mechanics of Scrum roles, events, and artifacts, and least interested in the Scrum Values — yet the Scrum Guide says successful Scrum depends on living those values.
- The five values: commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage.
- Commitment means putting the team's needs first; successful teams help each other and take on tasks they don't enjoy.
- Focus means not being pulled by competing or changing priorities and internalizing the customer's needs.
- Openness is the hardest value: it's often easier to tell people what they want to hear than what they need to hear.
- Respect means recognizing each person's skills and not tolerating disrespect.
- Courage follows from respect and openness — the author's team-room poster read, "It is better to do the right thing than to do things right."
- The framework was a less important predictor of success than the values being lived.
