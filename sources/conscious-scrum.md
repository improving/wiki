# Conscious Scrum — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/conscious-scrum/conscious-scrum.md`

## Summary

Michael Bishop asks how to pay down technical debt in Scrum, weighing "tech stories" against fixing debt "while you're in there." Applying Conscious Capitalism's multiple-stakeholder view to a Scrum project — "Conscious Scrum" — he concludes tech stories are best for significant debt because they can be prioritized transparently and deliver value to all seven-plus stakeholder types.

## Key Claims

- Two approaches to technical debt: a prioritized tech story, or fixing debt while working in the code.
- The "while you're in there" approach works for small debt payable in an hour or two.
- The tech story approach is better for significant debt because it enables transparent prioritization with stakeholders.
- Conscious Capitalism holds that businesses have multiple stakeholders and must create value for all.
- A Scrum project's stakeholders include end users, the paying manager, developers, QA, the Scrum Master and Product Owner, sales and marketing, and suppliers — seven or more.
- Up to ninety percent of project expenditures occur during the maintenance phase, so paying debt early pays off.
- Tech stories deliver value to each stakeholder: cost savings, feature flow, a better codebase, less QA pressure, more visibility, and competitive advantage.
- The author's favorite project, a NASA space operations planning toolset, had an inspiring higher purpose.
