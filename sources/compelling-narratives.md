# Compelling Narratives - storytelling in the consulting business — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/compelling-narratives/compelling-narratives.md`

## Summary

Prompted by reading *How Clients Buy*, O'Hara examines how stories work in consulting — their structure and function rather than their content. A compelling narrative is the bridge between a solution and the client's buy-in: jargon and diagrams make eyes glaze over, while painting a post-implementation "day in the life" makes clients lean in. He covers knowing the audience, the problem-solution-benefit arc, authenticity, and visual and testimonial support.

## Key Claims

- A compelling story can be the difference between a proposal that falls flat and one that gains enthusiastic buy-in.
- Solutions and intricate plans don't sell themselves; storytelling demystifies strategy and makes it relatable.
- Tailor to the audience: executives want ROI and value, reached via differentiators and a problem-solution-benefit arc.
- People think in images, so visuals clarify and testimonials let clients see themselves in a peer's success.
- Authenticity is key — sharing missteps beats a pitch that "smells funny".
