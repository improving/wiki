# Always More Efficient — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/always-more-efficient/always-more-efficient.md`

## Summary

O'Hara pushes back on the constant drive for efficiency. We multi-task (a myth, he notes), optimize schedules, plan meetings to the minute, and squeeze every drop from the day—often missing important details and losing the chance to connect deeply because we are focused on what comes next. He argues we should work to squeeze connection out of our meetings and care more about the value we provide than the schedule we keep. His commitment: to be intentionally less efficient at at least one thing in the coming week so he can be more thorough.

## Key Claims

- The drive to be more efficient is constant and largely counterproductive.
- Multitasking is a myth, yet people keep trying.
- Efficiency focus causes missed details and shallower connection.
- Meetings should be squeezed for connection, not merely scheduled.
- Value provided matters more than the schedule kept.
- Concrete commitment: pick one thing to do less efficiently in order to do it more thoroughly.
