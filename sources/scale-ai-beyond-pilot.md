# How to Scale AI Beyond the Pilot — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/scale-ai-beyond-pilot/scale-ai-beyond-pilot.md`

## Summary

Most enterprises have a working AI pilot that never reaches production — "pilot purgatory" — and the cause sits in the organization around the model, not the model. The post calls the missing work AI organizational context adaptation and frames scaling as a jump from woodshop to factory, offering a five-stage framework (Discover, Design, Develop, Demonstrate, Deploy) to sequence it.

## Key Claims

- BCG puts the share of enterprises struggling to scale AI past the pilot stage at 74%.
- Five blockers stall a pilot, and all five must move together: AI governance, integration debt, talent gaps, budget politics, and AI change management.
- A pilot gets a clean dataset, motivated users, and a personally invested sponsor; production offers none of those, so a pilot was never a real test.
- Woodshop-to-factory analogy: governance is the safety system, integration the material specification, talent the maintenance protocol, budget the capital commitment, change management the retraining of people working alongside the machine.
- The five-stage sequence is Discover, Design, Develop, Demonstrate, Deploy; naming it forces the organizational work to happen in order.
- Use case one is the slowest — say 12 weeks building every pattern from scratch — while use case ten might take two weeks reusing them.
- A regulated regional bank had three blockers stacked at once, and its most valuable use case (agents verifying multi-layered compliance testing) was its least visible one.
- A minimum viable AI Center of Excellence is two people with clear ownership of the blockers and standing authority to say no to an unready pilot.
