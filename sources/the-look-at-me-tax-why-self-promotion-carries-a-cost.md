# The Look-at-Me Tax: Why Self-Promotion Carries a Cost — Summary

**Original Source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-look-at-me-tax-why-self-promotion-carries-a-cost/the-look-at-me-tax-why-self-promotion-carries-a-cost.md`

## Summary

Visibility matters, but self-promotion earns [corporate currency](/concepts/corporate-currency.md) at a discount. The post argues the [look-at-me tax](/concepts/look-at-me-tax.md) is structural, not punitive: when a claim about your work carries a visible motive, listeners rationally discount the whole message, unable to separate signal from self-interest. The efficient counter-strategy is to build other people's reputations, which produces recognition that sticks and compounds.

## Key Claims

- Third-party endorsement arrives without apparent motive and is registered as credible signal; self-endorsement arrives with motive visible and is discounted in full.
- The tax lands hardest on explicit credit-claiming in groups, unsolicited win broadcasts, and comparative positioning; it is lighter for asked-for context and written documentation.
- The rule of thumb: mention someone else's contribution at least twice for every mention of your own — a disposition check, not a formula.
- Reputation built by others compounds and persists; self-promotional reputation requires constant maintenance and fades the moment you stop.
- Giving credit tends to produce reciprocal recognition and marks you to observers as outcome-focused rather than self-advancing.
