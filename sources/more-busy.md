# More Busy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/more-busy/more-busy.md`

## Summary

The post pushes back on "busy" as the reflexive answer to "How are you doing?" The author argues that we are constantly trading days of our life for something—necessities, health, or personal goals—and that we can only judge whether the trade is right if we know our purpose. Defining that purpose is hard, ongoing work, but it clarifies decisions; as a result, the author now answers with "I'm on mission" or "I'm not on mission."

## Key Claims

- "I'm really busy" or "Life's good but busy" has become the default response, and it is neither healthy nor the right focus.
- We trade each day of our life for something: necessities, things good for us, or personal goals.
- You can only assess the trade if you know what you are aiming for—your purpose.
- Defining purpose is difficult and never finished, but it shows whether you're trading life for what you want.
- Replacing "busy" with "on mission"/"not on mission" makes the answer meaningful.
