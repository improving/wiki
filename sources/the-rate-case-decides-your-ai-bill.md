# The Rate Case Decides Your AI Bill — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-rate-case-decides-your-ai-bill/the-rate-case-decides-your-ai-bill.md`

## Summary

Argues that the utility rate case, not the model or deployment choice, is where AI's cost structure gets locked in for the next 30 years. Ohio and Virginia have already made data centers pay for reserved capacity whether or not they use it, and a single trade-association report shifted a multi-billion-dollar cost allocation. The practical question for businesses is whether their trade association is in that regulatory room before the tariff is written.

## Key Claims

- Ohio requires data centers drawing more than 25 MW to commit on long-term contracts to pay for the capacity they reserve; AEP then cut its large-load forecast from 30 GW to 13.
- Virginia went further with a 14-year minimum commitment, with data centers on the hook for 85% of transmission and distribution demand regardless of actual usage.
- The Ohio Manufacturers Association moved the policy with a report almost nobody read — not a lobbying campaign or a Senate hearing.
- Utilities build against forecasts, not actual demand, so over-forecasting strands costs that land on everyone else's bill.
- Most AI conversations treat infrastructure cost as background noise, yet rate cases set the cost structure.
