# Why AI Pilots Fail to Scale — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/why-ai-pilots-fail-to-scale/why-ai-pilots-fail-to-scale.md`

## Summary

Enterprise AI pilots fail not because the AI is bad but because the experience around it doesn't fit the user's workflow, context, and mental model, so people default back to old habits. The post argues scaling AI is a design, system, and leadership challenge built on three things: workflow fit, trust by design, and measurable outcomes — with behavior change at the center.

## Key Claims

- BCG finds 74% of companies struggle to achieve and scale value from AI.
- The typical arc: a promising idea, excited leadership, approved budget, a good demo, a pilot — then momentum fades, usage drops, and the pilot stalls.
- The issue is rarely AI capability alone; AI still has to solve a real human problem and fit the user's existing workflow and mental model.
- Too often the workflow stays the same, so people default to old habits, and no one owns the last mile: training, escalation paths, QA, and change management.
- Teams optimize for model accuracy while ignoring trust and usability under real pressure; the pilot proves a concept without proving a business outcome — the system may work while the experience around it does not.
- Gartner predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 because of cost, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls.
- Three things matter: workflow fit (where AI shows up, when, and for whom), trust by design (explainability, feedback loops, confidence signals, clear human oversight), and measurable outcomes (moving something the business cares about).
- Behavior change is central: identify the exact action users need to take, what stops them, what makes it easier, and how the product supports repetition until it becomes routine.
- The post closes by asking DFW leaders in finance, healthcare, energy, and entertainment about their biggest pilot-to-production blocker.
