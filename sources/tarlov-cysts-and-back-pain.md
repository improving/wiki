# Tarlov Cysts and Back Pain — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/tarlov-cysts-and-back-pain/tarlov-cysts-and-back-pain.md`

## Summary

O'Hara describes roughly six weeks of what he assumed was a hamstring problem: tightness, failed PT and dry needling, then migrating nerve pain down his right leg and a numb big toe. An MRI revealed two grape-like Tarlov cysts hanging off the bottom of his spinal cord, and a technician slipped him a note naming the condition. A pain specialist, Dr. Nathan Walters, pointed out that Tarlov cysts are usually not symptomatic and offered an injection that clarified the actual pain driver; the pain resolved and O'Hara stopped all medication. His takeaway is that the medical system is not set up to advocate for you — second opinions and different differentials only happened because he kept pushing. He stresses he is not a doctor and this is not advice.

## Key Claims

- Symptoms began as assumed hamstring tightness and progressed to radiating nerve pain and a numb big toe over about six weeks.
- An MRI showed two Tarlov cysts on the spinal cord; a technician unofficially flagged the name to him.
- Dr. Nathan Walters noted Tarlov cysts are typically not symptomatic, a point that reframed the diagnosis.
- A clarifying injection resolved the pain, and he came off Gabapentin, Robaxin, Tylenol, and Aleve.
- The system doesn't push patients toward second opinions; he credits his own persistence for the outcome.
