# Mississippi Didn't Get Lucky — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/mississippi-didnt-get-lucky/mississippi-didnt-get-lucky.md`

## Summary

The post uses Mississippi's dramatic improvement in fourth-grade reading and math to argue that execution—not the reform idea—is the hard part. Rather than explain, form committees, and declare progress, Mississippi picked a few structural changes and held the line for years through pushback and the unglamorous middle. The author sees the same pattern in consulting: groups that succeed refuse to quit on a good plan before it has had time to work.

## Key Claims

- Mississippi moved fourth-grade reading from 49th to 9th in the country and math from 50th to 16th.
- It chose a small number of structural changes—how reading is taught, how kids progress, what accountability means—and stuck with them for years.
- The reform itself usually isn't the hard part; persisting past the point where it stops feeling like progress is.
- Most leaders can commit to a change for a quarter; fewer persist when early data is flat and critics are loud.
- Clients often lose nerve in the messy middle or get distracted by the next initiative.
