# Interstitial Space - its the space between the big things — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/interstitial-spaces/interstitial-spaces.md`

## Summary

O'Hara borrows the biological term "interstitial" — the space between cells, tissues, and organs — and applies it to the whitespace in our lives and work. He argues that innovation and collaboration happen in those gaps: hallway chats, casual lunches, water-cooler talk, a shower, or an evening walk with the dogs. Because modern life is relentless, we overschedule and overcommit, wringing out every bit of perceived value and thereby eliminating the very spaces where creativity occurs. His call is to recognize and protect those interstitial spaces.

## Key Claims

- Interstitial spaces are the gaps between the "meaningful parts" — and may themselves be the meaningful part for creativity.
- Innovation shows up in fleeting, unplanned moments: hallway chats, casual lunches, water-cooler talk.
- Personal examples: a shower aha moment, clarity during an evening walk with the dogs.
- Overscheduling, overcommitting, and overdoing work eliminate these spaces.
- The remedy is to breathe air back into our lives and let creativity flow into those gaps.

## Relevance

Informs personal productivity and creativity themes; complements posts on focus and recovery by arguing for deliberate unstructured time.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. A short opinion essay with vivid personal examples and no evidence; persuasive rather than empirical.
