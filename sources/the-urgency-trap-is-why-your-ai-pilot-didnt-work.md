# The Urgency Trap Is Why Your AI Pilot Didn't Work — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-urgency-trap-is-why-your-ai-pilot-didnt-work/the-urgency-trap-is-why-your-ai-pilot-didnt-work.md`

## Summary

Explains why most AI pilots produce no measurable gains: firms attack their most urgent, visible problem, which is usually demand-limited work, instead of finding compressible tasks with unlimited demand. Ninety percent of senior executives report no measurable productivity gains over three years. Microsoft's $2.5 billion and 6,000 embedded engineers are a diagnosis that the model was never the bottleneck.

## Key Claims

- 90% of senior executives across four countries report no measurable AI productivity gains over three years.
- Microsoft committed $2.5 billion and 6,000 of its own engineers to embed within customer companies for deployment.
- Task compression (AI does the task faster) differs from expanding the demand curve for the work.
- Paying invoices faster is demand-limited; proactive outreach at scale is demand-unlimited.
- Value compounds in demand-unlimited, highly compressible tasks, not in the most urgent pain.
