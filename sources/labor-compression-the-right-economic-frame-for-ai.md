# Labor Compression: The Right Economic Frame for AI — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/labor-compression-the-right-economic-frame-for-ai/labor-compression-the-right-economic-frame-for-ai.md`

## Summary

"Will AI replace our jobs?" is a binary question that produces binary thinking and leaves strategy on the table. Liles proposes labor compression — the reduction of hours of effort required in a role due to technology — as the frame that produces strategy. The historical record of automation is mostly compression, not elimination: ATMs accompanied a rise in bank tellers, and the spreadsheet shifted work from clerks to analysts. The strategic question is what happens to the freed capacity, and whether demand or execution capacity limits value creation. He answers three challengers, including Acemoglu and Restrepo's "so-so automation."

## Key Claims

- ATMs in the US grew from ~60,000 (1985) to ~400,000 (2010) while teller jobs rose from ~485,000 to ~575,000.
- Since 1980 the US lost ~400,000 accounting/bookkeeping clerk jobs and gained ~600,000 accountant/analyst jobs.
- Labor compression works through two mechanisms: productivity amplification (faster at existing tasks) and task automation (whole tasks absorbed), which call for different responses.
- In demand-limited roles, freed capacity becomes surplus unless new demand absorbs it; execution-limited roles should redeploy immediately.
- Four fates for freed capacity: grow demand, redeploy within the role, reduce costs, or invest in capability.
