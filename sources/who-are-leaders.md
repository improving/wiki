# Who are Leaders — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/who-are-leaders/who-are-leaders.md`

## Summary

The post answers the objection "I don't have the role, the letters, or 'Senior' in my title — how can I be a leader?" The author observes that this question is typically asked by the person most likely to lead, because we want the leadership mantle conferred rather than earned. Real leaders, he argues, simply do what they are best at, guide others, and carry a quiet self-confidence. A Jeff Foxworthy-style checklist makes the point: people look to you for answers, teammates come to you when code won't compile, customers escalate to you, standup falters when you're absent — you might be a leader. It's behavior, not title, that decides.

## Key Claims

- The people most likely to lead are often the ones asking whether they're allowed to.
- Leadership is not conferred by role, credentials, or a "Senior" title.
- True leaders do what they're best at, provide guidance, and show self-confidence others lack.
- Diagnostic behaviors: others seek your answers, teammates seek your help, customers escalate to you, standups suffer when you're absent.
- What you do, more than what your title is, determines whether you actually lead.

## Relevance

A concrete statement of [leading by example](/concepts/leading-by-example.md): authority demonstrated through daily behavior rather than position.
