# Empathy: The Secret Sauce in Good Design — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/empathy-the-secret-sauce-in-design/empathy-the-secret-sauce-in-design.md`

## Summary

UX consultant Tony Brown argues that empathy — seeing the world through users' eyes — is the "secret sauce" of good design because it reveals what clients need, not just what they ask for. Two stories make the case: a legacy system that technically worked but failed users' need for trust and confidence, and an impersonal car-maintenance experience. He then offers three ways to build empathy: role play, observation, and empathy mapping.

## Key Claims

- A client's 8-year-old legacy system "worked" but users described the comparison workflow in terms of trust, accuracy, and confidence it could not deliver.
- Because search results depended on spaces, term order, and date ranges, users printed and compared lists line by line, adding hours of work.
- When the client is not the end user, they often hold wrong preconceptions about what users value.
- Empathy can be built through role play (feeling the user's emotions), observation (watching users do the task), and empathy mapping (capturing what users see, do, hear, feel, and think).
- The same empathetic lens applies to services, not just software.
