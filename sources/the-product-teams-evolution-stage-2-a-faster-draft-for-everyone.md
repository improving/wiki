# The Product Team's Evolution, Stage 2: A Faster Draft for Everyone — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-2-a-faster-draft-for-everyone/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-2-a-faster-draft-for-everyone.md`

## Summary

Opens a series tracking four non-engineering product roles — UX designer, product owner, business analyst, and product manager — up an eight-stage AI maturity model shared with the engineering series. Stage 2 is where most product teams sit: each person has a capable assistant and reaches for it when it saves time, but the work flow barely moves. The series' central idea is the competency floor, which is not artifact production but the judgment that catches output which looks polished and is quietly wrong. Stage 2 gains are real but personal and ephemeral, so the team cannot compound its learning.

## Key Claims

- Most product teams sit at Stage 2 today, using capable AI tools without changing much about how they work.
- Each role's competency floor is judgment: elicitation for analysts, value and "done" for owners, research rigor for designers, strategy and market read for PMs.
- A plausible-sounding invented requirement is harder to spot than a blank page; a good draft saves an hour, a bad one costs two.
- Every Stage 2 gain is personal and ephemeral — two owners write stories at two quality levels and neither standard survives the week.
- The real risk is quiet drift: a well-written artifact that misframes the problem flows downstream with only a busy human as the check.
