# Books Every Developer Should Read — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/books-every-developer-should-read/books-every-developer-should-read.md`

## Summary

After a conversation at AgileDotNet with SMU students visiting Houston, Devlin posts the reading list he promised: a six-book must-read sequence followed by books to read and own eventually. The list leans on software-craftsmanship fundamentals, with a couple of additions for teams working across development and operations or building distributed messaging systems.

## Key Claims

- The must-read list, in order: Head First Object-Oriented Analysis and Design, Clean Code, Code Complete, Refactoring to Patterns, Working Effectively with Legacy Code, and Head First Design Patterns.
- Design Patterns (Gang of Four) originated design-pattern terminology, but its Smalltalk and C++ examples show their age; the Head First book is recommended over it while the patterns remain timeless.
- The Phoenix Project is recommended for teams trying to tighten their relationship with operations.
- Enterprise Integration Patterns should be the "bible" for any distributed messaging application.
- Most websites with APIs are effectively distributed applications, making that book broadly relevant.
