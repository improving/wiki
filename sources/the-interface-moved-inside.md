# The Interface Moved Inside — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-interface-moved-inside/the-interface-moved-inside.md`

## Summary

O'Hara describes a 68-year-old woman with primary lateral sclerosis speaking clearly again through a brain-computer interface built by Paradromics. The company's Connexus system implants more than 400 electrodes below the brain's surface and uses AI to decode neurological signals into speech, distinguishing words she tries to say, imagines saying, and hears. He reads the moment as the next step in a progression from holding a device, to wearing one, to implanting one — the interface moving inside the body.

## Key Claims

- Paradromics' Connexus uses 400+ implanted electrodes plus AI to decode language in real time.
- The system separates attempted, imagined, and heard speech — a genuine advance over prior work.
- Interfaces have moved from pocket, to face (AR glasses), to inside the body.
- Every meaningful technology carries large possibilities in both directions; the answer is intentionality about who builds it and why, not a pause.

## Relevance

Extends O'Hara's earlier argument that interfaces are moving from the pocket to the face, and raises questions about intent, trust, and who controls deeply personal technology.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Short and personal; the author discloses he is an early investor in Paradromics, so the enthusiasm is admittedly biased.
