# Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail #5: Reinventing the Wheel — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/reinventing-the-wheel/reinventing-the-wheel.md`

## Summary

Chasing originality at all costs leads teams to rebuild capabilities that proven tools already deliver, spending a year reproducing what a foundation model or SaaS API could do in weeks. Because the AI ecosystem moves faster than internal development cycles, a bespoke solution is often obsolete by the time it ships. The post argues for starting close to off-the-shelf and earning the right to customize.

## Key Claims

- Teams "spend a year reproducing baseline capabilities of a foundation model or a SaaS API that could be delivered today," with exploding costs and no differentiation.
- Four causes: innovation bias, not-invented-here syndrome, poor time-to-value awareness, and the absence of a buy-build-blend framework.
- Start near the shelf: cloud services, foundation models, vector databases, and orchestration frameworks as the baseline.
- Use a buy-build-blend decision model — buy when time-to-value matters, build when defensibility requires it, blend when integration wins.
- Customize only where it counts: domain grounding, proprietary tools, or unique datasets.
- Plan differentiation phases: early wins on standard tools, later sprints for hardening, compliance, and unique extensions.
- Example: a legal-sector client spent millions on a custom knowledge system still unready for production; Improving shipped a working version with a commercial LLM plus SharePoint retrieval in weeks, then layered in custom redaction and compliance.
