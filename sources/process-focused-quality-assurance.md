# When QA Goes Further Than Testing Software — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/process-focused-quality-assurance/process-focused-quality-assurance.md`

## Summary

Mora Quinteros argues that QA is more than testing: the greatest value begins before development, when QA reviews requirements, surfaces ambiguities, and challenges assumptions, and continues after defects when it asks why the process allowed the bug rather than only fixing the symptom. This process-focused stance makes QA a quality facilitator across the lifecycle.

## Key Claims

- Testing is only one expression of QA; the highest value often starts long before the first end-to-end test is executed.
- QA can contribute at kickoff by reviewing requirements, asking uncomfortable questions, identifying ambiguities, and ensuring shared understanding and ownership definitions.
- Many bugs originate in conversations, unclear requirements, misunderstood business rules, or architecture decisions that seemed reasonable at the time — not in code.
- When a defect is found, the more important question is why it happened: an unclear requirement, easy-to-misuse architecture, missing communication, or an overlooked assumption.
- A process-focused QA treats mistakes and oversights as opportunities to improve how the whole team works, not as individual failures.
- These activities don't replace testing; they make it more effective because quality has been considered throughout the lifecycle.

## Relevance

A view of [quality assurance](/concepts/quality-assurance.md) that stretches past [manual testing](/concepts/manual-testing.md) and [test automation](/concepts/test-automation.md) into [defect management](/concepts/defect-management.md) and root-cause process improvement.
