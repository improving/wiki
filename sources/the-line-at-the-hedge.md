# The Line at the Hedge — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-line-at-the-hedge/the-line-at-the-hedge.md`

## Summary

Two adjacent fields endure the same August drought — one green, one the color of a paper bag — and differ only by a plough. Drawing on rancher Bart Simmons and writer Sama Hoole, O'Hara argues that regenerative farming is not a method but a relationship with time. A Herefordshire field unploughed since the eighteenth century sustains roughly forty plant species with roots deep enough to reach water August cannot; a neighbor reseeded four years ago looks more intentional and is brown. He turns the lesson toward work and organizations: how much are we optimizing for this season at the expense of the foundation beneath it?

## Key Claims

- Two fields in the same drought look entirely different; the only difference is a plough.
- A field unploughed since the 1700s supports ~40 plant species with deep roots; a reseeded neighbor is brown.
- Regenerative farming is a relationship with time — two centuries of dunging, grazing, and rainfall are the feature, not a starting condition to improve.
- We are good at the plough — turning over and reseeding with something measurable — and then wonder why things don't hold.
- Organizations and individuals routinely optimize for the current season at the expense of the foundation.

## Relevance

Informs [time horizon](/concepts/time-horizon.md) and long-term thinking — trusting a system that develops over decades rather than forcing measurable short-term progress.
