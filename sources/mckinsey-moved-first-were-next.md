# McKinsey Moved First. We're Next. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/mckinsey-moved-first-were-next/mckinsey-moved-first-were-next.md`

## Summary

The post argues that AI has finally cracked the billable hour. Earlier disruptions—offshoring, the cloud—left time-and-materials billing intact, but AI compresses time rather than complexity, turning weeks of analyst work into hours and exposing how much work actually takes when done well. The author reads McKinsey's shift toward outcome-linked fees and rising buyer preference for fixed fees as a verdict, not a trend, and expects the gap between outcomes-priced and hours-selling firms to widen.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey tied 25% of its global fees to outcomes.
- 67% of consulting buyers now prefer fixed-fee over time-and-materials, up from 41% three years ago.
- Firms growing 11–12% are implementation-led and outcomes-priced; those stuck at 5.7% still sell advisory hours.
- AI compresses time, not complexity, which is what breaks T&M's logic.
- This forces the industry to own outcomes instead of hours—harder, but the line between firms that matter in five years and those that shrink.

## Relevance

Informs [outcome-based pricing](/concepts/outcome-based-pricing.md) and the economics of the consulting business model.
