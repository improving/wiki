# Controlling Emotion and Mindset — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/controlling-emotion-and-mindset/controlling-emotion-and-mindset.md`

## Summary

O'Hara separates emotion from mindset: emotion is always a reaction and so cannot be intentional, while mindsets can be reactionary or intentional — and intentional is usually better, except when a reflex (seeing a snake in the woods) saves your life. The trouble is that reactionary bias lets feelings cloud the logically correct course. We cannot prevent emotions, he argues, but we can consciously choose how we act once they arise, and reasoning through a situation and its expected outcomes changes our future associations with it. Priming beforehand can shift what we feel.

## Key Claims

- Emotion is always reactive and cannot be intentional; mindsets can be intentional or reactionary.
- Reactionary bias lets feelings cloud what makes the most sense.
- We can't control having emotions, but we can choose how we act beyond the emotion's rise.
- Rationalising a situation and expected outcomes raises the chance of feeling differently next time.
- Priming before a situation has been shown to be "HUGELY effective" at altering felt emotion.
