# UX Maturity: A Holistic Approach to Design — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ux-maturity-a-holistic-approach-to-design/ux-maturity-a-holistic-approach-to-design.md`

## Summary

This post defines design maturity as the extent to which design is integrated into an organization's culture, processes, and decision-making, and argues that raising it demands organization-wide commitment rather than visual polish. It draws on a maturity model to show the path from afterthought to strategic driver.

## Key Claims

- Design maturity means aligning design practices with user needs and business goals, not just aesthetics.
- The Nielsen Norman Group UX maturity model has six stages, from "Absent" (design as afterthought) to "Integrated and Optimizing" (design as core strategic driver).
- Lower-maturity organizations make decisions without user feedback; higher-maturity ones are user-centric, data-driven, and integral to strategy.
- An InVision study found only a small percentage of companies reach the highest design-maturity levels — a significant opportunity.
- Reaching maturity requires leadership advocacy and activities like user research, collaboration, experimentation, and integration into the product lifecycle.
