# Responding to Fear — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/fear/fear.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that fear is an emotional response you cannot reason someone out of, because they are subsumed by the emotion. The effective response is not to display fearlessness but to show compassion, understand the person, and share the experience so they can rise out of the emotional response. Leaders often must acknowledge emotions the person has not yet recognized, and he closes by asking how you can tell when you yourself are caught in an emotional response.

## Key Claims

- You can't rationalize someone out of a fear response; they're subsumed by the emotion.
- The opposite of fear isn't showing no fear — it's showing compassion for their fear.
- Being understood and sharing the experience is what helps someone elevate out of the emotional response.
- You often have to acknowledge emotions the person hasn't recognized to help them process them.
- Self-check question: how do you know when you're caught in an emotional response?

## Relevance

Informs [emotional regulation](/concepts/emotional-regulation.md) and [Empathy](/concepts/empathy.md) — responding to others' fear starts with managing your own reaction and meeting them with compassion.
