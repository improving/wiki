# AI Change Management: Why AI Transformations Fail on People, Not Technology — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ai-change-management/ai-change-management.md`

## Summary

Every AI change management deck warns that transformations fail on people, not technology, but the diagnosis explains every failure and therefore none in particular. The post argues the real issue is sequencing: organizations deploy the tool before establishing the trust conditions to use it, and the four standard levers — leadership alignment, communication, role evolution, resistance management — are four appearances of that same sequencing error.

## Key Claims

- Prosci's 2026 research (1,100+ professionals) puts human factors at 56–64% of AI implementation challenges, with user proficiency alone at 38%.
- A 2018 DevOps mapping found tooling mature while adoption stalled on "trust in technologists": CI pipelines went in but stayed optional, and infrastructure as code was abandoned at the production boundary because production was "too critical to automate."
- Leadership alignment is treated as an event (a briefing plus a status update), after which a manual sign-off is quietly reintroduced "just for now."
- The real communication problem is framing, not distribution; "co-pilot, not autopilot" works because it is falsifiable and names which decisions still require a human, and it only holds if leadership keeps the line even when the AI performs well.
- Role evolution follows demand: capped demand shrinks a team and frees space for new work, uncapped demand pushes work upstream and blurs boundaries; informal coaches are usually mid-level practitioners.
- A top-ten global technology company trained roughly 80 engineering teams on AI-assisted development; usage fell below 5% within weeks of training, and recovery took about a year of team-by-team work.
- Resistance is rational until proven otherwise; the fix is visible proof that early adopters were not harmed, not more enthusiasm or better training.
