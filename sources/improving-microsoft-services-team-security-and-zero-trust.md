# Improving Microsoft Services Team: Security and Zero Trust — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/improving-microsoft-services-team-security-and-zero-trust/improving-microsoft-services-team-security-and-zero-trust.md`

## Summary

Part 1 of a series on Improving's Microsoft Services Team covers Microsoft's Zero Trust philosophy — never trust, always verify — and its shift from trust-by-default to trust-by-exception. It lays out Microsoft's three guiding principles and seven technology pillars, then illustrates them with a credit union's cloud transformation.

## Key Claims

- Zero Trust became more critical as almost all companies had to adapt to remote and mobile work, needing to secure applications, data, devices, and people.
- Microsoft's three guiding principles: verify explicitly, use least-privilege access (Just-In-Time and Just-Enough-Access), and assume breach.
- Microsoft backs the principles with seven pillars: secure identity, endpoints, applications, data, infrastructure, networks, and visibility/automation/orchestration.
- Towpath Credit Union adopted Zero Trust, using Defender for Endpoint for real-time monitoring and Microsoft Intune to enforce device policies.
- When deviations are detected, access to critical business systems is blocked; Intune also lets employees use mobile devices while protecting company data.
