# Who Owns AI When the Chief Scientist Isn't the One Running It — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/who-owns-ai-when-the-chief-scientist-isnt-the-one-running-it/who-owns-ai-when-the-chief-scientist-isnt-the-one-running-it.md`

## Summary

On August 5, Google split its top AI research job. Demis Hassabis moved from CEO of Google DeepMind to chair of DeepMind and chief scientist of Alphabet, while CTO Koray Kavukcuoglu took day-to-day operations as an SVP reporting to Sundar Pichai. Liles reads this as a clean separation of prestige from authority: the bigger title went one way, the budget and roadmap went the other. He argues this is what happens when a research organization outgrows one person credibly holding both the scientist and operator roles, and that Jeff Dean's and Sanjay Ghemawat's departures fit the same pattern.

## Key Claims

- Hassabis kept the marquee title but lost the budget, roadmap, and reporting line; Kavukcuoglu gained operational control.
- Early DeepMind assumed the best scientist and best operator were the same person; at a few hundred people that mostly held, at Google DeepMind's current scale it does not.
- Research authority becomes advisory under the new structure — to prioritize a project, Hassabis must persuade Kavukcuoglu and Pichai.
- Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat left Discovery Loop within days of the restructuring.
- Two readings of the departures — talent follows authority, or a values mismatch — land on the same fact: the title now carries credibility, not power.
