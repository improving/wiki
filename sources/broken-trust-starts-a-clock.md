# It Wasn't the Mistake That Impressed Me — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/broken-trust-starts-a-clock/broken-trust-starts-a-clock.md`

## Summary

An early version of Grok Build was uploading full repositories, history included, under the banner of "agent context." Within a day, xAI disabled the feature, purged what had been collected, and shipped real controls — a disable flag and a privacy setting — instead of a press release. The author connects this to a client incident where a tool his team built reached beyond its scope through an overly wide permission, and argues clients remember whether you called them before they had to call you. His core claim: trust is not a balance you spend down with each misstep, it is a clock the mistake starts, and what restarts it is showing up fast with the truth and a fix that actually works. Most leaders build governance to prevent every failure; fewer build the muscle to respond well when failure happens anyway.

## Key Claims

- xAI disabled the feature, purged collected data, and shipped a disable flag and privacy setting within roughly a day.
- Clients don't remember the incident report; they remember whether you called before they had to call you.
- Trust is a clock, not a balance: the mistake starts it, and speed, truth, and a working fix restart it — not a clean record.
- "We're looking into it" is not a fix; a disabled feature or a deleted dataset is.
- Fewer leaders build the response muscle than build preventive governance, though failure will happen anyway.
