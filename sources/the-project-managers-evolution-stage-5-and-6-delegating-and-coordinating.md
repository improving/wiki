# The Project Manager's Evolution, Stage 5 and 6: Delegating and Coordinating — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-project-managers-evolution-stage-5-and-6-delegating-and-coordinating/the-project-managers-evolution-stage-5-and-6-delegating-and-coordinating.md`

## Summary

The final post looks up the [AI maturity model](/concepts/ai-maturity-model.md) to Stage 5 and 6. Stage 5 hands whole delivery workflows to autonomous agents that pull live signals and act without a human starting each step; the defining requirement is [trusted signals](/concepts/trusted-signals.md) backed by deterministic checks and [adversarial validators](/concepts/adversarial-validators.md) that challenge whether a green status is really green. Stage 6 has multiple agents working in parallel across a portfolio on shared infrastructure, where the PM's work becomes arbitration rather than authorship. Both stages require developer-level skill; a non-technical PM caps at Stage 4.

## Key Claims

- Each layer of orchestration costs roughly seven times the effort of the one below it, which is why most organizations stall moving from Stage 4 to Stage 5.
- Without trusted signals, delegation is just unattended risk reported with confidence.
- At Stage 6 a weak status-integrity gate two layers down multiplies false confidence before anyone reads a line of it.
- Roughly sixty percent of people make the shift comfortably; the rest need support, a different role, or time.
- The craft never fades: the PM must still judge whether a plan is credible, a risk real, and a status honest.
