# Don’t Let the Past Hold the Pen — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/dont-let-the-past-hold-the-pen/dont-let-the-past-hold-the-pen.md`

## Summary

O'Hara contrasts people who treat their past as a stepping stone with those who treat it as their whole story. Drawing on Shane Parrish's warning about people who make being wronged their identity, and on Adlerian psychology from *The Courage to Be Disliked*, he argues our lives are shaped less by what happened to us than by the meaning we assign and the choices we make next. A personal anecdote about a divorce-care group illustrates the pull of a story he wanted to move beyond.

## Key Claims

- "Keep your distance from people who've made being wronged their identity. They're not looking for solutions; they're recruiting." (Shane Parrish)
- Adlerian psychology holds that lives are not determined by events but by the meaning assigned to them and forward choices.
- In the divorce-care group, members had turned their divorce into a community and an identity rather than a past event.
- That identification was not malicious and was comforting to many, yet it pulled the author deeper into a story he wanted to leave.
- "The past will always leave its fingerprints, but it doesn't get to hold the pen unless you hand it over."

## Relevance

Informs [internal narrative](/concepts/internal-narrative.md) and [limiting beliefs](/concepts/limiting-beliefs.md) — the post is a compact argument that identity is authored, not inherited.
