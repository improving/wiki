# Creating Better Data Visualization Experiences: Part 2 of 2 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/creating-better-data-visualization-experiences-part-ii/creating-better-data-visualization-experiences-part-ii.md`

## Summary

As data shifted from scarce and expensive to abundant and hard to analyze, visualization became the means of turning it into actionable perspective. The second part of the series covers modeling graph data, choosing chart types that fit how humans perceive, and selecting a JavaScript visualization library.

## Key Claims

- Data modeling turns ideas about data into a logical model that decides which entities become nodes, links, or discarded.
- Humans estimate length and position in two dimensions well but angles, area, and color poorly — so pie charts, which encode values three ways, compare badly.
- Dashboards compound the problem: many charts on one page, changing rapidly, make information harder to discern.
- BI splits into reporting systems and interactive, graphical self-service analytics; data storytelling connects the two and helps people collaborate.
- Library selection should weigh customization, rendering method, learning curve, support/documentation, and interactivity.
- The right choice depends on understanding how users intend to use the tool, not just the tool's features.
