# The Power of Yes — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-power-of-yes/the-power-of-yes.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle, often told she is good at saying no, explains that she actually coaches people to say "yes, and…" — accepting a request while naming the trade-offs it requires, so yes becomes the start of a conversation rather than a blanket commitment.

## Key Claims

- People equate "no" with "never" and with personal defeat; the post reframes most refusals as "not right now".
- "Yes, and…" accepts the request while stating what it costs — more resources, more time, or removing something else.
- Prioritization is the first lever: yes and no sit on a spectrum of importance, not a binary.
- Listening matters: hear why a request matters before explaining why it must wait, since a flat no kills creativity.
- A "no coach" helps people choose, in a safe environment, what needs to change to reach yes.
- The approach applies beyond work — volunteer commitments, kids' sports teams, and packed personal schedules.
