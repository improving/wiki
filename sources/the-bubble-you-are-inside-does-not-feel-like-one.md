# The Bubble You Are Inside Does Not Feel Like One — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-bubble-you-are-inside-does-not-feel-like-one/the-bubble-you-are-inside-does-not-feel-like-one.md`

## Summary

Reacting to a Business Insider piece by someone who lived through the dot-com collapse, O'Hara asks whether the companies his teams work inside are building on the real thing or on the narrative. He accepts that the largest AI investors are profitable businesses with genuine cash flow, but argues the infrastructure, chip, and AI-adjacent layers are priced on a straight line from "building AI stuff" to "making money from AI stuff" that has never held in tech. The bubble, in his view, sits in projects that exist because someone had to say yes to something AI-related rather than because AI solved a real problem.

## Key Claims

- Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon embed AI into products people pay for, so their investment case is not dot-com math.
- Chip plays and AI-adjacent platforms trade on valuations assuming an unbroken line from AI activity to AI revenue, a line that "has never existed in tech. Not once."
- In client conversations he watches the ratio of "had to say yes" AI projects to "solved a real problem" projects — still uncomfortably low, but not zero.
- Urgency to deploy is outpacing discipline to deploy well; when the cycle turns, urgency-built companies will struggle to explain what they got for it.
