# Gaming for the Right Things — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/gaming-for-the-right-things/gaming-for-the-right-things.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that people game whatever they're measured against, and that this is a good thing — provided leaders put the right metrics in place. Using sales as the example, he says measures like client acquisition, revenue generation, and customer satisfaction motivate behavior and shape strategy and decision-making. The leadership job is to choose the right measures at the right time and evolve them as the organization and business lifecycle change.

## Key Claims

- "People game for whatever they're measured against" — metrics drive professional behavior.
- This is a good thing, provided the right metrics are in place.
- Candidate measures include client acquisitions, revenue generation, and customer satisfaction.
- The right measures depend on where the organization is and what outcomes it needs now.
- As the business landscape evolves, so must the metrics; they are "the pulse of the sales profession."

## Relevance

Informs [Goodhart's Law](/concepts/goodharts-law.md) — the post's premise that people optimize the measure, so the measure must align with the goal.
