# From “Tell Developers What To Build” To “Co‑Create With Machines” — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/from-tell-developers-what-to-build-to-co-create-with-machines/from-tell-developers-what-to-build-to-co-create-with-machines.md`

## Summary

Generative AI cracks open the old model where business-side roles decided what to build and handed requirements to developers, argues Tim Dickey. When AI can analyze feedback, turn plain-language descriptions into prototypes, and monitor competitors, the business side's value shifts from producing artifacts to orchestrating value: judgment, framing, and storytelling. The post offers three practical tips — become an excellent prompt engineer for business, lead cross-functional AI experiments rather than solo heroics, and reframe your career from a role to a capability stack.

## Key Claims

- AI clusters customer feedback, spots trends, drafts stories and flows, and recommends roadmap shifts, so the business side now co-creates with machines.
- The new center of gravity is judgment (choosing aligned ideas), framing (sharper questions), and storytelling (turning data into a narrative).
- Turn recurring tasks into reusable, strategy-aware prompts, and always pair AI output with a simple decision rule anchored to outcomes.
- Lead cross-functional experiments: AI-augmented discovery days, backlog refinement with AI as a "junior analyst," and AI-assisted go-to-market sessions where the team critiques the draft.
- Treat AI as an operating system change, not a personal productivity hack.
- Reframe career identity from title to capability stack — "comb-shaped" rather than T-shaped — and tell your story in outcomes, not tasks.
- High-value capabilities like framing, synthesis, facilitation, and ethical judgment age well in an AI-heavy environment.
