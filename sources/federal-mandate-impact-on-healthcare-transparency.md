# Does the Federal Mandate for Healthcare Cost Transparency Protect Consumers? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/federal-mandate-impact-on-healthcare-transparency/federal-mandate-impact-on-healthcare-transparency.md`

## Summary

Adam Clark examines whether federal healthcare cost-transparency mandates actually help consumers. He frames the problem as twofold: low health insurance literacy among consumers and a lack of clear, comparable cost information from insurers. The Transparency in Coverage Final Rule (2020), Consolidated Appropriations Act (2021), and No Surprises Act are described as the largest health-plan changes since the ACA, though they still leave final responsibility on consumers.

## Key Claims

- Over half of U.S. adults rate their health insurance literacy as inadequate, per the Health Reform Monitoring Survey.
- NPR/Kaiser Health News "Bill of the Month" examples include a $489,000 air ambulance bill and a $19,000 bill from a child's minor bike accident.
- The 2020 Transparency in Coverage Final Rule mandates transparency in coverage and service costs.
- The 2021 CAA added transparency requirements and the No Surprises Act, which protects against certain surprise medical bills.
- Mandated changes are phased from 2021 through 2024, and delays have already begun.
- Insurers must publish machine-readable files with detailed covered-item and drug prices, and offer internet-based price comparison tools covering in- and out-of-network providers.
- Member ID cards must show deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, and assistance contacts; members get good-faith estimates and advance EOBs.
- Legacy systems must be retired or retrofitted, and third-party vendors (pharmacy benefits, provider finders, card production) take on new responsibilities and contracts.
