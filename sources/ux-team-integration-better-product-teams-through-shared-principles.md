# UX Team Integration: Better Product Teams through Shared Principles — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ux-team-integration-better-product-teams-through-shared-principles/ux-team-integration-better-product-teams-through-shared-principles.md`

## Summary

Drawing on a virtual Lunch & Learn, the author argues that embedding UX in product teams works best through adaptable shared principles rather than a fixed rulebook. He compares Lean Startup, Agile, and Design Thinking as complementary routes to the same goal — solving real user problems — and lists ten integration principles from short cycles and retrospectives to radical transparency and making learning first-class backlog work. The payoff is a collaborative, cross-functional culture that keeps the customer at the center.

## Key Claims

- Agile responds to change through short sprints; Lean Startup builds only what is necessary around an MVP; Design Thinking cycles on real user problems — no single methodology fits all.
- Ten principles: work in short cycles, hold retrospectives, center the customer, go-and-see (gemba walks), test high-risk hypotheses, do less research more often, train as a team, be radically transparent, create aligned incentives, and make learning a first-class backlog item.
- "What gets visualized gets done" — learning work must be on the backlog to happen.
- Silos should give way to a collaborative, cross-functional mindset that shares findings, successes, and failures openly.
- The principles are meant to be adapted to context, not prescribed as rigid rules.
