# The Product Team's Evolution, Stage 4: Designing the System That Decides — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-4-designing-the-system-that-decides/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-4-designing-the-system-that-decides.md`

## Summary

Third in the product-team series, covering Stage 4, where hand review becomes programmatic. The checking moves out of the individual's head and into the workflow via a protection harness — automated gates that refuse to pass an artifact that fails them — and workflow sequencing dictates automating the simplest workflows first. Stage 4 is a team event that pulls in design ops, the PMO, and engineering, and it is where most organizations pause. It also surfaces a hard ceiling for non-technical practitioners: they can advise on and operate within team-built workflows, but authoring and debugging them is engineering work.

## Key Claims

- The protection harness is the set of automated checks between steps; sequencing means proving the simplest gates hold before climbing to harder ones.
- Product-team gates mirror lint, build, and test: story quality, requirements sufficiency, research evidence, and design-system conformance.
- A weak gate does not stay contained — a format-only story gate passes a quarter of confidently well-formed features that solve the wrong problem.
- The harness is only as good as its weakest gate, and a gate stamps the author's blind spot across outputs as fast as their judgment.
- The honest cap for a non-technical practitioner is operating within team-built Stage 4 workflows; authoring the harness requires technical depth.
