# Two Ways AI Changes Your Job — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/two-ways-ai-changes-your-job-demand-limited-vs-collapsable-tasks/two-ways-ai-changes-your-job-demand-limited-vs-collapsable-tasks.md`

## Summary

Liles argues that "does AI take your job?" is too coarse a question, and splits AI's effect on work into two task-level categories. A demand-limited task gets faster execution but faces a fixed demand constraint, so the freed time must be redirected to higher-value work or it becomes surplus capacity. A collapsable task is one AI absorbs entirely, removing the human step and forcing a conversation about what the role is for. The two demand different responses.

## Key Claims

- The analysis builds on Autor, Levy, and Murnane's 2003 "The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change," which replaced jobs with tasks as the unit of analysis.
- Demand-limited diagnostic: "If we could do this task in half the time, what would we do with the other half?" A clear valuable answer means execution is the constraint; "we'd do it less often" means demand is.
- Contract drafting example: four hours compressed to forty-five minutes; value depends on whether freed time goes to higher-value legal work.
- Collapsable examples: data transcription and normalization, routine summarization, and first-draft generation at scale — work done by humans only because they were the available engine.
- Jevons (1865) cuts against a fixed-demand assumption: cheaper power found new uses, so demand may expand as cost falls, requiring the diagnostic to be re-asked at each new price.
- Responses: demand-limited tasks call for role evolution; collapsable tasks call for role redefinition, plus keeping a quality gate over residual judgment.
