# People Watching - and maybe they're watching you — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/people-watching/people-watching.md`

## Summary

O'Hara describes a game he and his wife play, "What's that story?", in which they invent elaborate backstories for strangers they observe. What begins as entertainment turns into introspection: the stories we fabricate reflect our own values, assumptions, and beliefs rather than the subjects. Noticing which details he chose — and which he could have chosen instead — prompts him to ask how often the narratives we tell ourselves are projections of our own experiences, biases, and fears.

## Key Claims

- People-watching naturally turns into storytelling about strangers.
- Those stories reveal the storyteller's values and assumptions as much as the subject's reality.
- Examining the details chosen (and the alternatives available) is a useful introspective exercise.
- The narratives we build about others may mirror the narratives we hold about ourselves.
- Self-examination can start from the stories we fabricate about those around us.
