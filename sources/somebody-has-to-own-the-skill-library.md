# Somebody Has to Own the Skill Library — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/somebody-has-to-own-the-skill-library/somebody-has-to-own-the-skill-library.md`

## Summary

The post argues that the gap in enterprise agent adoption is not adoption but ownership: nobody has been assigned the job of deciding who may publish a skill, who reviews it, who owns it once live, and who may deprecate it. [Gartner](/entities/gartner.md) projects 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% a year earlier, while [Cloud Security Alliance](/entities/cloud-security-alliance.md) research places most organizations at minimal-control levels and Obsidian Security found 91% have deployed agents but only 10% have a governance strategy covering them. Picking either a centralized or federated model uniformly is the mistake; the answer is hybrid, with review rigor scaled to what a skill can touch.

## Key Claims

- Adoption is running ahead of governance; the missing piece is an assigned owner for publishing, review, and deprecation.
- Centralize everything and the domain expert must convince a platform team of what they already know; federate everything and a skill reaching regulated data ships with no more scrutiny than a formatting helper.
- Accountability splits along expertise: data governance owns reach policies, AI/platform owns the evaluation pipeline, security/compliance own the audit trail, domain teams own workflow fidelity.
- CSA converges on a cross-functional governance board with real authority to approve, pause, or reject a deployment.
- [Anthropic](/entities/anthropic.md)'s February 2026 enterprise plugin management — org-wide provisioning, admin-scoped roles, a marketplace functioning as the governance layer — is a live instance of the structure.
