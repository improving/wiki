# Agile Leadership Starts With You — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/agile-leadership-starts-with-you/agile-leadership-starts-with-you.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle argues that bringing in Agile coaches to "fix" teams or enforce practices rarely works, because teams then complete activities without committing to them. Real agility comes from instilling Agile ideals and applying continuous improvement to the coach's own work. She urges coaches to listen to why teams struggle, create space for transparency and experimentation, and recognize that management is an often-overlooked part of team coaching.

## Key Claims

- Enforced Agile practices lead teams to complete activities mechanically rather than commit to the practice.
- A deep understanding of why we work precedes how we work; coaches should coach, not enforce.
- Coaches should apply continuous improvement to their own approach, making small changes based on what is missing.
- If daily stand-up runs long, listen to why — it may signal a lack of space for collaboration or support.
- Psychological safety is the most important aspect of Agile; teams cannot improve without it.
- An Agile shift cannot happen in a vacuum — if the organization doesn't value people, collaboration, and dynamism, nothing about how teams work will succeed.
- Management needs coaching too, and communication with management about accomplishments and needs is essential.
