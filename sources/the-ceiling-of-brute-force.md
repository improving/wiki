# The Ceiling of Brute Force — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-ceiling-of-brute-force/the-ceiling-of-brute-force.md`

## Summary

Prompted by the phrase "the ceiling of brute force," O'Hara reflects on the point at which grinding harder stops producing growth. He observes that entrepreneurs default to rolling up their sleeves and applying more effort, but brute force drains energy while leaving the goal no closer — often without the person realizing why they are tired, burnt out, and failing. He attributes this to comfort-seeking and entropy, and notes the human tendency toward denial or rationalization instead of ownership. He then describes an ongoing personal experiment to become aware of the ceiling and break through it.

## Key Claims

- There is a real point at which working harder no longer works; brute force saps energy and puts you no closer to the goal.
- Humans tend toward comfort; systems tend toward chaos, so sustained progress is inherently uncomfortable.
- The avoidance shows up as denial ("I'm still growing!") or rationalization (blaming externalities rather than owning decisions).
- His first intervention is tracking his attention and every decision for one month — what the ask was, what he decided, and the factors used — an exercise he has done for nearly 15 years.
- His next step is to identify who else can make each decision, aiming for at least two people per decision, then to define the alignment, resources, support, and feedback needed to guide them.
