# Go Lean or Go Home — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/go-lean-or-go-home/go-lean-or-go-home.md`

## Summary

Gary Bergmann argues Agile and Lean are a "1-2 combination knock-out": Agile breaks work into small increments to reduce complexity and risk, while Lean focuses on efficiency at the pull of the customer by eliminating waste, errors, and delays. He walks through Lean's seven principles, showing how each maps to concrete Agile team practices, and concludes that adding Lean reframes and reinforces any Agile effort, including transformation.

## Key Claims

- The seven Lean principles: eliminate waste, build quality in, create knowledge, defer commitment, deliver fast, respect people, and optimize the whole.
- Eliminate waste means watching for Muda (wastefulness), Mura (imbalance), and Muri (overload) — e.g. outside problem-solving, extra approvals, or refining backlog items more than 1–2 sprints ahead.
- Build quality in means quality early and often, using Ishikawa diagrams and "five whys" for root cause, and poka yoke to mistake-proof product and process.
- Create knowledge happens in Scrum events, especially retrospectives practicing Hansei and Kaizen; swarming and non-solo work grow the team.
- Defer commitment means taking decisions at the last possible moment and architecting for flexibility so fewer increments are irreversible.
- Deliver fast via shorter sprints, incremental releases, and DevOps continuous delivery to reduce the risk of a bug inventory.
- Respect people means building self-managing, autonomous, enabled teams with clear objectives and a working agreement; optimize the whole means seeing how each team fits the value stream, often via value stream mapping.
