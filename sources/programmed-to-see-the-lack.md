# Programmed to See the Lack — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/programmed-to-see-the-lack/programmed-to-see-the-lack.md`

## Summary

O'Hara retells a parable: a math professor writes the nine times table on the board, gets "four times nine" wrong, and watches the class eagerly flag the single error while no one notes the nine correct answers. His point is that humans see the lack before the plenty — pattern-matchers by nature, but prone to looking for absence. The remedy is deliberate gratitude: instead of wishing for Friday or vacation, appreciate what you already have, one thing at a time, and say it aloud so your subconscious hears it. Gratitude, he argues, starts a virtuous cycle.

## Key Claims

- We notice the one wrong answer over nine right ones — we see the lack before the plenty.
- Pattern-matching is normal, but the propensity to look for absence is what hurts us.
- Gratitude for the present moment is difficult but worth the effort.
- Speaking gratitude aloud makes the subconscious register it and deepens the experience.
- Gratitude starts a virtuous cycle that lifts you up regardless of circumstances.
