# Why Isn’t Your Daily Stand Up Working? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/why-isnt-your-daily-stand-up-working/why-isnt-your-daily-stand-up-working.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle argues that many daily stand-ups fail because they amount to a check-in rather than goal-oriented collaboration, and some actively make employees feel less trusted. Poor stand-ups are a symptom of a larger issue, not the problem itself. She diagnoses three root causes: Scrum Masters forgetting to remove impediments, teams not being heard, and meetings that become prescriptive read-outs.

## Key Claims

- Daily stand-ups are used across business, not just Agile, to check that employees' needs are met and direction is clear.
- Per the Scrum Guide, the Scrum Master ensures the event follows process and removes impediments, but most focus on the read-out rather than blockers.
- Running long often signals team members cannot collaborate during the day — a barrier to work.
- Teams that stop sharing status often lack psychological safety; people who skip the meeting are not having their needs met.
- Meeting notes and consistency help, but not at the expense of doing the work; sometimes letting one meeting go off script sets a better standard.
- Stand-ups should be meaningful and, for many teams, a time for bonding as well as reporting.
