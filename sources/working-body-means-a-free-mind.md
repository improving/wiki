# Working Body Means A Free Mind — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/working-body-means-a-free-mind/working-body-means-a-free-mind.md`

## Summary

The author, who spends most of his week sedentary and is paid for his mind rather than his body, argues the two are less separate than he once thought. His favorite activity of the week is mowing the lawn: rote work that frees his mind to wander, pray, reflect, and process ideas — what his wife calls "the loudest quiet time imaginable." That time has let him clarify thoughts and game-plan interactions he knows are coming. It leads him to a delegation question: what would it take to hire someone to mow, and what else could he hand off so he can keep the time? Family and job, he decides, stay.

## Key Claims

- A sedentary knowledge-work life does not mean mind and body are separate concerns.
- Mowing the lawn is rote work that frees the mind for prayer, reflection, and processing ideas.
- That quiet time lets him clarify thoughts and rehearse future interactions for better results.
- The time is valuable enough that replacing it would be a large trade-off.
- Delegation question: what else could be outsourced to preserve the mowing time — though not family or job?

## Relevance

A concrete illustration of [delegation](/concepts/delegation.md) as protecting high-value thinking time rather than merely offloading chores.
