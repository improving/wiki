# What Are You Full Of? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/what-are-you-full-of/what-are-you-full-of.md`

## Summary

Starting from the line "If you want to know what you're full of, notice what comes out of you when others bump into you," O'Hara treats small collisions — traffic, a disrupted calendar, a genuine question — as tests of what we carry inside. The spill reveals beliefs, habits, and emotional patterns: snapping, sighing, or a defensive "I'm busy" offered as a shield. Whatever comes out is usually what has been brewing, so the work is to be intentional about what we allow in.

## Key Claims

- Reactions to small bumps reveal internal state more than reflex.
- Stress, anxiety, or resentment carried inside is what others receive.
- Practicing gratitude, reflection, and presence changes the spill toward compassion, curiosity, or grace.
- Poor sleep measurably degrades emotional regulation — the "emotional volume" turns up.
- The point isn't perfection but preparation; the bump is a reflection you can choose to shape.

## Relevance

Informs [emotional regulation](/concepts/emotional-regulation.md) and [self-awareness](/concepts/self-awareness.md) as daily, practiced capacities rather than fixed traits.
