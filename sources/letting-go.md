# Letting Go — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/letting-go/letting-go.md`

## Summary

O'Hara opens by owning his identity as a "geek" who has long been most comfortable in front of an IDE or command line. He explains that the blog began when he became President of [Improving](/entities/improving.md) as an outlet for the less technical side of things, but he kept writing — and hosting — it the technical way, using Jekyll with Heroku, Git/GitHub, and Atom. He announces a relaunch on the Ghost platform, still self-hosted, so he can focus more on content and less on the technical how, hoping it reduces the friction of writing. He notes that reducing friction, once a joy in development, has become a theme across his life.

## Key Claims

- The author took identity from being a technologist, and that comfort became an Achilles heel as much as an asset.
- The blog started at his transition to President of Improving to share the less technical side of his work.
- He kept it technical (Jekyll, Heroku, Git/GitHub, Atom), which scratched a personal itch but added friction.
- Relaunching on Ghost lets him focus on content over the mechanics of publishing.
- Reducing friction — making things better and less painful — has become a recurring focus now applied outside development.

A career-and-identity reflection: a leader consciously letting go of hands-on technical control, with tangential relevance to writing practice and career transitions.
