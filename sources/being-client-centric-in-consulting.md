# Being Client-Centric and Building Trust in Consulting — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/being-client-centric-in-consulting/being-client-centric-in-consulting.md`

## Summary

Understanding client needs, not just nodding along, is the foundation of a client-centric practice. The author argues consultants must dive deep to uncover what clients express and what they truly need, then translate business problems into technical solutions once success criteria are agreed. He organizes the advice around three trust behaviors from Covey's *Speed of Trust* — Clarify Expectations, Create Transparency, and Listen First — and stresses proactive communication, honest delivery of bad news, and empathy. Delivering beyond expectations and protecting the relationship, he says, is what differentiates a consultant in a competitive market.

## Key Claims

- Clients often describe their problems in terms that do not reflect the best solution; consultants must help them articulate outcomes and translate problems into technical solutions.
- Clarify Expectations, Create Transparency, and Listen First are three of the thirteen trust behaviors in Covey's *Speed of Trust*.
- Proactive communication — reaching out before the client must — and regular check-ins validate progress and prevent problems.
- Honesty, even delivering bad news, plus empathy can turn a negative situation into a stronger relationship.
- Happy clients are the best marketing: word-of-mouth outperforms advertising and retains existing business.
