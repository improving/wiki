# Growth Lines - periodicity of growth and the effort needed — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/growth-lines/growth-lines.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that growth as a benefit — getting better, more knowledgeable, more experienced — comes in bursts, is never linear, and always carries a cost. Drawing on the mindsets in George Leonard's *Mastery*, which Improving uses regularly as illustrations, he points to the plateau present in every one: after climbing a growth curve, you pull back from the peak to a plateau, and most people live there. Crucially, the plateau is not flat; it slopes downward because of entropy. Decay comes naturally and is the opposite of growth. He describes being thrust into a new growth period because the business needed it, calling it painful, challenging, not always fun, but rewarding every day.

## Key Claims

- Growth as a benefit is non-linear and always has a cost.
- Each *Mastery* mindset curve includes a plateau, and maintaining a peak indefinitely is impossible.
- The plateau slopes down, not flat, because entropy moves life toward disorder.
- Decay is what comes naturally; growth requires sustained effort against it.
- O'Hara entered a new growth period because the business required it, climbing another curve to the next level.
