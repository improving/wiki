# Empowering Your Data Estate: Modern Practices for Data Quality — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/empowering-your-data-estate-modern-practices-for-data-quality/empowering-your-data-estate-modern-practices-for-data-quality.md`

## Summary

The post argues data quality is a critical asset rather than a byproduct, and that poor quality costs millions annually through corrections, missed opportunities, compliance penalties, and reputational harm. It lays out seven foundations of quality, a four-level data-maturity model, practical governance steps, and tooling — insisting culture matters as much as technology.

## Key Claims

- Poor data quality affects efficiency and the bottom line through error correction, missed opportunities, compliance penalties, and lost trust.
- Seven foundations: accessibility, accuracy, completeness, consistency, precision/relevance, timeliness, and observability.
- Real consequences cited: incorrect credit scores affecting loan approvals for thousands, and data errors leading to homes mistakenly demolished.
- Four data-maturity levels: Data Neutral (reactive, manual), Data Aware (basic validation, Excel/SQL), Data Critical (proactive governance, KPIs, audits), and Data Driven (AI-ready, embedded).
- Practical steps: start small on pain points, build departmental ownership with data stewards, establish policies and playbooks, and hold a regular governance cadence.
- Tooling: MDM (Informatica, TIBCO), testing/validation (dbt, Validator), and cataloging/observability (Microsoft Purview, Monte Carlo).
- Culture matters — cites Toyota's Kaizen and cross-departmental shared responsibility.
