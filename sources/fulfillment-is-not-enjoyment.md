# Fulfillment Is Not Enjoyment — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/fulfillment-is-not-enjoyment/fulfillment-is-not-enjoyment.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles distinguishes enjoyment (what feels good now) from fulfillment (what matters in hindsight), arguing that treating them as the same thing leads people to chase the visible surface of fulfillment and miss the difficulty that produces it. Drawing on the hedonic treadmill, lottery-winner research, flow theory, and self-determination theory, he contends that enjoyment pursued as an end adapts away, while fulfillment arises from productive challenge that slightly exceeds current skill.

## Key Claims

- Brickman and Campbell's 1971 "hedonic treadmill" and the 1978 lottery-winner study show winners were not meaningfully happier and took less pleasure from ordinary events.
- Enjoyment is not the enemy — hedonism is; the fullest wellbeing comes from lives high in both hedonia and eudaimonia.
- Csikszentmihalyi's flow requires challenge to match (and slightly exceed) skill; competence, per self-determination theory, can only be earned against resistance.
- Not all difficulty is good: productive challenge gives feedback and traction, while overload produces anxiety and depletion; the skill is telling them apart.

## Relevance

Connects to [time horizon](/concepts/time-horizon.md): both argue that chasing the immediate reward erodes the durable outcome that deferred, compounding effort produces.
