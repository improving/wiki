# How Do You Handle Being Wrong? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/how-do-you-handle-being-wrong/how-do-you-handle-being-wrong.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that admitting error is essential for leaders because teams watch how executives handle mistakes, and owning up builds the trust that lets a culture of honesty take hold. He prescribes a sequence: play detective on the root cause, watch for deflection anti-patterns, clean up with a clear corrective plan, and embrace the learning. He illustrates each step with well-known cases — Steve Jobs and the iPhone 4 antenna, Netflix's Qwikster, Kodak's digital blind spot, and Johnson & Johnson's Tylenol response.

## Key Claims

- Owning a mistake publicly builds trust and models honesty for the team.
- Diagnose root cause first: incomplete data, emotion, or ignoring a warning from a teammate.
- Deflecting blame, making excuses, or ignoring the problem are anti-patterns that tank morale and credibility.
- Corrective plans should be clear, communicated openly, and co-created with the team.
- Mistakes are invaluable lessons; resilience and adaptability strengthen with use.
