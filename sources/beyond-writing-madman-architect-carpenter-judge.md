# Beyond Writing: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, Judge — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/beyond-writing-madman-architect-carpenter-judge/beyond-writing-madman-architect-carpenter-judge.md`

## Summary

Revisiting Betty Flowers's 1981 framework, the author argues it has almost nothing to do with writing and everything to do with how we think through anything complex. The Madman generates unfiltered, associative ideas; the Architect groups them into structure; the Carpenter refines the structure into something that holds; the Judge evaluates it against purpose. Each role has an anti-pattern that appears when one role invades another's stage, and the real insight is that the roles must happen in sequence with space between them. He applies the sequence to strategy, team dynamics, and building AI systems.

## Key Claims

- Flowers identified four modes that must occur in order: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, Judge.
- The Madman's anti-pattern is the Judge arriving early and killing divergent thinking; protect it with a no-deletion window, akin to Brené Brown's Shitty First Draft.
- The Architect needs distance — a week or more — so you organize around insight rather than enthusiasm, and should not start before the Madman has emptied the tank.
- A Carpenter refining unsettled structure produces "polished bad structure."
- The Judge needs a deadline; otherwise editing never ends and nothing ships.
