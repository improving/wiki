# Applause Is Not an Audit — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/applause-is-not-an-audit/applause-is-not-an-audit.md`

## Summary

Unverified vendor claims and unanimously praised governance proposals share one flaw: neither has faced an outsider with something to lose from getting the answer wrong. IBM reported a 45% productivity gain across more than 6,000 internal adopters of a Claude-based tool, but IBM measured IBM and nobody reproduced the number. Similarly, Demis Hassabis's "FINRA for frontier AI" drew near-universal praise that costs its supporters nothing, because no body exists yet to delay a release. Applause, like a self-reported number, is not an audit.

## Key Claims

- IBM reported an average 45% productivity gain across more than 6,000 internal adopters of an Agent Development Lifecycle tool built around Claude; the tool was still in private preview.
- Nobody outside IBM reproduced the number or defined the baseline, leaving selection bias standing in for peer review.
- Hassabis proposed an independent, industry-funded body reviewing models up to 30 days before release, with a majority-independent board; Altman, Musk, and Sacks all endorsed it.
- The real FINRA took decades and statutory authority to earn its bite, and is only as strong as the first time compliance costs a member something.
- Internal monitoring and external audit (Improving's RSM SOC 2 audit) are different evidence classes; only the external one a client can rely on.
