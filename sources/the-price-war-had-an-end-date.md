# The Price War Had an End Date — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-price-war-had-an-end-date/the-price-war-had-an-end-date.md`

## Summary

Argues that the assumption "open-weight tokens get cheaper every quarter" was a bet dressed up as a law. DeepSeek's R1 release in January 2025 began eighteen months of price cuts the rest of the market matched or undercut, and the streak was long enough to feel like physics. A price war ends either when the worse-cost competitor runs out of runway or when the volume winner runs out of margin to subsidize. On August 6, 2026 DeepSeek warned its own developers that API prices would rise "significantly," revealing the second story. A low price backed by a genuinely lower cost structure is durable; one backed by absorbed losses is a revocable subsidy, and from outside the two look identical.

## Key Claims

- DeepSeek told its own developers on August 6, 2026 that API prices were about to rise "significantly," with no percentage or date attached.
- Eighteen months of price cuts followed the R1 release, with model quality climbing across R1, V3, and V4 while price per token kept falling.
- OpenCode's August 1 usage numbers on DeepSeek V4 Flash pointed to adoption at a scale only plausible if a real share of the open-weight coding-assistant market routed through DeepSeek by default.
- A price war ends when the worse-cost competitor runs out of runway or the volume winner runs out of margin to keep subsidizing.
- Any architecture that priced a specific vendor's rate as a long-term constant made an unstated bet on which of those two stories was true.
