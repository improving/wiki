# Overcoming Imposter Syndrome: A Retrospective on My First Consulting Experience — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/overcoming-imposter-syndrome-a-retrospective-on-my-first-consulting/overcoming-imposter-syndrome-a-retrospective-on-my-first-consulting.md`

## Summary

Mason Steeger reflects on the imposter syndrome he felt starting at Improving and on his first client engagement, and on the habits that helped him past it. His prescriptions are continuous feedback-seeking, getting comfortable with not knowing, doing the work to the best of his ability, and deliberately acknowledging his own accomplishments.

## Key Claims

- Imposter syndrome began with self-doubt and grew into questions of self-worth and feeling like a fraud when he joined his first client.
- He asked peers and his Delivery Lead constant questions and sought continuous feedback, which reaffirmed he was meeting expectations; silence and guessing are "your worst enemy."
- Not having an immediate answer does not make you an imposter — it is an opportunity to grow, since learning only happens when you don't know something.
- Doing the work repeatedly, at the best of his ability, and cycling through code review feedback steadily reduced his self-doubt.
- A compliment from an engineer on his last day — that he dives deep to find solutions — prompted him to reflect on his accomplishments.
- He argues self-promotion and admiring your own work are critical, because a common symptom of imposter syndrome is not recognizing your accomplishments.
