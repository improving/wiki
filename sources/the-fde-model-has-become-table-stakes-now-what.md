# The FDE Model Has Become Table Stakes. Now What? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-fde-model-has-become-table-stakes-now-what/the-fde-model-has-become-table-stakes-now-what.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that forward-deployed engineering has stopped being cutting-edge now that TCS — a firm built on offshore cost delivery — plans to convert up to 8,900 engineers into forward-deployed roles. Where IBM, Deloitte, Accenture, and PwC announced FDE programs as offensive strategic plays from the top of the market, TCS is the defensive response from the cost-delivery side, which turns the model into the floor rather than a differentiator. He follows the shift from his earlier post on the opportunity to this one on what happens when the opportunity becomes the baseline, and asks what comes after forward deployed.

## Key Claims

- TCS announced plans to convert up to 8,900 engineers into forward-deployed roles.
- Job postings for forward-deployed roles grew 729% in twelve months.
- TCS's 8,900-person cohort is 1–1.5% of a 594,000-person firm — a structural rearchitecting of delivery, not a boutique practice.
- He counsels caution, recalling the "Prompt Engineer" craze and its vanished listings: "Tread lightly, but keep walking."
- Once FDE exists at that scale it becomes a baseline clients expect; differentiation shifts to who delivers it, how close they get to the business problem, and whether they drive outcomes rather than just showing up on-site.
