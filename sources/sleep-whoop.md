# Sleep & Whoop — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/sleep-whoop/sleep-whoop.md`

## Summary

O'Hara opens a personal sleep-tracking journey, recounting that he has long slept little and that it eventually became seriously bad. He cites a Wall Street Journal article stating only about 1–3% of people can survive on five hours of sleep or less, so most readers are not among them. After years of failed interventions — polyphasic sleep, medications, vitamins, minerals, crystals, powders, pillows, masks, and one 73-hour wake attempt to reset his circadian rhythm — he adopted the WHOOP wearable. What drew him was its battery-pack charging, which avoids removal and enables near-100% uptime his Apple Watch and Fitbit could not deliver. He frames the effort as an experiment, hoping more data helps refine his pursuit of better sleep.

## Key Claims

- Only about 1–3% of people can survive on five hours of sleep or less, per the WSJ.
- His attempts at hacking sleep (polyphasic sleep, medications, supplements, devices) failed.
- A 73-hour wake attempt to reset his circadian rhythm did not work.
- WHOOP's on-wrist battery pack enables near-continuous wear, unlike his Apple Watch and Fitbit.
- He expects no silver bullet, only better information to refine his efforts.
