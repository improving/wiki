# The Unseen Impact to Your Agile Transformation, Part II — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-unseen-impact-to-your-agile-transformation-part-ii/the-unseen-impact-to-your-agile-transformation-part-ii.md`

## Summary

Part 2 of Gary Bergmann's series argues that Agile transformations fail when coaches stop at adopting defined processes. Teams go through the motions without understanding the "why," and adding more processes makes things worse. The fix is to reframe process as practice, building continuous improvement, ownership, and accountability.

## Key Claims

- Introducing a process as a starting point is fine, but stopping at adoption usually does not deliver the desired results.
- Signs of the process adoption trap: a Scrum Master acting as process cop/project manager, estimates used only to show readiness, everyone required at every meeting, retros producing no meaningful action, and velocity reported without understanding.
- Causes include pressure to quantify Agile adoption via a maturity checklist, emotional exhaustion driving the path of least resistance, confirmation bias, and lack of experience.
- Shifting from process to practice builds shared ownership and accountability, since teams understand why a practice works and how to improve it.
- Practical shifts: tie processes to real problems, don't boil the ocean, embrace storming, and keep practices simple by starting from first principles.
- A practicing team has a shared definition of ready and done, produces specific retro actions, and uses velocity as a tool rather than a leadership report.
