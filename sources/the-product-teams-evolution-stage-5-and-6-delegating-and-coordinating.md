# The Product Team's Evolution, Stage 5 and 6: Delegating and Coordinating — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-5-and-6-delegating-and-coordinating/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-5-and-6-delegating-and-coordinating.md`

## Summary

Closes the product-team series by looking up the curve at Stage 5 (Delegate) and Stage 6 (Coordinate). Stage 5 hands whole workflows to agents that run on their own, which requires trusted signals — deterministic checks and adversarial validators that challenge whether an output is actually good before any handoff completes. Stage 6 runs many agents in parallel, making the practitioner's work arbitration rather than authorship. Each layer of orchestration costs roughly seven times the effort of the one below, which is why most organizations stall between Stages 4 and 5. Stage 5 demands developer-level skill, so non-technical practitioners cap at Stage 4.

## Key Claims

- Each layer of orchestration costs roughly seven times the effort of the one below: a month-long workflow becomes several months for an autonomous system and most of a year for a coordinated network.
- Delegation without trusted signals is just unattended risk; adversarial validators exist only to challenge whether an output is good.
- At Stage 6 a coordinating layer can assemble a quarter of roadmap from a dozen streams, multiplying any weak value gate two layers down.
- Stage 5 requires developer-level skill, so practitioners who stayed non-technical cap at Stage 4, advising on and operating within team-built workflows.
- Roughly sixty percent of people make the shift to encoding their standards comfortably; the rest need support, a different role, or time.
- Stages 7 and 8, supervision and orchestration, are aspirational today, with no reliable patterns yet.
