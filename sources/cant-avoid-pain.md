# Can't Avoid Pain — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/cant-avoid-pain/cant-avoid-pain.md`

## Summary

The author pushes back on the belief that enough information, planning, or trying can let you avoid pain. Pain is inevitable, and it comes in two forms: the pain of effort, the grindy, difficult work that anything worthwhile demands and that ultimately produces results, and the pain of regret, which he considers worse because it replays maybes and could-haves for unrealized potential. The only way to dispel regret is to lean into the pain of effort. Rather than avoiding pain, he recommends figuring out what the pain is for, using your "why" and goal as the focus that carries you through.

## Key Claims

- Pain cannot be avoided through more information, planning, or effort.
- Anything worthwhile requires difficult, often drudgery-like work; professionals and athletes testify that the hard things produce results.
- The pain of regret is worse than the pain of effort because it fills the mind with unrealized maybes.
- Regret for things done can be tempered by time, but regret for things not done is inconsolable (quoting Sydney J. Harris).
- The productive move is to identify what the pain is for and lean into it via your "why" and goal.
