# Struggling to Deliver Done Work Every Sprint? Part 1: Dependencies — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-1-dependencies/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-1-dependencies.md`

## Summary

Blake McMillan names dependencies as the first of three recurring reasons Scrum teams fail to finish work each Sprint. Dependencies, he argues, are a holdover from traditional project management that optimized specialist utilization rather than value delivered, so teams stay busy instead of producing done, feedback-ready work. The remedy is a single focused Sprint experiment with no external dependencies, run to prove the team can turn one idea into a real product update and turn skeptics into advocates.

## Key Claims

- The top three complaints from students and teams are dependencies, stakeholder pressure to take on work regardless of capacity, and unclear requirements.
- Dependencies come from traditional project management, where work flows through specialist phases: Analyze, Design, Build, Test, Deploy.
- Rotating experts across projects to avoid idle time maximizes headcount ROI on spreadsheets but optimizes staying busy over delivering value.
- Scrum teams still do all that work, but the change is doing it in a single Sprint to turn a hypothesis of value into a product update.
- You cannot change an entire organization on an idea alone; you need evidence.
- Seeking case studies from similar companies often creates excuses ("we don't have their funding") instead of momentum.
- The recommended first step is a one-Sprint experiment: bring all the right people together, with no other distractions, and see what they can do.
- Demonstrating one idea turned into a real update can create a hunger for more and convert skeptics into advocates.
