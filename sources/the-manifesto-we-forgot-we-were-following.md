# The Manifesto We Forgot We Were Following — Summary

**Original Source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-manifesto-we-forgot-we-were-following/the-manifesto-we-forgot-we-were-following.md`

## Summary

The [Agile Manifesto](/entities/agile-manifesto.md)'s four values were trade-offs formulated against a specific 2001 constraint — scarce human execution capacity — not timeless principles. Twenty-five years of practice hardened the "over" into an "instead-of," and now that AI removes the original constraint, those defaults produce what the author calls Manifesto debt. The fix, mirroring [technical debt](/concepts/technical-debt.md), is to name the pattern, ask whether the constraint still binds, and reformulate around intent using the P.O.S.T. framing.

## Key Claims

- The post defines technical debt as "the eventual consequence of continually trading craftsmanship for short-term velocity" — the pattern of normalization, not a single tradeoff, is the debt.
- [Ward Cunningham](/entities/ward-cunningham.md) coined the debt metaphor in 1992; its mechanism is normalization, which Manifesto misapplication reproduces.
- Defaults like "documentation is overhead" were defensible in 2001 and are dangerous in 2026, where AI can maintain documentation from code and conversations.
- P.O.S.T. restores intent: Individuals & Interactions × Processes & Tools = Productivity; Working Software × Comprehensive Documentation = Outcomes.
- Rebuttals: the text was misapplied rather than wrong; AI-generated docs risk recreating the failure and need quality gates; the constraint moved to human judgment rather than vanishing.

## Relevance

Connects to [technical debt](/concepts/technical-debt.md) and [software craftsmanship](/concepts/software-craftsmanship.md), and argues the review/judgment constraint now needs reformulating rather than trading off.
