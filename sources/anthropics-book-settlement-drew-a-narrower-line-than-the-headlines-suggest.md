# Anthropic's Book Settlement Drew a Narrower Line Than the Headlines Suggest — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/anthropics-book-settlement-drew-a-narrower-line-than-the-headlines-suggest/anthropics-book-settlement-drew-a-narrower-line-than-the-headlines-suggest.md`

## Summary

Anthropic's $1.5 billion copyright settlement is the largest on record, but the ruling underneath it holds that training language models on books is fair use. What actually cost Anthropic was narrower: it assembled and retained a central library of roughly 7 million pirated books, separate from books it purchased and scanned through legitimate channels. Training on the purchased material survived the fair-use test; warehousing pirated copies did not. For enterprise buyers, the settlement makes acquisition provenance — not the training question — the diligence that now matters.

## Key Claims

- Anthropic will pay $1.5 billion, roughly 500,000 titles at about $3,000 per book, the largest copyright payout on record.
- Training on purchased and scanned books survived fair use; building and holding a permanent repository of pirated copies did not.
- The class-action suit was brought by authors Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber, and Kirk Wallace Johnson.
- It is the first settlement among a wave of near-identical suits against OpenAI, Microsoft, and Meta.
- Buyers should request a named accounting of a training corpus broken out by acquisition method, comparable to a SOC 2 report.
