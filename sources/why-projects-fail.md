# Why Projects Fail — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/why-projects-fail/why-projects-fail.md`

## Summary

Responding to a Jeffrey Palermo article about software's dismal project success rate, Liles agrees the industry must do better but disagrees that the fix is only "software engineers need to change." He argues failure is a combination of business values that don't align with great software, the belief that sacrificing quality helps hit dates, and developers guessing at needs because what's asked for is often wrong. His remedy has three parts: open and honest communication, developer discipline, and trust built through consistent delivery of small, high-quality increments.

## Key Claims

- The Standish Group report shows a less-than-50% project success rate; even the more successful agile side implies a doctor with a 42% diagnosis rate.
- The only measure of software development that matters is working quality software delivered; focusing on weekly delivery would force the process to change.
- The iron triangle misleads: craftsmen cost more to hire but can produce quality software whose marginal cost per delivered unit is equal to or lower than "production line" developers.
- Business asking for a solution rather than presenting a problem locks teams into a technical mandate and stifles creativity.
- Trust is built by consistently delivering 3–4 features every two weeks, ideally 1–2 per week, not big systems every three months.

## Relevance

A classic [software craftsmanship](/concepts/software-craftsmanship.md) argument about aligning business values, quality, and delivery discipline.
