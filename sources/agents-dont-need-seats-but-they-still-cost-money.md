# Agents Don't Need Seats. But They Still Cost Money. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/agents-dont-need-seats-but-they-still-cost-money/agents-dont-need-seats-but-they-still-cost-money.md`

## Summary

When OpenAI moved workspace agents to token-based credit pricing on July 6, cost began scaling with what an agent actually does rather than whether it was invoked. O'Hara argues this is a new category of financial risk most enterprise procurement teams have not priced in. Replacing seat-licensed users removes the seat cost, but the agent's token spend is variable, usage-driven, and nearly invisible to the budget owners who approved the AI initiative. He notes a single autonomous 3 a.m. batch run can exceed the monthly seat cost it replaced, and concludes governance must cover the spend model, not just behavior.

## Key Claims

- OpenAI moved workspace agents to token-based credit pricing on July 6; cost scales with agent activity, not invocation.
- The $234B "SaaS-at-risk" figure is discussed as a vendor problem; the flip side—variable agent token spend—is not.
- Seat costs disappear when agents replace seat-licensed users, but token spend is variable and invisible to budget owners.
- A single autonomous 3 a.m. batch run can exceed the monthly seat cost it replaced.
- 98% of FinOps teams now manage AI spend as a priority, but procurement and legal lag behind.
- Without an owner for the forecast, spike alerts, and throttling authority, a company has an AI experiment with an open tab, not governance.
