# Better Power Apps by Design — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/better-power-apps-by-design/better-power-apps-by-design.md`

## Summary

Power Apps lets non-developers build apps quickly, but bare-bones results look poor, are hard to use, and resist change. The fix is to embrace design — even a modest investment in UX/UI and architectural principles produces a more intuitive, consistent, and maintainable app at far less cost than traditional development.

## Key Claims

- Power Apps saves the time and expense of traditional development but loses its luster when the result is ugly, awkward, or built on a brittle architecture.
- The UX designer and Power App developer should collaborate from the start: joint stakeholder interviews, low-fidelity wireframes, and shared user flows.
- Consistency is key — use page layout templates, keep controls in familiar places, and design a "happy path" that removes clicks.
- Prototype early and test with real users rather than writing documentation; refine and repeat on feedback.
- Of 60+ available controls, a quality app should use fewer than ten, grouped into components, preferring out-of-the-box features.
- Use global styling and global components to cut duplicate work, and separate UI concerns from data-source structure with atomic saves for large records.
- A client's 8-week traditional build became a one-week bare-bones Power App; adding design resources extended it to three weeks for a far better product.
