# The Pocket Barrier Is Gone — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-pocket-barrier-is-disappearing/the-pocket-barrier-is-disappearing.md`

## Summary

O'Hara imagines glasses that pull up a passerby's LinkedIn before you speak, and argues the real shift in wearables is not the hardware but the removal of friction. The "pocket barrier" — pulling out a phone, opening an app, typing — is an accidental pause between impulse and action. Remove it and impulse buying, trip planning, and decisions become commands rather than projects. He warns that some of the cognitive overhead being eliminated was doing useful work, and that trust will decide which companies get access to the most intimate parts of daily life.

## Key Claims

- The device transition (glasses, pins, pendants) matters less than the friction it removes.
- The pocket barrier is a pause between impulse and action; without it, impulse buying rises sharply.
- Commands replace projects — "plan a spring break trip" becomes one utterance, eliminating cognitive overhead that was doing useful work.
- Qualcomm's CEO frames trust — which companies get access to intimate daily life — as the next decade's battleground.
- Winning will be about character and trust, not just the best product.
