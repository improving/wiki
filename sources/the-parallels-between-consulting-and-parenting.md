# The Parallels Between Consulting and Parenting — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-parallels-between-consulting-and-parenting/the-parallels-between-consulting-and-parenting.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle argues consulting and parenting share a method: rather than solving problems for people, provide guidance and let them work through issues; create psychological safety so experimentation and minor failure are safe; and accept that skills are built through repetition, not a single explanation. She draws on cooking with her daughter, shoelace tying, and Marine and martial-arts drilling, concluding that trust is the common thread.

## Key Claims

- Provide guidance over solutions: letting people struggle and learn builds capability, as when her daughter learned to pull eggs off the heat.
- Creating a safe space means not removing tasks people are capable of trying, and celebrating lessons from minor failures.
- Experimentation should be safe except where failure is catastrophic — "we do not experiment with chainsaw juggling at home."
- Repetition, not one explanation, builds competence; Marine drill and martial-arts kata illustrate how training becomes automatic.
- "But I already told them" misjudges how learning works — experienced people forget what it is like to be a beginner.
- Trust is the common thread across parenting, coaching, and managing; without it the partnership needed to grow cannot form.
