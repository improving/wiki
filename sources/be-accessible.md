# Be Accessible — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/be-accessible/be-accessible.md`

## Summary

The author challenges the common "open door" policy, which many companies and individuals claim but do not honor. He takes the opposite stance, telling people he is always available, which he says breeds trust because they know they can reach him when they need to. Being available does not mean dropping everything on demand: he makes room and lets people prioritize their own needs within the queue that is his job. That hands responsibility to them, and he finds most people self-filter and only reach out when it matters, with edge cases handled as exceptions.

## Key Claims

- Open-door policies are frequently claimed but not genuinely honored.
- Stating that you are always available breeds trust in the people who hear it.
- Accessibility does not require dropping everything; it means making room and letting people help prioritize their own requests.
- Pushing prioritization to the requester trusts them not to abuse access, and most people avoid being a bother.
- Some people will take advantage, but those are edge cases to treat as exceptions rather than reasons to filter everyone.

## Relevance

Frames personal availability as a deliberate [trust building](/concepts/trust-building.md) behavior in a leadership relationship.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Very short personal-opinion piece; asserts the trust payoff rather than evidencing it, and offers no concrete examples.
