# Building Operational Visions for Improving — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/operational-visions/operational-visions.md`

## Summary

The author describes a practice he has followed for decades: writing an operational vision for the coming year. He defines it as more than a plan — a blueprint describing, in detail and as if already achieved, what success will look like. It deliberately avoids the "how" (that is strategy) and instead sets the target. The post gives the general structure, the benefits, and three hard-won lessons from running the exercise.

## Key Claims

- An operational vision is a snapshot of the future written as if it has already happened; it states outcomes, not the strategy for reaching them.
- The structure has three parts: Timeframe (typically end of next year; 3- and 10-year versions also possible), Context (assumptions, beliefs, culture — "the soil"), and Outcomes (a mix of objective and subjective milestones that often group into themes).
- Visions create clarity, alignment, and visibility, helping teams see how their roles contribute to the larger mission.
- Writing goals down is a proven motivator, and the vision extends that principle to the organization.
- Lessons: write in the present tense, do not draft in a vacuum (a group of about 6–7 or fewer), and include concrete measures where they make sense.
- There have been times the team "fell victim" to omitting measures, so err toward more and let the team sort objective from subjective.
