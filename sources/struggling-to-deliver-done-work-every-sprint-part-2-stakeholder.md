# Struggling to Deliver Done Work Every Sprint? Part 2: Stakeholder Pressure — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-2-stakeholder/struggling-to-deliver-done-work-every-sprint-part-2-stakeholder.md`

## Summary

Blake McMillan names the top three blockers to delivering done work every sprint — dependencies, stakeholder pressure, and unclear requirements — and focuses on the second. When a stakeholder drops an "urgent request" mid-sprint, he argues, teams should make the trade-offs explicit: check whether the Sprint Goal still holds, let the people doing the work decide what gives, and record the change. The alternative is hidden scope creep and burnout.

## Key Claims

- Urgent items (production support, cybersecurity, legal, compliance) should be the exception, not the rule; new ideas and changes are the other, non-urgent disruptions.
- First ask whether the change makes the Sprint Goal obsolete — "ninety-nine times out of a hundred, the answer is no."
- Take the request to the team; those doing the work get the first say on what to remove, defer, or shrink.
- Trade-offs are real scope decisions, not extra hours, weekends, or "just push harder."
- The Product Owner makes the final call, but the conversation is transparent so everyone sees the cost.
- Capture the change in the Sprint Backlog with a reason comment to spot patterns over time.
- Over time, patterns reveal recurring stakeholders or "urgent" item types and can drive a policy tweak.
