# The Build Decision Changed the Relationship — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-build-decision-changed-the-relationship/the-build-decision-changed-the-relationship.md`

## Summary

O'Hara treats the finding that a third of enterprises skipped a software purchase because they could build it themselves with AI coding tools as a relationship change, not a procurement trend. He notes his own career-long default was "buy," and still is in the right situations, but argues agentic coding has moved the question from "should we buy this platform?" to "wait, can we just build this?" The consulting opportunity, he contends, was always judgment rather than labor, so firms must reposition around what clients genuinely cannot do for themselves.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey's State of AI 2026 found 32% of organizations skipped at least one software purchase because they could build an equivalent internally with agentic coding tools.
- Among AI high performers — the 6% attributing more than 5% of EBIT to AI — that figure climbs to nearly 50%.
- The EBIT impact of AI held flat at 37% of companies despite the building activity, and one in five organizations limits AI use because of operating costs.
- Building capability did not automatically produce the judgment to build the right things, in the right order, with the right governance — he has seen impressive things built that nobody needed.
- Surviving firms reposition around figuring out what is worth building, measuring whether it worked, and knowing when to stop before sunk-cost thinking takes over.
