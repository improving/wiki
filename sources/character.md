# Character — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/character/character.md`

## Summary

Starting from the idea that "character is the sum total of our choices in life," the author argues character is not fixed or purely innate but something we shape through deliberate choices. He walks through small daily decisions — picking a blue suit over a T-shirt and jeans because meetings called for it, choosing food carefully because gluten leaves him foggy for days, deciding to get up and work out because being a "fitness guy" is part of the character he wants. Reflecting on how many choices he makes daily, he wonders how intentional he really is, and closes by asking what outcomes and character he wants, and where his choices do or don't align with them.

## Key Claims

- Character is the sum total of our choices, so it can be shaped rather than treated as immutable.
- We make hundreds of choices a day; many seem trivial but reveal how intentional we are.
- Small choices — attire, food — matter when tied to a desired outcome, such as showing up professional or avoiding a multi-day energy crash.
- Choosing actions aligned with a desired identity (for example, a "fitness guy") is how character is built.
- The productive question is what character you want and where your choices do and don't align with it.

## Relevance

A personal-development reflection on deliberately shaping identity through choice, illustrating [self-awareness](/concepts/self-awareness.md) about one's own intentionality.
