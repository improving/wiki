# Rethinking Intelligence: From Brains to Systems — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/rethinking-intelligence-from-brains-to-systems/rethinking-intelligence-from-brains-to-systems.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues that the leap in human intelligence came not from bigger brains but from building tools that externalize and compound learning — separating the "how to build" from the "how to use." Writing and math created symbolic storage; the scientific method, markets, and protocols enabled collaboration; and constitutions and laws added governance. Today's AI, he contends, has scaled the neural "brain" while lacking the symbolic memory, collaborative frameworks, and governance that make intelligence robust and trustworthy.

## Key Claims

- Tools democratize capability by distributing expertise: a few invent, many apply, and users need not understand the underlying engineering.
- Tool-augmented LLMs (GPT-4 with tool use, Gemini) mirror this by calling external APIs or running code to separate reasoning from execution.
- Symbolic storage is echoed in AI by knowledge graphs, databases, and retrieval-augmented generation, and by memory-augmented networks and vector databases that persist context across sessions.
- Collaboration shows up in multi-agent systems and federated learning (Google's Gboard, Apple), with AlphaFold and open-source communities like Hugging Face as examples.
- Governance is shifting from static rules to dynamic governance agents that monitor and audit "thinking agents," as in Anthropic's Constitutional AI and AutoGen critic agents.
- Current systems hallucinate, struggle with provenance, and cannot always explain reasoning because they were built without symbolic memory, collaboration, and governance scaffolding.

## Relevance

Frames a systems-level view of [symbolic AI](/concepts/symbolic-ai.md), [retrieval-augmented generation](/concepts/retrieval-augmented-generation.md), [multi-agent coordination](/concepts/multi-agent-coordination.md), and [AI governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md), arguing intelligence lives in the system rather than the model.

## Quality

Essay by Devlin Liles; broad and readable, drawing analogies rather than evidence, and light on specifics of how such systems get built.
