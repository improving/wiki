# Wow versus Wonder — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/wow-versus-wonder/wow-versus-wonder.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara separates two experiences leaders often conflate. "Wow" is a sudden burst of surprise and amazement; "wonder" is an ongoing state of curiosity and awe. Relying only on wow moments is shortsighted because hedonistic adaptation pulls people back to their baseline happiness, as with bonuses, raises, or drug use. Wonder, by contrast, endures and drives exploration and growth.

## Key Claims

- "Wow" seizes attention and sparks innovation but lacks lasting impact
- Hedonistic adaptation explains why wow experiences fade quickly
- "Wonder" leaves a durable impression and fuels creativity and growth
- Wow and wonder are not opposites; their fusion creates transformative experiences
- Leaders should combine fleeting awe with continuous curiosity to captivate and sustain interest

## Relevance

Informs leadership practice around motivation, [growth mindset](/concepts/growth-mindset.md), and building environments where people keep exploring rather than chasing one-off thrills.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. A short reflective essay built on personal observation rather than data; hedonistic adaptation is the only external concept cited, and the author ends by asking readers for their own examples.
