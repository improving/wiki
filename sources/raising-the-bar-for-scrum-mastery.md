# Raising the Bar for Scrum Mastery — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/raising-the-bar-for-scrum-mastery/raising-the-bar-for-scrum-mastery.md`

## Summary

Don McGreal cites ScrumMatch data that 38% of Scrum Masters have little to no understanding of Scrum's core principles, often never having read the Scrum Guide, and blames certification-focused hiring over practical proficiency. He describes Improving's three-part response: rigorous assessment and selection, continuous learning and development, and practical experience with mentorship. The post argues for depth and real-world application over certificates.

## Key Claims

- ScrumMatch reports that 38% of Scrum Masters have little to no understanding of Scrum's core principles.
- Many organizations emphasize certification at the expense of real-world application.
- Improving assesses candidates on applying Scrum in real-world scenarios, not just certification.
- Training is practical and immersive, using case studies, simulations, and peer learning.
- Experienced Scrum Masters mentor newer practitioners in real project environments.
