# Communication Myths and Truths — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/communication-myths-and-truths/communication-myths-and-truths.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle argues most organizations misunderstand communication, treating it as an all-or-nothing broadcast of every detail rather than a repeated, contextual exchange. She debunks three myths — that communication means telling everything, that saying it once suffices, and that information is actionable when delivered — and offers three practices: share often with room for feedback, be honest, and respond when communication goes quiet.

## Key Claims

- Myth: communication means telling everything. Focus on the behaviors you want changed; private detail is rarely needed to drive action.
- Myth: you can say it once. Reaching a large group takes repeated messaging across different channels.
- Myth: information delivered is actionable at that moment. Time and context matter, and messages compete for attention.
- Share big-picture information often and be explicit about whether you are informing or seeking feedback.
- "A lack of communication does not quell concerns; it feeds paranoia" — silence gets filled with misinformation and unrest.
- Effective communication builds trust and leads to more people carrying the load toward shared goals.
