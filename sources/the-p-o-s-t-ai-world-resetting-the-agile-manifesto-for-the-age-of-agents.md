# The P.O.S.T. AI World: Resetting the Agile Manifesto for the Age of Agents — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-p-o-s-t-ai-world-resetting-the-agile-manifesto-for-the-age-of-agents/the-p-o-s-t-ai-world-resetting-the-agile-manifesto-for-the-age-of-agents.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues the Agile Manifesto was a product of scarcity — its "over" was an admission that human execution capacity could not afford both sides. As AI removes that constraint, both sides can multiply instead of competing: individuals and interactions × processes and tools, working software × comprehensive documentation. He maps the shift across three eras — Constraint, Synergy, and Intent — and argues the real roadmap is the evolution of human-AI trust, with governance, not a smarter model, as the unlock.

## Key Claims

- The manifesto's either/or trade-off is collapsing; the "over" becomes a multiplication sign, with both sides amplifying and compounding.
- Three eras: Constraint (AI as tool, humans approve everything, capability levels 0–2), Synergy (task and workflow agents, governance replacing individual permissions, levels 3–6), and Intent (meta-agents coordinating swarms, humans define what and why, `(I&I × P&T) ^ Intent`, levels 7–8).
- The critical inflection point sits between permission mode and autonomy mode; most organizations stall there, and the unlock is better governance, not better AI.
- Three infrastructure layers are needed: hybrid intelligence, a governance engine (audit trails, boundary enforcement, quality gates), and an operating model for managing many agents.
- Generated artifacts need the same verification gates as generated code; comprehensive-but-wrong documentation is worse than none, which is why the governance engine sits at the center.
