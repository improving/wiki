# Judgment-Bound vs. Form-Bound: The AI Split You're Not Making — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/judgment-bound-vs-form-bound-the-ai-split-youre-not-making/judgment-bound-vs-form-bound-the-ai-split-youre-not-making.md`

## Summary

AI-assisted work produces either a genuine multiplier or a cleanup job, and the difference is the category of work, not the model or prompt. Liles splits knowledge tasks into judgment-bound work (reasoning only the domain expert can supply) and form-bound work (knowing what good structure looks like). Both can be accelerated, but the approaches differ completely, and confusing them produces hollow deliverables that look finished. Using a business analyst writing a user persona, he shows how AI can generate the form while supplying none of the substance.

## Key Claims

- Every knowledge task is judgment-bound or form-bound; they are two separate process types, not a spectrum.
- For judgment-bound work the right AI application is Socratic — it asks questions, organizes answers, and pressure-tests reasoning while the human makes every call.
- A checklist is a forcing function that keeps experts from skipping steps under time pressure; AI-enforced prompting makes it hold at scale.
- For form-bound work the right application is generative: AI produces the artifact, human verifies against a well-defined rubric a non-expert could apply.
- If review requires judging whether content is right, the work has slipped into judgment-bound territory.
