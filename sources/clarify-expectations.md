# Clarify Expectations — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/clarify-expectations/clarify-expectations.md`

## Summary

Gabriela Garza-Ramos, a recruiter at Improving, describes how she uses the trust behavior "Clarify Expectations" from Stephen M.R. Covey's *The Speed of Trust* in both client engagements and candidate hiring. She argues that failing to confirm what the other person actually wants wastes time and resources, and that "clear is kind" — even when clarity means a candidate isn't right for a role.

## Key Claims

- Making assumptions about what a client wants is easy and harmful; the fix is to reflect back what you understood and ask, "Is this correct?"
- A client describing a "red square" that the listener hears as a rectangle is the classic failure mode of premature agreement.
- Recruiters must set expectations during interview, hiring, and onboarding so new hires know their role and how to succeed from day one.
- Clarifying expectations can reveal a poor fit without breaking the relationship — the recruiter can return with a better-matched opportunity later.
- The practical starting move is to ask questions: step back, listen first, understand the other perspective, then ask.
