# The Power of Incremental Change in Teams: Like Water on Rock — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/like-water-on-rock/like-water-on-rock.md`

## Summary

Gary Bergmann argues that rushing teams through change backfires; like water carving a canyon, transformation requires patience, persistence, and attention to culture before process. He advises understanding the team first, earning trust, and starting with small culture-oriented steps before introducing process changes.

## Key Claims

- Teams are still producing and often behind; they cannot absorb rapid successive changes and will revert to what they know.
- Understand the audience first — interview team members, leaders, and stakeholders for pain points and history — before thinking about change.
- Culture and belief systems are the hardest things to change; trust acts are small cracks that widen faster than the surface.
- Set clear outcomes, how to measure them, and how much change is enough.
- Culture-first steps: make work visible and measurable, get the team talking about value, shift from "I" to "we," identify customers, and solve problems together.
- Process steps follow: daily stand-ups, smaller deliverable units, a planning cadence, DevOps delivery, retrospectives, and coaching as a team member rather than an outsider.
- Slow change feels like no change unless you reflect on where you started.
