# The Most Important Person in Your AI Governance Program Isn't in Your C-Suite — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-most-important-person-in-your-ai-governance-program-isnt-in-your-c-suite/the-most-important-person-in-your-ai-governance-program-isnt-in-your-c-suite.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues that most AI governance org charts describe the right executive roles but omit the person closest to the work. Governance needs two layers: a governance layer (board, CAIO, CRO, CISO, DPO, enterprise architect) and an operational layer (a standing council plus the underrated AI Champion). The Champion converts governance from a top-down mandate into a ground-level quality signal, catching subtly wrong output before it compounds.

## Key Claims

- One AI Champion per automated workflow pilots the agent, reviews failures weekly, updates the skill definition, and iterates until satisfaction reaches roughly 95% before org-wide publication.
- Two intake filters matter most: the complexity-versus-difficulty distinction (difficulty work needs mandatory human-in-the-loop) and demand constraint (bounded vs. unconstrained output).
- Human-in-the-loop must be specified as a concrete control — e.g. "any external communication containing a dollar figure above $10,000 routes to a human" — not as a vague feeling.
- Organizations with AI Champions catch problems in iteration; those without find them in production, in front of customers, regulators, and finance processes.
- The rule applied in client work: AI does not communicate with external humans on the company's behalf.
