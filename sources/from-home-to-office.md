# From Home to Office: A Journey of Flexibility and Team Bonding — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/from-home-to-office/from-home-to-office.md`

## Summary

Writing from a flexible-work team, the author argues for balance between home and office rather than a polarized either/or. His team started all-remote in July 2020, then asked to meet in the office for ceremonies (planning, review, retro, refinement, design). In-person sessions improved communication and collaboration, earned client trust, and led to a client-site visit where the team gathered firsthand UX observations on sticky notes and synthesized them at a whiteboard. He journals a typical week: office days for collaboration and hallway chats, home days for deep work.

## Key Claims

- The team began all-remote in July 2020 and chose to return to the office for sprint ceremonies four months into the project.
- In-person presence improved communication and collaboration with stakeholders and helped earn client trust.
- A client-site visit let the team see operations firsthand and gather observations; they synthesized them on sticky notes at a whiteboard.
- Virtual tools have improved but still cannot match a room of people moving stickies on a shared whiteboard.
- Office days produce hallway conversations; one such chat led to a weekly tennis group running two and a half years.
- Home days support focused deep work; the author is productive in both environments.
- The author proposes office activities such as cross-project pair programming, songwriting, and improv.
