# Growing into Leading Remote — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/remote-leadership/remote-leadership.md`

## Summary

O'Hara asks how leadership skills are challenged when employees are dislocated from one another. He frames the post as a question he has wrestled with privately and now raises publicly. While he is skeptical of a "me-centered" desire for remoteness, he accepts that workforce demand for distributed work is rising and argues leaders should not fight it but lead toward a better way — starting by changing themselves. The open problem is how to build connection when a team is not together physically.

## Key Claims

- Focusing only on results (e.g. ROWE, or "I can work anywhere as long as I get it done") overlooks the human need for connection.
- The emotional, productivity, and engagement synergies of working together physically have been repeatedly demonstrated.
- Telepresence has improved but still falls short of bridging the gap created by remote work.
- Demand for dislocated work is increasing; leaders should adapt rather than resist.
- Leaders must grow new skills to bridge an increasingly disconnected team, and typically change themselves first.

## Relevance

Informs [deep connection](/concepts/deep-connection.md) and the leadership challenge of sustaining it across distance.

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Mostly a framing of open questions rather than answers; the claim about co-location synergies is asserted without citation.
