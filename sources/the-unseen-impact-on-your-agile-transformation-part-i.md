# The Unseen Impact on Your Agile Transformation, Part I: Confirmation Bias — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-unseen-impact-on-your-agile-transformation-part-i/the-unseen-impact-on-your-agile-transformation-part-i.md`

## Summary

Part 1 of Gary Bergmann's series argues that confirmation bias leads Agile coaches and organizations to apply familiar frameworks regardless of fit. He traces where the bias comes from and offers five practices to recognize and counter it, keeping the focus on client needs and outcomes.

## Key Claims

- Confirmation bias is processing information to support existing beliefs, and people are especially prone to it when the issue is important or self-relevant.
- Sources include an organization believing a framework like Scrum or Disciplined Agile will fix all impediments, experts deeply trained in one framework, and sunk cost in a single certification path.
- Applying Scrum where experimentation and inspection are not needed — such as a help desk resolving urgent tickets with known solutions — is a poor fit.
- Countermeasures: gather evidence-based current-state data, keep focus on client needs, consider factors outside the team, research other frameworks, and return to Agile/lean values and principles.
- Coach the mindset first, since all things Agile and lean are built on values and principles, not tools.
- Incrementally confirm assessments and changes, keeping the goal in sight so focus stays on outcomes rather than solutions.
