# My AI Journey as a UX Designer — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ux-designer-ai-journey/ux-designer-ai-journey.md`

## Summary

The popular "three days to three hours" framing of AI in design misses the point: producing mediocre work faster is not better work. The author's reframe is that AI is most valuable when it helps him learn faster — accelerating research synthesis, widening ideation, and enabling early prototyping and critique — while conversations with users and the judgment calls that make design meaningful stay human.

## Key Claims

- AI can build UI faster and explore multiple layout directions in minutes, but that production power is worthless without the right conversations first.
- Conversations with users still happen at the speed of humans; the sequence is understand the problem, then the user, then the business value, and only then bring AI in to explore solutions.
- Synthesis — making sense of scattered research into actionable insights — is design's quiet tax; AI rarely surfaced something unnoticed, but regularly helped articulate a hunch into a framed insight, so the gain was confidence rather than time saved.
- Ideation: AI generates a wide spread of directions quickly as provocations; the surprising ones seed new directions, and the human stays the decision maker — "what AI produces is raw material, what you choose to pursue is the design."
- Prototyping: AI makes rough prototypes from written descriptions within hours of a framing conversation, creating a "30-second wow" that provokes genuine user reactions early.
- Critique is embedded in the back-and-forth with AI, pushing on assumptions and alternatives, so by team review the conversation shifts from "what is this?" to "is this the right call?"
- The author is candid that he is still early, especially with agentic workflows, and has no clean answers to designing for partially self-automating processes or keeping humans meaningfully in the loop when the loop moves faster.
