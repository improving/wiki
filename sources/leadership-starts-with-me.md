# Leadership Starts with Me — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/leadership-starts-with-me/leadership-starts-with-me.md`

## Summary

O'Hara contends that managers are limited by cognitive load: because "stuff" can't think, they must, and their thinking capacity becomes the bottleneck on what can get done. Managing people implicitly treats them as incapable of thinking — as things — whereas leading distributes that cognitive load across the people in your care. That frees the leader to create space for others to reach their maximum capacity. Growth of others therefore requires the leader to grow first, [leading by example](/concepts/leading-by-example.md), with the primary goal of serving others rather than collecting individual achievements.

## Key Claims

- Managers are bottlenecked by their own cognitive load, since things cannot think for them.
- "Managing" people implies they are incapable of thinking and therefore only things.
- Leadership distributes cognitive load across the team, letting the leader focus on creating space for growth.
- To grow others you must grow as a leader, demonstrating that growth through example.
- Success is measured not by individual heights reached but by how far you bring others along.

## Relevance

Frames [leading by example](/concepts/leading-by-example.md) as the mechanism by which leaders multiply capability rather than merely directing work.
