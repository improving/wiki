# The Skill Leveling Effect: What Happens When AI Helps Everyone Equally — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-skill-leveling-effect-what-happens-when-ai-helps-everyone-equally/the-skill-leveling-effect-what-happens-when-ai-helps-everyone-equally.md`

## Summary

Challenges the conventional wisdom that more skilled workers benefit most from AI. The data points the other way: gains skew sharply to junior and mid-level workers because AI supplies the non-expert knowledge that was their limiting factor, while experts' judgment bottlenecks don't compress. The organizational consequence is that the performance floor rises, and the second-order effect is that judgment — the work AI can't level — becomes relatively scarcer and more valuable.

## Key Claims

- A study of Fortune 500 AI deployments found roughly +35% productivity for junior and mid-level workers versus about +3% for subject matter experts and senior practitioners.
- Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond found the largest gains among the newest, least skilled customer-support agents, with little measurable effect on the most experienced.
- Noy and Zhang found ChatGPT improved professional writing most for the weakest writers, compressing the gap between participants.
- Dell'Acqua's "jagged frontier" refines the claim: leveling holds inside AI's capability, while tasks just outside it degrade performance for nearly everyone.
- Hiring and management shift toward the operating model: if AI shrinks the gap between good and average, the marginal value of a top-10% hire over a top-40% hire decreases.

## Relevance

Defines the [skill leveling effect](/concepts/skill-leveling-effect.md) and argues its main organizational consequence is the primacy of the [operating model](/concepts/operating-model.md) over individual talent, with [judgment-bound work](/concepts/judgment-bound-work.md) as the scarce premium skill.
