# Truth — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/truth/truth.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that truth is divisive by nature, but since lies are equally divisive, divisiveness itself is not the problem — what you divide into is. He contends that some division is necessary: without boundaries there is no shape to relationships or direction, and too many open possibilities paralyze people (few things are as terrifying as a blank sheet of paper). He warns that forcing simplicity has sharp edges too: you can turn a non-binary decision into a [false dichotomy] and miss a better combinatorial answer, sometimes called compromise. His conclusion is a rule for handling contested issues — speak your truth, but be mindful of what you divide into, because division along shared values or shared belief in a problem produces cooperation and positive change, while division along differences only produces conflict.

## Key Claims

- Truth divides, but lies divide too; divisiveness is not the underlying issue.
- Without division there are no boundaries, and relationships and direction suffer.
- Too many choices paralyze; simplicity is a virtue with sharp edges.
- Treating a non-binary decision as binary is a false dichotomy that forfeits compromise.
- Dividing into groups based on shared values or a shared belief in an issue can turn divisiveness into a force for good.

## Relevance

A philosophical treatment of the [false dichotomy](/concepts/false-dichotomy.md) and of how a contested claim can be framed to build cooperation rather than conflict.
