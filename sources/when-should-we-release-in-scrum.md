# When Should We Release In Scrum? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/when-should-we-release-in-scrum/when-should-we-release-in-scrum.md`

## Summary

Blake McMillan argues the default answer to "when should we release?" is as often as possible, because a product backlog is a set of hypotheses and delay postpones both learning and value. He acknowledges constraints that push releases later — complexity, dependencies, big ideas, and transaction costs — and lays out "it depends" factors: automation of deployment, validation, and back-out; ease of implementation approval; and customer adoption rate.

## Key Claims

- Release as often as possible to shorten the feedback loop on backlog hypotheses and stop investing in unvalidated ideas.
- Common reasons to delay: product complexity, dependencies with other groups, big ideas, and expensive transaction costs.
- One client moved from annual to quarterly releases, showing progress without full continuous delivery.
- "It depends" factors: how automated deployment, validation, and rollback are; whether policies and processes are conducive to change; and how fast customers adopt changes.
- If adoption lags capability, focus on making the change easy and intuitive for customers.
- Three recurring questions: how long do you want to delay learning, delay value, and invest in unvalidated ideas?
