# What Gap? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/what-gap/what-gap.md`

## Summary

O'Hara picks up Martin Varsavsky's hypothetical: three single mothers raising ten-year-old boys on $50,000, $200,000, and venture-fund incomes. Despite different zip codes and schools, the kids watch the same shows and face the same anxieties — the gap between the kids is smaller than the gap between what they have and what they think they should have. That last gap, he argues, is managed by expectations, and most of us quietly raise it every year without deciding to.

## Key Claims

- The distance between what you have and what you want is adjustable, not fixed.
- Most people adjust it upward each year without any deliberate decision.
- Children provided a house, car, and lifestyle can come to feel they deserve it, because they never earned it themselves.
- What matters is what you model — your posture toward what you have — not what you say.
- If children see restlessness with what is, they learn restlessness; gratitude is caught, not taught.
