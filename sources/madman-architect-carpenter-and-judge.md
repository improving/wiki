# 4 Roles: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, and Judge — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/madman-architect-carpenter-and-judge/madman-architect-carpenter-and-judge.md`

## Summary

The author revisits Betty Flowers' 1981 model of the writing process, which splits writing into four distinct roles performed in sequence: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, and Judge. Doing them intentionally—rather than all at once—lets each stage get the attention it needs, since generation and evaluation pull in opposite directions. He maps his own workflow onto the four stages and recommends taking a break between them.

## Key Claims

- Separating generation from evaluation into discrete stages improves writing.
- Madman: capture raw ideas freely—scribbles, mind maps, scraps—and produce a shitty first draft (Brené Brown's SFD) while silencing the inner critic.
- Architect: after a break (the author waits about a week), group related ideas and surface the underlying argument.
- Carpenter: refine structure, logical flow, and sentence clarity; tools like Grammarly help maintain unity.
- Judge: critically review for audience, clarity, and comprehension, and seek honest feedback from a trusted person.
