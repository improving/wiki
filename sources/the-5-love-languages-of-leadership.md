# The 5 Love Languages of Leadership — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-5-love-languages-of-leadership/the-5-love-languages-of-leadership.md`

## Summary

Mark Runyon adapts Gary Chapman's *The 5 Love Languages* to the workplace, arguing that employees, like partners, need appreciation expressed in ways specific to them. He proposes five "love languages of leadership" — recognition, professional growth, autonomy, financial fairness, and feedback — and urges leaders to discover which each person values. The unifying point is curiosity and honest communication about what motivates people.

## Key Claims

- A Great Place to Work survey found recognition is the most important driver of great work.
- Recognition is not one-size-fits-all: some want private praise, others public acknowledgment, so leaders must ask how each person prefers it.
- LinkedIn's Workforce Learning Report found 94% of employees would stay longer at a company that invests in their growth.
- Growth plans should be customized per person rather than cookie-cutter.
- Autonomy — over schedule, location, and decisions — makes people happier, more engaged, and accountable, though its degree varies by skill and experience.
- Pay must reflect worth; leaders should pursue pay equity and eliminate race- and gender-based discrimination.
- In a PwC survey, 60% of employees want feedback weekly or even daily, making annual reviews insufficient.
