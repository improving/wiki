# Surface Area of Your Life - it's why you're busy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/surface-area/surface-area.md`

## Summary

O'Hara borrows the geometric idea of surface area to explain why people feel perpetually busy. Each addition to life — another house, more friends, money, beliefs — enlarges the surface that must be maintained, and growth is slightly more than linear, not less. Delegating to assistants doesn't shrink it; it scales responsibility behind a layer of abstraction, and beliefs add a mental landscape to defend. His conclusion is that busyness, stress, and the sense of being behind come from excess surface area rather than scarce time, so the need is more focus, not more time.

## Key Claims

- Every element of life (house, friends, money, beliefs) adds to your surface area.
- Expansion is slightly more than linear — more surface means disproportionately more work, mental and physical.
- Delegating and hiring assistants scale the surface area of responsibility rather than reducing it.
- Held beliefs require defending and reinforcing a broader mental landscape.
- The happiest people keep their surface area small, focusing on a few things; you need more focus, not more time.

## Relevance

Treats [delegation](/concepts/delegation.md) as an illusion of relief and [intentional focus](/concepts/intentional-focus.md) as the actual remedy for busyness.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Conceptual analogy rather than measured argument; the "slightly above linear" claim is asserted, not demonstrated.
