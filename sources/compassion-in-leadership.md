# Compassion In Leadership — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/compassion-in-leadership/compassion-in-leadership.md`

## Summary

Opening from a Henri Nouwen quotation, O'Hara argues leadership is hard not because of the decisions, financials, or headcount, but because it demands balancing compassion with competence. Leaders tend to lean into competence — an "I got here because I'm good at what I do" attitude — and lean out from the caring that signals people are seen. Tough calls are unavoidable; living permanently in that toughness is not. He closes by challenging the reader to make compassion their primary mode today.

## Key Claims

- Leadership's real difficulty is the balance of compassion and competence, not the decisions themselves.
- Leaders use assumed competence and specialism as an excuse to avoid the harder work of compassion (Nouwen).
- Necessary tough decisions don't justify a standing posture of toughness.
- Compassion is a daily choice, framed as adopting it as your "primary mode".
