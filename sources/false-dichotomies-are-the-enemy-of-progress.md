# False Dichotomies Are the Enemy of Progress — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/false-dichotomies-are-the-enemy-of-progress/false-dichotomies-are-the-enemy-of-progress.md`

## Summary

Liles argues that conference-talk either/or framings — waterfall vs. agile, testing vs. coding, operations vs. development, documents vs. working software — are false dichotomies that produce tribal camps rather than good software. A dichotomy is false when the correct answer depends on context, which it almost always does. He extends the argument to AI-era pairs (human vs. AI, assistance vs. autonomy, speed vs. safety), noting that governance enables speed at scale rather than opposing it.

## Key Claims

- The diagnostic question: does the right answer depend on context? If so, the presented choice is probably false.
- "Testing vs. coding" is a false opposition — skipping tests means shipping code whose correctness you cannot verify.
- DevOps was the synthesis that dissolved the operations/development split.
- The Agile Manifesto's "working software over documentation" was a capacity constraint, not a claim that documentation is bad; in the Intent Era AI enables both.
- Assistance vs. autonomy is a continuum: the 8-stage trust evolution treats autonomy as a function of infrastructure, governance, and task, not a binary.
