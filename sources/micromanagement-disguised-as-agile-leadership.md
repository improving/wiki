# The Quiet Trap: How Micromanagement Disguises Itself as Agile Leadership — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/micromanagement-disguised-as-agile-leadership/micromanagement-disguised-as-agile-leadership.md`

## Summary

Agostina Formia argues that micromanagement hides behind Agile dashboards — tracking individual velocity, demanding task-level check-ins, and treating every sprint as a performance review — which contradicts the self-managing teams Scrum requires. She contrasts it with servant leadership: measure the team, remove impediments, ask questions, and trust by default.

## Key Claims

- Agile micromanagement focuses on the individual rather than the team, contradicting the Scrum Guide's self-managing teams who decide who does what, when, and how.
- Individual metrics incentivize ticket hoarding, silo behavior, inflated estimates, and loss of psychological safety.
- Scrum.org's PAL-E framework says leaders should focus on outcomes, not output.
- Servant leadership (coined by Robert K. Greenleaf in 1970) makes serving the team, removing obstacles, and growing people the leader's primary responsibility.
- Track team velocity, cycle time, deployment frequency, and team satisfaction rather than individual story points.
- Trust by default: leaders' involvement should be invited, not imposed, and the best leaders create conditions where the team wants to do great work.

## Relevance

An applied account of [agile leadership](/concepts/agile-leadership.md) and [servant leadership](/concepts/servant-leadership.md), arguing that individual metrics undermine [psychological safety](/concepts/psychological-safety.md). It reframes progress as [outcomes over outputs](/concepts/outcomes-over-outputs.md).
