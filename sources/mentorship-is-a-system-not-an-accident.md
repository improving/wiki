# Mentorship Is a System, Not an Accident — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/mentorship-is-a-system-not-an-accident/mentorship-is-a-system-not-an-accident.md`

## Summary

The author argues that the common pattern — a struggling person gets a few helpful conversations that fade as both parties get busy — is crisis support, not mentorship. Mentorship that produces career advancement and better judgment is designed, not discovered. He names three design decisions: a specific ask (thirty minutes on a concrete challenge, not "be my mentor"), a recurring cadence (monthly minimum), and mentee-owned agendas. From the mentor's side the primary obligation is honest, specific, sometimes uncomfortable feedback rather than encouragement. AI can supply knowledge and frameworks, but not the specific context or social navigation a mentor who knows you provides.

## Key Claims

- Accidental mentorship fails three ways: reactive engagement, vague asks, and undefined cadence.
- "Can you mentor me?" is unactionable; a specific ask makes the request answerable and forces the mentee to do the thinking.
- Monthly is the minimum effective cadence; quarterly is better than nothing.
- The mentee owns the agenda, and the mentor's primary obligation is honest feedback, not validation.
- AI cannot provide mentor-specific context or social navigation, so deliberate design still matters.

## Relevance

Informs career and organizational-systems thinking, complementing peer-based accountability practices in technology organizations.
