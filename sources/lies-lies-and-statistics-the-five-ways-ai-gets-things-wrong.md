# Lies, Lies, and Statistics: The Five Ways AI Gets Things Wrong — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/lies-lies-and-statistics-the-five-ways-ai-gets-things-wrong/lies-lies-and-statistics-the-five-ways-ai-gets-things-wrong.md`

## Summary

Most advice about AI reliability focuses on hallucination, but fabrication is only one of five categories of AI mistruth, and a defense built only against it leaves the other four open. Liles catalogues commission (fabrication), omission (incomplete picture), perspective (true data, misleading synthesis), bias (training-data limits), and frame of reference (misapplied context), each illustrated with a concrete failure. The value of the taxonomy is that each category requires a different defense, and four of the five survive plain fact-checking. He closes by answering two objections and recommending an auditor's posture toward AI output.

## Key Claims

- Commission failures are the easiest to detect; the defense is verification of specific claims, especially citations and statistics.
- Omission failures are invisible — every stated fact is true — so the defense is asking for the strongest counterarguments and missing context.
- Perspective failures, like a $180,000 average salary skewed by executives, are caught by asking for the distribution behind any average.
- Bias failures look like confident, miscalibrated advice; the defense is supplying explicit context about your situation.
- Frame-of-reference failures come from comparative data whose context does not transfer; ask where the benchmark came from.
