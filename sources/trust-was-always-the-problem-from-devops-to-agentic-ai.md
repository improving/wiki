# Trust Was Always the Problem: From DevOps to Agentic AI — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/trust-was-always-the-problem-from-devops-to-agentic-ai/trust-was-always-the-problem-from-devops-to-agentic-ai.md`

## Summary

Liles argues that DevOps adoption stalled for a decade not because of tooling — Jenkins, Git, and Docker all worked — but because of trust: developers, operations, and leadership did not trust automated pipelines or each other. The pre-DevOps manual handoff culture was itself a trust management system that produced legible accountability. Agentic AI is now running the identical pattern, stalling on governance questions about scope, reversibility, auditability, and responsibility rather than capability.

## Key Claims

- The limiting factor in DevOps was trust, not tooling; automation needed governance infrastructure to become safe to rely on.
- Agentic AI adoption conversations answer the capability question quickly, then stall on trust.
- The 8-Stage AI Trust Evolution runs from "AI as reference" through "AI as organizational nervous system," each stage demanding trust at a new level (output, process, integration, governance, architecture, institutional, existential).
- Most organizations sit at Stage 2 or 3 and stall because they have not built the trust infrastructure to operate Stage 4 or 5 safely.
- The DevOps template for building it: observability first, reversibility as a design constraint, boundary enforcement before capability extension, and governance treated as a feature rather than overhead.
