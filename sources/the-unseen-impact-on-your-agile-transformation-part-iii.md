# The Unseen Impact on Your Agile Transformation, Part III — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-unseen-impact-on-your-agile-transformation-part-iii/the-unseen-impact-on-your-agile-transformation-part-iii.md`

## Summary

When a transformation underdelivers, teams tend to give up, switch process, or carry on hoping things improve. The recovery path, the author argues, is to stop and revisit the goals, then re-anchor to the Agile Manifesto's values and the five Scrum values as the basics that refocus agile adoption.

## Key Claims

- The natural reactions to a stalled transformation — quit, switch process, or persist — should give way to revisiting goals and desired outcomes.
- The Agile Manifesto's four values are the place to start: individuals and interactions over processes and tools; working software over comprehensive documentation; customer collaboration over contract negotiation; responding to change over following a plan.
- The five Scrum values — commitment, courage, focus, openness, respect — explain why agile teams work and can reframe a transformation.
- Respect builds the trust that agility requires; focus on craftsmanship and Sprint Goals aligned to the Product Goal delivers valuable Increments.
- Teams should re-read the Scrum Guide, updated as recently as 2020.
- Working agreements that articulate how a team will live the five values help it move through challenges together.
