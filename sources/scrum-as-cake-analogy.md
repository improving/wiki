# Scrum as Cake Analogy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/scrum-as-cake-analogy/scrum-as-cake-analogy.md`

## Summary

O'Hara offers a cake-baking analogy for agile adoption. Given a proven recipe, a novice who skips the flour and swaps oil for cooking spray and then blames the recipe for a ruined cake is like a team that strips pieces out of Scrum and declares the process broken. His point is that following the process as written first teaches you what each piece does and why it matters, so you earn the understanding needed before adapting it.

## Key Claims

- Going by the book first reveals how each practice operates, its intent, and why it matters.
- Replacing parts without that understanding is likely to spoil the outcome.
- Scrum works; Scrummerfall, Waterscrum, and duct-tape hybrids may or may not.
- Blame for failed adoption belongs to the team, not the process.
- Scrum's purpose is to make problems visible so they can be addressed and value delivered.

## Relevance

Informs agile adoption and organizational change themes; argues for fidelity to a practice before tailoring it.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. A short, purely analogical argument with no data; persuasive opinion drawn from consulting experience.
