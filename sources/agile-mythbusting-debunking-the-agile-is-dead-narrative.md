# Agile Mythbusting: Debunking the 'Agile is Dead' Narrative — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/agile-mythbusting-debunking-the-agile-is-dead-narrative/agile-mythbusting-debunking-the-agile-is-dead-narrative.md`

## Summary

Shervin Joseph, an agile coach with 22 years in technology, argues the "agile is dead" narrative mistakes ubiquity for decline: agile has become a standard, like power steering or denim, so it is discussed less because it is assumed. He contends agile's core — continuous value delivery, feedback, and the ability to pivot — is here to stay and has spread beyond software into hiring, supply chains, and other domains. He concedes real challenges in scaling agile and changing culture, but calls them surmountable.

## Key Claims

- The author has 22 years in technology and transitioned from Waterfall to agile.
- Agile is a mindset, culture, and philosophy, not just practices or ceremonies.
- Its benefits — continuous value, feedback, and pivoting — are here to stay.
- Agile principles have spread beyond software to hiring (Kanban), supply chains, and even oil-well drilling (Scrum).
- Scaling agile, maintaining momentum, and change-resistant cultures are real but surmountable challenges.
- What is "dead" is agility being framed as a new offering.
