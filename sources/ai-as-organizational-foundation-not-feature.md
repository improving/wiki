# AI as Organizational Foundation, Not Feature — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/ai-as-organizational-foundation-not-feature/ai-as-organizational-foundation-not-feature.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles frames the diagnostic question of AI strategy: is AI a feature added to the organization, or the foundation it is built on? The feature model yields bounded 10-30% improvements on existing processes. The foundation model redesigns processes around the constraint AI dissolves — that knowledge work requires human execution — producing an AI-native organization. He then steelmans three objections and argues the foundation layers are model-agnostic.

## Key Claims

- The feature model produces 10-20% improvement on existing processes, maybe 30% with good integration, because the ceiling is the underlying process efficiency.
- The foundation model asks what processes you would design if starting today, echoing Michael Hammer's "Don't Automate, Obliterate" and Paul David's electrification record.
- An AI-native organization shows four marks: roles designed around judgment, the Intelligence Operating System built as infrastructure, different measurement, and competing on the operating model.
- Rebuilt organizations run projects that would need teams of 10 with teams of 3, because the AI execution layer absorbs the extra work.
- Objections: reengineering's failure record, kaizen's compounding alternative, and technology moving too fast — the last has least force because foundation layers survive model churn.
- The mistake is doing renovation while calling it transformation.
