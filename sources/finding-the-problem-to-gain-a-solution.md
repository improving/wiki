# Finding the Problem to Gain a Solution — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/finding-the-problem-to-gain-a-solution/finding-the-problem-to-gain-a-solution.md`

## Summary

Drawing on 30+ years in the corporate world, Barbara Schultz observes that problem-solving meetings almost never actually define the problem — they jump to solutions and devolve into "you don't understand." Without a shared, nonjudgmental problem statement, teams cannot agree on a solution. She offers a backward technique: have participants describe the world without the problem, then translate that desired state back into a problem statement.

## Key Claims

- Meetings assembled to solve problems usually spend an hour or two debating solutions without defining the problem.
- Very smart people struggle to state the problem nonjudgmentally in one or two sentences; answers split between blaming another department and listing pains.
- Asking "what would your world look like without the problem?" breaks the deadlock, even if it requires a "magic wand" exercise.
- A desired state such as "when the order arrives it has X, Y, and Z" translates cleanly into the problem "when the order reaches me it is missing X, Y, and Z."
- Document the pain from all angles — the people on the call, anyone impacted, and the effect on customers, the bottom line, and brand image.
- Reaching a shared problem definition is "the starting line," after which solutions come more easily.
