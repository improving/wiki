# Opinion & Discourse — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/thats-your-opinion/thats-your-opinion.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that people's right to hold and express opinions is being eroded by a habit of labeling dissent as "racist," "narrow-minded," or otherwise illegitimate. He contends that this labeling is itself a form of bias, not proof of correctness, and that suppressing disfavored views in the name of harmony breeds discord instead. The remedy he proposes is inward: use debate to clarify and inform your own thinking rather than to change the other person's mind.

## Key Claims

- Between 2014 and 2022 the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression counted 877 attempts to punish scholars for protected expression; 60% resulted in sanctions, including 114 censorship incidents and 156 firings (44 of them tenured professors) — more than during the McCarthy era.
- Labeling an opposing opinion does not make the labeler right; it signals bias.
- Repression of academic freedom appears systemic and must be actively resisted.
- Opinions are not fixed and can evolve, but changing others' views should not be the goal of discourse.
- Civil discourse is not zero-sum: it aims at deeper understanding, empathy, and a more inclusive society.
