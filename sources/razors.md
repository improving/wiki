# Philosophical Razors Unpacked — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/razors/razors.md`

## Summary

O'Hara defines a philosophical razor as a rule of thumb that shaves off unlikely explanations or unnecessary actions, then unpacks five of them as practical shortcuts for clearer thinking and better dealings with people. Each razor is given its statement, a plain-language gloss, and an example or caveat. The post argues that applying these heuristics improves how we interpret others' behavior and evaluate claims.

## Key Claims

- **Alder's razor** (Newton's flaming laser sword): "If something cannot be settled by experiment or observation, then it is not worthy of debate" — but use cautiously, since history, politics, and ethics can't be experimented on.
- **Grice's razor**: address what a speaker meant, not their literal words; people communicate poorly, so give the benefit of the doubt and ask clarifying questions.
- **Hanlon's razor**: "Never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by neglect" (also stated with stupidity or laziness).
- **Hume's guillotine**: "What ought to be cannot be deduced from what is" — causes must be sufficient to produce the observed outcomes.
- **Occam's razor**: among competing hypotheses, select the one with the fewest assumptions, since each added assumption multiplies the chance of error (the raccoon-versus-intruder trash example).
