# Accessibility is a Winning Strategy for Inclusive UX Design — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/accessibility-is-a-winning-strategy-for-inclusive-ux-design/accessibility-is-a-winning-strategy-for-inclusive-ux-design.md`

## Summary

A UX designer argues that accessibility deserves to be designed in from the start rather than tacked on at the end, because disabled users are a large audience and accessible design improves the experience for everyone. He frames the work through the Paralympics and guide runners as an analogy for running alongside users.

## Key Claims

- Nearly 5% of the US population has some form of color blindness, and 3.6% has a visual impairment, including over a million blind people.
- About one million people have Parkinson's, alongside others with tremors or muscular dystrophy, all facing difficulty with poor navigation.
- Assistive technologies — magnification, screen readers, keyboard-only or voice navigation — only work if sites are built to cooperate with them.
- Accessibility improves SEO through labeled images, clear headings, and descriptive link text, and prepares sites for future regulation.
- The author's earlier career treated accessibility as a budget-expired afterthought; Improving's government and education sites now meet the requirements.
