# Where Is My Mind? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/where-is-my-mind/where-is-my-mind.md`

## Summary

The author describes getting stuck in mental loops that replay conversations and situations, driven partly by wanting to get things right and partly by insecurity. Those reruns rarely help, he concludes, because the past is fixed — citing the idea that forgiveness means giving up the right to a better past. The shift came from changing the questions he asks: instead of fixating on what went wrong, he focuses on what he missed, how to prepare better, and how to respond differently next time. That small change in perspective converts rumination into genuine learning and moves him forward.

## Key Claims

- Replaying past conversations is common and rooted as much in insecurity as in wanting to get things right.
- Mental reruns rarely help, since the past cannot be edited.
- Forgiveness can be framed as giving up the right to a better past.
- The same replays become valuable when approached with different questions.
- Asking "what did I miss / how do I prepare better" turns rumination into forward-looking learning.
- The value of reflection lies not in what we remember but in how we choose to use it.

## Relevance

An applied example of [cognitive reframing](/concepts/cognitive-reframing.md), specifically rewriting the [internal narrative](/concepts/internal-narrative.md) attached to past events.
