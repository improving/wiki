# How Conscious Capitalism Can Help You And Your Business Grow — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/a-case-for-conscious-capitalism/a-case-for-conscious-capitalism.md`

## Summary

Republishing a Forbes Dallas Council piece, David O'Hara argues that conscious capitalism—running a business for a purpose greater than profit—has been genuinely impactful for his firm, so much of what the company does is rooted in its philosophy. He stresses that this is not nonprofit thinking: profit is assumed, and the real question is how profit is spent and where value is delivered. The essay links that stance to the stakeholder model, to deliberate culture-building, and to leaders growing themselves.

## Key Claims

- Conscious capitalism holds that business exists for a higher purpose while still assuming profit is required to survive.
- The stakeholder model widens focus beyond shareholders to partners, the community, and the industry; O'Hara rejects the "zero-sum" view that helping competitors hurts you.
- Value can be measured externally, e.g. via the Inc. 5000 and Best Places to Work lists, across financial and cultural dimensions.
- Culture "happens by default or decision"; aspirational values on a wall are worthless next to values leaders live daily.
- "Do for one what you wish you could do for all"—small investments (free meeting space, pizza) build community goodwill.
- The best way to grow a business is to grow yourself.
