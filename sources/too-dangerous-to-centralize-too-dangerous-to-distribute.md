# Too Dangerous to Centralize, Too Dangerous to Distribute — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/too-dangerous-to-centralize-too-dangerous-to-distribute/too-dangerous-to-centralize-too-dangerous-to-distribute.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles notes that in the same month Mark Zuckerberg argued advanced AI is too dangerous to centralize (pledging open releases) while Meta open-sourced the 30-billion-parameter Muse Glimmer, and Anthropic moved toward an IPO built on the argument that frontier AI is too dangerous to distribute without careful access control. Both positions, he argues, arrived exactly on schedule with the business case each company needed them to be true. He concludes that enterprise platform decisions should rest on an evaluation baseline and governance fit, not either company's stated mission.

## Key Claims

- Meta's openness argument lands after a year of trailing OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google on frontier benchmarks, when openness becomes the differentiator raw capability isn't.
- Meta open-sourced Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter model small enough to run on a laptop GPU, four days before Zuckerberg's 6,500-word essay.
- Anthropic's safety-first posture best supports a valuation resting on being the frontier lab that took risk seriously; Gavin Baker's bull case cites ~$100B annualized revenue and a $2T floor.
- Baker runs a hedge fund with plausible exposure to the trade, making his valuation call a position dressed as analysis.
- The pattern holds from earlier dueling letters: a lab's philosophical position tends to arrive when its business needs it to be true.
- Enterprise selection should build an evaluation baseline against the actual workload, then test what each option's governance architecture can enforce at the boundary that matters.
