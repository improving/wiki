# The ADA Compliance Deadline You’re Ignoring Was Last Week — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-ada-compliance-deadline-youre-ignoring-was-last-week/the-ada-compliance-deadline-youre-ignoring-was-last-week.md`

## Summary

Under Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, public entities serving at least 50,000 people must make their websites comply with WCAG 2.1 Level AA, originally by April 24, 2026 — a deadline the DOJ extended to April 26, 2027 just four days before it hit. The post warns against waiting, noting rising digital accessibility lawsuits that use WCAG 2.1 as a benchmark, and argues the real motivation is human: equal access. It describes a real legacy project's scope and recommends adding accessibility to the Definition of Done.

## Key Claims

- The DOJ extended the deadline to April 26, 2027 after recognizing many entities were struggling with the volume of work.
- Penalties can include daily fines per infraction, and private lawsuits over digital accessibility are increasing.
- WCAG 2.1 aims for a perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust experience.
- According to the CDC, more than 1 in 4 US adults live with a disability; globally the figure exceeds one billion people, and Vispero estimates 73% of the world is affected when family and caregivers are counted.
- Accessibility is not only about extreme cases — most people lose some vision as they age.
- The author's legacy project spans well over 150 pages plus hundreds of RDL reports, and accessible PDFs need meaningful alt text, logically tagged headers and tables, and proper rendering.
- Tools used: Accessibility Insights for Web, ARC Toolkit, Adobe Acrobat Pro, Excel's built-in checker, and AI tools such as Windsurf.
- Suggested actions: a "No Mouse Monday" keyboard test, a UI scan, a screen-reader walkthrough, a vision-impairment simulation, and raising it at standup.
