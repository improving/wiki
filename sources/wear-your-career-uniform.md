# Wear Your Career Uniform — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/wear-your-career-uniform/wear-your-career-uniform.md`

## Summary

After several weeks of dressing more formally than usual for client and business needs, O'Hara notices a change in how people behave around him. He rejects the maxim "the clothes make the man" as nonsense — he is not his designer label — but argues that clothing is an outward manifestation of something internal, and that people inevitably read it. Since we are prejudicial and make snap judgments, anyone whose job involves appearances is at a disadvantage by ignoring this.

## Key Claims

- "The clothes make the man" is false; you are not what you wear.
- Clothes don't make you work harder or more productively.
- They are an outward manifestation of something internal, and people see you through them.
- Everyone makes snap judgments; fighting that reality is futile.
- A practical experiment: shift how you dress for three weeks and watch what changes in your own attitude and others'.
