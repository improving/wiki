# Building Trust at the Intersection of Science, AI, and Food Innovation — Summary

## Summary

The Institute of Food Technologists (IFT) approached AI as a startup-within-a-legacy-organization project, treating its 85-plus years of journals and expertise as an asset to deliver differently. Because many food formulations are trade secrets, data privacy and system security were paramount, and trust had to be foundational from day one. IFT partnered with Improving as an embedded co-developer to build Sous, a Microsoft Azure software-as-a-service platform, deliberately designed not to train on customer-submitted data and to augment rather than replace scientists.

## Key Claims

- IFT had more than 85 years of proprietary journals, insights, and expertise, and saw an opportunity and responsibility to deliver that value differently.
- Many formulations rely on trade secrets rather than patents, making data privacy and system security paramount.
- Improving joined as a co-developer embedded with IFT's team every step of the way rather than as a distant vendor.
- Sous was designed with responsible-AI principles, including a decision not to train on customer-submitted data.
- Trust was woven into design and delivery rather than treated as a feature to add later.
- Building on Microsoft Azure brought enterprise credibility with customers who view IFT primarily as a science and standards organization.
- The platform was designed to augment human expertise — automating administrative work so scientists spend more time on creativity, formulation, and consumer connection.
