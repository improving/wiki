# UX by Everyone Pt 1: The UX Mindset — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ux-by-everyone-pt-1-the-ux-mindset/ux-by-everyone-pt-1-the-ux-mindset.md`

## Summary

Martino Liu opens the UX Mindset series by arguing that if products and services fail human expectations, the companies behind them struggle — so UX cannot be confined to designers. The post traces design's history from the Bauhaus and industrial design through Silicon Valley and Don Norman's coining of "user experience," and calls for a mindset that makes UX part of human-centered digital transformation.

## Key Claims

- UX is essential to any team or organization delivering products in a tech-driven landscape, not just to designers.
- Common misconceptions reduce UX to aesthetics or interfaces, ignoring its impact on functionality, user satisfaction, and business success.
- The Bauhaus (Germany, 1919) fused art, craft, and technology, framing design as a method of problem-solving.
- Carnegie Mellon established the first industrial design program in 1934, professionalizing the field.
- Don Norman coined "user experience" in the early 1990s at Apple.
- The internet and mobile revolutions shifted design from static graphics to dynamic, interactive experiences.
- UX is now treated as a component of business strategy alongside service design and customer experience, and is everyone's responsibility.
