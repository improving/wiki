# Create Transparency — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/create-transparency/create-transparency.md`

## Summary

Mark Runyon, part of a series on Stephen M.R. Covey's *The Speed of Trust*, explores the third trust behavior: Create Transparency. He argues that hiding information erodes integrity, and that openness, timely disclosure, owning failures, and concise communication build a healthy, innovative culture — while acknowledging that some information is legitimately confidential.

## Key Claims

- Covey's guidance: "tell the truth in ways people can verify for themselves. Err on the side of telling too much rather than too little."
- Openness should extend to employees, customers, and vendors, echoing the Conscious Capitalism model; free information flow breeds innovation.
- Timing matters: raising blockers on Tuesday when a Friday deadline is at risk is transparent; waiting until Thursday or Friday is not.
- Failing to own your failures is the quickest way to lose trust — outline what went wrong, how you'll heal it, and how you'll prevent recurrence.
- Answer status questions transparently and concisely so people can act rather than doubt.
- Boundaries exist: trade secrets and confidential information shouldn't be shared, and messages should be tailored to the audience.
- Cited evidence: a Tiny Pulse survey found transparency was the top factor in employee happiness, and an HBR study found 70 percent of employees felt most engaged when leaders communicated goals and strategy.
