# Value Prop is Not Just for Marketing — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/value-prop-is-not-just-for-marketing/value-prop-is-not-just-for-marketing.md`

## Summary

The post borrows a bakery metaphor — loyal "Cookie Monsters," obvious "Hard-No's," and on-the-fence "Cookie Curious" customers — and maps it to talent acquisition. Its claim is that a well-articulated Employee Value Proposition (EVP) lets candidates self-identify their fit, saving recruiters time and improving retention, while positioning recruiting as relationship-building rather than sales messaging.

## Key Claims

- A clear EVP screens out candidates who won't work and is organically attractive to those who share your language.
- EVPs mix tangible and intangible elements: growth, total compensation, technology, culture, and more.
- A spelled-out EVP reduces candidate effort to evaluate fit and lets them self-identify, saving recruiter time and energy.
- Employees who "get" the EVP make similar referrals, pose less retention risk, and onboard more smoothly into culture, values, and systems.
- The author frames the "Cookie Curious" (roughly 80%+ likely to convert) as the group worth investing effort in.
- Building an EVP draws on engagement/candidate/new-hire surveys, People & Culture data (demographics, promotions, benefits, retention, compensation), a TA marketing asset inventory, and direct conversations with employees.
