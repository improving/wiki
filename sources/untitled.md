# Pointing Fingers and Slow Death — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/untitled/untitled.md`

## Summary

Reading chapter 2 of *Deep Change*, O'Hara is struck by two claims: that people "practice denial," and that organizations are rarely free of some form of slow death. Denial usually takes the shape of rationalization — reasons why we didn't, couldn't, or why someone else should act. He argues the deeper failure is refusing to admit that *you* are part of the problem, and that pointing at "they" ignores your own participation in decline.

## Key Claims

- Organizations commonly suffer a "slow death" that members deny or rationalize away.
- People readily deny what they see, but are skilled at denying what they can do about it.
- "They don't get it" is an indictment that ignores your own inclusion in the finger-pointing.
- If you see something that can be corrected, doing it is the only non-contribution to decline.
- "Excellence is a form of deviance"; you become excellent by doing what normal people avoid (p. 176).
