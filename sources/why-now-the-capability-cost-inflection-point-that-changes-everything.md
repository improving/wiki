# Why Now: The Capability-Cost Inflection Point That Changes Everything — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/why-now-the-capability-cost-inflection-point-that-changes-everything/why-now-the-capability-cost-inflection-point-that-changes-everything.md`

## Summary

Liles argues AI is a genuine general-purpose-technology inflection rather than hype, resting the case on capability and cost data rather than enthusiasm. Benchmark performance has crossed human thresholds across math, science, and software tasks, while the per-token price decline of 2023–2025 has reversed: frontier flagships now cost several multiples of the GPT-5 floor as a compute crunch forces providers to ration scarce inference. Because reasoning and agentic workflows burn far more tokens per task, effective costs rise faster than sticker prices, so the strategic move is to build token economics and operating models now rather than wait for cheaper inference.

## Key Claims

- FrontierMath: leading models reach ~27% on problems most professional mathematicians cannot solve quickly, from near-zero in 2021.
- GPQA Diamond clears the high 80s in 2026, up from ~36% at GPT-4's 2023 launch, past human expert range.
- A frontier model at ~$30 per million input tokens in early 2023 fell to ~$1.25 by the GPT-5 launch in August 2025, then reversed to $5–$10 input / $25–$50 output by mid-2026.
- Claude Fable 5 (June 9) ships at $10 input / $50 output, exactly double the Opus 4.8 flagship.
- Reasoning models burn 50–500x more tokens per task; agentic workflows fire 10–20 calls per request; enterprise AI bills roughly tripled even as unit prices fell.
- Frontier training may hit the ceiling of available human text in 2027–2028, shifting gains to synthetic data, tool use, and modality expansion.
