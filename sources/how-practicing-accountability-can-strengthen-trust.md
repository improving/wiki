# How Practicing Accountability Can Strengthen Trust for Business Success — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-practicing-accountability-can-strengthen-trust/how-practicing-accountability-can-strengthen-trust.md`

## Summary

Josh Barbieri argues that consultants take on their clients' risks, so accountability — owning what you say and deliver throughout an engagement — is what builds trust. He breaks accountability into ownership, communication, and equity, and describes the "trust tax" that appears when people do not hold themselves accountable.

## Key Claims

- By supporting a client's business, a consultant takes on their risks and is accountable for what they say, telling them when they cannot deliver, and offering alternatives.
- When people do not hold themselves accountable, velocity drops and a "trust tax" is added whenever micro-managing becomes necessary.
- The three ingredients of accountability are ownership, communication, and equity.
- People often decline uncomfortable work from a lack of support; peer review, time to learn, and a supportive environment help them say yes.
- Stand-ups succeed because they reduce the enormity of accountability, giving people a low-cost place to ask for help.
- Equity means everyone gets the same level of inquiry, support, and camaraderie, sharing both success and failure.
