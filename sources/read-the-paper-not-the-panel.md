# Read the Paper, Not the Panel — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/read-the-paper-not-the-panel/read-the-paper-not-the-panel.md`

## Summary

Triggered by a client asking whether Claude is conscious after hearing a podcast clip, the post shows how coverage drifts from primary research. Two July results illustrate the gap: a benchmark score read as approaching AGI, and an interpretability finding read as evidence of consciousness. The author offers a four-question checklist — the number/model/condition, the researchers' own disclaimers, whether the finding generalizes, and who benefits from the dramatic reading — to separate a finding from the framing built on top of it.

## Key Claims

- GPT-5.6 Sol became the first model to beat a full ARC-AGI-3 game, scoring 7.8% (up from a 0.37% baseline) — still failing more than 92% of tasks, a milestone rather than AGI.
- Anthropic's July interpretability paper used a "Jacobian lens" to identify "J-space," a small set of middle-layer representations usable for multi-step reasoning.
- The paper explicitly states its experiments do not show Claude can have experiences; coverage still asked whether Claude is conscious and said the finding "mirrors a theory of consciousness."
- The checklist: name the number, model, and one condition; treat the researchers' disclaimer as the ceiling; ask whether the finding generalizes past one case; ask who benefits from the more dramatic reading.
- The pattern catches drift, not deceit — findings usually lose their edges on the way from preprint to panel.
