# Ego — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/ego/ego.md`

## Summary

O'Hara opens with the line "I have a really big ego; it's just not attached to what you think it's attached to," then lists what he does not derive identity from — position, name, authority, bank account, car — and what he does: being loving, values-driven, flawed, blessed, a goofball. He acknowledges that truly seeing ourselves is hard and that honesty about it is painful, but argues that doing the work brings satisfaction and peace, and that anchoring identity in status or possessions is not wrong, merely not him. He ends by asking readers what their ego is attached to.

## Key Claims

- Many people claim identity in position, name, authority, wealth, or possessions, but that claim is often untrue.
- It is hard to be objective about oneself and painful to be honest.
- Doing the work to understand, acknowledge, and live out of your real identity yields satisfaction and peace.
- Finding identity in status or possessions is not bad — "it's just not me."
- You should be the best possible version of yourself, not someone else.

## Relevance

A direct exercise in [self-awareness](/concepts/self-awareness.md) and [internal narrative](/concepts/internal-narrative.md); it is the author's most explicit piece on identity and values.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Reflective and personal rather than evidential; its strength is a concrete self-inventory prompt, with the caveat that all claims are the author's own introspection.
