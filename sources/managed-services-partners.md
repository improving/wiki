# Managed Services Partners: A Buyer's Guide to Choosing the Right Fit — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/managed-services-partners/managed-services-partners.md`

## Summary

Choosing a managed services partner means handing over responsibility for the systems the business runs on, so the wrong fit shows up in downtime and security gaps. The guide profiles ten companies and lays out how to assess them on service-level commitments, security maturity, delivery model, and reporting transparency, while distinguishing managed services from staff augmentation and break-fix support.

## Key Claims

- The global managed services market is projected to grow from $437.3B in 2026 to $847.4B by 2033 (Grand View Research); managed security services are growing at a 16.4% CAGR (Research and Markets).
- Delivery models include fully managed IT, managed security (MSSP/MDR), CSP and licensing management, co-managed IT, and application/platform production support.
- Managed services differs from staff augmentation (no SLA guarantee) and break-fix support (reactive, unpredictable) by combining proactive operation with contractual SLAs.
- Criteria: defined SLAs and escalation paths, true 24/7 coverage, security and compliance alignment, rigorous onboarding, readable reporting, licensing cost optimization, and delivery-model fit.
- Improving case: Canada Post's Track and Trace platform — 24/7 monitoring and automated incident response produced a 95% reduction in unscheduled downtime and cut Mean Time to Acknowledgment from 10.3 to 5.11 minutes.
- PwC built an AI-driven detection and response service on a multi-year, $400M Google Cloud investment.
- The other profiles include Accenture, Wipro, HCLTech, Innowise, BairesDev, Sherweb, JourneyTEAM, and Classic Informatics.
