# Reading the Room: Where Your Organization Actually Is on the AI Curve — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/reading-the-room-where-your-organization-actually-is-on-the-ai-curve/reading-the-room-where-your-organization-actually-is-on-the-ai-curve.md`

## Summary

The post argues the most common strategic error in AI adoption is making Stage 5 or 6 investment decisions while operating at Stage 2 capability, caused not by ambition but by a measurement problem: organizations measure activity (tool usage, projects with "AI" in the name) instead of capability (what they can reliably do, at what autonomy, with what governance). It combines Everett Rogers' diffusion curve with an AI maturity ladder so that adoption position and capability level are assessed together.

## Key Claims

- Overestimation produces investment misalignment: buying Stage 5 solutions before Stage 3 governance, hiring for Stage 6 before Stage 4 workflows exist, announcing Stage 7 strategies from a Stage 2 baseline.
- Rogers' segments: innovators ~2.5%, early adopters ~13.5%, early majority ~34%, late majority ~34%, laggards ~16%.
- AI maturity is a distribution, not a single number — innovators may run at Stage 4–5 while the early majority sits at Stage 1.
- The diagnostic question is not "what are we doing with AI?" but "what can we reliably do, with what governance, at what scale?"
- Reliable is the key word: one successful agent run is not Stage 3; reliability across a defined task class with auditable outcomes is.
