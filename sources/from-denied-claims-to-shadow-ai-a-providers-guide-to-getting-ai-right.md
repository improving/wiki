# From Denied Claims to Shadow AI: A Provider's Guide to Getting AI Right — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/from-denied-claims-to-shadow-ai-a-providers-guide-to-getting-ai-right/from-denied-claims-to-shadow-ai-a-providers-guide-to-getting-ai-right.md`

## Summary

Health systems are under pressure from physician burnout, rising administrative burden, and thin margins, and AI is often pitched as relief. In a Q&A, Improving's Scott Poulin argues the gap between a real AI strategy and a pile of disconnected tools is where value is lost. He covers four areas — revenue cycle, clinical documentation, workforce integration, and shadow AI — and stresses that data foundations and governance come before any tool.

## Key Claims

- Initial claim denials hit 11.8% in 2024, with nearly half from missing or inaccurate data.
- Error rates can drop from 46% to 32%, 20%, then 10% as AI learns from an organization's own workflows.
- Expecting AI to be right 100% on day one is impossible; it improves over time.
- Clinical documentation AI works as a "second set of eyes" — a partnership with, not a replacement for, physician judgment.
- An emergency room that deployed 30 different AI systems saw clinician performance drop 30% because staff had to manage 30 interfaces for one decision; integration, not more tools, reduces burden.
- Shadow AI is flagged the #1 risk for healthcare providers in 2026; Improving addresses it with a four-pillar framework: policy, technology, training, and monitoring.
- About 96,000 physicians are projected to exit the healthcare workforce in 2026.
