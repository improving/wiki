# How to Write a Developer Resume, Part 1 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-to-write-a-developer-resume-part-1/how-to-write-a-developer-resume-part-1.md`

## Summary

Victoria Ochoa Landeros, a talent acquisition manager at Improving Nearshore with ten years of IT recruiting experience, lays out how developers should structure a resume. She covers the seven standard sections, then focuses on the two that take the most work: the summary and the experience section, arguing that specificity and measurable accomplishments beat lists of technologies.

## Key Claims

- A resume should be at most two pages; recruiters give an average resume about a six-second glance.
- The seven sections: summary, experience, certifications and awards, education, technical skills, contact information, and additional links (LinkedIn, GitHub, HackerRank, portfolio).
- Always submit a PDF, use consistent fonts, check for typos, and put phone, email, and city at the top.
- The 5–6 line summary should answer what your experience is, what you care about, and what you want to achieve.
- Use the "Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]" formula that Amazon and Google employees use for resumes and promotions.
- Tailor each resume to the role without fabricating experience, and aim for about six responsibility/achievement bullets per position.
- Mentoring or training others is worth listing because companies value people who grow the team.
