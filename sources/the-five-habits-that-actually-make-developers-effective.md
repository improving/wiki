# The Five Habits That Actually Make Developers Effective — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-five-habits-that-actually-make-developers-effective/the-five-habits-that-actually-make-developers-effective.md`

## Summary

Drawing on his VP years, Liles argues the gap between effective and ineffective developers has little to do with technical skill and more to do with five habits: Passion, Simplicity, Relationships, Balance, and Team. Ordered as a maturity model, each habit enables the next—simplicity cannot survive without passion behind it, and team membership requires all four prior habits. The habits compound and should be built in sequence.

## Key Claims

- Passion is a disposition—caring about the quality and real-world effect of the work—not enthusiasm, which fades; it is contagious, as is its absence.
- "Keep It Simple" means reaching for the simplest solution that meets the need; simple code is readable, maintainable, and the only source of sustainable velocity.
- "Make Friends" is a professional habit: getting things done depends on who reviews your PR quickly, gives honest feedback, and flags risks before they become crises.
- Hero developers are a failure mode; balance forces teams to distribute knowledge and avoid depending on any one person's continuous presence.
- "Be Part of the Team" is an identity choice: caring about outcomes beyond your own tickets and the shared health of the codebase and team velocity.
