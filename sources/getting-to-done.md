# Getting to Done — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/getting-to-done/getting-to-done.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that work on a system can reach defined levels of "done," but work with people never can, because people are complex and constantly evolving. He recounts naively asking whether he could finish all his presidential work in 35 hours, a question he now finds humorous. The practical response is to distinguish the work you must do from the work you can safely ignore, prioritizing attention rather than trying to complete a finite list.

## Key Claims

- Andy Grove, quoted from *High Output Management*: a manager's work is never done — always more than can be done.
- Systems have levels of "done"; people do not, since they are complex and ever evolving.
- Leaders must separate the work they must do from the work they can ignore.
- "Be a fire marshal not a fire fighter": install smoke detectors to tell a dangerous blaze from someone's poor cooking skills.
- One alarm can be ignored or coached; the other deserves full attention immediately.

## Relevance

Informs leadership attention management and [delegation](/concepts/delegation.md) — routing attention to genuine problems and coaching rather than absorbing every signal.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Short and anecdotal; its value is the fire-marshal framing rather than evidence — a single first-person leadership reflection with no data.
