# Great Leaders are Hard to Find Because... — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/quote/quote.md`

## Summary

Prompted by a friend's line — you have to be willing to share your responsibility and give up your expertise to be a great leader — O'Hara reflects on his own path. He excelled through technical skill and fast learning, becoming a "go-to" person and, honestly, taking identity from it. Growth requires stepping into new roles knowing you'll struggle and maybe fail, and he now sees his biggest growth came from fostering growth in others — multiplicative rather than singular. The hard part is what to do when someone isn't going to succeed; leaving them in place invites the Peter Principle, so success criteria and timeframes should be set and reviewed regularly.

## Key Claims

- Great leaders must be willing to share responsibility and give up their expertise.
- Deriving identity from being the "go-to" person limits growth.
- The biggest growth comes from growing others, which is multiplicative ("network effort") rather than singular.
- Not everyone will succeed, and leaving them in place creates a Peter Principle problem.
- Success criteria and timeframes should be agreed up front and reviewed regularly.
