# Time For Work — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/time-for-work/time-for-work.md`

## Summary

O'Hara deliberately argues both sides of where extra work time comes from. His first position: time is consumed and cannot be returned, so when a meeting, conversation, or report needs more hours, those hours are stolen from family rather than from employers — because families love us and forgive us while employers do not, and our drive to excel at work overrides our drive to excel at home. His counter-position: work is purpose, provision, and example, so diligence at work is not theft but a demonstration of diligence to his children, and "you can't steal what is given freely." He then rejects balance itself as the goal, comparing it to holding a yellow M&M over the sun until it appears the same size: unequal things cannot be balanced, and a single dimension like time is not a real measure of significance.

## Key Claims

- Time is consumed, not returned; extra work time is usually taken from family.
- We take that time from family (who forgive) rather than from employers.
- Counter-view: work fulfills purpose, provides, and models diligence for children.
- "You can't steal what is given freely" — families give time freely where employers do not.
- Balance is not the goal: two fundamentally unequal things cannot be balanced, and time alone does not make one more significant than the other.

## Relevance

A direct treatment of [work-life balance](/concepts/work-life-balance.md), including a pointed critique of framing the problem as balance at all.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Openly an exploration ("I'm arguing both sides"), resting on emotional framing the author concedes is perception rather than fact.
