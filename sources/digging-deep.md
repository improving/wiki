# Going Deep: Learning to dig in and find your thing — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/digging-deep/digging-deep.md`

## Summary

Prompted by a Feynman quote that "nearly everything is really interesting if you go into it deeply enough," O'Hara contrasts depth with a culture of speed and surface skimming. He urges readers to give themselves permission to dig in, embrace a little boredom, and ask "why" like a five-year-old. His second thread is to chase what you want to do rather than what you want to be, learning through trying.

## Key Claims

- Feynman's line — nearly everything is interesting if you go deep enough — frames the whole post.
- A world rewarding speed and instant gratification pushes people toward skimming and dopamine chasing.
- Digging in, tolerating boredom, and asking "why" turns the seemingly mundane into something fascinating.
- Focus less on what you want to be and more on what you want to do today, tomorrow, next week.
- Trying things teaches; realizing something is not your thing is still progress — he deliberately avoids the word "fail."
