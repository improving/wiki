# The Rule of Humans by Machines Manufactured by Corporations — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-rule-of-humans-by-machines-manufactured-by-corporations/the-rule-of-humans-by-machines-manufactured-by-corporations.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles takes seriously Jill Lepore's argument, made on the Hard Fork podcast, that AI amounts to the rule of humans by machines manufactured by corporations. He situates it in his own field of enterprise [AI governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md), noting that his work governs the terms of the tenancy while Lepore asks who owns the building itself. He finds the speed claim hard to argue with but pushes on the equivalence between owning infrastructure and ruling, concluding the real open question is whether accountability architecture can move at the speed the technology already has.

## Key Claims

- Lepore traces a recurring channel of American power: the corporation, the administrative state, and the Cold War-era predictive-modeling firm Simulmatics, each arriving wrapped in the same "efficient, technical, inevitable" justification.
- Frontier AI went from research curiosity to embedded enterprise infrastructure in roughly four years, versus the decades or centuries prior waves took.
- The terms governing a frontier model today are mostly private contract, written and amended unilaterally, which is less accountable than either corporate or administrative law ever was.
- Enterprise AI governance gives an organization control over what it does with a model but says nothing about changes to the terms underneath it, a boundary governance work was never designed to reach.
- Whether markets erode infrastructure concentration is unsettled; both the competitive-erosion and structural-resistance cases are real.
