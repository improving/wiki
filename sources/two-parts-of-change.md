# Two Parts of Change — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/two-parts-of-change/two-parts-of-change.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that a change always has two parts, and leaders routinely communicate only one. The first part is the change itself, which almost always produces fear in the people it touches. The second is the reason for the change, and that is where courage comes from: it anchors the vision of future potential and supplies the rationale for walking into the pain. When the reason is present it is inspiring and, in his phrase, the mother of courage. When it is absent, people are left with the change alone plus whatever willpower they can summon — and with the stories they tell themselves to fill the gap. Giving only the first part, he concludes, is not a plan for healthy change.

## Key Claims

- Every change has two parts: the change itself and the reason for it.
- The change itself almost always produces fear in those affected.
- The reason is the anchor for the vision of future potential and the rationale for accepting pain.
- A present reason is inspiring and is the source of courage; its absence leaves only willpower and self-authored stories.
- Communicating only the change is not a healthy change plan.
