# Intro to Deliberate Practice — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/intro-to-deliberate-practice/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md`

## Summary

O'Hara explains that [Improving](/entities/improving.md) uses [George Leonard](/entities/george-leonard.md)'s book [Mastery](/entities/mastery.md) to set mindset, then builds [deliberate practice](/concepts/deliberate-practice.md) toward that goal. Quoting Geoff Colvin's definition — activity designed to improve performance, repeatable, feedback-rich, mentally demanding, and "not much fun" — he stresses it is more than 10,000 hours: doing something and practicing it are not the same. He enumerates nine traits of deliberate practice and closes on its limits: world-class performance also needs luck, and the commitment costs other parts of life.

## Key Claims

- Deliberate practice is designed to improve performance, repeatable, feedback-rich, mentally demanding, and not fun.
- It is more than 10,000 hours of repetition; it is intentional growth and preparation.
- Traits include structure, discomfort, rest, feedback, coaching, intrinsic motivation, long time horizons, intense focus, and the spacing effect.
- Sessions should cap at 60–90 minutes and no more than 3–4 hours a day, with sleep consolidating learning.
- Measure useful outcomes, not vanity metrics: paid subscribers matter, email subscribers do not.
- Deliberate practice alone yields a good performer; world-class also needs luck and randomness.
