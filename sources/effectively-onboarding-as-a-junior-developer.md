# Effectively Onboarding as a Junior Developer — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/effectively-onboarding-as-a-junior-developer/effectively-onboarding-as-a-junior-developer.md`

## Summary

Daniel Scheufler addresses the new-junior-developer dilemma of when to ask for help, arguing the answer is a repeatable loop: do everything you know how to do first, then ask for help. He frames onboarding as a learning job, not just an answer-fetching one, and gives a concrete time-boxed process for turning a stuck ticket into a valuable, specific question.

## Key Claims

- Onboarding takes work from both the junior and the team; by teaching, the teacher learns the material better too.
- "Do everything you know to do first. Then ask for help" is the whole method.
- Start by writing out in plain English what work the ticket requires; even that is transferable value and a starting point for the senior.
- Don't fall into comparing yourself to senior teammates yet; focus on whether you did everything you could do.
- Time-box with a Pomodoro (25 minutes of focus, 5 to review); spend no more than an hour before getting the work checked.
- When researching, never copy answers blindly — manually retype and understand a solution so you actually learn.
- Break a challenge down (e.g., computing a cart total into subtotal, tax, coupons) into pseudo-code signatures the senior can quickly correct.
