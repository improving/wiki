# Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Explained — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning-explained/artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning-explained.md`

## Summary

An introductory explainer on AI and machine learning, from Turing's "can machines think?" and the 1956 Dartmouth conference to how ML turns data into predictions, and why enterprises need it.

## Key Claims

- AI programming rests on three cognitive skills: learning, reasoning, and self-correction.
- Algorithms are predefined rules a computer follows; recommendation feeds like TikTok's adapt and self-correct.
- AI goals split into a human approach (think and act like humans) and an ideal approach (think and act rationally).
- Weak AI solves problems within predefined rules and is what is prevalent today; strong AI would resemble human consciousness and emotion.
- ML uses learning algorithms fed data sources to produce outputs and improve without explicit programming.
- With big data, ubiquitous computing, and IoT, ML lets enterprises find patterns and make timely, accurate decisions, from credit scoring to self-driving cars.
