# Wisdom — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/wisdom/wisdom.md`

## Summary

The post defines wisdom as the right application of knowledge in a context, then notes the irony that we gain wisdom through the wrong application of it. Leaders can bring others along in their learning, letting them grow and learn from the leader's mistakes — but only mistakes we own are mistakes we can learn from. The post closes with a question for leaders: how are you including those who need to grow in a way that lets them gain wisdom while you own your mistakes?

## Key Claims

- Wisdom is knowledge correctly applied within a given context.
- Wisdom is acquired through the wrong application of knowledge — mistakes are the mechanism.
- We can only learn from mistakes we own.
- Others can grow by sharing in a leader's owned learning and mistakes.
- Closing question: how are you including those who need to grow so they gain wisdom from your owned mistakes?

## Relevance

A compact statement of the [experiential wisdom](/concepts/experiential-wisdom.md) distinction, tying wisdom acquisition to [learning from failure](/concepts/learning-from-failure.md) and the ownership of mistakes.

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Very short aphoristic reflection; definitional and opinion-based with no evidence.
