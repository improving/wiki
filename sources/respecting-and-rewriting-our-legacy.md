# Respecting and Rewriting Our Legacy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/respecting-and-rewriting-our-legacy/respecting-and-rewriting-our-legacy.md`

## Summary

Through the fictional stories of Jonathan and Miriam, the post argues that legacy systems — the software that built a business — deserve respect, but that replacing them should be driven by real business value rather than a faithful line-by-line replica. Modernization should prioritize user experience, the highest-value features, and the biggest pain points, while weighing build versus buy.

## Key Claims

- Legacy systems may be the reason a company still exists; they and the people who built them deserve respect.
- Estimate scope from lines of code, database structures, screens, reports, and business logic — while accepting the new system won't merely replicate the old.
- Do not rebuild line by line: many legacy features create more pain than value and should not be carried over.
- UX/UI matters now: employees expect work software as intuitive as the apps on their phones.
- Separate wants from needs and weigh cost against value before spending resources.
- Build in automated testing, especially for features critical to operations.
- Shift away from CRUD-only design toward workflows that reflect how the business operates today, and prefer off-the-shelf solutions where they fit.
