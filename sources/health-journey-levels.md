# Health Journey: Levels — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/health-journey-levels/health-journey-levels.md`

## Summary

O'Hara describes choosing [Levels](/entities/levels.md) for a three-month [continuous glucose monitoring](/concepts/continuous-glucose-monitoring.md) experiment, preferring its pricing and app experience over Nutrisense and specifically wanting the Dexcom G6 sensor for real-time data. Using the device became a mild obsession: he learned how food order and combinations change his glucose response, and was surprised that creative work alone could spike his readings. He plans to stop continuous use after month three but may continue intermittent sensors.

## Key Claims

- Levels and Nutrisense cost roughly the same; the deciding factors were app quality and Dexcom G6's continuous real-time data.
- He adopted [meal ordering](/concepts/meal-ordering.md) — vegetables first, then protein, then starch — to blunt spikes.
- Adding mushrooms to eggs blunts the spike cheese would otherwise cause; a bad day can make him reactive for two more days.
- Creative work (writing, whiteboarding, building talks) produced a 90-point glucose spike with no food involved; a walk afterward brings numbers down.
- Complaints: unopt-outable "Guidance" content and an apparent app memory leak requiring restarts.

## Relevance

Informs [continuous glucose monitoring](/concepts/continuous-glucose-monitoring.md) and [meal ordering](/concepts/meal-ordering.md) as self-experimentation practices, and the broader Health Journey series.
