# Patience — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/patience/patience.md`

## Summary

O'Hara separates two kinds of patience. Passive patience is sitting and waiting for the world to hand you what you want — a belief he says violates Newton's third law, because no action yields no result. Active patience is taking meaningful steps now to set up future success: saving money, growing skills, and fostering relationships so you're in the right place when opportunity arrives. He frames active patience as stacking the deck in your favor, invoking the adage that luck favors the prepared.

## Key Claims

- Two types of patience exist: passive (waiting) and active (preparing).
- Passive patience "violates" Newton's third law — no action, no result.
- Life doesn't owe you anything, so waiting passively can mean waiting a very long time.
- Active patience means strategically preparing: saving, upskilling, and building relationships.
- Luck favors the prepared; going first and doing it consistently makes the world do much of the heavy lifting.
