# How to Measure the Success of a Learning Strategy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-to-measure-the-success-of-a-learning-strategy/how-to-measure-the-success-of-a-learning-strategy.md`

## Summary

Following her earlier post on building a learning strategy, the author explains how to measure whether it worked, using a three-tier model that climbs from learning activity metrics to performance metrics to business outcomes. Completion rates and knowledge retention show the training happened and stuck; behavior and engagement measures show it changed how people work; ROI, client satisfaction, and project success show it moved the business. Continuous-improvement metrics keep the content current.

## Key Claims

- Learning activity metrics: training completion rate (on-time completion) and knowledge retention via assessments and quizzes.
- Performance metrics: observable behavioral change plus employee performance, productivity, engagement, and satisfaction surveys.
- Business outcomes should be the ultimate goal: return on investment, client satisfaction, and project success rates.
- Business measures include completed deliverables, adherence to project timelines, and client feedback.
- Continuous-improvement metrics — frequency of feedback and implementation of improvement ideas — keep the strategy relevant.
- The metric stack is meant to move from activity at the bottom up to outcomes at the top.
