# Tiny Thought: Commitments - handling commitments better — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/tiny-thought/tiny-thought.md`

## Summary

A two-line practice note. O'Hara observes that leaders too often tell people what their commitment is and then hold them to the leader's expectation. The better pattern is to ask people for their own commitment and then hold them to that expectation. The whole argument rests on who authors the commitment: when the leader supplies it, the commitment is borrowed and the accountability is external; when the person supplies it, the commitment is theirs and the accountability is self-imposed. It is a small, memorable reframing of a routine management moment rather than a developed argument, and it leaves open the practical questions — what to do when the commitment offered is too small, and how to renegotiate one that has slipped.

## Key Claims

- The common failure is telling someone their commitment, then holding them to your expectation.
- The better pattern is asking for their commitment, then holding them to their expectation.
- The person making the commitment should be the one who authors it.
- Ownership of the commitment is what makes the expectation enforceable.

## Relevance

A compact statement of [Commitment Accounting](/concepts/commitment-accounting.md) and of the shift to [Personal Accountability](/concepts/personal-accountability.md) that follows from letting people set their own commitments.
