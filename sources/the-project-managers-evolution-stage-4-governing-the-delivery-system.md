# The Project Manager's Evolution, Stage 4: Governing the Delivery System — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-project-managers-evolution-stage-4-governing-the-delivery-system/the-project-managers-evolution-stage-4-governing-the-delivery-system.md`

## Summary

Stage 4 is where the project manager's role finishes changing. The hand review of Stage 3 becomes programmatic: checking moves out of one person's head and into a [protection harness](/concepts/protection-harness.md), the automated checks that sit between workflow steps and refuse artifacts that fail them. The working phrase is to offload workflows behind a protection harness, least complex first. Personal agents must be reconciled into shared workflows the whole delivery organization agrees on, which pulls in the PMO and IT and makes the PM a governor of the delivery system rather than an assembler of reports.

## Key Claims

- A gate can check non-code artifacts: a status-integrity gate reconciles reported status against live signals; a plan-sufficiency gate checks dependencies; a risk-coverage gate checks known categories; a change-control gate requires impact before baseline.
- A weak gate does not stay contained: a portfolio of confidently green projects can roll up to a steering committee while one is quietly slipping, surfacing as a missed go-live months later.
- Agent failures are correlated, so one misreading repeats across every status, which is why gates matter more than spot-checking; feedback is editing the specification, not coaching.
- A non-technical PM caps out at advising on and operating within team-built Stage 4 workflows; building and debugging their own gates requires technical depth.
