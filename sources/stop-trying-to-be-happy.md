# Stop Trying to be Happy — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/stop-trying-to-be-happy/stop-trying-to-be-happy.md`

## Summary

Drawing on his gratitude practice, O'Hara argues that many of his struggles come from focusing on the wrong things. He contends that people are poor at predicting what will make them happy — often guessing wrong or choosing something too short-term to matter — and that chasing happiness directly leads to materialism and comparison. Real happiness, he says, lies in contentment with who you are, and people are happiest when they are growing toward a better self. His conclusion: make growth, not happiness, your life's goal.

## Key Claims

- People are typically wrong when they guess what will make them happy (he links a study suggesting asking a stranger predicts better than self-guessing).
- Chasing happiness directly leads to materialism and comparison ("Do I have what they have?").
- True happiness is found in contentment with who you are, not what you have.
- Vacation may provide rest and recharge, but that is not the same as happiness.
- We are happiest when growing, because we recognize our potential and move toward a better self.
