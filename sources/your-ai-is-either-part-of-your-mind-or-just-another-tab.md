# Your AI Is Either Part of Your Mind or Just Another Tab — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/your-ai-is-either-part-of-your-mind-or-just-another-tab/your-ai-is-either-part-of-your-mind-or-just-another-tab.md`

## Summary

Drawing on Clark and Chalmers' "The Extended Mind," the post distinguishes two ways of using an external cognitive resource: as a tool you consult and set aside, or as a prosthetic so integrated that removing it changes what you are capable of. It argues that most early AI adopters are tool users with bounded gains, while a smaller group is building prosthetic integration that produces more capable — but more dependent — users. The practical response is to design the boundary between human cognition and model inference deliberately.

## Key Claims

- Clark and Chalmers (1998) argue an external resource is part of the mind when it is constantly accessible, reliably deployed, and integrated into cognitive process rather than consulted episodically.
- Mode one (tool) leaves the thinker unchanged; mode two (prosthetic) means removal does more than slow you down — it changes your capability.
- Andy Clark extended the thesis to generative AI in a 2025 Nature Communications piece, arguing models meet the extended-cognition criteria under those functional conditions.
- Over-reliance research warns that deep integration risks capability loss when the AI assumes too much decision authority — the "notebook gets lost" failure mode.
- Three responses follow: explicit integration-depth targets per role, capturing the model's reasoning chain (not just outputs), and review that can detect prosthetic dependency.
