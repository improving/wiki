# What is the Core of Agile? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/what-is-the-core-of-agile/what-is-the-core-of-agile.md`

## Summary

Emily Stickle argues Agile is not one thing — practice, mindset, or toolbox — but the alternative to rigid traditional project management, best defined by the four values of the Agile Manifesto. She walks through each value and why it confronts the PMBOK's process-heavy approach.

## Key Claims

- Traditional (Waterfall) project management is governed by the PMBOK, and until its most recent version said little about value.
- Agile developed as a direct criticism of traditional project management, and its values confront what the PMBOK long prescribed.
- The four values: individuals and interactions over processes and tools; working software over comprehensive documentation; customer collaboration over contract negotiation; responding to change over following a plan.
- The values read as a plea to leaders to empower the people who own the work, and each is confrontational and demands a different leadership posture.
- Agile's approach became standard in software development and now benefits all areas of business.
- Not every Agile practice fits every department; the framework is an alternative to a proven approach rather than a universal prescription.
