# Judgment Was Built by Proxy. AI Automated the Proxy. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/judgment-was-built-by-proxy/judgment-was-built-by-proxy.md`

## Summary

For every prior generation, volume activity served as a proxy for developing judgment: writing a thousand mediocre loops gave the raw material for intuition. AI is removing exactly that routine work first, while expanding the work that already requires judgment. Liles argues the apprenticeship bundle has broken — the junior's first job is now to evaluate AI output, which requires judgment on day one — and that organizations must deliberately manufacture what used to arrive as a byproduct, designing programs with protected reflection time rather than hoping proximity to AI builds calibration.

## Key Claims

- PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer finds AI removing routine apprenticeship work while increasing demand for judgment earlier in careers.
- Harvard Business Review (Feb 2026): experienced people gain productivity while juniors often cannot tell whether AI work is good, widening the gap.
- Three emerging programs: curated failure libraries, intent-specification drills, and structured evaluation exercises.
- Peter Naur's "Programming as Theory Building" (1985) explains why tacit theory requires presence, iteration, and visible reasoning to transfer.
- The gap shows up around 2031: teams treating apprenticeship as automatic will feel it in architecture and scoping quality.
