# Are you leaving or quitting? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/are-you-leaving-or-quitting/are-you-leaving-or-quitting.md`

## Summary

O'Hara shares a distinction offered by a colleague, Curtis, during a conversation about someone moving on: "The difference between leaving and quitting is the momentum of the company you're exiting. If it's in a good place and moving forward, you're leaving. If it's in decline or struggling, you're quitting." He finds it impossible to unhear and uses it to re-examine his own past career moves, asking himself whether he left or quit. He closes with a clarification that he is doing neither—Improving is the best place he has ever worked.

## Key Claims

- The difference between leaving and quitting is the momentum of the company being exited.
- A company in a good place and moving forward means you are leaving.
- A company in decline or struggling means you are quitting.
- The framing prompts reflection on one's own past decisions and moves.
- O'Hara explicitly notes he is neither leaving nor quitting; Improving is the best place he has worked.
