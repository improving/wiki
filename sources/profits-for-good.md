# Profits for Good — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/profits-for-good/profits-for-good.md`

## Summary

O'Hara asks whether business can be both good and profitable, rejecting the image of capitalism as a fat businessman on a pile of others' money. He introduces Conscious Capitalism — a movement started by John Mackey (Whole Foods) and Kip Tindell (Container Store) — and its stakeholder model, which widens the focus beyond shareholders and customers to employees, suppliers, industry, and community. He then argues Improving was built on exactly those values from the start: relationships first, suppliers treated as friends, free space for user groups, conference participation, fundraising, and genuine care among colleagues.

## Key Claims

- Business requires profits to keep running and to provide work for people and families.
- Conscious Capitalism, founded by Mackey and Tindell, argues profits are not evil but should feed the ecosystem that produced them.
- The stakeholder model includes employees, suppliers, industry, and community, not just shareholders and customers.
- Improving's founding idea was to be "THE place people want to work," prioritizing people and relationships.
- O'Hara concedes Improving is not perfect but says it demonstrates the idealistic model can be a reality and a success.
