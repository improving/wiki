# The Neuron Count Tell — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-neuron-count-tell/the-neuron-count-tell.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles dissects a recurring move in AI-consciousness discussion: setting a biological neuron count beside an AI parameter count as if the comparison were evidence. He traces "eighty-six billion" to Suzana Herculano-Houzel's peer-reviewed neuron count and traces "neuron" in computing to McCulloch and Pitts' 1943 simplified logic-gate model. The two numbers share a word and nothing else, and their ratio measures nothing. Liles calls it a "tell" rather than a weak argument, because it borrows the shape of quantitative evidence while committing to no claim anyone could push back on.

## Key Claims

- Eighty-six billion is Herculano-Houzel's widely cited count of human brain neurons, roughly sixteen billion in the neocortex, with on the order of a hundred trillion synaptic connections.
- The word "neuron" entered computing in 1943 with McCulloch and Pitts; an artificial neuron is a weighted sum through a nonlinear function, nothing more.
- GPT-3 shipped in 2020 with 175 billion parameters — each a single learned weight inside a matrix multiplication — a scale figure never intended as a claim of equivalence.
- Consciousness research has no agreed definition and no instrument that measures it, so a citable number moves into the space where a working definition should be.

## Relevance

Touches the wiki's AI-philosophy themes on definitional precision and what counts as evidence in capability claims; relevant wherever model scale is used rhetorically.
