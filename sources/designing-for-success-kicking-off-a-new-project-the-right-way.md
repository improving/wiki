# Designing for Success: Kicking Off a New Project the Right Way — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/designing-for-success-kicking-off-a-new-project-the-right-way/designing-for-success-kicking-off-a-new-project-the-right-way.md`

## Summary

Writing as a design leader, the author argues project success starts at kickoff by aligning client goals, user needs, and technical feasibility. He lays out five phases — discovery and alignment, goals and scope, strategy and planning, collaboration and communication, and deliverables — each with concrete activities like stakeholder interviews, journey maps, design workshops, and preliminary wireframes. Design leadership, he says, is about driving alignment and outcomes, not just producing interfaces.

## Key Claims

- Phase 1 (discovery): stakeholder interviews, user research, and competitive analysis uncover needs and opportunities.
- Phase 2 (scope): a problem statement, measurable goals, and defined scope prevent ambiguity and scope creep.
- Phase 3 (strategy): journey maps, personas, content audits, and early developer collaboration ground designs in feasibility.
- Phase 4 (collaboration): design workshops, a communication plan, and clear roles keep teams aligned and accountable.
- Phase 5 (deliverables): a kickoff presentation, research plan, and preliminary wireframes set the foundation.
- One engagement's redesign lifted user interaction 30% after goals were tied to engagement metrics.
- Early developer collaboration saved weeks by addressing an integration issue before build.
