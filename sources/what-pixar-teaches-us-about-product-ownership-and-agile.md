# What Pixar Teaches Us About Product Ownership and Agile Leadership — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/what-pixar-teaches-us-about-product-ownership-and-agile/what-pixar-teaches-us-about-product-ownership-and-agile.md`

## Summary

Blake McMillan draws on *Creativity, Inc.* by Ed Catmull and Amy Wallace to argue that Pixar's feedback model shows what product ownership should look like. Pixar's "Brain Trust" — experienced filmmakers with no authority over the director — surfaces problems and root causes without mandating solutions, leaving the director and team to own the outcome. McMillan contrasts this with the common anti-pattern where stakeholders hand Product Owners a list of solutions rather than problems.

## Key Claims

- The Brain Trust consists of movie-making subject matter experts who have made films themselves, so their critiques are grounded in shared experience.
- The Brain Trust has no authority over the director's decisions; it brings problems to the surface rather than prescribing fixes.
- A common anti-pattern: stakeholders create the list of solutions (requirements) and the Product Owner only sequences them, if that.
- Three roadblocks to adopting the Pixar model: Product Owners lack decision authority, teams aren't trusted to create solutions, and stakeholders are incentivized to write solutions instead of naming problems.
- Benefits of real ownership: decisions made closer to the work, less waste on low-value items, and higher team engagement.
