# Profound Impacts — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/profound-impacts/profound-impacts.md`

## Summary

O'Hara jokes that compilers are easier to work with than people: a compiler tells you when you're wrong, you fix it, and you move on — with people, fixing the error doesn't mean the anger is gone, assuming they even tell you. Visiting folks from a recent acquisition, he asked and truly listened, and they opened up. A change had been communicated but not heard, leaving people in "NIMSU" (No Information, Make Shit Up); no one on the executive team said "I'm sorry," which blocked progress until it was said.

## Key Claims

- With people, unlike compilers, fixing the problem doesn't mean everything is fine.
- A communicated change that isn't heard leaves people inventing their own explanations ("NIMSU," credited to Rex Miller).
- The communication failure was a leadership fall-down, and the missing apology compounded it.
- Sharing the "why" moved everyone forward, but the apology had to come first.
- People will mess up; the response is to own it, make amends with actual words, and define how to move forward together.
