# AI Complexity vs Difficulty — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ai-complexity-vs-difficulty/ai-complexity-vs-difficulty.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles distinguishes complexity (many steps but a knowable recipe) from difficulty (no rulebook, evolving variables requiring judgment). AI handles complexity well — data collection, number-crunching, large data sets — while humans should handle difficulty: weighing evidence, intuition, and the impact of decisions. The recommendation is integration of the two, not pitting AI against humans.

## Key Claims

- Complex problems have many steps but a repeatable path — e.g., rolling out an HR benefits program globally.
- Difficult problems have no rulebook and require adapting to evolving variables — e.g., navigating a major market disruption.
- AI, while not matching human sophistication, compensates with its capacity to handle complexity.
- Split business problems into data-centric tasks (AI-suited) and decision-making processes (human-suited).
- Use AI to manage large data sets while humans navigate decision-making and trade-offs.
- The framing is integrating AI and humans to tackle problems, not choosing one over the other.
