# Career Capital in the Agentic Era — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/career-capital-in-the-agentic-era/career-capital-in-the-agentic-era.md`

## Summary

Liles argues the career question is not "will AI take my job?" but which of the tasks that constitute your work are vulnerable to substitution, which are becoming more valuable, and what that implies for how you invest. Career capital—accumulated capability, reputation, and relationships—is being repriced as the agentic era changes which forms appreciate. He maps what is depreciating against what is appreciating and draws investment implications.

## Key Claims

- Depreciating: execution speed in repeatable tasks; credential-as-signal without demonstrated judgment; siloed expertise without systems thinking.
- Appreciating: judgment (evaluating whether an output is right, not just whether it looks right); mentorship as structure; the capacity to move "from doing to defining."
- The 8-stage trust evolution requires humans at the judgment layers, making mentorship a structural requirement rather than a nice-to-have.
- Investment should weight toward deepening domain judgment, building structured mentorship capacity, and extending your time horizon.
- AI assistance accelerates the short-term execution game but not the long-term judgment and relationship development game that compounds.
