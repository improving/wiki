# Accenture Just Set the Reskilling Bar — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/accenture-just-set-the-reskilling-bar/accenture-just-set-the-reskilling-bar.md`

## Summary

O'Hara treats Accenture's decision to exit 11,000 staff who cannot be reskilled on AI as a milestone. It is not performance-based and not a restructuring; the CEO's phrase "compressed timeline" makes the criterion explicit—adapt or leave. He argues this further reprices the consulting talent model: the pyramid was always going to go, but now firms must answer who is making the AI transition and what happens if they don't. He notes Improving ran another round of internal skills testing and certifications, and warns the same standard is coming for every consulting organization.

## Key Claims

- Accenture is exiting 11,000 staff who cannot be reskilled on AI—not for performance and not as restructuring.
- The CEO's "compressed timeline" framing makes the adapt-or-leave criterion explicit and public, an accountability standard that did not exist 12 months earlier.
- The consulting pyramid was already repricing; this raises the bar to individual adaptability.
- The internal question shifts from "are we investing in AI training?" to "who is making the transition, and what if they don't?"
- Improving ran another round of internal skills testing and certifications.
- Firms that fix their reskilling story now gain a hard-to-replicate talent advantage; those who wait face their own moment without the PR budget.
