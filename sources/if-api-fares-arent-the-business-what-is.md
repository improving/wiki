# If API Fares Aren't the Business, What Is — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/if-api-fares-arent-the-business-what-is/if-api-fares-arent-the-business-what-is.md`

## Summary

Liles reviews the top entries in Dwarkesh Patel's AI blog prize, focusing on third place's comparison of AI labs to Hong Kong's MTR—a transit system that profits not from fares but from the property built around its stations. He argues the analogy has a hole: MTR's malls exist because of a one-off government land grant that no lab can simply assume.

## Key Claims

- Michael Li's third-place essay says the per-token API is the fare—covering costs at best—and that labs must own the "malls": data-trusteeship deals, forward-deployed integration work, and accumulated RL data.
- MTR got its development rights from the Hong Kong government decades before opening a line; almost no other subway system gets that deal by default, and the essay never explains how a government would hand such access to a single lab.
- First place argued the OpenAI Foundation should spend $40–60 billion over a decade on far-UVC lighting to end airborne disease transmission—a hedge that pays off whether or not the AI business justifies its valuation.
- Both essays assume the metered API is not where the story ends, yet neither defends the model-answering-a-prompt product as what makes any of it work in ten years.
