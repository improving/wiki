# Buying the Team Without Buying the Company — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/buying-the-team-without-buying-the-company/buying-the-team-without-buying-the-company.md`

## Summary

Nvidia is paying $6 billion to license Poolside's "Model Factory" software and hire more than a hundred of its staff, while Poolside keeps its name, cap table, and remaining investors. Liles argues this is the same structure Microsoft used on Inflection and Amazon on Adept: license the technology, hire the people who built it, and leave the corporate shell standing. The outcome is an acquisition; the paperwork avoids one.

## Key Claims

- Three buyers, three targets, one identical structure: license the technology, hire the team, leave the shell.
- A licensing agreement paired with individual job offers is a different transaction under US law than a straight acquisition above the pre-merger filing threshold, so it draws no waiting period or review.
- Inflection and Adept kept their names and investors but lost the teams and technology that made them valuable.
- Nvidia—already closest to a monopoly finding in AI infrastructure—moving up the stack via this mechanism is the detail that should draw the most attention.
- For enterprises, a vendor's team and technology can be extracted with no merger announcement, no regulatory filing to search, and no customer notification requirement.

## Relevance

Informs AI vendor due diligence and governance: "proximity" between a small vendor and one hyperscaler is the practical tell that its research team is one licensing conversation from leaving.
