# What College Is Actually For — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/what-college-is-actually-for/what-college-is-actually-for.md`

## Summary

After dropping his son Declan at Texas State, O'Hara reads a *Free Press* piece by Carlos Carvalho, president of the University of Austin, whose convocation address told the class of 2030 to "forget yourself and discover the durable joy of being absorbed in something greater than you." O'Hara contrasts that with the self-discovery most universities promise, and argues the real prize of four years is the capacity to think critically and engage civilly with people you disagree with — a rarer skill than any technical one.

## Key Claims

- Carvalho's core message was absorption in something greater, not self-discovery.
- Universities have traded "What is true and good?" for "How does this make you feel?"
- The combination of holding a real position while genuinely listening to the opposite one is rarer than any technical skill.
- When something serious is on the line, people turn to the one who can think, not the loudest one.
- College is four years of hard, uncomfortable, worthwhile practice.
