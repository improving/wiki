# The Billing Model Just Broke — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-billing-model-just-broke/the-billing-model-just-broke.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that AI is breaking consulting's time-and-materials billing model. McKinsey is moving 25% of its global fees to outcome-based pricing, Bain says 30% of its work is already AI-enabled and heading to 50%, and clients watching a 40-hour analysis compress into 15 minutes are refusing to pay for the hours saved. When fees are tied to outcomes, O'Hara contends, the incentive flips: get to the result faster and better and share the benefit, a win-win though not a panacea. He notes the firms moving fastest do so because it's defensible, and that the value shift is to judgment rather than production — the decisions about what to produce, how to frame results, and what to ask next, which models cannot compress.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey is moving 25% of its global consulting fees to outcome-based pricing.
- Bain says 30% of its work is already AI-enabled and heading toward 50%.
- Clients watching AI compress 40-hour analyses into 15 minutes refuse to pay for the hours saved, breaking the billing model.
- Time-and-headcount fees can run sideways from what the client wants; outcome fees flip the incentive toward faster, better results with shared benefit.
- Firms adopting outcome pricing fastest do so because it's defensible and locks in margin durability before AI-driven production compression fully lands.
- The value shifts to judgment, not production: deciding what to produce, how to frame it, and what to ask next — work models can't do and may make more valuable.
