# How Agile Learning Transformed Our Internal Academies — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-agile-learning-transformed-our-internal-academies/how-agile-learning-transformed-our-internal-academies.md`

## Summary

Faced with no time to run traditional instructor-led academies, Yanet Morales Ramirez adopted eduScrum for a JavaScript academy of 20 junior developers in Mexico: learners split into self-managed teams, set weekly objectives, held daily calls, presented their work, and ran retrospectives. The model scaled to a Cloud academy of roughly 60 Improvers across nine self-created teams, overseen by a junior Scrum Master.

## Key Claims

- Traditional academies — learning plan, content, labs, assessments, teaching time — compete with meetings, client visits, and urgent work, delaying the program.
- eduScrum splits attendees into self-managed teams given only learning objectives and a weekly check-in to review progress and doubts.
- The Mexico JavaScript academy involved 20 junior developers and ran in weekly learning cycles.
- Each cycle: weekly goals, daily team calls, end-of-week presentations, and a retrospective to improve the process.
- Attendees learned teamwork, problem-solving, and self-management, and produced weekly mini projects.
- The Cloud academy was organized in three weeks, launched with ~60 Improvers across nine teams, and ran six learning cycles.
- Running the academy gives junior Scrum Masters a safe environment to gain experience and confidence.
