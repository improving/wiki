# Training vs Trying — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/training-vs-trying/training-vs-trying.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that "trying" to lead contains an escape route — a failure path and an excuse — whereas training to lead has only one failure mode: not doing it. Training is progress plus work plus discipline. He then names two leadership qualities worth training. Positivity, because no one follows an Eeyore; his practice is daily gratitude, listing three things each morning (no repeats for 30 days), whether in a journal or a text chain with a trusted group. Resilience, because leaders make decisions that disappoint people they care about, which takes grit; he builds it through positive outlook and self-care. He cites Improving's own leadership model as one of many available frameworks.

## Key Claims

- "Trying" implies an out; the only failure of training is not doing it.
- Positivity is a core leadership trait, and daily gratitude — three items, no repeats for 30 days — is the easiest way to build it.
- "Comparison is the thief of joy"; stop looking around and start looking in.
- Resilience is required because leaders must accept unpopular decisions that affect people's careers and families.
- Self-care reduces reactivity: exercise 30 minutes a couple of times a week, walk between meetings, watch carbs and sugar, meditate 10 minutes daily.
