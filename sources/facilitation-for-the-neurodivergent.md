# Facilitation for the Neurodivergent — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/facilitation-for-the-neurodivergent/facilitation-for-the-neurodivergent.md`

## Summary

Randall Dunigan, an Agile coach with ADHD, describes five facilitation practices he uses to draw out neurodivergent participants: frequent breaks, silent writing, tactile interaction, setting and honoring expectations, and communicating through every channel. He cites an estimate that 30–40% of the population is neurodiverse and argues most meetings are not built to unlock that potential.

## Key Claims

- An estimated 30–40% of the population is neurodiverse; the share is likely higher on software teams.
- Vary topics every ~10 minutes and set a break every 30 minutes; a 4-hour planning meeting otherwise becomes something to survive rather than prepare for.
- Silent writing on sticky notes or a shared board gives anxious or polite people a low-risk way to contribute — often their ideas are the best in the room.
- Tactile/digital-board interaction helps people with weak object constancy stay engaged.
- Publish and rigorously honor expectations; a 30-minute meeting must end in 30 minutes for those expectations to comfort anyone.
- Share materials, agendas, and chat channels ahead of time and in multiple formats, but don't take on monitoring everything yourself.
