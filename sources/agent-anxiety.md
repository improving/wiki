# Agent Anxiety — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/agent-anxiety/agent-anxiety.md`

## Summary

O'Hara describes "agent anxiety": the low-grade discomfort of seeing idle AI agent capacity and feeling pressed to give it work. He once had a self-regulating system—when his brain gave out he dropped to mundane tasks—and AI broke it. Now he checks agents constantly, producing the same self-reinforcing loop as multitasking and costing the roughly 25 minutes it takes to recover from an interruption. His diagnosis: compute is now on demand while human attention remains scarce, and a brain evolved for scarcity reads unused capacity as a problem. Time is still the constraint; he has added a new category of interruption dressed up as leverage, and has no answer yet.

## Key Claims

- Multitasking is a myth; the brain context-switches, and each switch costs roughly 25 minutes of recovery.
- His prior self-regulating system—dropping to low-stakes work when capacity ran out—has been broken by AI.
- "Agent anxiety" is the irrational discomfort of idle agent capacity, which triggers constant checking.
- The checking loop mirrors multitasking: the more he checks, the more anxious he feels when not checking.
- Compute is now on demand while human attention stays scarce, so an evolved-for-scarcity brain reads unused capacity as a problem.
- Idle agents cost nothing meaningful; the real cost is fragmented attention, and the instinct that made us productive in scarcity may now make us less productive.
