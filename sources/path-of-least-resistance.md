# Path of Least Resistance — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/path-of-least-resistance/path-of-least-resistance.md`

## Summary

Using an admittedly inexact Thoreau line — the path of least resistance makes men and rivers go crooked — O'Hara argues that the easiest option is frequently the damaging one over the long run. Humans "roll downhill" toward the least friction, confrontation, and pain, which is why quick-fix schemes thrive. He illustrates compounding compromise with an embezzlement case he guesses began "about $50k at a time," where each small shortcut makes the next easier. The counter is to dig in, do the boring and difficult work, and treat consistent hard choices as a force multiplier.

## Key Claims

- People, like water, tend to choose the least friction, confrontation, and pain.
- Get-rich-quick schemes, 5-minute abs, and fad supplements rarely deliver as promised.
- A multi-million-dollar embezzlement likely started small — "about $50k at a time" — and grew through repeated self-justification.
- Small daily deviations compound into large impacts across time.
- Doing hard things now and repeatedly is a "force multiplier" toward the outcomes you actually want.
