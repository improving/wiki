# Screw Finding Your Passion — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/screw-finding-your-passion/screw-finding-your-passion.md`

## Summary

O'Hara shares a post by Mark Manson and endorses its alignment with *So Good They Can't Ignore You*, a book he ranks in his all-time top five. The theme is that passion is not a thing you discover and then follow; the problem is never passion, it is priorities. O'Hara extends the point: when he feels there's no time for something, that usually signals it's missing from his priority list, so he asks whether it is a priority and, if not, drops it. He also embraces the "every job has bedpans" mentality — even a dream job is partly a grind.

## Key Claims

- "The problem isn't passion. It's never passion. It's priorities." (quoted from Mark Manson).
- We make time for what is important; not making time means it is not important enough — so ask honestly whether something is a priority, and drop it if not.
- Every job sucks sometimes; there is no passionate activity you will never tire of, never stress over, never complain about.
- Manson lives his dream job yet still hates about 30% of it, sometimes more — the "every job has bedpans" mentality.
- "You already enjoy something... You're just choosing to ignore them" — the author asks what joy he is overlooking, since that appreciation fuels him through the grind.
