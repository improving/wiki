# Offshore Engagement Models: 7 Options Compared for Cost & Risk — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/offshore-engagement-models/offshore-engagement-models.md`

## Summary

Faizan Fahim lays out seven offshore engagement models and argues that a mismatch between business expectations and the chosen model drives cost overruns, delays, and quality issues. Each model defines ownership, pricing, communication flow, delivery accountability, and risk distribution differently, and each fits a specific project type, budget pattern, and maturity level. The post compares their trade-offs and closes with selection factors and a short FAQ.

## Key Claims

- The seven models are fixed price, dedicated development team, time & material, staff augmentation, managed services, SLA/milestone-based, and hybrid.
- Fixed price suits small, well-defined projects but offers low flexibility and risks compromised quality if scope is underestimated.
- A dedicated development team fits long-term product work and complex domains, but demands higher commitment and active client-side product ownership.
- Time & material suits agile and exploratory work with fast iteration, but challenges budget predictability and requires strong governance.
- Staff augmentation adds individual specialists into existing teams while the client retains architecture and delivery ownership, at the cost of limited delivery accountability.
- Managed services shift operational risk to the provider against SLAs and KPIs, but limit rapid change and risk vendor dependency.
- SLA/milestone-based models tie payment to measurable checkpoints, fitting regulated industries, at the cost of flexibility and heavy upfront planning.
- Hybrid models blend structures per workstream and suit large enterprises or AI platforms mixing research flexibility with production reliability.
- Selection depends on project purpose, technical expertise, budget constraints, scope stability, team size, and development stage.
