# Why 80% of Governance Programs Fail (And What the Other 20% Do Differently) — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/why-80-of-governance-programs-fail-and-what-the-other-20-do-differently/why-80-of-governance-programs-fail-and-what-the-other-20-do-differently.md`

## Summary

Liles takes Gartner's February 2024 prediction — that 80% of data and analytics governance initiatives will fail by 2027 — and focuses on the diagnosis rather than the number: programs fail because they run governance as data hygiene and control rather than tying it to business outcomes. He identifies three recurring failure patterns (compliance trap, accountability gap, tool-first) and cites Wavestone/NewVantage survey data showing ethics policies and confidence remain flat. The programs that work, he argues, pick a specific outcome, embed governance into existing engineering workflows, and establish ownership with real consequences.

## Key Claims

- Gartner (Saul Judah) attributes failure to "a lack of a real or manufactured crisis" and center-out, command-and-control governance.
- Only 42.3% of organizations have well-established data and AI ethics policies, flat across three consecutive surveys.
- Only 15.9% of respondents believe their sector does enough on data and AI ethics.
- The share claiming a "data-driven organization" roughly doubled to ~43%, which researchers attribute to GenAI-driven pressure — the manufactured crisis.
- Durable programs treat governance controls as engineering deliverables: data contracts in PRs, Unity Catalog access in infrastructure-as-code, observability thresholds in the data product spec.
