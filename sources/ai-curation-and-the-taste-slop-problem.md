# AI Curation and the Taste Slop Problem — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/ai-curation-and-the-taste-slop-problem/ai-curation-and-the-taste-slop-problem.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles works through the "taste slop" idea raised on a Hard Fork culture-desk segment: what happens to culture when a handful of AI systems do all the recommending. Kyle Chayka's Filterworld already described platform-era flattening, but that homogenization happened through dozens of independently built systems that converged on similar incentives. Chatbot curation is different — a few general-purpose models would sit under every cultural category at once, consolidating the flattening into a single source.

## Key Claims

- Chayka's 2024 Filterworld argued algorithmic recommendation (Spotify, Netflix, Instagram) had flattened culture toward one global aesthetic, which he called AirSpace.
- Netflix has long said its recommendation engine drives roughly 80% of what people watch.
- Platform flattening was parallel: Spotify's model had no visibility into Netflix's or Instagram's, so convergence was an emergent property of similar economics, not a shared source.
- If ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and a few others become the interface for recommendations, distinct systems collapse to four or five foundation models with authority over the whole map.
- It is the difference between five industries hiring their own consultant and five industries hiring the same one.
- Whether taste slop becomes accurate depends on whether these models keep independent data and incentives or converge on similar architectures.

## Relevance

A culture-level essay on concentration risk in AI-mediated recommendation; connects to [AI Governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md) concerns about single-source failure.
