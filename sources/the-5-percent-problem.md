# The 5 Percent Problem — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-5-percent-problem/the-5-percent-problem.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara observes that enterprise AI investment is set to double this year while the share of companies reporting earnings impact has not moved since 2025 — most organizations are paying more to stay exactly where they started. He cites McKinsey and PwC survey data showing individual productivity gains that never reach the income statement, and argues the gap between what AI does for individuals and for organizations is the unnamed problem. The companies that break into the roughly 5% with measurable impact share a pattern that is not about model choice: they embed AI in high-volume workflows with clean before-and-after metrics, redesign roles rather than layering AI on top, and measure rigorously enough to know when it isn't working.

## Key Claims

- Enterprise AI investment is set to double this year, yet the share of companies saying AI moved earnings is unchanged since 2025.
- McKinsey's 2026 State of AI: 37% of organizations say AI contributed to EBIT, the same as last year while investment scales up.
- PwC's CEO survey: 56% of CEOs saw neither revenue growth nor cost reduction from AI in the past year; only 5–8% of enterprises show measurable impact at scale.
- Winners deploy AI in high-volume workflows with real process metrics (cycle times, claims denial rates, call resolution), not generic time savings.
- They redesign roles rather than layering AI on existing ones, and few governance conversations address measurement cadence and who owns the accountability number.
