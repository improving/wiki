# Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail - #1: Starting Without a Problem — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/starting-without-a-problem/starting-without-a-problem.md`

## Summary

Eight out of ten AI projects do not deliver the value their sponsors expected, and the root cause is misalignment rather than technology. When teams chase AI because it is fashionable rather than because it solves a meaningful problem, the work becomes innovation theater: slick demos with no adoption, no ROI, and no lasting impact. The fix is to anchor every initiative in a measurable business outcome before writing code.

## Key Claims

- "Standing up a model isn't the hard part"; the symptoms of failure are a slick demo, no adoption, and no metric moved six months later.
- Four causes: chasing trends over outcomes, vague goals ("innovation" instead of quantified improvement), misjudged readiness, and no workflow definition.
- Every initiative should begin with a one-sentence problem statement: "We're doing this to improve X by Y% for Z users."
- Tie AI to a business outcome with a dollar sign — saving time, increasing revenue, or reducing errors — and name the decision or workflow being changed.
- Constrain the first win to a shippable use case within 8–12 weeks, then expand.
- Require a one-page value hypothesis documenting problem, target metric, owner, and timeline; if it does not fit, it is not ready to build.
- Validate readiness via a problem-selection workshop and data readiness check before modeling, and assign a business owner to the outcome.
- Example: a stalled healthcare chatbot was reframed around reducing review cycle time in one workflow, achieving a 27% labor reduction within 90 days.
