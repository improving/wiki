# Inside CodeLaunch 2026 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/codelaunch-2026-travelspoken-hackathon-story/codelaunch-2026-travelspoken-hackathon-story.md`

## Summary

A first-person account of Improving's CodeLaunch 2026 hackathon, where a deliberately unconventional team built a TravelSpoken booking feature and finished the 20-hour marathon nearly three hours early. The author argues the result came from preparation — 8 to 10 hours of upfront tech-stack review and playbook refinement — and from picking people without preconceptions about what a hackathon team should look like.

## Key Claims

- CodeLaunch is a free, zero-equity startup accelerator that pairs early-stage founders with professional software development teams over two 10-hour days, with no cost and no strings to the founders.
- Improving does not just support CodeLaunch, it produces it; the Professional Coder League (PCL) is a roster made entirely of volunteer Improvers.
- Captain Tim Dickey deliberately picked people without practical hackathon experience: Jake Stephens, Mason Steeger, and his daughter Matea as UX designer; the team named itself D-Underdog (Dallas Underdogs).
- The team invested roughly 8 to 10 hours beforehand reviewing the tech stack, speaking with the startup partner, and refining a pre-hackathon checklist and playbook.
- The feature was described as a mashup of Pinterest and a baseball card: see a destination, flip it over to view booking details and book — live on TravelSpoken's site.
- The team recorded a demo video for founders Eni and Chris even though it wasn't required, so Eni could pitch from the recording before she arrived.
- In-person collaboration mattered: better energy, visible whiteboarding, better paired programming, and smoother demos.
