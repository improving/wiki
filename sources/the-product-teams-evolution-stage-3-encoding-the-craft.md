# The Product Team's Evolution, Stage 3: Encoding the Craft — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-3-encoding-the-craft/the-product-teams-evolution-stage-3-encoding-the-craft.md`

## Summary

Second in the product-team series, covering Stage 3, where practitioners write down how they actually work and encode their standards into personal task agents. The day shifts from producing artifacts to directing and reviewing them, and each role gains a new title — requirements architect, backlog architect, experience director, strategy director. Encoding a standard makes it apply to everything the agent produces, so the bar on the practitioner's judgment rises with their reach. The stage's ceiling is that everything remains personal: a team of five owners still holds five definitions of good.

## Key Claims

- A skill or task agent is a written job description for a unit of work; writing it forces tacit standards to become explicit and shareable.
- Stage 3 health is measured by agent-output acceptance in the low nineties as a percentage — a thermometer, not a target to maximize.
- Once a standard is encoded it applies to everything the agent produces, so a fuzzy opinion that was survivable on one artifact becomes expensive.
- Agents fail differently from people: a story agent can handle a complex epic and then botch a trivial one with total confidence.
- The stage's ceiling is that agents stay personal and review stays a human act performed one artifact at a time.
