# I Don't Know — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/i-dont-know/i-dont-know.md`

## Summary

In this brief early post, O'Hara contrasts saying "I don't know" with inventing an answer. He concedes that admitting ignorance feels like an admission of a shortcoming, but argues the alternative is worse: fabricating a response conveys the same lack of knowledge while adding a lie. A gap in knowledge can be remedied, whereas an unwillingness to admit reality is fundamental and hard to fix. He adds that the other person usually already knows, and simply saying "I don't know" lets both parties move forward.

## Key Claims

- "I don't know" is an admission of a lack of knowledge or understanding, and it is hard to say.
- Fabricating an answer signals the same lack but adds a lie about reality.
- We are all inadequate more often than we like to admit; the difference is choosing to ignore reality.
- A knowledge gap is fixable; dishonesty about reality is intrinsic and hard to remedy.
- Your conversation partner will detect the lie, even if they are too polite to say so.

## Relevance

An early statement of [Intellectual Humility](/concepts/intellectual-humility.md) and honesty about the limits of one's knowledge.
