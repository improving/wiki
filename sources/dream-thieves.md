# Dream Thieves — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/dream-thieves/dream-thieves.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that dreams get stolen — by our upbringing, by things said to us by family and friends, and by what happened to us when we were young — but that it is our response to those things, not the things themselves, that does the stealing. Because "we are what we think about," intentional effort around our thinking lets us shift course. He points to his brother Shaun as someone who never let his dreams be stolen, and urges control of the inner voice even while wrestling with impostor syndrome.

## Key Claims

- Dreams can be stolen by situation, by words from parents, family, or friends, and by childhood events.
- The thief is our response to those things, not the things themselves.
- "We are what we think about," so we can shift ourselves through intentional effort around our thinking.
- Everyone wrestles with impostor syndrome; what matters is whether you let it win.
- His brother Shaun is held up as an example of someone who never let his dreams be stolen.
