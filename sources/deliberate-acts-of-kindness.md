# Deliberate Acts of Kindness — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/deliberate-acts-of-kindness/deliberate-acts-of-kindness.md`

## Summary

O'Hara pushes back on the popular "random acts of kindness" framing, preferring kindness that is deliberate and cultivated rather than opportunistic. He observes that the people we are least kind to are usually those closest to us — family and co-workers who absorb our bad days. He closes with a small, concrete challenge to notice and name the contributions of people we interact with daily.

## Key Claims

- Being kind should be a cultivated, other-focused mindset, not a random or opportunistic gesture.
- The people we are typically least kind to are those closest: family and co-workers.
- Small deliberate gestures matter — learn someone's name and use it, say something kind about their work.
- Telling people what their efforts mean to you is what actually matters.
- Random kindness still beats no kindness at all.
