# It's Not You, It's Me — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/its-not-you-its-me/its-not-you-its-me.md`

## Summary

O'Hara challenges the assumption that our reactions are a direct response to someone else's behavior. Instead, he argues, our responses often shape how others behave toward us through learned behavior that develops over repeated interactions. If a colleague keeps interrupting and you never address it, you may be signaling that the behavior is acceptable. Rather than trying to change the other person directly, change how you respond — meeting a flaky friend with understanding, or a critical partner with openness — and their behavior may shift. The upshot is empowerment: the power to change a dynamic starts with you.

## Key Claims

- Reactions are not simply caused by others' behavior; our responses also shape theirs.
- Unaddressed behavior — like repeated interruptions — teaches others that it is acceptable.
- Learned behavior evolves through our interactions and experiences with one another.
- Changing your own response can influence the other person's actions without confronting them.
- Reflection prompt: "What would happen if I reacted differently?" — the change starts with you.
