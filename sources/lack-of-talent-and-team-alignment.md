# Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail #4: Lack of Talent and Team Alignment — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/lack-of-talent-and-team-alignment/lack-of-talent-and-team-alignment.md`

## Summary

Many AI projects stall because the knowledge lives in one person's head. A single talented specialist prototypes something clever, but without alignment, documentation, or ownership the solution is brittle and impossible to scale — the "AI wizard in the corner" pattern. The post argues AI must be run as a team discipline, with overlapping expertise, a named business owner, and executive sponsorship for change management.

## Key Claims

- The failure quote: "one specialist… built something clever. Nobody really understands it or owns it… a brittle solution, stalled adoption, and really no path to a broad-scale production release."
- Four causes: skill gaps across siloed functions, no business ownership, poor communication between tech and strategy, and limited change management.
- Build squads combining data engineers, ML and GenAI engineers, domain SMEs, and application developers, each understanding how their part drives the business outcome.
- Every initiative needs a named business owner accountable for success metrics, ROI, and adoption: "no owner means no project."
- Executive sponsors must define why now and champion adoption; host weekly business reviews tied to outcomes rather than technical demos.
- Maintain shared visibility — documentation, runbooks, dashboards — so no knowledge is trapped in one head.
- Example: for a Microsoft Copilot field-services deployment, SMEs joined ride-alongs to inform prompts and tooling and business owners attended backlog sessions, so adoption soared.
