# The Activity Trap — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-activity-trap/the-activity-trap.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that most AI programs mistake activity for outcomes. He cites that 61% of leaders feel more pressure to prove AI ROI than a year ago while only 23% can, and describes clients who have AI budgets, dashboards, and steering committees but no P&L line that moved — only a rising cost line. Measuring activity is easy and measuring outcomes is hard, so organizations avoid the uncomfortable conversation about what the spend was supposed to produce and who owns it. The programs that land real ROI, he says, tie each initiative to a specific outcome with a dollar figure and a single named owner, asking the question before building rather than rationalizing afterward.

## Key Claims

- 61% of leaders say they face more pressure to prove AI ROI than a year ago; only 23% can.
- Demos, pilots, and dashboards tracking chatbot opens are activity, not ROI — the two are measured interchangeably but are not the same.
- Organizations drift into the trap because measuring activity is easy and measuring outcomes is hard.
- Attempting to "defuzz" ROI after results arrive is post-hoc rationalization, not a route to true value.
- Successful programs tie each initiative to a specific outcome with a dollar figure and one named owner, not a committee, and verify it delivered.
- 2026 is the year the "we're still learning" answer stops working in board conversations.
