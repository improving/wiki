# Lifebook — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/lifebook/lifebook.md`

## Summary

O'Hara offers a high-level look at "Lifebook," a tool used in the IBP, which he describes as an approach to examining the future, clarifying goals, and aligning present focus toward those ends. It starts from the premise that life is a complex mix of aspects that cannot be split into "work" and "personal" — it is all one connected self. The program examines eight areas and then a roughly 20-year horizon, writing as if looking back across the span of a life, and he offers to walk readers through the remainder.

## Key Claims

- Life cannot be divided into simply "work" and "personal"; it is all connected and it all matters.
- Lifebook focuses on eight aspects: Personal, Career, Health, Financial, Social, Family, Spiritual, and Love.
- The exercise works on a long-term horizon, typically 20 years.
- Writing as if 20 years have passed and looking back unlocks creativity, freeing exploration of what might be rather than only what is possible.
- More detail — images, descriptions — makes the exercise more effective, and clarifying what we want yields perspective on what to do now.

## Relevance

A personal-development and goal-setting framework: it links purpose, time horizon, and work-life integration, and is relevant to the author's recurring purpose theme.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. A promotional, high-level overview with no evidence; it names the eight areas and method but leaves the actual exercises behind an offer to walk through them.
