# Emotions - follow your passion and feelings is poor advice — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/emotions/emotions.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that emotions are "great servants but poor masters." Being emotional is not a weakness — being unemotional is worse — and leaning into feelings is healthy, an idea he credits to Brené Brown. But feelings are not identity and should not direct action: they should be recognized and felt, not obeyed. This, he says, is why "follow your passion" fails: it puts emotions ahead of action, whereas in reality working hard, becoming good at something, and thereby coming to love it is the true order of operations.

## Key Claims

- Emotions are great servants but poor masters.
- Being unemotional is worse than being emotional; having and leaning into feelings is healthy.
- Feelings are not who we are and should not guide our actions.
- Most people can name only about four feelings — a sign that emotional recognition is underdeveloped.
- "Follow your passion" fails because it inverts cause and effect: competence at something produces passion, not the reverse.

## Relevance

A compact statement of [emotional regulation](/concepts/emotional-regulation.md) and a direct rebuttal of the [follow your passion myth](/concepts/follow-your-passion-myth.md).

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Short and opinionated, citing Brené Brown and a secondhand observation about naming feelings; argumentative rather than evidence-based.
