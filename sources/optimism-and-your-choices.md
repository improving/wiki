# Optimism and Your Choices — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/optimism-and-your-choices/optimism-and-your-choices.md`

## Summary

A short, quotable post arguing that optimism is not a personality trait but a precondition for action. Opening with a Noam Chomsky line — that unless you believe the future can be better, you are unlikely to take responsibility for making it so — the author treats optimism as a requirement for living and for building a better world for our children. He urges leaning into change, regardless of the pain or effort, on the grounds that it is ultimately worth it.

## Key Claims

- Optimism is a requirement for living and for making a better world, not an optional disposition.
- Belief that the future can be better is what makes people step up and take responsibility for changing it.
- Change is good, and one should lean into it despite the pain or effort involved.
- The effort is worth it in the end.

## Relevance

A concise statement of [Optimism](/concepts/optimism.md) as a practical stance; pairs with the author's later reflections on pessimism and incentives.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Very short and motivational rather than argued; built around a single quotation and a call to action.
