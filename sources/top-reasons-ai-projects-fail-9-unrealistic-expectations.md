# Top Reasons AI Projects Fail #9: Unrealistic Expectations — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/top-reasons-ai-projects-fail-9-unrealistic-expectations/top-reasons-ai-projects-fail-9-unrealistic-expectations.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues that unrealistic expectations sink AI initiatives faster than technical problems: when leaders expect instant, "sci-fi" breakthroughs, teams deliver only incremental step changes, morale sours, and funding dries up. The failure is structural, arising from mismatched definitions of success, poor visibility into the journey, impatience for ROI, and no baseline for comparison. The fix is expectation management — transparent roadmaps, before/after baselines, and visualizing cumulative value — so genuine progress is not mistaken for under-delivery.

## Key Claims

- Few things sink an AI initiative faster than over-promising; progress usually arrives as a sequence of small, compounding improvements.
- Leaders, customers, and even teams sometimes want "sci-fi," while teams deliver step changes and incremental improvements.
- When early results fail to wow, enthusiasm fades, budgets shrink, and teams building real value lose momentum.
- Four causes: lack of shared definitions of success, poor visibility into the journey, impatience for visible ROI, and no context for comparison.
- Without expectation management, genuine progress looks like under-delivery.
- Prevent it with a transparent roadmap linked to KPIs, before/after baselines, and value burnups showing cumulative savings.
- Highlight short-term wins to fund long-term goals: one Improving project began with a 12% cycle-time reduction that freed capacity and justified phase-two funding.
- Treat AI progress as a compounding process, make progress visible, visualize cumulative value, and keep communication continuous.
