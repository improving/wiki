# Social Capital in the Age of Autonomous Agents — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/social-capital-in-the-age-of-autonomous-agents/social-capital-in-the-age-of-autonomous-agents.md`

## Summary

Extends the author's Corporate Currency framework — the informal economy of human trust — to how organizations relate to autonomous AI. The core claim is that organizational confidence in an AI system is its [social capital](/concepts/social-capital.md): earned through consistent, reliable behavior within a defined scope and depleted rapidly by high-profile failures. Most organizations violate the framework in the same direction, extending too much trust too fast without the track record that should precede it. The governance infrastructure recommended elsewhere — audit trails, boundary enforcement, quality gates, human approval — is recast as the machinery that keeps the trust balance from depleting.

## Key Claims

- Trust should be extended in proportion to accumulated social capital; the 8-Stage [AI Trust Evolution](/concepts/ai-trust-evolution.md) is a framework for earning it incrementally.
- Broken commitments are the most expensive action: an autonomous system failing visibly depletes trust faster than the original commitment was worth.
- Without an [audit trail](/concepts/audit-trail.md), failure is invisible until it produces consequences; the social-capital hit comes from the consequences, not the failure.
- Without [boundary enforcement](/concepts/boundary-enforcement.md), an out-of-scope action signals the system cannot be trusted to stay in its lane.
- Announcing "AI transformation" while still early-stage applies the [look-at-me tax](/concepts/look-at-me-tax.md), drawing on credibility not yet accumulated.

## Relevance

Informs [social capital](/concepts/social-capital.md), [AI Trust Evolution](/concepts/ai-trust-evolution.md), [boundary enforcement](/concepts/boundary-enforcement.md), and [audit trail](/concepts/audit-trail.md) in AI governance.
