# Software Craftsmanship Is Not a Job Title — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/software-craftsmanship-is-not-a-job-title/software-craftsmanship-is-not-a-job-title.md`

## Summary

Drawing on a question the author has asked since 2013, the post argues that [software craftsmanship](/concepts/software-craftsmanship.md) is an identity defined by the standards a developer refuses to abandon under pressure, not by a tool list or a title. It grounds the idea in the medieval guild masterpiece, Richard Sennett's "The Craftsman," and the 2009 Software Craftsmanship Manifesto, then frames professional integrity as a moral dimension of technical decisions: shipping untested code because a deadline moved is a choice with compounding cost, not neutral pragmatism. It closes by arguing craftsmanship must be a team culture and community norm, because a standard held by one developer is a career risk.

## Key Claims

- A craftsman is defined by the standards they refuse to abandon, not their testing framework or CI configuration.
- "Be professional and unflinching... be prepared to say no to people who can fire you" — the commitment is tested when authority says standards are negotiable.
- Friends don't let friends code without tests, requirements, or [acceptance criteria](/concepts/acceptance-criteria.md); letting a colleague cut corners is a failure of [peer accountability](/concepts/peer-accountability.md).
- Automation is a professional obligation; skipping it accumulates a debt that charges interest.
- Dan North's critique — craft language risks artisan self-indulgence — misses the economic core: untested code and manual deploys slow delivery and raise defect rates.

## Relevance

Informs [software craftsmanship](/concepts/software-craftsmanship.md), [peer accountability](/concepts/peer-accountability.md), and the [woodshop-to-factory transition](/concepts/woodshop-to-factory-transition.md) the author applies to AI adoption.

## Quality

Practitioner blog post by Devlin Liles. A values essay rather than evidence; its strength is a coherent, decade-consistent position, its caveat that the say-no posture is acknowledged as harder for those without leverage.
