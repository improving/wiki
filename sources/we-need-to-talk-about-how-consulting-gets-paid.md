# We Need to Talk About How Consulting Gets Paid — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/we-need-to-talk-about-how-consulting-gets-paid/we-need-to-talk-about-how-consulting-gets-paid.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that agentic AI has finally broken the hourly billing model that has been challenged on and off for most of his career, and that most consulting firms — including ones he respects — are unprepared. He reads a Bloomberg pricing forecast as a consulting delivery story in disguise: once clients pay for outcomes in one part of their business, they will ask why every other vendor relationship, including consulting, doesn't work the same way.

## Key Claims

- Bloomberg estimates subscription pricing falls from 60% of software deals to 30% this decade, while outcome-based pricing rises from 10% to 60%.
- If a client's AI agent resolves support tickets alone, nobody pays a human for the hours spent babysitting the queue.
- Hourly billing is safer and more predictable for both sides, which is exactly why clients question it once an agent does in six minutes what took a team a week.
- A pricing model that can't explain that gap will be explained by the client — usually at renewal.
- Firms that adopt outcome-based delivery now, before being forced, will own the next decade.
