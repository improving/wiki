# Before You Open Your Mouth — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/before-you-open-your-mouth/before-you-open-your-mouth.md`

## Summary

A seller accepted the lower offer, not from bad math, but because the other buyer felt safer. Drawing on Kaustubh Deo's newsletter (Issue 135) about the "punchable face" problem in small-business acquisitions, the author argues the dynamic is really about trust: people decide whether to trust you before you have said anything meaningful, reading your face, energy, and whether you seem like someone who will stay and show up or someone who will extract and exit. The same failure shows up in consulting when a client reads a brilliant consultant as "here to bill hours." Because misalignment between words and true intent is detectable, the only real answer is actually caring about the business, the people, and the outcome — and that care shows before you speak.

## Key Claims

- A seller picked the lower offer because the other buyer felt safer.
- People judge trustworthiness before substantive speech, from face and energy.
- The "punchable face" problem cuts both ways: projecting PE-fund energy into a room of builders loses the deal, just as a consultant read as a biller loses a discovery call.
- You cannot fake this; people detect misalignment between what you say and what you think.
- Genuine care changes how you ask questions and what you notice, so trust is built by intention, not by the pitch.

## Relevance

An argument about [trust building](/concepts/trust-building.md) in consulting relationships, where presence precedes and outweighs the pitch.
