# When Are We Doing Hard Things? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/hard-things/hard-things.md`

## Summary

O'Hara builds a short reflection around a line he found in his notes — "We don't fall asleep in the fire and rain but rather we doze in the easy sunshine" — whose origin he cannot confirm. It aligns for him with the ideas in *Deep Change*: absent a severe triggering event, we rarely look to fundamentally shift ourselves or our goals toward a bigger future. It is human nature to believe we can succeed simply by showing up, doing work, and claiming victory, but that is not true change and not a hard thing. Hard things hurt, stretch us, and force us to grow; they are hard to attempt when we are comfortable or feel we are already achieving.

## Key Claims

- "We don't fall asleep in the fire and rain but rather we doze in the easy sunshine."
- Outside of severe impacting events, people rarely seek fundamental change toward a bigger future.
- Believing that showing up, doing work, and claiming victory equals change is a mistake.
- Hard things hurt, stretch us, and force growth, or remake us into something new and better.
- Comfort and a feeling of achievement are the main obstacles to doing hard things.
- "Don't mess with what ain't broke" is exactly the instinct that should be challenged: experiment, then drive toward a different future.
