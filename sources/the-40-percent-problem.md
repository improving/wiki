# The 40 Percent Problem — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-40-percent-problem/the-40-percent-problem.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara takes Gartner's projection that 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 and argues the failures are self-inflicted. In his experience, projects die not because the AI didn't work but because nobody defined what "working" meant before starting, so six months in no one can answer finance's question about value. O'Hara notes that Gartner's three cited causes — escalating costs, unclear business value, and governance gaps — are all answerable before a single sprint begins through a project viability review covering scope, success metrics, a governance model, and a cost ceiling. He frames the cancellation rate as an opportunity for firms that sell structured decision-making.

## Key Claims

- Gartner projects 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled — not paused — by the end of 2027.
- The cited causes (escalating costs, unclear business value, governance gaps) are all pre-launch questions, answerable before work starts.
- A project viability review — scope, success metrics, governance model, and cost ceiling — determines whether the glamorous work survives.
- Each canceled project represents an organization without a disciplined go/no-go process, which is a solvable problem.
- The test before approving an initiative: who owns the kill criteria? If no one can say in a sentence, it's a project design problem, not merely a governance one.
