# Drucker's Blueprint: Product Owner to Effective Executive Pt. 3 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/druckers-blueprint-product-owner-to-effective-executive-pt-3/druckers-blueprint-product-owner-to-effective-executive-pt-3.md`

## Summary

Closing the series, this post argues that the only way to know whether someone can lead is to have them lead — and that the Product Owner role in an agile transformation supplies exactly that hands-on practice. It uses the chef analogy and Toyota's job rotations to make the case.

## Key Claims

- Drucker's driving question is how to train the next generation of effective executives, who must work through others and integrate local optimizations into a systemic whole.
- Chefs learn by cooking, not by reading recipes; likewise executives need real practice before we can trust their skills.
- Practice should occur in unfamiliar situations: Toyota moves potential leaders between plant areas (e.g., from assembly to the paint shop).
- An agile transformation gives the same opportunity — Product Owners codify local optimizations into new business processes and work through their teams.
- Approached this way, the leadership practice yields more lasting value than any software delivered.
