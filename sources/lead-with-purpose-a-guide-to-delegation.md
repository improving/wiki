# Lead with Purpose: A Guide to Delegation — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/lead-with-purpose-a-guide-to-delegation/lead-with-purpose-a-guide-to-delegation.md`

## Summary

A newly promoted leader cannot absorb all the added work, so Randall Dunigan argues for delegating deliberately rather than through heroics or bare prioritization. He offers three tools for choosing what to hand off — reversibility (one-way vs two-way doors), risk level (scraped knees vs broken bones), and Management 3.0's delegation poker — plus the motivation payoff of autonomy, mastery, and purpose.

## Key Claims

- Working longer hours or only prioritizing does not scale for a promoted leader; it leads to burnout or becoming a bottleneck.
- One-way doors are hard to reverse and riskier to delegate; two-way doors are easily reversed, lowering the risk of handing work to less experienced people.
- The "scraped knees vs broken bones" test (credited to Improving trainer Blake McMillan) asks whether a teammate's failure is a minor lesson or serious injury.
- Management 3.0's delegation poker rates activities from one (no delegation, tell them what to do) to seven (full delegation) across factors like ability, knowledge, risk, accountability, and frequency.
- Doing delegation poker as a group surfaces misconceptions and makes expectations clear.
- Delegation yields breathing room, more team autonomy, reduced risk, and a way to train future leaders.
- Dan Pink's *Drive*: people are motivated by autonomy, mastery, and purpose.
