# Building Trust By Listening First — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/building-trust-by-listening-first/building-trust-by-listening-first.md`

## Summary

Mark Kovacevich argues that active listening — presented as "Listen First" in The Speed of Trust — is a conscious, deliberate practice to seek understanding while withholding judgment. Though it looks simple, it is hard in a world of notifications and reactive social media, yet it builds trust and feeds a virtuous cycle.

## Key Claims

- Active Listening is a deliberate practice designed to seek understanding while withholding judgment.
- Constant information and social media encourage snap judgments and instant reactions that work directly against listening.
- Listen First yields three benefits: people feel heard and valued, you gain a more detailed and nuanced understanding, and it builds trust.
- Emerging leaders who observe the behavior, consciously or subconsciously, pass it on — a virtuous cycle.
- Techniques: listen without judgment, listen without preparing to respond (respond with clarifying questions), and listen neutrally.
- Because time is scarce, situational awareness is needed to decide when to deploy Listen First.
- Listening first supports other trust behaviors — extending trust, showing respect, creating transparency, talking straight, and righting wrongs.
