# Thinking about Death — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/thinking-about-death/thinking-about-death.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that thinking about death, though it can seem morbid, is clarifying. The trigger was realizing his parents are getting older, which pulled mortality into the present. Working from cited life-expectancy tables, he notes that a typical 70-year-old American has roughly 13–15 more years (male) or 15–17 (female), and that seeing parents only once a year yields on the order of 15 remaining interactions. That arithmetic pushes him to ask what those last visits should look like and to notice how the "tyranny of the schedule" crowds out the quality of relationships. He acknowledges his own families live in DFW so he sees them far more often, but still resolves to be more intentional.

## Key Claims

- A 70-year-old American has about 13–15 years left if male and 15–17 if female, per the cited New York DOH and SSA tables.
- At one visit a year, that amounts to roughly 15 more interactions with a parent.
- Contemplating finitude redirects attention to the present and to what one wants from the time remaining.
- The demands of the schedule crowd out the quality of our relationships.
- He sees his DFW-based families far more often than the arithmetic assumes, yet still commits to greater intentionality.
