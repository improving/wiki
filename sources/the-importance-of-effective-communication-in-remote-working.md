# The Importance of Effective Communication in Remote Working — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-importance-of-effective-communication-in-remote-working/the-importance-of-effective-communication-in-remote-working.md`

## Summary

A team misses a deadline even though everyone "did their part" — the failure is communication, not effort. Drawing on Albert Mehrabian's model, the post argues remote work strips out most non-verbal signal, so alignment must be built deliberately. Patrick Lencioni's five dysfunctions explain the root cause: absent trust, people stay silent rather than admit confusion or disagree.

## Key Claims

- Mehrabian's model attributes roughly 55% of communication to body language and 38% to tone, leaving only 7% to words — though the post notes context matters and words remain essential in written and technical settings.
- Virtual work loses that non-verbal richness, so people lean on written and verbal clarity alone, which isn't always enough for alignment.
- Lencioni's model holds that the root challenge is usually a trust gap, not lack of effort — the confidence to admit uncertainty or ask questions that feel obvious.
- Fear of conflict makes even well-intentioned disagreement feel risky, so people nod along instead of questioning.
- Agreement is not alignment: alignment is active and depends on shared interpretation, not on hearing the same words.
- Remedies: encourage curiosity over certainty, model vulnerability, normalize "can we double-check we're on the same page?", and invite healthy tension.
