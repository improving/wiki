# 5 Ways to Design Better — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/5-ways-to-design-better/5-ways-to-design-better.md`

## Summary

UX designer Meagan Thompson argues that blindly following "best practices" makes designs stale, and that changing your mindset is how you design better. She offers five tips: let go of your ego, ask questions correctly, think about UX in all aspects of life, keep learning, and break the rules on purpose.

## Key Claims

- If you wholeheartedly disagree with user feedback, you are either asking the wrong questions or need to ask more.
- Avoid leading and close-ended questions, and resist putting your own biases on users.
- Dig past surface requests to the underlying "why" — the Henry Ford "faster horses" anecdote illustrates the danger of staying superficial.
- Treat everyday surroundings as UX research: try new apps, airport kiosks, and observe how people navigate public spaces.
- Technology and social shifts (smartphones, the pandemic) change how people interact, so designing on 15-year-old context produces a 15-year-old app.
- Use established building blocks for predictability, then break rules — adjust shades, vary grid columns, offset elements — to make designs memorable.
