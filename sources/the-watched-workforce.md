# The Watched Workforce — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-watched-workforce/the-watched-workforce.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues that enterprise AI monitoring systems often unknowingly rebuild Bentham's panopticon: a mechanism whose power comes not from watching people but from making them believe they might be watched. Because employees change behavior when they merely might be flagged, broadly scoped, opaque monitoring suppresses exactly the organizational signals — raised concerns, free brainstorming, honest feedback — that risk management depends on. He closes with governance guidance: narrow the scope, maximize transparency, and measure second-order effects.

## Key Claims

- As of 2025, 78% of US employers use online monitoring tools and 61% deploy AI-powered analytics to measure employee productivity or behavior (ExpressVPN survey of 1,500 employers).
- A compliance system activates on violations; a panoptical system changes behavior before any violation occurs — the stated purpose and actual effect run on different mechanisms.
- Research consistently finds monitored environments produce more self-censorship, less creativity, reduced dissent, and higher cognitive load from continuous privacy management.
- A highly accurate monitoring system can suppress more behavior than a less accurate one, because perceived certainty of detection amplifies the panoptical effect.
- Broad, unbounded "analyze all communications for risk signals" scopes modify behavior orders of magnitude more than narrow, enumerated ones.

## Relevance

Frames [AI governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md) questions for workplace monitoring, emphasizing second-order behavioral effects over detection accuracy.
