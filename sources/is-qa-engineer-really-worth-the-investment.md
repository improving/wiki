# Is QA Really Worth the Investment? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/is-qa-engineer-really-worth-the-investment/is-qa-engineer-really-worth-the-investment.md`

## Summary

Responding to the common question of whether QA is worth the cost, the author argues QA is risk mitigation, user trust, and long-term savings rather than an expense. Skipping it hurts user experience, reputation, and developer morale as bugs surface in production. She reframes the question around the cost of lost customers and hotfix delays, and explains how to keep QA valuable: automate repetitive work, integrate it with developers from day one, and align it with business goals.

## Key Claims

- The case against QA: developers can test their own code, automation replaces manual testing, and post-release fixes are normal.
- Skipping QA harms user experience, damages reputation publicly, and causes developer burnout through late bug fixes.
- Reframe the question: measure the cost of losing a customer to a bug, a hotfix delay, and lost brand trust.
- QA becomes a sinkhole when it is overly manual and repetitive, siloed from developers, or treated as a formality.
- Fix it by automating repetitive checks (e.g., Selenium and Cypress), collaborating from day one, and testing what matters to users.
- QA is framed as a strategic safeguard and an investment in trust, efficiency, and reputation.
