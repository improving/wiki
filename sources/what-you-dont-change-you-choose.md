# What You Don't Change, You Choose — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/what-you-dont-change-you-choose/what-you-dont-change-you-choose.md`

## Summary

Uses a remark from the author's wife — "what you don't change, you choose" — to argue that tolerating the status quo is itself a decision. The example is non-cross-functional teams: by not fixing dependencies and coordination waste, teams are choosing delays, and the fix is to start small by embedding missing skills.

## Key Claims

- "What you don't change, you choose"; Rush's "If you choose not to decide, you still have made a choice" (Neil Peart) makes the same point.
- Scrum expects cross-functional teams with all the skills to take an idea from spark to ship — designers, developers, and testers together — yielding speed and ownership.
- Non-cross-functional teams create delays waiting on other teams, dependencies tied to others' priorities, coordination overhead, clashing priorities, and rising complexity.
- Teams often live with these pain points for years not because they like chaos but because they haven't chosen to change.
- The move: start small — invite a UX expert part-time, embed an Infrastructure/DevOps specialist for a few sprints — and focus on growing the capability in the team, not just borrowing it.
- Experiment, reflect, and tweak; a perfect cross-functional team isn't needed overnight, but choosing the status quo must stop.
