# So Mad — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/so-mad/so-mad.md`

## Summary

O'Hara reflects on a Dallas Willard quote — that nothing can be done with anger that cannot be done better without it — and connects it to his earlier post on responding to fear. He argues emotional responses wreck effectiveness: they color reasoning, cloud judgment, spin worst-case scenarios, and make us feel justified in poor decisions. Since emotional situations are inevitable, he asks how to recognize being caught in emotion, and answers with his own personal checklist across three channels: physical signals (dry mouth, sweating, flushed neck), word choice (overusing "I," speaking in absolutes), and non-verbals (interrupting, over-simplifying, closed posture). He closes by asking readers what their own cues are.

## Key Claims

- Anger adds nothing that a calmer state cannot do better (Dallas Willard).
- Emotions color reasoning, cloud judgment, and make us feel justified.
- Emotional situations are inevitable, so self-recognition is the practical skill.
- Physical cues include dry mouth, sweating, and a flushing neck.
- Verbal cues include overusing "I" and speaking in absolutes; non-verbal cues include interrupting and closed posture.
