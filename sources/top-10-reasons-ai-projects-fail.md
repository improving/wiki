# Top 10 Reasons AI Projects Fail — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/top-10-reasons-ai-projects-fail/top-10-reasons-ai-projects-fail.md`

## Summary

Eight out of ten AI projects never deliver their intended value, and the cause is rarely the technology — it is management, measurement, and mindset. The article distills Improving's experience into ten recurring failure patterns, from starting without a problem to ignoring ethics, and consolidates them into five delivery disciplines that move organizations into the twenty percent that succeed.

## Key Claims

- The ten failure patterns: starting without a problem; undefined success metrics; garbage in, garbage out; lack of talent and team alignment; reinventing the wheel; overambitious scope; poor integration planning; no ownership or maintenance; unrealistic expectations; and ignoring ethics and compliance.
- Fixes include a problem-selection workshop and one-page value hypothesis, KPIs locked before sprint one with telemetry, data readiness checks and CI quality gates, cross-functional squads with a named business owner, a buy-build-blend approach, and 12-week thin vertical slices.
- Design for production from day one: authentication, observability, drift detection, cost controls, human-in-the-loop review, and audit logs.
- Own the lifecycle: a product owner, MLOps/GenAIOps telemetry and alerts, and fixed prompt-refresh and retraining cadence.
- The five disciplines are start with business value, build small and scale fast, design for production, own the lifecycle, and govern with purpose.
- Supporting examples: an underwriting assistant cut review time 25%; a manufacturing model was constrained to SKUs with 18+ months of data; a legal-tech project replaced a year of custom work with commercial LLMs plus SharePoint retrieval; a contact-center assistant reached 90% adoption in the first month; a 12% cycle-time reduction doubled freed capacity over two quarters.
- A healthcare case study reframed a stalled chatbot around one workflow, handling 80% of repetitive cases and cutting labor hours 27%.
