# From Doing to Defining, From Coding to Orchestrating — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/from-doing-to-defining-from-coding-to-orchestrating/from-doing-to-defining-from-coding-to-orchestrating.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles unpacks the POST-AI framework's claim that AI shifts work "from doing to defining, from coding to orchestrating," tracing its pedigree to Licklider's 1960 "Man-Computer Symbiosis." He clears away three misreadings: coding still matters, doing is not deprecated, and AI does not replace the human — the word "synergy" carries the weight. The orchestrator's irreducible contribution is intent, which he equates with Peter Naur's 1985 notion of the theory builders hold about a system.

## Key Claims

- You orchestrate from competence; the developer who cannot read code, judge its quality, and reason about its architecture will not effectively orchestrate AI that produces code.
- Every prior abstraction shift (FORTRAN and after) moved work up a level without excusing ignorance of the level below.
- At Stage 8 of the trust evolution the human contribution is intent — goals, constraints, guardrails, quality minimums — which AI cannot define for itself.
- Three objections are conceded in part: the junior pipeline problem, leaky abstractions (the leak rate is what the trust evolution measures), and the historical record of CASE/4GL/low-code promises.
