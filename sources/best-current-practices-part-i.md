# Best Current Practices, Part 1 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/best-current-practices-part-i/best-current-practices-part-i.md`

## Summary

Daniel Scheufler argues there is no static "best practice": practices are time-bound and carry implicit context, so they are better called "best current practices" and should be inspected for their assumptions before adoption. Follow them deliberately as trade-offs, not dictates.

## Key Claims

- Practices change as tools and constraints change — the monolith was once a best practice.
- The tools available shape the solutions we consider: "to a man with only a hammer, the world is full of nails".
- Best practices embed unspoken assumptions: microservices assume reuse, maintenance capacity, and a mature ops organization.
- Netflix's Chaos Monkey only makes sense for organizations primed to understand and respond to the failures it induces.
- Treat a practice as a decision rule: ask when it applies, when it doesn't, when it gives way, and what it commits you to.
- Get choices and rationales evaluated by respected peers before implementing them.
