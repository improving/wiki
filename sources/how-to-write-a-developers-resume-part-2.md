# How to Write a Developer Resume, Part 2 — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-to-write-a-developers-resume-part-2/how-to-write-a-developers-resume-part-2.md`

## Summary

Part 2 of a resume-writing series, by Improving Nearshore's talent acquisition manager, finishes the remaining resume sections and answers common questions. It covers certifications, education, technical skills, links, and how to handle length, short stints, and seniority.

## Key Claims

- A strong resume has seven parts: summary, experience, certifications and awards, education, technical skills, contact information, and links such as LinkedIn, GitHub, Hackerrank, and portfolio.
- Include only the most significant certifications and awards, and only if relevant to the role.
- Omit elementary and high school; list only degrees (bachelor's, master's, doctorate) with the field and school name.
- Group technical skills by stack and spread them across the resume rather than piling them into one section.
- Always include an updated LinkedIn URL, since recruiters use it as a primary sourcing tool.
- For 10+ years of experience, keep the resume to two pages and add a disclaimer offering more detail on request; omit months for roles under a year to avoid looking like a job hopper.
- Include seniority only for senior developer or senior software engineer roles; recruiters assess mid and junior seniority in interviews.
