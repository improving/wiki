# Optimal Stopping (Secretary Problem) & Decision Making — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/optimal-stopping/optimal-stopping.md`

## Summary

The author connects leadership to the Optimal Stopping problem, also known as the Secretary Problem. His argument is that successful leaders are better, intuitively, at recognizing the point at which a decision can be made, rather than endlessly analyzing every option in search of the single best choice. He contends that a leader's style is bound up with how they process risk and choices amid unknowns, and that comfort with a certain level of uncertainty is what produces a path forward.

## Key Claims

- Leaders who succeed are intuitively better at the Optimal Stopping (Secretary) problem.
- Decisions rarely carry the significance we attach to them — they are typically not fatal.
- The gravity we assign to decisions often surpasses their actual impact.
- A leader's style is intricately linked to how they manage risk and evaluate choices amid unknown variables.
- Effectiveness comes from striking a balance between deciding when adequately informed and exhaustively considering every avenue — weighing the risk of doing the wrong thing against the risk of doing nothing.

## Relevance

Informs leadership decision-making and [Risk Tolerance](/concepts/risk-tolerance.md): how much uncertainty a leader will accept before acting.
