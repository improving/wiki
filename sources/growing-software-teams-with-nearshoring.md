# How to Grow Software Teams with Nearshoring — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/growing-software-teams-with-nearshoring/growing-software-teams-with-nearshoring.md`

## Summary

The author argues that South American countries in US-aligned time zones make ideal nearshoring partners, and that the real work is culture and communication rather than proximity. Improving South America (formerly Leniolabs) went remote-first in 2013 and adopted Agile Manifesto principles centered on teammate communication, which became nearshoring almost by accident. The post offers five practices for building and managing nearshore teams and warns that surveillance tools backfire.

## Key Claims

- Remote work lets companies hire talent anywhere and support work-life balance, but fails for firms unprepared to invest in culture and communication.
- Remote teams lose office perks and water-cooler serendipity, so communication must be the pillar, supported by tools like Slack and Microsoft Teams.
- Improving South America went remote-first in 2013 rather than lose talent to long commutes, adopting Agile communication principles.
- Five practices: optimize the onboarding framework; implement structured communication (standups, sprint reviews, one-to-ones, async tools); leverage integrated collaboration tools (Jira/Asana/Azure DevOps, Slack/Teams, Confluence/Notion); define data-driven KPIs and dashboards (velocity, cycle time, code quality, SLA adherence, deployment frequency); build and sustain team culture.
- Forcing screen-recording monitoring pushes away talented professionals.
- Cultural alignment with the US eases integration but is hard to quantify.
