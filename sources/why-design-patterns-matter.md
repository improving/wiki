# Why Design Patterns Matter — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/why-design-patterns-matter/why-design-patterns-matter.md`

## Summary

Greg Chartrau uses a 1984 Panasonic microwave — whose counterintuitive controls stumped him after 40 years — to illustrate why design patterns matter. He traces how early one-off UX solutions made interfaces inconsistent, then shows how patterns for registration forms, navigation menus, and product listings became standardized through user testing and research.

## Key Claims

- The NE-6850's interface, including setting the clock and a default-lowest power level, was nearly impossible to operate without the manual.
- Early UX designers solved each problem uniquely, making it hard for users to move between interfaces and systems.
- Design patterns are tried-and-tested solutions to common design problems that give designers a common language.
- Registration form patterns (clear labels, fewer required fields, clear CTAs) raised conversion and consistency.
- Navigation menu patterns added clear labeling, hierarchy, and visual cues; product listing patterns added clear images, concise descriptions, and CTAs.
- Patterns reduce cognitive load, ensure predictability, and incorporate accessibility best practices, while still leaving room for creative solutions.
