# How Do I Choose New Technologies for an Organization — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/how-do-i-choose-new-technologies-for-an-organization/how-do-i-choose-new-technologies-for-an-organization.md`

## Summary

Marcus King offers three under-considered questions for evaluating a new technology: your own motivations, the team's current competencies, and the size of the market talent pool.

## Key Claims

- Check your motives: don't twist a project to fit a technology you want on your résumé; look for overlap between your interest and the organization's.
- Choose technologies near the team's existing languages and tooling, branching out strategically rather than overloading them.
- Example: a C# team adopting Angular with TypeScript is approachable given VS Code, Microsoft backing, and syntax similarities.
- Introducing too many new things at once drowns a team in a new learning curve, pushing them back to the comfortable and less likely to try new things.
- Assume the team will change over time; pick technology with a large talent pool so backfills are easy and the employer isn't captive to a codebase.
- Run these questions at the end of an assessment to align competing interests; the answers have changed his recommendations.
