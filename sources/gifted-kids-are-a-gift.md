# Gifted Kids are a Gift — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/gifted-kids-are-a-gift/gifted-kids-are-a-gift.md`

## Summary

O'Hara distinguishes giftedness from high achievement: gifted is defined statistically as two standard deviations above the mean, so the gifted child thinks differently about the world, problems, and life, and may sit outside many friend groups because of it. He warns that parents often equate their children's happiness with fitting in, an unrealistic expectation that sets kids up badly. The better aim, he argues, is growing into the fullness of one's abilities and using one's gifts, and the social struggle itself is something kids need to work through rather than have shortcut.

## Key Claims

- Giftedness is typically defined as two standard deviations above the mean — statistically "way out there."
- The gifted child is not the same as the achiever (straight A's, band, student council, volunteering); that child is a high performer, not necessarily an outlier.
- Gifted kids often think differently and end up outside normative friend groups.
- Parents frequently want their kids to be "happy" and equate that with fitting in, which is an unrealistic expectation to set for themselves or their children.
- Linking fitting in with happiness creates a false dichotomy: be who you are, or fit in.
- Parents should not shortcut the social wrestling; that struggle is how children become more capable as they grow.

## Relevance

Informs [false dichotomy](/concepts/false-dichotomy.md) as a thinking trap — framing belonging and authenticity as mutually exclusive choices.
