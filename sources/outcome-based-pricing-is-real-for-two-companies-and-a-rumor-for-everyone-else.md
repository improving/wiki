# Outcome-Based Pricing Is Real For Two Companies And A Rumor For Everyone Else — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/outcome-based-pricing-is-real-for-two-companies-and-a-rumor-for-everyone-else/outcome-based-pricing-is-real-for-two-companies-and-a-rumor-for-everyone-else.md`

## Summary

Accenture lost 18% of its market value in a single session, and analysts tied it to doubt that the firm can reprice work fast enough as AI erodes the billable hour. Most current "outcome-based pricing" is outcome language bolted onto a fixed-fee contract, taking downside risk without upside. Real value-based pricing needs three things most firms lack: standardized delivery, tooling that tracks whether outcomes happened, and contracts covering client-caused failure.

## Key Claims

- Accenture's 18% single-session drop was its worst day since 2016, attributed to a guidance cut and doubts about repricing work.
- Real value-based pricing requires repeatable delivery, outcome-tracking tooling, and contract terms for client inaction — the number-one cause of failed outcomes.
- Endava is deliberately restructuring toward outcome-based engagements; utilization and margin dip during the transition and deal cycles take three to four times longer, while its AI-driven business runs at higher margin.
- Globant's AI Pods are token-metered (roughly 100M tokens for ~$20,000/month), a break from headcount-based revenue but not strict outcome pricing.
- Accenture and Infosys disclose no concrete share of outcome-priced revenue, and sector filings now list the shift as a defensive risk factor.
