# The Amplifier Paradox — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-amplifier-paradox/the-amplifier-paradox.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara argues that the AI-as-amplifier story is true but incomplete: the same tool that multiplies output can quietly atrophy the judgment underneath it. He cites a year-long study of cancer specialists whose output metrics improved while their judgment eroded — a phenomenon the researchers called "intuition rust" — and a coding study where heavy AI reliance correlated with 17% lower scores on manual-mastery tasks. The uncomfortable question for consultants, he writes, is not whether AI replaces us but what happens when it doesn't: you keep the job and the client relationships, while the repetition that built your judgment is handled faster and merely adequately. Adequate is not the same as good, and the gap between them is what clients pay for.

## Key Claims

- A year-long study of cancer specialists found AI improved output metrics while eroding the judgment that made those metrics meaningful — researchers called it "intuition rust."
- More than 11 million gateway jobs disappeared in 18 months, removing the entry-level work where pattern recognition was built — a training-infrastructure loss, not just a labor-market one.
- A 2026 coding study found individuals who relied heavily on AI tools scored 17% lower on manual-mastery tasks.
- The risk is not replacement but the loss of the repetition that builds expertise, producing drift that goes unnoticed until the skill is needed and absent.
- The amplifier story is true and incomplete; deliberate practice of skills without assistance is what preserves judgment.
- The judgment calls you stop making because the tool makes them adequately are exactly the value you are paid to provide.
