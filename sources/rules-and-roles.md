# Rules and Roles — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/rules-and-roles/rules-and-roles.md`

## Summary

O'Hara offers what he calls an unpopular perspective: rules differ for different people because they play different roles, and that is acceptable. People have different strengths, weaknesses, abilities, and experiences — not everyone can be an actor, salesperson, firefighter, or accountant. He argues that forcing homogeneity in the name of "fair" is destructive and ultimately unfair because it denies reality, while explicitly excluding oppression or intentional mistreatment. The real enemy, he says, is comparison and jealousy, a problem that lies within us. His remedy is to stop comparing ourselves to others and instead compare against our own potential.

## Key Claims

- Rules differ by role because people's abilities and experiences differ, and that is OK.
- Trying to create homogeneity under the guise of "fair" is destructive and unfair because it contradicts reality (excluding oppression or intentional mistreatment).
- The real enemy is comparison and jealousy — an internal problem.
- Comparing yourself to others robs you of joy and gratitude for being yourself.
- Compare yourself instead to where you could be at your full potential, and strive for that; such achievement brings joy and fulfillment.
