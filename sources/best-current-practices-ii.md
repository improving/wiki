# Best Current Practices, Part 2: When to NOT Follow the Rules — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/best-current-practices-ii/best-current-practices-ii.md`

## Summary

Daniel Scheufler follows his first post on the implicit assumptions in best practices by naming three situations where deliberately deviating is worthwhile: when learning by experience, when optimizing cost on an unproven product, and when a team lacks the skills a practice requires. He stresses that practices should generally be followed, but adoption must account for people and organizational maturity.

## Key Claims

- Best practices solve real gaps; deviate intentionally and with eyes open, not by default.
- Benefit 1, learning: experiential pain is acceptable for personal practice at low cost, but dangerous in production without a migration roadmap.
- Benefit 2, cost: a gold-plated build on an unproven product bets money on unvalidated market fit; a true MVP can be cheaper and validate demand first.
- Quote: "Learn the rules like a pro, so you can break them like an artist" (credited to Picasso).
- Benefit 3, people: a command-and-control culture lacks the delegation skills agile practices require; moving from directive to delegating cannot happen in one step.
- Account for skill level and organizational maturity when prescribing process, so people can practice it successfully.
