# Right Wrongs: What We Learn as Children and Forget as Adults — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/right-wrongs/right-wrongs.md`

## Summary

Ed Utley argues that Right Wrongs, a trust behavior from The Speed of Trust, is something we learn as children and abandon as adults once ego sets in. He defines it as restoring a relationship by taking responsibility, and warns that unresolved wrongs become landmines in workplace relationships.

## Key Claims

- Right Wrongs means seeking to restore a relationship by taking responsibility and continuing to right the wrong in the future.
- His family asks for forgiveness rather than only saying sorry, giving the wronged person a genuine choice.
- Ego and pride cause adults to stop righting wrongs, especially when conflict is not black and white and others share the fault.
- Ignoring a conflict plants a landmine that may detonate later with lasting consequences.
- "It's just business" is not an excuse to treat others harshly; he describes being excluded from a partnership and hurt by that phrase.
- "I'm sorry, but..." puts the apologizer back in a self-righteous victim role and is not a genuine apology.
