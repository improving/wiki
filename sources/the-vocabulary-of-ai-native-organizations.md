# The Vocabulary of AI-Native Organizations — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-vocabulary-of-ai-native-organizations/the-vocabulary-of-ai-native-organizations.md`

## Summary

Devlin Liles argues that the AI era has produced a vocabulary problem at organizational scale: terms like "AI adoption," "AI integration," "hallucination," and "autonomous AI" are so broad they convey almost nothing, causing decisions to be made with participants who mean different things. He calls this a thinking problem, not merely a communication problem, echoing his claim that "the quality of our thoughts cannot exceed the quality of our language." The fix is a shared vocabulary practice, not a glossary.

## Key Claims

- "AI adoption" spans everything from one employee using ChatGPT to Stage 7 autonomous systems, so the term is nearly information-free; stage numbers (via the AI Trust Evolution) restore precision.
- "AI-augmented" versus "AI-native" is a categorical difference in operating model, not a matter of degree.
- "Hallucination" names only one of five failure mechanisms (also Commission, Omission, Perspective, Bias, Frame of Reference), so reviewing for it creates false confidence.
- Terms that have stabilized in his workshops include stacking errors, labor compression, demand-limited vs. collapsable tasks, and the woodshop-to-factory transition.
- The output should be a habit — asking "what specifically do you mean?" — not a glossary that ages poorly.
