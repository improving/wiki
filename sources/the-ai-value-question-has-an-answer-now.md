# The AI Value Question Has an Answer Now — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-ai-value-question-has-an-answer-now/the-ai-value-question-has-an-answer-now.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara uses Uber's experience — burning its entire 2026 AI budget in four months while its COO admitted it was hard to draw a line from usage stats to "25% more useful consumer features" — to frame AI's real problem as proof, not adoption. He then highlights Cognition's launch of an AI Productivity Guarantee for Devin: if the tool doesn't deliver the engineering value a customer is paying for, Cognition funds their usage until it does, up to $10 million. O'Hara reads this as a contractual bet on measurability, since underwriting the guarantee requires estimating human-equivalent engineering hours per session. When the vendor is financially exposed to the outcome, he argues, the conversation shifts from negotiating ROI projections to holding receipts.

## Key Claims

- Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI budget in four months, and its COO could not draw a line from usage stats to a 25% increase in useful consumer features.
- The recurring client question is what the growing token spend is actually producing; productivity gains are real in some places and invisible in others.
- Cognition launched an AI Productivity Guarantee: if Devin doesn't deliver the engineering value paid for, Cognition funds usage until it does, up to $10 million.
- Backing the guarantee required a system that estimates human-equivalent engineering hours per session — a concrete number, not a "transformation index" or NPS score.
- Uber's problem was not adoption but proof; heavy incentives drove the budget burn without a demonstrable link from tokens to features.
- When vendors are financially exposed to outcomes, buyers hold receipts instead of negotiating ROI projections.
