# Hybrid Intelligence: Neural + Symbolic + Governance — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/hybrid-intelligence-neural-symbolic-governance/hybrid-intelligence-neural-symbolic-governance.md`

## Summary

Liles argues the decades-old neural-versus-symbolic debate rests on a false dichotomy, and that reliably intelligent systems at scale combine both paradigms plus a third component—governance—that neither camp discusses enough. He cites AlphaGo's pairing of learned neural evaluation with explicit tree search as proof the hybrid approach already won.

## Key Claims

- Neural alone is powerful but untrustworthy; symbolic alone is rigid and limited; both without governance are organizationally undeployable.
- The 1980s expert systems boom showed symbolic constraints without neural capability fail at rule boundaries, with a knowledge-acquisition bottleneck that helped trigger an AI winter.
- A well-designed system shows all three across intake validation, neural core processing, symbolic output validation, a governance layer, and a feedback loop.
- Rich Sutton's "Bitter Lesson" is a claim about capability, not accountability: even a model that never erred would still need audit trails, access control, and policy enforcement, because institutions demand evidence of control.
- Falling error rates move where thresholds sit; they do not remove the need for thresholds—aviation kept its checklists as engines became more reliable.

## Relevance

Defines [hybrid intelligence](/concepts/hybrid-intelligence.md) as neural plus [symbolic AI](/concepts/symbolic-ai.md) plus [AI Governance](/concepts/ai-governance.md); cites [AlphaGo](/entities/alphago.md) as the canonical hybrid example and [Herbert Simon](/entities/herbert-simon.md)'s physical symbol system hypothesis as the debate's origin.
