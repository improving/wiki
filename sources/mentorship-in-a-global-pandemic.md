# Mentorship in a Global Pandemic — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/mentorship-in-a-global-pandemic/mentorship-in-a-global-pandemic.md`

## Summary

Shervin Joseph recounts how a LinkedIn message from a fellow University of Texas at Austin alum, Cale Ownby, turned into a sustained mentorship during the pandemic. What began as a requested 30-minute call ran to 90 minutes and grew into coaching on Scrum, resumes, and career goals — ending with Cale accepting a Scrum Master role. Joseph argues mentorship is a two-way street worth pursuing despite imposter syndrome.

## Key Claims

- The pandemic left many people doing the minimum socially; Joseph admits to faking uplift on calls and retreating once they ended.
- Cale Ownby, a Scrum Master who had graduated from Joseph's alma mater, reached out through Texas Exes for Scrum and Agility guidance.
- Joseph initially tried to answer by message and avoid a call, but Ownby's persistence led to a 30-minute call that ran nearly 90 minutes — one of Joseph's top three pandemic conversations.
- The pair met repeatedly, covering Scrum, agility as a mindset, retrospectives, real versus fake agility, resumes, and career planning.
- On September 17, Ownby texted that he had accepted a Scrum Master offer.
- Joseph credits Improving's leadership with encouraging him to mentor widely, and notes imposter syndrome can make new mentors want to run away.
