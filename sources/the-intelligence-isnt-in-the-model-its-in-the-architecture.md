# The Intelligence Isn't in the Model. It's in the Architecture. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-intelligence-isnt-in-the-model-its-in-the-architecture/the-intelligence-isnt-in-the-model-its-in-the-architecture.md`

## Summary

Argues technology leaders ask the wrong question — how smart is the model — when the useful question is how well the system distributes intelligence across all the nodes that do cognitive work. Drawing on Edwin Hutchins' distributed cognition, it frames software delivery teams as distributed cognitive systems whose collective intelligence is a function of architecture, not talent. AI adds a new node class, and what it does to system-level intelligence depends on integration, not model capability. An AI-native organization designs AI in as a first-class node; treating it as an add-on speeds individual nodes without making the system smarter.

## Key Claims

- Hutchins' Cognition in the Wild (1995) showed navigation intelligence is distributed across navigator, charts, instruments, radio, protocols, and crew habits — remove any node and the system degrades.
- GPS did not just improve position data; it shifted where cognitive work happened and created new failure modes when the instrument fails.
- A 2025 paper on AI in remote operations (air traffic control, industrial automation, intelligent ports) found AI changes the topology of team cognition, for better or worse depending on design.
- Human judgment remains the highest-value node — novel situations, ethical tradeoffs, relationship context, accountability — while model inference handles high-volume, well-defined reasoning.
