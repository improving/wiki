# AI Algorithm Bias: Why Awareness is Key — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/ai-algorithm-bias-why-awareness-is-key/ai-algorithm-bias-why-awareness-is-key.md`

## Summary

AI algorithm bias is what happens when AI systems produce unfair or skewed answers that reflect societal inequalities, or when a leading question steers the model toward the answer the user seems to want. Because AI learns from data generated by people, it amplifies human bias — and marginalized groups are most often affected. The post argues awareness and AI governance are the necessary starting points.

## Key Claims

- Bias can be introduced during data collection and labeling, in algorithm design, and when predictions are interpreted and applied.
- AI is "entirely reliant on humans" and cannot itself discern biased from unbiased input, so human biases permeate the tools people build.
- Example: many people asked ChatGPT "Will Edmonton win the Stanley Cup?" and got statistics skewed in favor of Edmonton — the model inferred the desired answer. Edmonton did not win.
- Example: AI-generated images of animals in organizational roles depicted only male animals in top positions and erased women from the workforce entirely.
- Mitigations begin with AI governance: compliance with laws and ethical standards, trust through privacy and security, fairness techniques such as counterfactual fairness, transparency, human oversight, and reinforcement learning to reduce bias.
- Awareness alone is not enough — users should understand AI's limits and fact-check outputs, especially for decisions that affect other people.
