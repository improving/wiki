# AI Readiness Is Mostly a Psychology Problem — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/ai-readiness-is-mostly-a-psychology-problem/ai-readiness-is-mostly-a-psychology-problem.md`

## Summary

AI readiness is mostly a psychology problem, not a tooling problem. Leaders default to the technology conversation because it is concrete and measurable, but the organizations that genuinely transform are those that shift mental models. The most pervasive blocking belief is not fear of job loss but the assumption that using AI well means trusting it implicitly; the workable posture treats AI as a capable collaborator whose output needs skilled review, like a smart colleague's first draft. Three shifts matter: from "AI replaces" to "AI compresses," from "I need to understand it" to "I need to manage it," and from "autonomy is risk" to "governance makes autonomy safe."

## Key Claims

- The dominant blocking mental model is the belief that using AI well means trusting it implicitly, producing an all-or-nothing posture.
- Labor compression is a more accurate frame than job replacement: AI reduces the hours a role takes rather than eliminating roles.
- The "10% understanding is enough to start" principle reflects the real practical threshold; judgment matters more than deep comprehension.
- Governance infrastructure — audit trails, quality gates, human approval points — makes autonomy deployable rather than constraining it.
- Organizations stuck at Stage 2 of the AI trust evolution cannot reach Stages 5–8 without the mental-model shift.
