# The AI Reckoning in Healthcare: What Payers Can't Afford to Ignore — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/the-ai-reckoning-in-healthcare-what-payers-cant-afford-to-ignore/the-ai-reckoning-in-healthcare-what-payers-cant-afford-to-ignore.md`

## Summary

Health plans face a trust crisis over AI-driven claim denials, even as AI promises lower costs and faster decisions. In a Q&A, Improving's Scott Poulin argues the answer is not less AI but better governance, flexible systems that adapt as state rules diverge, and genuine accountability for licensed tools. He ties this to approaching CMS interoperability deadlines and a zero-trust security posture.

## Key Claims

- The AMA reports 60% of physicians believe AI tools cause unnecessary claim denials, and 80% of prior authorization denials are overturned on review; UnitedHealthcare, Cigna, and Humana face lawsuits.
- AI governance is the foundation: observability, bias testing, formal risk analyses, acceptable-risk thresholds, and human-in-the-loop reviews for gray-area decisions.
- Texas, Arizona, and Maryland prohibit fully automated medical necessity denials; Colorado mandates annual AI impact assessments.
- CMS interoperability by 2026 requires 80% real-time prior authorization decisions using FHIR, with denial reasons made public.
- The WISER Model is a six-year Medicare pilot using AI to review prior authorizations.
- CMS holds health plans accountable for the AI they license; "we bought it from a vendor" is not a defense.
- Recommends zero-trust architecture, micro-segmentation, and HIPAA-compliant AWS built in from day one.
