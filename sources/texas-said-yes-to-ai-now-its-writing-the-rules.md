# Texas Said Yes to AI. Now It's Writing the Rules. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/texas-said-yes-to-ai-now-its-writing-the-rules/texas-said-yes-to-ai-now-its-writing-the-rules.md`

## Summary

O'Hara reports that ERCOT's large-load queue nearly quadrupled in a year to 439 gigawatts requested, 89% of it data centers, and that Governor Abbott directed the PUC and ERCOT to make data centers pay for their own grid infrastructure instead of passing costs to residential customers, with a joint memo due July 17. He argues this is not a reversal of Texas's pro-AI stance but maturation: every serious AI infrastructure hub eventually governs its growth, as California and Virginia did, and Texas is doing it faster because the load arrived faster. For clients with Texas data center plans, he says the July 17 memo is not background noise — it sets the cost structure for years.

## Key Claims

- ERCOT's large-load queue nearly quadrupled in a single year, to 439 gigawatts requested.
- 89% of that requested load is data centers.
- Governor Abbott directed the PUC and ERCOT to make data centers pay for their own grid infrastructure rather than shift costs to residential ratepayers.
- A joint memo was due July 17; O'Hara treats it as the document setting the cost structure for the next several years.
- The shift from "Texas said yes" to "Texas is writing the rules" is maturation, not a reversal — mirroring California and Virginia.
