# The Productivity Paradox: When AI Helps Everyone Equally — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/the-productivity-paradox-when-ai-helps-everyone-equally/the-productivity-paradox-when-ai-helps-everyone-equally.md`

## Summary

Argues a structural paradox: if AI makes everyone 40% more productive, the market adjusts, prices compress, and the gain produces no competitive advantage for anyone. The post separates two mechanisms of labor compression — demand-limited tasks, where the bottleneck is hours available rather than work to do, and collapsable tasks, where AI substitutes for a category of human involvement entirely. AI also produces a skill-leveling effect that narrows the gap between top and average performers, shifting the premium from execution skill to judgment. Advantage then concentrates where AI does not help everyone equally: access to context, speed of integration, and judgment at the margin.

## Key Claims

- If every consultant, developer, analyst, and writer gains 40% more productivity, prices compress and clients expect to pay for six hours, not ten.
- Demand-limited tasks produce efficiency gains that may or may not translate to revenue; collapsable tasks are substitution, not acceleration.
- The skill-leveling effect narrows the top-to-average gap; if AI brings average performers to 80% of top output, the ROI of talent-density recruiting changes materially.
- The premium shifts from execution skill — producing high-quality output — to judgment skill — evaluating whether output is right.
- Advantage concentrates in access to context, speed of integration into the operating model, and judgment at the margin.
