# Building Career Wealth in IT: The Time-Horizon Model — Summary

**Original source**: `references/devlinliles.com/building-career-wealth-in-it-the-time-horizon-model/building-career-wealth-in-it-the-time-horizon-model.md`

## Summary

Most developers manage their careers like income rather than wealth, measuring progress by salary instead of by what they have built. Devlin argues careers compound through consistent behavior and organizes the compounding behaviors into three time horizons: daily tactical habits, weekly planning behaviors, and quarterly investment behaviors. The wealth framing matters because relationships, reputation, and demonstrated judgment are more durable than skills that depreciate.

## Key Claims

- Daily habits: carry a notepad, practice 5-minute favors, ask when in doubt, send confirmation on completing commitments, and track time like an accountant.
- Weekly behaviors: plan the week before it starts, check existing commitments before saying yes, keep a mentor list with specific asks, and measure results rather than activity.
- Quarterly investments: small actionable personal development plans, "walk the talk," a regular mentorship cadence, and investing outside your comfort zone.
- Skills depreciate — languages and frameworks fall out of fashion — while wealth (relationships, reputation, judgment) does not depreciate the same way.
- "Luck looks a lot like hard work": the person who gets the interesting opportunity is usually the one who built the wealth that made it possible.
