# Is Scrum Certification Training Worth It? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/is-scrum-certification-training-worth-it/is-scrum-certification-training-worth-it.md`

## Summary

Don McGreal takes on the common question of whether Scrum certification is worth it, conceding the debate over its value as a resume stamp but arguing it has real value anyway. The post-class test motivates review, practice tests teach through explanation and repetition, and study groups supply cadence and accountability — so certification marks the start of a learning journey rather than the end.

## Key Claims

- The most popular certifications come from Scrum.org (PSM, PSPO) and the Scrum Alliance (CSM, CSPO); Scrum Alliance requires a class, Scrum.org does not.
- Improving is the largest provider of Scrum.org training in North America.
- Certifications are sometimes accused of being a money-making scheme for training and consulting.
- A post-class test pushes participants to review materials, do research, and take practice tests, unlike a non-certified class where books close for good.
- Practice tests like Scrum.org's Open Assessments explain why each answer is right or wrong, making them a valuable learning tool.
- Study groups provide cadence, accountability, and emotional support, and often persist after certification as book clubs or communities of practice.
- Those who certify tend to reach a higher minimum competency and speak a more consistent language.
