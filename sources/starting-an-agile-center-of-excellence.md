# Starting an Agile Center of Excellence — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/starting-an-agile-center-of-excellence/starting-an-agile-center-of-excellence.md`

## Summary

Allison Pollard argues an Agile Center of Excellence should be a helpful, consultative group that helps an organization become more excellent — including spotting and promoting internal excellence — rather than a monopoly on good ideas or an instrument of governance and compliance. She warns that a COE can easily become the internal compliance police, and urges defining success by business outcomes rather than by the services the group provides.

## Key Claims

- Pollard dislikes the name "Center of Excellence" because it is not about monopolizing excellence, but helping the organization become more excellent.
- An Agile COE should be a helpful, consultative group, not a strict instrument of governance or compliance.
- There is real danger in a COE becoming the internal compliance police, even though it may define mechanisms for product and team health transparency.
- She uses an elevator-statement format and Jason Little's strategic change canvas to align on the group's mission.
- Defining success criteria is a major challenge: services and activities are the how, and measuring them gives only leading measures.
- The deeper goal is business outcomes — customer satisfaction, cost savings, revenue, shorter time to market — and the reason for agile in the organization.
- Tying COE success to the organization's why forces the group to change, evolve, and pivot its offerings, which is itself an act of agility.

## Relevance

Informs [agile transformation](/concepts/agile-transformation.md) and [agile leadership](/concepts/agile-leadership.md), reframing a center of excellence as a consultative enabler of organizational change rather than a control body.
