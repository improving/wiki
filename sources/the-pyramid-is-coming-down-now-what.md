# The Pyramid Is Coming Down. Now What? — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-pyramid-is-coming-down-now-what/the-pyramid-is-coming-down-now-what.md`

## Summary

McKinsey cut 3,000–4,000 people this year — about 10% of the firm — citing AI-driven productivity, but O'Hara argues that framing credits AI too much and scrutinizes the business model too little. The pyramid was never a delivery model; it was a pricing model. A junior analyst did the analysis, a manager packaged it, a partner presented it, and clients paid for all three even when the analysis took hours instead of weeks. AI made that math visible, and the opportunity now belongs to mid-market firms that never had a pyramid to begin with.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey cut 3,000–4,000 people (~10%) this year, citing AI-driven productivity.
- The pyramid was a pricing model, not a delivery model: the middle manager layer justified the invoice.
- AI compressed the analysis layer, so firms can no longer charge for the full stack.
- This isn't AI replacing consultants; it's AI exposing headcount that was priced in, not necessary to the work.
- The advantage belongs to leaner, outcomes-focused mid-market firms.
