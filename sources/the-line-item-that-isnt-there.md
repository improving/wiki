# The Line Item That Isn't There — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-line-item-that-isnt-there/the-line-item-that-isnt-there.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that the vendor economics of AI agents look clean — tokens, sessions, seats — while hiding what happens when an agent confidently goes wrong in the middle of a workflow. Because it doesn't throw an error, it keeps going, and cleanup lands on people: a senior consultant spending two hours reconstructing what the agent decided, an engagement manager telling a client that already-acted-on output must be redone. These "hidden human cleanup costs" appear on no invoice, on either side of the table, and quietly erode the margin.

## Key Claims

- Agents fail silently: a confidently wrong step in a fifteen-step workflow doesn't error, it just keeps going.
- Cleanup is real labor — hours of reconstruction and client remediation — that shows up nowhere.
- Every agent deployment has an intervention rate nobody measures, and it compounds fast even at modest hourly cost.
- The vendor doesn't measure it (it happens after the sale), the client treats it as normal delivery friction, and consultants absorb it silently.
- If you can't name the cost, you can't manage or negotiate on it, and ROI stays in limbo.
