# A Snake and Kindness: A harsh tale about reality and desire — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/a-snake-and-kindness/a-snake-and-kindness.md`

## Summary

O'Hara retells the parable—used in the film *Natural Born Killers*—of a woman who nurses a frozen poisonous snake back to health only to be bitten, the snake's reply being that she knew what it was. He draws two lessons. First, we often want things to be a certain way and then act surprised when they are not, mistaking our desired perspective for how things should be; you have to deal with life as it is. Second, we should not expect someone to act against their nature, which he likens to a spouse believing they can remake the person they married.

## Key Claims

- The story illustrates how readily we confuse our desired perspective with reality.
- Insisting on how things "should" be is not only wrong-headed but often dangerous.
- You must deal with life as it is, not as you wish it to be.
- Expecting someone to act counter to their nature is hubris at minimum.
- People can change, but you cannot be the one to change them simply because you want it.
