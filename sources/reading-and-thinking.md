# Reading and Thinking — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/reading-and-thinking/reading-and-thinking.md`

## Summary

O'Hara reflects on his love of reading and the mild book-FOMO that accompanies it: he never feels he reads enough, and sometimes mentally wanders to other books while reading the one in hand. The value of reading, for him, is that it supplies material to think about — more perspectives and data mean more possibilities for assessing a challenge. He deliberately seeks out new, unheard-of books rather than relying on others' recommendations, and enjoys browsing used bookstores to find them.

## Key Claims

- Reading gives him things to think about: different perspectives to apply and different data to weigh against a challenge.
- He prefers finding new books over relying on recommendations, though he balances the two.
- He cites Haruki Murakami: "If you only read the books that everyone else is reading, you can only think what everyone else is thinking."
- He finds new books by wandering local used bookstores (Half Price Books; Recycled Books in Denton).
- He asks readers how they find new books to read.
