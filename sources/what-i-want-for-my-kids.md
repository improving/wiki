# What I Want For My Kids... — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/what-i-want-for-my-kids/what-i-want-for-my-kids.md`

## Summary

O'Hara rejects the common parental wish that children "have a better life" than their parents did. Life isn't always an up-slope, and parents' wanting doesn't guarantee it — plenty of people who are hurting now didn't start there. What he wants to teach his children, more than anything, is an appreciation for their own life. If they simply appreciate what they have, he argues, they'll be better off than many around them, including some who have more.

## Key Claims

- Parents' desire for their kids to "have a better life" doesn't control how life turns out.
- Life isn't always an up-slope; his children may face struggles he never did.
- Appreciating what you have matters more than accumulating more.
- The trap of comparison makes people focus on the delta between their things and someone else's.
- He doesn't want slackers — he wants presence, gratitude for the now, and awareness that many are worse off.
