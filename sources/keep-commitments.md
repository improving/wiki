# The Significance of Keeping Commitments — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/keep-commitments/keep-commitments.md`

## Summary

Shelby Sansone Stephens examines "Keep Commitments," the twelfth trust behavior in Stephen M.R. Covey's *The Speed of Trust*, which Covey calls the "Big Kahuna" of them all. In an age where cancelling is a text away, she argues that the fastest way to build trust is to make fewer promises and keep the ones you make — and that breaking a commitment does lasting damage.

## Key Claims

- If you have any doubt you can keep a commitment, do not make it; a "maybe" or "no" is better than a cancelled promise.
- Covey calls keeping commitments the quickest way to build trust in any relationship, yet notes "common sense is not always common practice."
- The author's own story: a boss promised a Monday–Friday schedule, changed it to weekends six months in, and she left shortly after — the broken word, not the hours, was decisive.
- An unkept commitment can ruin your reputation, and trust is very hard to win back once broken.
- At Improving, she sees busy people keep commitments and people transparently say "no" or "I'll think about it," which she links to the transparency trust behavior.
