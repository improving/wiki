# The Rollout Worked. The People Didn't. — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-rollout-worked-the-people-didnt/the-rollout-worked-the-people-didnt.md`

## Summary

Argues that failed AI rollouts are a leadership failure, not a technology failure. Twenty-nine percent of employees admit to sabotaging their company's AI rollout, and confidence in company AI strategy fell from 47% in 2025 to 31% in 2026. The factor separating smooth implementations from struggling ones is leadership trust, not tool selection or training spend.

## Key Claims

- 29% of employees admit to sabotaging their company AI rollout.
- Employee confidence in company AI strategy fell from 47% (2025) to 31% (2026).
- Smooth implementations scored leadership trust at +1.65 versus -1.50 for struggling ones — a 3.15-point spread.
- That trust gap explains more than tool selection, training budget, or vendor choice combined.
- Resentment is a slow leak: efficiency gains are real, but so is the belief the change is done to people.

## Relevance

Informs [Change Management](/concepts/change-management.md) and [trust building](/concepts/trust-building.md) in AI adoption.

Practitioner blog post by David O'Hara. Strong survey figures and a steering-committee perspective; the causal link between trust and adoption outcomes is asserted rather than proven.
