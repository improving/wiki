# Walk the Walk But Don't Talk — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/walk-the-walk-but-dont-talk/walk-the-walk-but-dont-talk.md`

## Summary

O'Hara argues that the best way to inspire others is to model the behavior yourself. He describes a colleague who works longer hours than anyone else out of genuine love for the work, and whose passion is infectious — the whole group gets caught up and strives to do better, an honest, self-sustaining cycle. The caution is that naming your own effort, even innocently, changes how people read your motivation.

## Key Claims

- Modeling a behavior is like magic: people may wonder how you do it, but they try to imitate.
- One person's genuine passion for the work spreads and raises the whole group's effort.
- Pointing out how many hours you work plants doubt: "Does he do this just to hold himself up for doing it?"
- Living a behavior inspires; speaking of it, even to inform rather than brag, lessens its effect.
- The takeaway: walk the walk, but don't talk about it.
