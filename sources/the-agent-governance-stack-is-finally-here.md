# The Agent Governance Stack Is Finally Here — Summary

**Original source**: `references/driventodevelop.com/the-agent-governance-stack-is-finally-here/the-agent-governance-stack-is-finally-here.md`

## Summary

David O'Hara reports that five enterprise AI governance products shipped within a thirteen-day window in late September and early October — from SAP, Collibra, Dataiku, Island, and Broadcom — which he reads as the market finally delivering tooling rather than philosophy. For two years the conversation about why agent governance matters stalled because acting on it meant building software yourself, a task made harder by a limited talent pool. O'Hara observes a common pattern across the launches: inventory first, then control, since you must know what agents are running before you can govern them, and most enterprises remain stuck at that first step.

## Key Claims

- Five enterprise AI governance products shipped in twelve days (SAP, Collibra, Dataiku, Island, Broadcom) inside a thirteen-day window in late September and early October.
- The signal is that the market wanted tooling, not philosophy; the tooling just arrived, though the talent shortage remains.
- Every product follows the same pattern: inventory first, then control.
- Dataiku names the problem "agent sprawl" — employees creating agents faster than central teams can observe or manage them — and connects to AWS Bedrock, Databricks, Google Vertex, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Salesforce Agentforce, and Snowflake Cortex.
- Broadcom's AgentMinder takes an identity-layer approach, verifying each agent's identity and intended mission before authorizing action.
- For most organizations the question is which layer to own first: inventory, policy, monitoring, or identity — each separating into its own product category and demanding integration ownership.
