# Why Successful Software Projects Require More Than Technical Skills — Summary

**Original source**: `references/improving.com/why-successful-software-projects-require-more-than-technical-skills/why-successful-software-projects-require-more-than-technical-skills.md`

## Summary

A developer argues that clients don't buy tests or beautiful code — they buy outcomes — so communication and understanding the business problem matter as much as technical craft. Developers should grasp why a project exists before debating implementation details.

## Key Claims

- The "Pizza-Driven Development" joke: locking developers away with detailed requirements and sliding pizza under the door fails.
- Understanding what problem needs solving, and why, improves the code and makes developers more engaged.
- A business lead's line — "We are not here to write code. We are here to sell stuff" — shows code is downstream of business purpose.
- Questions about cloud choice, tech stack, or button colors are premature; first understand stakeholder needs.
- Scrum adopted without understanding why it exists leads teams to wrongly conclude "scrum is not for us."
- Complex skills are best practiced in small chunks with fast feedback — e.g., three sprints in three days on a real project with a new team.

## Relevance

A case for [effective communication](/concepts/effective-communication.md) as a core developer skill, tied to [product ownership](/concepts/product-ownership.md) and [agile transformation](/concepts/agile-transformation.md).
