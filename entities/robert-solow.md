# Robert Solow

Robert Solow is an economist whose 1987 observation that "you can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics" became the canonical statement of the productivity paradox. Both sources in this corpus invoke him to caution against assuming that a fast new technology converts directly into measured value.

## Overview

One post uses Solow's paradox to argue that seven decades of tech-economy forecasts share the same structural flaw: they extrapolate task exposure rather than measured adoption. The other applies the same logic to AI coding, where gains attenuate as work moves from commits to shipped features to real value, because downstream processes were never redesigned. Together they frame Solow as the intellectual ancestor of today's AI productivity sceptics.

## Key Claims

- Solow's paradox is the historical precedent for doubting confident, quantified tech-economy forecasts
- Across 100,000+ developers, commits rose 180%, released software 30%, and consumer usage stayed flat
- O-ring theory: a bottleneck stage caps output regardless of speedups elsewhere
- Acemoglu's 1.1–1.6% GDP estimate sits about one-fifth below Goldman's, echoing the paradox
- Teams pairing tooling with Stage 4 workflow redesign ship 12–30% more features, crossing 50% with governance
