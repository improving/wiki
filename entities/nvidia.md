# Nvidia

Nvidia is the dominant supplier of AI accelerators. In this corpus it is a bellwether for AI demand, a participant in the licensing-plus-hiring acquisition pattern, a sponsor of pro-open-weight lobbying, and a vendor packaging model routing. Its demand story is increasingly told through the physical buildout of the regions that buy its silicon.

## Key Claims

- Nvidia's $6 billion deal to license Poolside's Model Factory and hire its staff used the same structure as Microsoft-Inflection and Amazon-Adept ([buying the team](/sources/buying-the-team-without-buying-the-company.md))
- Nvidia posted an earnings beat and was still sold off, evidence that capital markets no longer fund AI buildout on forecasts alone ([built for demand that hasn't arrived](/sources/built-for-demand-that-hasnt-arrived.md))
- Its letter opposing premature restrictions drew roughly fifty signatories, while OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google sat out ([two open-weight letters](/sources/two-open-weight-letters-zero-neutral-parties.md))
- NeMo Switchyard packages [model routing](/concepts/model-routing.md) into a default feature, but a routing savings percentage without an accuracy delta is half an experiment ([read the accuracy number](/sources/read-the-accuracy-number-next-to-the-cost-number.md))
- Aggregate routing accuracy can hide worse failure rates on the hardest requests.
- Demand for its class of accelerators shows up as concrete regional infrastructure: Wistron picked Fort Worth for a $761M AI supercomputing facility and 248 data centers are planned across Texas, making DFW load-bearing for AI capacity ([fort worth just became a tier-one AI city](/sources/fort-worth-just-became-a-tier-one-ai-city.md)).
