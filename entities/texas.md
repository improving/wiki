# Texas

Texas is the state where the AI data center boom and the grid math behind it collide most visibly. Across this corpus it moves from enthusiastic host to rule-writer, as the cost of powering compute becomes a regulatory question.

## Overview

Texas leads the nation in both proposed AI data centers and the gas plants meant to supply them, with 248 data centers in the state pipeline. Sites such as [Fort Worth](/entities/fort-worth.md) sit inside this build-out, and the state's decisions now set the cost structure for the whole [regional AI economy](/concepts/regional-ai-economy.md).

## Key Claims

- ERCOT's large-load queue nearly quadrupled in a year, with 89% of it data centers.
- [Greg Abbott](/entities/greg-abbott.md) directed the PUC and ERCOT to make data centers fund their own grid infrastructure; the July 17 memo sets that cost structure.
- On August 3 the governor paused data center approvals to audit energy usage, amid partly manufactured opposition.
- Ohio and Virginia now force data centers to pay for reserved capacity, and rate cases — not model choices — lock in AI's energy costs for decades.
- Restricting compute shifts builds to other states and countries; AI regulation is now a 2027 Texas legislative priority.
