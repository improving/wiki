# Grammarly

Grammarly is an AI writing assistant that appears in this corpus alongside discussions of how writing gets done. It matters less as a product review than as a concrete example of tooling that sits inside the writing process and can either serve or short-circuit its stages.

## Overview

Both sources center on [Madman Architect Carpenter Judge](/concepts/madman-architect-carpenter-judge.md), the framework [Betty Flowers](/entities/betty-flowers.md) proposed for separating drafting into distinct modes. Writing tools like Grammarly occupy the carpenter-and-judge end of that sequence, and the corpus's warning is that assistance applied too early substitutes polish for thinking.

## Key Claims

- Flowers identified four thinking modes that must occur in order, and the Judge showing up early kills divergent generation ([Beyond Writing: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, Judge](/sources/beyond-writing-madman-architect-carpenter-judge.md)).
- Distance between stages prevents organizing around enthusiasm instead of insight — the same reason a [shitty first draft](/concepts/shitty-first-draft.md) is useful.
- The framework applies to strategy, teams, and building AI systems, not just prose ([4 Roles: Madman, Architect, Carpenter, and Judge](/sources/madman-architect-carpenter-and-judge.md)).
- The madman generates raw ideas freely, the architect groups and shapes them after a break, the carpenter refines structure and clarity, and the judge reviews against the audience.
