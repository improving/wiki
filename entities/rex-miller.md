# Rex Miller

Rex Miller is an author whose work surfaces twice in this corpus: in writing about how change actually lands on people, and in a personal account of trying to solve sleep through tracking. The two threads share a theme — people and bodies are not systems that respond to a fix alone.

## Key Claims

- With people, fixing the error does not mean all is good; you must communicate, apologize, and make amends ([profound impacts](/sources/profound-impacts.md)).
- Unheard change communication leaves people in NIMSU — making things up — which is why change has to be announced, not merely implemented ([profound impacts](/sources/profound-impacts.md)).
- Owning mistakes and saying sorry is required to move forward ([profound impacts](/sources/profound-impacts.md)).
- On sleep, only one to three percent of people can survive on five hours or less; the author tried polyphasic sleep, medications, and a 73-hour wake reset with no luck ([sleep and Whoop](/sources/sleep-whoop.md)).
- More data, not a magic device, is the hope for better sleep; Whoop's battery-pack charging enables near-100% tracking uptime ([sleep and Whoop](/sources/sleep-whoop.md)).
