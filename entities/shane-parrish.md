# Shane Parrish

Shane Parrish is the decision-making writer whose ideas appear in this corpus on two related problems: how much authority the past should have over the story we tell next, and how to act when the future cannot be predicted. Both point at the same lever — the small, repeatable choices rather than the dramatic ones.

## Key Claims

- The past informs us but does not have to define us; Adlerian psychology says meaning and choices, not events, determine us ([don't let the past hold the pen](/sources/dont-let-the-past-hold-the-pen.md)).
- People who make being wronged their identity are recruiting, not seeking solutions ([don't let the past hold the pen](/sources/dont-let-the-past-hold-the-pen.md)).
- Beware communities that pull you deeper into a story you want to leave ([don't let the past hold the pen](/sources/dont-let-the-past-hold-the-pen.md)).
- When you know what is coming, prepare specifically for it; when the future is uncertain, position yourself against loss ([prepare vs position](/sources/prepare-vs-position.md)).
- Positioning comes from small daily habits, not big decisions ([prepare vs position](/sources/prepare-vs-position.md)).
