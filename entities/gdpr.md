# GDPR

The General Data Protection Regulation is the EU's data protection framework. In this corpus it appears in two roles: as the floor that data governance must exceed, and as a force pushing identity-level recognition toward the edge so raw personal data stays on-premises.

## Overview

One post argues that governance exists to make the knowledge stage of the data pipeline trustworthy, not merely compliant. Meeting GDPR is the minimum; the value comes from turning governed data into insight and action. Compliance alone does not produce a decision.

The other post frames GDPR as an architectural constraint. Running facial recognition at the edge keeps raw imagery on-premises, shrinking the compliance surface, while still answering access decisions in 30–100ms. Edge matching turns a data-transfer problem into a data-locality property.

## Key Claims

- Governance makes the knowledge stage trustworthy, not merely compliant with rules like GDPR.
- Edge matching keeps raw facial imagery on-premises, shrinking the compliance surface.
- On-device inference keeps access decisions working during WAN outages.
- Edge recognition answers in 30–100ms versus 800–2000ms in the cloud.
- Data is raw material; action is the only output that matters to a business.
