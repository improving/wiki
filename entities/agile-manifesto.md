# Agile Manifesto

The Agile Manifesto is the 2001 statement whose four values — individuals over processes, working software over documentation, collaboration over contracts, and responding to change over plans — were priority calls made under scarce human execution capacity. Across this corpus it anchors a debate about what happens to those trade-offs once AI removes that scarcity.

## Overview

Writers here argue the Manifesto encoded a constraint, not a timeless law. AI makes both sides of each "over" simultaneously achievable, so the trade-off becomes a multiplication governed by human intent. Twenty-five years of practice turned the original weighting into an "instead-of" default, a pattern one post calls Manifesto debt.

## Key Claims

- The four values were context-specific responses to scarce human execution capacity in 2001.
- AI dissolves that execution scarcity, making speed and documentation simultaneously achievable.
- P.O.S.T. reframes the trade-offs as four multiplicative dimensions: productivity, outcomes, satisfaction, time to market.
- Treating the values as timeless creates Manifesto debt that normalizes like technical debt.
- Human attention and judgment stay scarce even when artifact production is abundant.
