# Gemini

Gemini is [Google](/entities/google.md)'s family of AI models. In this corpus it is not evaluated on benchmarks; it appears instead as one pole of the enterprise AI platform question and as part of Google's push to give agents real organizational identities.

## Overview

The two sources treat Gemini from the platform-adoption side. One examines Google introducing persistent coworker agents in Workspace; the other argues that converging spend, code share, and valuation have effectively settled which enterprise AI stack to standardize on — a decision that turns on [model selection](/concepts/model-selection.md) and [agent identity](/concepts/agent-identity.md) as much as raw capability.

## Key Claims

- Google introduced persistent coworker agents with their own organizational identity in Workspace, turning agent governance into an identity-architecture problem ([The Day Your Agent Got Its Own Email Address](/sources/the-day-your-agent-got-its-own-email-address.md)).
- That post argues you cannot audit what you cannot attribute, nor revoke access you never granted — a [least privilege](/concepts/least-privilege.md) problem.
- On the platform question, one post reports Anthropic raised $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation, with [Claude](/entities/claude.md) holding 42–54% of enterprise code generation share and passing GPT in business AI spend per Ramp's AI Index ([The Stack You Chose Last Year Just Became the Right Answer](/sources/the-stack-you-chose-last-year-just-became-the-right-answer.md)).
- The implication drawn is that teams which committed twelve months earlier are twelve months ahead, so the choice matters more than the model.
