# Everett Rogers

Everett Rogers was a sociologist whose diffusion of innovations model describes how new ideas spread through a population in adopter segments — Innovators, Early Adopters, Early Majority, Late Majority, and Laggards. This corpus repurposes his curve as a diagnostic for AI adoption rather than a timeline.

## Work

Two posts treat Rogers' curve as the best available tool for locating an organization relative to the market. The curve describes populations at a moment, not a schedule, so mapping an organization's own adoption against the market reveals strategic options. The recurring mistake is applying an Innovator strategy while operating as a Late Majority organization.

A companion piece argues organizations overestimate AI maturity by measuring activity instead of capability, buying Stage 5 solutions while operating at Stage 2 capability. Rogers' model segments an organization's adoption distribution, so maturity is a distribution of capability levels rather than a single number.

## Key Claims

- The diffusion curve describes populations at a moment, not timelines.
- Mapping your adoption against the market reveals strategic options.
- In 2025 AI workflows sit at Early Adopter and agents at Innovator.
- Applying an Innovator strategy as a Late Majority organization is the common mistake.
- Maturity is a distribution of capability levels, not a single number.
