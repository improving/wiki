# Island

Island is an enterprise browser company that this corpus reframes as an agent-governance play: a governance company that happens to sit in the browser. It matters as evidence that agent governance is becoming a funded product category rather than a philosophical debate.

## Overview

Island's $400 million raise at a $6.4 billion valuation is treated as a signal about both the [agent identity](/concepts/agent-identity.md) market and the DFW region's rise as an enterprise AI hub. It appears alongside a cluster of governance products that shipped within a single thirteen-day window.

## Key Claims

- Island raised $400M at a $6.4B valuation for enterprise browsers, and is best understood as an agent governance company that happens to sit in the browser ([Your Next Browser May Come from Dallas](/sources/your-next-browser-may-come-from-dallas.md)).
- Five governance products shipped within a thirteen-day window, suggesting the market was waiting for tooling rather than philosophy ([The Agent Governance Stack Is Finally Here](/sources/the-agent-governance-stack-is-finally-here.md)).
- Every product in that cluster follows the same pattern: inventory first, then control — an [AI inventory](/concepts/ai-inventory.md) before a [control plane](/concepts/control-plane.md).
- Dataiku calls unchecked employee agent creation "agent sprawl," and Broadcom verifies agent identity and mission before authorizing action.
- DFW's Q3 2026 was the region's best VC quarter at $962.5M across 52 deals, part of the [Regional AI Economy](/concepts/regional-ai-economy.md) narrative.
