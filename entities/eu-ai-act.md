# EU AI Act

The EU AI Act is the European Union's risk-tiered regulation of AI systems. It anchors this corpus's governance writing because its high-risk obligations, data-quality rules, and liability provisions give compliance both a concrete deadline and a technical shape.

## Key Claims

- Article 10 imposes data quality requirements on the training data of high-risk AI, making data lineage auditable.
- Annex III high-risk obligations took effect on August 2, 2026, a deadline most organizations had not prepared for.
- EU Directive 2024/2853 imposes strict liability but does not resolve the moral question of responsibility when identical model behavior produces different outcomes.
- SOC 2 and EU AI Act Annex III together make task-specific model evaluation auditable.
- Compliance obligations are the forcing function that keeps the evaluation-governance flywheel turning.
- Only 8 percent of organizations rated their governance program highly successful.
- Article 50 transparency obligations went live on August 2, 2026, and fall on deployers as well as vendors — a vendor's compliance does not discharge the deployer's duty.
- Scope follows the output: EU-facing AI is in scope regardless of where the organization is headquartered.
- Penalties reach €35 million or 7% of global annual revenue.
- The enforcement deadline puts a due date on deferred governance, but an inventory is the precondition: you cannot govern what you have not mapped, and 36% of organizations have no agent supervision plan at all.
