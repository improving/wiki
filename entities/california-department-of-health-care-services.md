# California Department of Health Care Services

A state health agency (DHCS) cited as the constructive counterexample in the corpus's data-governance arc. Where most programs failed with bolt-on policy documents, DHCS rebuilt its platform so governance is engineered into the pipeline and compliance becomes the default.

## Overview

The corpus notes only 8% of organizations rated their governance programs highly successful in 2020, with barriers that were behavioral — policy adherence and usability — rather than budgetary. DHCS's rebuild embodies the shift from [bolt-on governance](/concepts/bolt-on-governance.md) to [embedded governance](/concepts/embedded-governance.md), where [active metadata](/concepts/active-metadata.md) is captured automatically as a byproduct of system operation.

## Key Claims

- DHCS rebuilt its platform with governance engineered into the pipeline.
- Bolt-on governance left catalogs stale and unused because curation was manual.
- Top governance barriers were behavioral: policy adherence and usability, not budget.
- Governance framed as enablement wins data-team support that compliance framing cannot.
