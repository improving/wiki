# George Leonard

George Leonard is the author of [Mastery](/entities/mastery.md), whose account of how skill actually develops underpins two sources in this corpus. His contribution is a corrective to the fantasy of steady, linear improvement.

## Overview

Leonard's growth-line model describes progress as bursts separated by plateaus that slope downward through entropy. That framing connects directly to [deliberate practice](/concepts/deliberate-practice.md), which supplies the method for pushing through those plateaus rather than sliding down them.

## Key Claims

- Growth comes in bursts and is never linear; every curve includes a plateau, a growth phase, and a pull-back ([Growth Lines](/sources/growth-lines.md)).
- The plateau is not flat — entropy makes it slope downward, so decay is the natural state and growth requires deliberate effort.
- [Deliberate practice](/concepts/deliberate-practice.md) is designed to improve performance, not just accumulate repetition ([Intro to Deliberate Practice](/sources/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md)).
- Practice sessions should run 60–90 minutes and at most 3–4 hours daily, with constant feedback and measurement driving improvement.
- Intrinsic motivation sustains mastery, and 10,000 hours alone is insufficient — a claim that echoes [Boredom Tolerance](/concepts/boredom-tolerance.md) and [growth mindset](/concepts/growth-mindset.md).
