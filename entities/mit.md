# MIT

MIT (the Massachusetts Institute of Technology) is a research university whose studies are cited repeatedly across this corpus as independent evidence about AI's real-world payoff. Its findings supply a skeptical counterweight to vendor and consultancy forecasts, and its affiliated economist [Daron Acemoglu](/entities/daron-acemoglu.md) anchors the corpus's measured view of AI's economic impact.

## Cited Research

- **Project NANDA (July 2025)** found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact despite $30–40 billion in spending — the canonical pilot-failure statistic.
- **MIT and IDC research** is cited for the twin figures that 88% of AI proof-of-concepts never reach production and 95% of enterprise AI solutions fail on data issues.
- **MIT Sloan research** is cited showing human-AI collaboration achieves 94% success with governance infrastructure versus 73% for full autonomy.
- A **2026 MIT/Wharton study** of 100,000+ GitHub developers found commits rose 180% and lines of code sevenfold while released software rose only 30% and consumer usage stayed flat.

## Significance

- The studies are used to argue the AI bottleneck is organizational — process architecture, governance, and data reliability — rather than model capability.
- Daron Acemoglu, an MIT economist, estimates AI will add just 1.1–1.6% to GDP over a decade, about one-fifth of Goldman Sachs' projection.
