# Collibra

Collibra is a data governance and [data catalog](/concepts/data-catalog.md) platform that this corpus uses as the emblem of [bolt-on governance](/concepts/bolt-on-governance.md): a catalog whose value depends on human curation that organizations underfund, so it goes stale and unused.

## Overview

The posts pair Collibra's category with the argument that manual tagging decays regardless of tooling. Taxonomy, ontology, and folksonomy all break down when upkeep is a human task; tags generated as a pipeline side effect survive. The recommended path is [embedded governance](/concepts/embedded-governance.md), where compliance is the default rather than a separate review step.

## Key Claims

- Only 8% of organizations rated their data governance programs highly successful, and Gartner predicts 80% of data and analytics governance initiatives will fail by 2027.
- Top governance barriers were behavioral — policy adherence and usability — not budget.
- Bolt-on governance left catalogs stale and unused because curation was manual.
- Three failure patterns recur: the compliance trap, the accountability gap, and tool-first failure.
- Successful programs pick a specific business outcome, embed governance in engineering workflows, and establish real ownership.
- The catalog category now sits inside a wider agent-governance stack: five enterprise governance products shipped within a thirteen-day window, and every one of them follows the same sequence — inventory first, then control ([the agent governance stack is finally here](/sources/the-agent-governance-stack-is-finally-here.md)).
- That is the same lesson the data-governance posts draw, so the market's arrival vindicates the inventory-before-control ordering rather than replacing the catalog's job.
- Vendors name the risk differently — Dataiku calls unchecked employee agent creation "agent sprawl", Broadcom verifies agent identity and mission before authorizing action — but converge on knowing what exists before governing it.
