# WCAG

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the international standard for making web content accessible to people with disabilities. In this corpus WCAG serves two roles at once: a practical design system for color, type, and interaction, and the benchmark referenced by US law and by digital-accessibility litigation.

## Conformance Levels

- WCAG defines three conformance levels — A, AA, and AAA. **AA is the practical minimum for products** (except decorative messaging), with AAA the goal in some cases.
- WCAG 2.1 aims for an experience that is **perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust**.
- Its ratio rules for foreground/background color contrast and text sizing should be used as a system for design decisions, not applied ad hoc.

## In Practice

- Foundational practices mapped to WCAG: keyboard navigation as a primary function, proper form labeling with a vertical layout, alternative text for informative images, and accessible color and font sizing.
- WCAG 2.1 Level AA is the level referenced by [ADA](/entities/americans-with-disabilities-act.md) Title II rules for large public entities and by rising accessibility lawsuits.
