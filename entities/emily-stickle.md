# Emily Stickle

An Improving writer and Agile coach focused on the human and leadership side of agility — how teams are coached, how they communicate, and why enforced practices fail. Her posts consistently redirect attention from process mechanics to listening, trust, and psychological safety.

## Topics and Work

- Debunks three communication myths — that communication means telling everything, that saying it once suffices, and that delivered information is automatically actionable — arguing communication must be repeated, contextual, and two-way.
- Argues that bringing in Agile coaches to "fix" teams rarely works, because teams complete activities without committing to them; coaches should coach continuous improvement and listen to why teams struggle, with [psychological safety](/concepts/psychological-safety.md) as the most important aspect of Agile.
- Explains Agile as the alternative to rigid waterfall management, defined by the four [Agile Manifesto](/entities/agile-manifesto.md) values, each demanding a different leadership posture.
- Diagnoses failing daily stand-ups as a symptom of deeper issues: Scrum Masters not removing impediments, teams not being heard, and prescriptive read-outs — often signals of missing safety.
- Coaches "yes, and…" as a replacement for binary yes/no, turning refusals into conversations about trade-offs and priorities.
