# Scrum Guide

The Scrum Guide is the definitive, periodically revised description of the Scrum framework, maintained by its creators. In this corpus it functions as the reference point against which practice is judged — the source of Scrum's roles, its five values, and the self-managing-team principle that many organizations quietly violate.

## What It Defines

- The **2020 Scrum Guide** frames the Scrum Master as a leader who serves — coach, blocker-remover, facilitator, and change agent — not a stand-up scheduler or "Jira police."
- It defines **self-managing teams** that decide who does what, when, and how; tracking individual velocity and demanding task-level check-ins contradicts this.
- It names **five Scrum Values** — commitment, focus, openness, respect, and courage — and says successful Scrum depends on living them, not just running the mechanics.
- It assigns the Scrum Master responsibility for ensuring events follow process and for removing impediments.

## How It Is Used Here

- The Guide is invoked to diagnose failing daily stand-ups: most Scrum Masters focus on the read-out rather than removing blockers.
- Its values, not the framework, are argued to be the real predictor of team success across XP, Scrum, or Kanban.
- ScrumMatch data cited here found **38% of Scrum Masters have little to no understanding of Scrum's core principles**, often never having read the Guide.
