# Meta

Meta is an AI developer and platform company. In this corpus it is a case study in how a vendor's safety philosophy and release strategy line up with its commercial interests, most visibly in its two-track model releases and its stance on open weights.

## Key Claims

- Meta and Anthropic reached opposite conclusions from the same technology in the same month, each landing where its business model needed it to ([too dangerous to centralize](/sources/too-dangerous-to-centralize-too-dangerous-to-distribute.md))
- The five-day gap between closed Muse Spark 1.2 (API-only, 12–20x tier spread) and open Glimmer (Apache 2.0) is deliberate segmentation serving two different customers ([two prices, one model family](/sources/two-prices-one-model-family-two-different-customers.md))
- API buyers pay for a relationship; self-hosters were never in the closed tier's market, and the open release chases developer mindshare Meta cannot invoice directly.
- Meta sat out Nvidia's pro-open-weight letter, showing signatory counts track interest groups rather than independent judgment ([two open-weight letters](/sources/two-open-weight-letters-zero-neutral-parties.md))
- Treating a vendor's stated mission as evidence is a category error; platform choice should follow an evaluation baseline.
