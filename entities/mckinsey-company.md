# McKinsey & Company

McKinsey is a global management consultancy. In this corpus it appears twice over: as one of five firms that published an AI maturity framework, and as one of the forecasters whose quantified AI economic projections are held up as structurally flawed.

## Key Claims

- McKinsey is one of five firms (with BCG, Gartner, Bain, and NIST) each describing a slice of the same [AI maturity model](/concepts/ai-maturity-model.md) shape, while none tells an organization where it stands ([five firms, five frameworks](/sources/five-firms-built-five-ai-frameworks-none-of-them-built-a-ruler.md))
- Its AI economic forecasts extrapolate task exposure rather than measured adoption, a pattern shared with Goldman, PwC, and the IMF ([70 years of tech hype](/sources/seventy-years-of-technology-hype-what-failed-predictions-tell-us-about-the-ai-economic-boom.md))
- Against those forecasts, Acemoglu's 1.1–1.6% decade-long GDP estimate is roughly one-fifth of Goldman's, and MIT NANDA found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact.
- The corpus argues AI value needs causal measurement, since model accuracy creates zero value without changing an action ([finding the causal root](/sources/finding-the-causal-root-measuring-value-when-no-single-model-owns-the-outcome.md))
- McKinsey moved first on pricing, tying 25% of global fees to outcomes — the shift other firms now follow as clients refuse to pay for hours AI compresses ([McKinsey moved first](/sources/mckinsey-moved-first-were-next.md), [the billing model just broke](/sources/the-billing-model-just-broke.md))
- It cut 3,000 to 4,000 people, about 10% of the firm, as AI compressed the analysis layer the pyramid's pricing depended on ([the pyramid is coming down](/sources/the-pyramid-is-coming-down-now-what.md))
- McKinsey, Bain, and BCG cut jobs in the same quarter, targeting junior research and back-office roles where AI compressed the workflow ([the premium you charged](/sources/the-premium-you-charged-was-for-knowing-things.md))
- Its research with Microsoft locates the rate limiter in leadership: 88% of organizations experiment with AI while 81% report no bottom-line impact, one in six has no C-level AI owner, and culture, manager support, and talent practices drive 67% of impact ([the leadership gap](/sources/the-leadership-gap-just-got-a-number.md), [culture is the constraint](/sources/culture-is-the-constraint.md))
- Its data also anchors the flat-returns story: 37% report AI contributing to EBIT unchanged as investment scales, only 5-8% show measurable impact at scale, and 32% of organizations skipped a software purchase by building internally with agentic coding tools ([the 5 percent problem](/sources/the-5-percent-problem.md), [the build decision](/sources/the-build-decision-changed-the-relationship.md))
- Manager support multiplies adoption 8.7x, making the management layer rather than IT the real AI strategy ([your manager is the AI strategy](/sources/your-manager-is-the-ai-strategy.md))
