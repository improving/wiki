# The Free Press

An independent media outlet whose essays supply several of this corpus's sources on culture, parenting, education, and work. Its pieces share a contrarian bent: they push back on prevailing defaults and argue for [risk tolerance](/concepts/risk-tolerance.md), serious thinking, and individual variation.

## Overview

Three Free Press essays appear here. "Let Them Climb the Tree" argues that rising risk aversion, not rising danger, drives generational retreat from childhood challenge. "What College Is Actually For" contends universities traded asking what is true and good for asking how things make you feel. "Work and Rest Aren't Opposites" holds that rest is personal wiring, not a universal prescription.

## Key Claims

- Helicopter parenting often protects the parent's own image rather than the child.
- Natural consequences teach what shielding children never can; stand close to help them process failure, not to prevent the fall.
- [Critical thinking](/concepts/critical-thinking.md) paired with [civil discourse](/concepts/civil-discourse.md) is rarer than any technical skill.
- Serious rooms turn to the person who can think, not the loudest one.
- About half of people experience [boredom](/concepts/boredom-tolerance.md) as generative fuel; for others, work itself is the restoration.
