# Mythos 5

Mythos 5 is a frontier model that appears in this corpus as a governance case study: the US ordered Anthropic to suspend it worldwide on June 12, 2026, and its vendor framed it with warnings about dangerous capability. Both events are used to argue that approval lists and risk tiers are weak governance instruments.

## Key Claims

- The US ordered Anthropic to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 worldwide on June 12, 2026.
- A lab announcing a model might be dangerous also announces it is powerful.
- Gating model access by vendor self-reported risk tiers is governing by label.
- Vendor trust does not buy continuity; an approved-model list has a shelf life of an afternoon.
- Graduated tiering preserves 84-97 percent of ungoverned velocity versus 45-65 percent for uniform human review.

## Work

The suspension shows that an approved-model list cannot guarantee continuity, while the safety warning shows that risk disclosure doubles as marketing. The corpus's alternative is runtime enforcement plus task-tiered oversight: Kang's Oversight Classification Model scores tasks on four dimensions and routes them to three tiers.
