# PwC

PwC is a global professional-services and consulting firm. In this corpus it appears chiefly as one of the institutions whose confident, quantified projections about AI's economic impact are held up as evidence that such forecasts should be discounted.

## Overview

PwC is grouped with Goldman Sachs, McKinsey, and the IMF in one post's critique of AI-economy forecasting: these firms extrapolate from task exposure rather than measured adoption, repeating a seven-decade pattern of wrong tech-economy predictions. The same corpus also treats PwC's consultancy peers as organizations designing deliberate apprenticeship, since AI removes the routine work that once built judgment.

Newer sources place PwC on both sides of that story. Its own CEO survey supplies the evidence that AI spend and measurable impact have decoupled, its forward-deployed engineering program makes it one of the firms whose play TCS's engineer conversion rendered table stakes, and its junior-role data underpins the consulting restructuring now underway.

## Key Claims

- PwC's AI economic forecasts are cited alongside Goldman, McKinsey, and the IMF as extrapolating task exposure, not measured adoption
- Sceptics counter with Acemoglu's estimate of 1.1–1.6% GDP gain over a decade — roughly one-fifth of Goldman's figure
- MIT NANDA found 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots produced no measurable P&L impact, undercutting top-down projections
- Consultancies themselves face the apprenticeship problem: AI automates the proxy work that built junior judgment
- PwC's CEO survey found 56% of CEOs saw neither revenue growth nor cost reduction from AI in the past year, even as enterprise AI spend doubled.
- PwC announced a forward-deployed engineering program — one of the top-of-market plays that TCS's plan to convert up to 8,900 engineers turned into table stakes.
- PwC data showed AI-exposed junior roles were seven times more likely to require advanced human skills going forward, informing the current consulting restructuring.
