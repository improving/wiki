# Accenture

A global technology and management consulting firm, referenced in this corpus as an indicator of how the market prices delivery work and how project failure is understood. Later sources also treat it as the clearest public example of AI-driven restructuring, reskilling accountability, and the naming of [forward deployed engineering](/concepts/forward-deployed-engineering.md) among large consultancies.

## Overview

Accenture appears as a market signal rather than a case study: its stock movement and pricing posture are used to show how fragile the shift to outcome-based pricing currently is.

## Key Claims

- Accenture lost 18% in a single session on doubts about repricing work — a market reaction the source uses to show that outcome-based pricing remains speculative for most firms.
- The corpus argues most "outcome-based" arrangements are fixed-fee contracts wearing KPI costumes, with real departures from time-and-materials still rare among large firms.
- In the project-failure argument, the Standish Group's under-50% success rate and the claim that client inaction, not vendor underperformance, is the top cause of failed outcomes both bear on how consultancies are judged.
- The prescribed fix — open communication, developer discipline, and trust built by frequent delivery — cuts against the [technical debt](/concepts/technical-debt.md) that accumulates when craftsmanship is traded for velocity.
- Accenture is exiting 11,000 staff who cannot be reskilled on AI; the CEO's phrase "compressed timeline" signals the clock already ran out, and the move sets a new accountability bar for knowing who is making the AI transition and who is not.
- In the restructuring argument, Accenture's 11,000 layoffs sit inside an $865 million AI restructuring alongside KPMG's 400 US advisory cuts and Deloitte's practice restructuring — evidence that the consulting talent model shift is present, not forecast.
- Its partnership with ServiceNow is used to name forward deployed engineering as a formal delivery model, validating a practice firms must claim and sell rather than leave as a side effect of how they work.
