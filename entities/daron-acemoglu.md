# Daron Acemoglu

MIT economist whose work supplies the corpus's sober counterweight to bullish AI economic projections. He is cited both for the [labor compression](/concepts/labor-compression.md) frame — automation shrinks hours per output rather than eliminating roles — and for a far lower estimate of AI's macroeconomic payoff than consultancy forecasts.

## Overview

Acemoglu estimates AI will add 1.1–1.6% to GDP over a decade, roughly one-fifth of Goldman Sachs' projection. The corpus attributes the gap to bullish models extrapolating task exposure rather than measured adoption. His framing favors [productivity amplification](/concepts/productivity-amplification.md) over replacement.

## Key Claims

- AI's decade GDP gain is 1.1–1.6%, about one-fifth of Goldman's estimate.
- Automation historically compresses hours per unit of output rather than deleting roles (ATM and spreadsheet cases).
- Value capture hinges on whether demand or execution capacity is the binding constraint — see [demand-limited vs collapsable tasks](/concepts/demand-limit.md).
- His caution rhymes with the [productivity paradox](/concepts/productivity-paradox.md) and [Jevons paradox](/concepts/jevons-paradox.md) debates.
