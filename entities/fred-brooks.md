# Fred Brooks

Fred Brooks is the software engineering author of The Mythical Man-Month, best known for Brooks's law: adding people to a late project makes it later. This corpus revives his coordination argument to explain why adding AI agents does not scale linearly either.

## Work

Writers apply Brooks to multi-agent delivery, arguing coordination overhead grows faster than agent count. The same reasoning underpins the single-agent ceiling — one perspective cannot hold every competing delivery concern — and the case for specialization, scope inhibition, and convergence checks borrowed from biological coordination.

## Key Claims

- Coordination overhead grows faster than agent count, echoing Brooks's law.
- Specialization is interface definition, not just role assignment.
- The 15 to 50 percent single-agent gain does not translate into delivered value.
- Ten steps at 95 percent accuracy yield only about 60 percent end-to-end reliability.
- Requirements act as constraints that keep agents from optimizing the wrong direction.
