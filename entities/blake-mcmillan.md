# Blake McMillan

An Improving writer and Professional Scrum trainer whose posts apply Scrum, Kanban, and product-ownership thinking to everyday delivery problems. He is a Professional Agile Leadership trainer, and his writing favors memorable framings and anti-patterns over heavy theory.

## Topics and Work

- Argues "what you don't change, you choose": tolerating non-[cross-functional teams](/concepts/cross-functional-teams.md) is itself a decision to accept delays, dependencies, and coordination waste; the fix is to start small by embedding missing skills.
- Uses Pixar's "Brain Trust" — experts who surface problems without authority over the director — as a model of real [product ownership](/concepts/product-ownership.md), contrasting it with stakeholders who hand Product Owners a list of solutions.
- Argues the default answer to "when should we release?" is as often as possible, because a [product backlog](/concepts/product-backlog.md) is a set of hypotheses and delay postpones learning; the real answer is "it depends" on automation, approvals, and adoption.
- Compares in-person, live-virtual, and self-paced learning, concluding certifications matter less than skills practiced daily for [continuous learning](/concepts/continuous-learning.md).
- Explains how Applying Professional Kanban (APK) and Professional Scrum with Kanban (PSK) differ, and how to choose between them by team context.
