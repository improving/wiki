# Aristotle

The ancient Greek philosopher whose account of wellbeing and practical wisdom supplies the conceptual vocabulary for two otherwise unrelated arguments in this corpus.

## Overview

Aristotle is invoked twice: once for the distinction between pleasure and a life well lived, and once for the practical wisdom that character-trained AI systems may or may not attain.

## Key Claims

- The hedonia/eudaimonia distinction is used to argue that chasing enjoyment adapts away on the hedonic treadmill, while fulfillment comes from meaningful challenge matched to skill; the fullest wellbeing has both.
- Flow requires a challenge that slightly exceeds current skill, and productive challenge gives feedback whereas destructive overload gives none.
- In the alignment argument, training models on first-person character traits generalizes better than encoding rules or optimizing for approval — the Parrhesia project raised trait correlation from 0.44 to 0.87 and scored 20/20 on golden prompts.
- Whether models can achieve Aristotelian *phronesis* — practical wisdom — remains unresolved, which is the corpus's stated open question for [character-based alignment](/concepts/constitutional-ai.md).
