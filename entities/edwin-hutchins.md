# Edwin Hutchins

Cognitive scientist whose theory of [distributed cognition](/concepts/distributed-cognition.md) holds that intelligence is spread across people, tools, and protocols rather than confined to an individual mind. The corpus adopts the theory as its frame for evaluating AI in delivery systems.

## Overview

If intelligence is distributed, then an AI model is a new class of node whose value depends on how well it is integrated, not on raw model capability. Treating AI as a productivity add-on speeds individual nodes without improving the system — the useful question is how well the system is designed. The same logic makes delivery tooling [cognitive scaffolding](/concepts/cognitive-scaffolding.md) that preserves team knowledge.

## Key Claims

- Intelligence lives across people, tools, and protocols, not inside one mind.
- AI's effect depends on integration into the architecture, not model capability.
- Treating AI as a productivity add-on speeds nodes without improving the system.
- Delivery tooling externalizes team knowledge; deploying agents without governance scaffolding repeats the CI-without-quality-gates mistake.
