# Akamai

A cloud and edge platform that publishes practitioner argument on AI agent governance. Two sources in this corpus advance its central thesis: oversight burden should scale with [reversibility](/concepts/reversibility.md) — whether an agent's mistakes can be undone — rather than with autonomy level or risk category.

## Overview

Akamai's argument is a critique of [uniform governance](/concepts/uniform-governance.md), which scrutinizes every action regardless of its cost. The writing proposes replacing the autonomous-versus-supervised axis with reversible-versus-irreversible buckets, and warns that most frameworks apply the lightest controls exactly where consequences are permanent.

## Key Claims

- Ask not how risky an agent is but whether its mistakes can be undone.
- Reversible and irreversible are more useful buckets than autonomous and supervised.
- Governance burden scales with reversibility, not autonomy.
- Most frameworks apply the lightest controls where consequences are permanent.
- [Gartner](/entities/gartner.md) projects 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission agents by 2027, and agents die from governance design rather than technical failure.
