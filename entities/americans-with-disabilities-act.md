# Americans with Disabilities Act

The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) is the US civil-rights law prohibiting discrimination against people with disabilities. In this corpus its digital reach is the legal driver behind accessibility work: it turns accessible design from good practice into a compliance obligation for public-facing and government-audited products.

## Digital Accessibility Requirements

- Under **Title II**, public entities serving at least 50,000 people must make their websites comply with [WCAG](/entities/wcag.md) 2.1 Level AA.
- The original deadline of April 24, 2026 was extended by the DOJ to April 26, 2027 — four days before it would have hit.
- Penalties can include daily fines per infraction, and private digital-accessibility lawsuits are increasing, using WCAG 2.1 as a benchmark.
- The ADA's digital reach makes accessibility a compliance matter for government-audited products, not merely a design preference.
- Practical guidance: add accessibility to the [Definition of Done](/concepts/definition-of-done.md) so new features meet the bar rather than being remediated later.

## Beyond Compliance

- The law sits behind everyday accommodations too — ADA-compliant buses with wheelchair lifts and accessible attractions at Disney are used here as a model of anticipating needs rather than reacting to frustration.
- More than 1 in 4 US adults live with a disability, and most people lose some vision with age, so the audience extends well beyond extreme cases.
