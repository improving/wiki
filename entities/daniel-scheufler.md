# Daniel Scheufler

Daniel Scheufler is an [Improving](/entities/improving.md) writer and speaker on AI-assisted development, human judgment, and software design. His essays consistently argue that AI shifts the scarce skill toward articulating intent and defining "done," rather than replacing human judgment.

## Work

- Authored "From AI Automation to AI Augmentation," framing AI as an adapter that trades one specialist's time for average output in another domain.
- Wrote "Why Human Judgment Matters More Than Ever in the Age of AI," on the bottleneck moving to reviews, approvals, and the junior-engineer pipeline.
- Authored practical how-tos: "Define Done Before You Build a Task Agent" and "Building a Prompt Evaluation Habit Your Team Can Trust."
- Wrote the API-design essay "APIs and the Walls They Build," reading architecture through Conway's Law and [Robert Frost](/entities/robert-frost.md).
- Contributed to the "Drucker's Blueprint" product-owner series and "Best Current Practices."

## Themes

- Recurring ideas: [AI augmentation](/concepts/ai-augmentation.md), [judgment-bound work](/concepts/judgment-bound-work.md), [prompt evaluation](/concepts/prompt-evaluation.md), and [Definition of Done](/concepts/definition-of-done.md).
- He frames AI maturity as a matter of consistent prompting and independent validation, not tooling.
