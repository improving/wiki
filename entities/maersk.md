# Maersk

Maersk, the global shipping and logistics company, appears in this corpus as the case study behind an argument that internal developer platforms must be designed user-centrically. Its IDP build is used to show the time, executive backing, and product discipline such a platform demands.

## Internal Developer Platform

- Maersk's IDP project lasted 18–24 months, showing the commitment required, and depended on executive support and budget.
- The company's legacy infrastructure left developers waiting indefinitely for support; the IDP cut provisioning wait times and enabled self-service.
- The platform standardized security practices and commit formatting, reducing cognitive load so developers could focus on product work.
- Key practices included user-centered design, friendly onboarding, continuous feedback loops, performance, accessibility, documentation, and user testing.
- Lessons: invest in training, manage stakeholder expectations, acknowledge the IDP cannot cover every use case, and secure executive sponsorship.
