# Samsung

Samsung appears in this corpus as one of the large technology employers anchoring the Dallas-Fort Worth market, and in writing about which regions win business and which drive it away. It functions as a marker of the region's industrial depth rather than as a subject of analysis in its own right.

## Key Claims

- CBRE ranked DFW eighth in North America and second in net new tech workers, while CompTIA put DFW fifth nationally for AI-specific hiring ([DFW AI talent](/sources/dfw-is-now-the-ai-talent-market.md)).
- Talent momentum tells you where a market is going, not where it is; the question shifts from whether to build in DFW to how to compete there ([DFW AI talent](/sources/dfw-is-now-the-ai-talent-market.md)).
- Pudu Robotics moved its U.S. headquarters to Richardson, an example of the region converting momentum into commitments ([DFW AI talent](/sources/dfw-is-now-the-ai-talent-market.md)).
- One post argues officials who built businesses govern better than career politicians who frame business as extraction and drive jobs away ([two kinds of public servant](/sources/two-kinds-of-public-servant.md)).
- New Jersey fell from 22 to 15 Fortune 500 companies as businesses left, while Pritzker's refusal drove the Bears from Chicago and Indiana said yes ([two kinds of public servant](/sources/two-kinds-of-public-servant.md)).
- Winning regions treat business as a partner in building community ([two kinds of public servant](/sources/two-kinds-of-public-servant.md)).
