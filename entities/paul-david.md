# Paul David

Paul David is the economic historian whose work on electrification this corpus uses to explain why AI's productivity gains lag its adoption. His famous finding — that electrification took roughly forty years to show up in productivity, because factories had to be redesigned around it — is the corpus's central analogy for treating AI as a general purpose technology.

## Work

One post frames AI as a GPT like electricity or the internet: pervasive, continuously improving, and spawning complementary innovation. Because AI's distribution layer already exists, the retooling required is organizational rather than physical. Diffusion follows Rogers' curve, and early adopters capture most of the advantage.

The companion post pushes the same analogy toward structure: a feature model caps improvement at 10–30% on existing processes, while a foundation model redesigns processes around where human judgment matters. Most digital transformation, it argues, is renovation mislabeled as transformation — and foundation layers are model-agnostic, surviving model churn. David's forty-year electrification lag is the cautionary precedent throughout.

## Key Claims

- General purpose technologies are pervasive, continuously improving, and spawn complementary innovation.
- Electrification's productivity gains took forty years, tied to factory redesign.
- AI's distribution layer already exists, so retooling is organizational, not physical.
- The feature model caps improvement at 10–30% on existing processes.
- Foundation layers are model-agnostic and survive model churn.
