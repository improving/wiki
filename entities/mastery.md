# Mastery

*Mastery* is the work on long-term skill development that supplies this corpus's frame for why growth arrives in bursts, why plateaus slope downward, and why practice must be sustained rather than willed. It matters here because it ties the mechanics of practice to the emotional experience of apparent stagnation.

## Key Claims

- Growth comes in bursts and is never linear; every curve includes a plateau, a growth spurt, and a pull-back ([growth lines](/sources/growth-lines.md)).
- The plateau is not flat — entropy makes it slope downward, so decay is the natural state and growth requires deliberate effort ([growth lines](/sources/growth-lines.md)).
- Deliberate practice is designed to improve performance rather than merely repeat it, with constant feedback and measurement driving improvement ([deliberate practice](/sources/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md)).
- Sessions should run 60-90 minutes and at most 3-4 hours a day ([deliberate practice](/sources/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md)).
- Intrinsic motivation is what sustains mastery; the 10,000-hour figure alone is insufficient ([deliberate practice](/sources/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md)).
