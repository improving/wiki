# Improving

Improving is the consultancy whose eight-stage AI maturity model, delivery research, and off-the-shelf-first ROI guidance run through this corpus as both methodology and author's context. Its later writing extends that research into AI-era positioning — outcome-based billing, forward-deployed delivery, and the manager layer of AI adoption.

## Key Claims

- Improving's eight-stage model supplies the missing ruler that the BCG, Gartner, Bain, McKinsey, and NIST frameworks lack.
- Most organizations are at Stage 2 while believing they are at Stage 4; trust cannot be bought by jumping stages.
- Improving recommends a 40/40/20 blend of AI investment time and customizing off-the-shelf platforms over building custom models.
- Reversibility tagging cut false rollback success from over 40% to under 5%.
- The Standish Group reports an under 50% project success rate, and only delivered working software is the measure that matters.
- Teams pairing tooling with Stage 4 workflow redesign ship 12-30% more features.
- Improving positions itself as a global boutique with AI delivery depth as AI doubles client consulting spend (0.8% to 1.7% of revenues) and the generalist mid-market firm is squeezed between scaled ecosystem integrators and deep specialists ([the consulting middle](/sources/consulting-middle-disappearing.md)).
- Its delivery already matches the forward-deployed model, but the firm had never packaged or priced it as the offering; with FDE now table stakes, differentiation shifts to closeness to the business problem and driving outcomes ([the forward deployed model](/sources/the-forward-deployed-model-changes-everything.md), [table stakes](/sources/the-fde-model-has-become-table-stakes-now-what.md)).
- Its writing argues an AI service line is marketing while restructuring delivery is the operational commitment buyers should demand — 70% of firms have an AI service line while graduate hiring is down 30% ([the service line](/sources/the-service-line-is-not-the-delivery-model.md)).
- The firm was built on Conscious Capitalism's stakeholder model from the start, serving employees, suppliers, community, and customers ([profits for good](/sources/profits-for-good.md)).
- Its events research quantifies commitment: free events draw about 50% attendance against signups, a $20 fee about 70%, and higher prices near 90% ([skin in the game](/sources/skin-in-the-game-the-cost-of-free.md)).
- It runs the internal platform Engage and a "Ship Faster with AI" video series that builds a real feature with AI tooling rather than a Hello World demo ([ship faster with AI](/sources/ship-faster-with-ai-series.md)).
- Its commentary locates the AI adoption gap in the manager layer: employees with AI-supportive managers are 8.7x more likely to say AI transformed work ([your manager is the AI strategy](/sources/your-manager-is-the-ai-strategy.md)).
- Leadership writing runs alongside the delivery research — charting careers, deliberate practice, assuming positive intent, and training rather than trying ([a new chapter](/sources/a-new-chapter.md), [deliberate practice](/sources/intro-to-deliberate-practice.md), [it's simple, right](/sources/its-simple-right.md), [training vs trying](/sources/training-vs-trying.md)).
