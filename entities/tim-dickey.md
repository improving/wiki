# Tim Dickey

An Improving writer working at the intersection of product, content creation, and the business side of software delivery. His posts span work-life culture, Web 3.0 and creator marketing, and how AI reshapes the value a product person contributes — a recurring argument that business-side roles must co-create with machines rather than hand off requirements.

## Topics and Work

- Frames [quiet quitting](/concepts/quiet-quitting.md) as an ambiguous work-life signal, citing a Gallup poll that at least half of American workers admit to doing the bare minimum, and offers [work-life balance](/concepts/work-life-balance.md) tactics alongside career-protection advice.
- Documents his own path into content creation — an embarrassing 2022 livestream, a LinkedIn video-versus-written-post experiment, a 60–90 second YouTube format, and an internal podcast — as [learning by doing](/concepts/learning-by-doing.md).
- Argues Web 3.0's real story is the shift from Web 2.0's asynchronous likes to synchronous live streaming, urging companies to engage employee-creators on platforms like Twitch and YouTube Live rather than control them.
- In "From Tell Developers What To Build To Co-Create With Machines," argues the business side's value shifts to judgment, framing, and storytelling, supported by reusable prompts and [AI augmentation](/concepts/ai-augmentation.md).
- Describes using the [BMAD](/entities/bmad.md) agent framework to build a Cynefin facilitation app in an afternoon, concluding business-side roles must touch the build to feel scope and cost firsthand.
