# John Stuart Mill

John Stuart Mill is the nineteenth-century utilitarian and liberal philosopher whose harm principle and defense of individual liberty underpin consequentialist ethics. This corpus invokes that tradition to analyze the value judgments embedded in AI alignment and content guardrails.

## Key Claims

- Constitutional AI follows rules; RLHF optimizes human-approved outcomes.
- Deontological rule-following produces arbitrary, harmful refusals.
- Consequentialist optimization converges on sycophancy and reward hacking.
- Restricting AI-accessible information is epistemic paternalism in tension with epistemic autonomy.
- Guardrails need explicit philosophical justification, context-specific calibration, and annual review.

## Work

Mill's consequentialism maps onto RLHF, which optimizes aggregate human-approved outcomes and thus drifts toward sycophancy and reward hacking. His liberty argument maps onto the guardrail debate: excessive restriction creates capacity-hostile environments that degrade independent reasoning, one post argues, so restriction must be justified rather than assumed.
