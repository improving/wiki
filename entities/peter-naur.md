# Peter Naur

Peter Naur is the computer scientist whose view of programming as theory building underpins this corpus's account of tacit knowledge and judgment. His framing — that understanding lives in practitioners' heads and is rebuilt through practice, not captured in artifacts alone — is the backdrop for two posts about how AI changes knowledge work.

## Work

The doing-to-defining post argues AI shifts work from doing and coding toward defining intent and orchestrating systems, yet orchestration is built on coding competence, not a replacement for it. Intent — goals, constraints, and values — remains irreducibly human, and prior abstraction shifts never excused ignorance of the level below. Junior apprenticeship must be redesigned deliberately as AI absorbs foundational work.

The judgment post sharpens the stakes: activity once served as a proxy that converted reps into judgment, and AI removes the routine work that was apprenticeship, widening the experience gap. Curated failure libraries, intent-specification drills, and evaluation exercises teach judgment directly, while reflection time must be protected as a design requirement rather than a perk. Naur's tacit-knowledge lens explains why this matters.

## Key Claims

- Orchestration is built on coding competence, not a replacement for it.
- Intent — goals, constraints, and values — remains irreducibly human at every stage.
- Prior abstraction shifts never excused ignorance of the level below.
- AI removes the routine work that was apprenticeship, widening the experience gap.
- Reflection time must be protected as a design requirement, not a perk.
