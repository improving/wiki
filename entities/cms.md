# Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) is the US federal agency that administers Medicare and Medicaid and sets the data and transparency rules for health plans and providers. In this corpus CMS is the regulator whose deadlines and accountability rules shape payer AI, interoperability, and cost-transparency work.

## Interoperability and Prior Authorization

- CMS interoperability by 2026 requires **80% of prior-authorization decisions to be real-time and to use [FHIR](/entities/fhir.md)**, with denial reasons made public.
- CMS holds health plans accountable for the AI they license — "we bought it from a vendor" is not a defense.
- The **WISER Model** is a six-year Medicare pilot using AI to review prior authorizations.

## Price Transparency

- Federal cost-transparency mandates — the 2020 Transparency in Coverage Final Rule, the 2021 Consolidated Appropriations Act, and the No Surprises Act — are described as the largest health-plan changes since the ACA.
- Insurers must publish machine-readable files with covered-item and drug prices and offer price-comparison tools; changes phased from 2021 through 2024 forced legacy systems to be retired or retrofitted.
- CMS sits in the same regulatory space as [HIPAA](/entities/hipaa.md), adding interoperability and transparency duties on top of privacy.
