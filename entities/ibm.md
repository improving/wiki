# IBM

IBM is an enterprise technology company. In this corpus it appears twice as a case study in AI claims and AI capacity: a self-reported productivity figure that nobody reproduced, and an inference cluster built ahead of confirmed demand. Later sources add a third and fourth role: an example of what a genuine AI-native delivery rebuild looks like, and the source of a shadow-AI statistic.

## Overview

- IBM's 45 percent productivity claim is self-reported and unreproduced outside the company.
- Internal monitoring and external audit are treated as different evidence statuses.
- IBM's Together AI inference cluster is not scheduled to go live until early 2027.
- The build-ahead bet illustrates that more AI capability does not automatically create demand for what it produces.
- Context: Nvidia posted an earnings beat and was still sold off, and capital markets no longer fund AI buildout on forecasts alone.
- IBM is cited as the contrast to rebranding: it embedded GPT-5.6 into the platform its consultants use daily, certified them, and added forward-deployed engineers, treating AI as delivery infrastructure rather than decoration.
- IBM's own finding anchors the shadow-AI argument — unsanctioned AI tools appeared inside 43% of security incidents, more than doubling in a year.
- Alongside Deloitte, Accenture, and PwC, IBM announced a forward-deployed engineering program as a top-of-market play before TCS turned the model into a baseline.
