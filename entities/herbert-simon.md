# Herbert Simon

Herbert Simon, the economist and early AI researcher, appears in this corpus through the idea of **satisficing** — accepting a good-enough answer rather than optimizing without limit. The posts apply it as a design principle for reasoning-model thinking budgets and for calibrating how much human review AI-assisted delivery actually needs.

## Overview

The argument is that accuracy gains from thinking tokens follow diminishing returns past a task-specific ceiling, so budgets should be calibrated empirically and allocated dynamically by request difficulty. Simon also frames the corpus's broader case for [hybrid intelligence](/concepts/hybrid-intelligence.md), where neural capability, symbolic constraints, and governance are needed together.

## Key Claims

- Accuracy gains from thinking tokens show diminishing returns past a task-specific ceiling.
- TALE-EP reached roughly 80% accuracy while cutting output token costs by 67%.
- Dynamic budget allocation matches large fixed budgets at substantially lower average cost.
- The neural-versus-symbolic debate rests on a false dichotomy: neural alone produces unpredictable outputs, symbolic alone produces brittle compliance, and governance is the most-often-missing component.
- Effective delivery throughput is gated by [review capacity](/concepts/review-bottleneck.md), not generation speed, so trust must be earned through progressive delegation.
