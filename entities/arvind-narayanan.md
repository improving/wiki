# Arvind Narayanan

AI researcher invoked in this corpus for the argument that AI is a **general purpose technology** — pervasive, continuously improving, and generative of complementary innovation — rather than a single tool to be adopted. That framing drives the corpus's claims about why organizational restructuring, not tool rollout, determines returns.

## Work

- One post argues AI should be understood alongside electricity and the internet, whose productivity gains took roughly forty years because factories had to be redesigned around them.
- Because AI's distribution layer already exists, the argument goes, the retooling is organizational rather than physical — which is why [diffusion of innovations](/concepts/diffusion-of-innovations.md) governs who captures the advantage.
- The corpus pairs this with the [capability-cost inflection point](/sources/why-now-the-capability-cost-inflection-point-that-changes-everything.md): frontier capability climbed while per-token prices first fell two orders of magnitude, then reversed upward.

## Key Claims

- General purpose technologies are pervasive, continuously improving, and spawn complementary innovation.
- Electrification's productivity gains took forty years and were tied to factory redesign, not the technology itself.
- Early adopters capture most of the advantage, so timing matters more than the tool.
- The cheap-token window is closing as capability climbs, raising the cost of waiting.
