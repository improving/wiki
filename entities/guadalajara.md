# Guadalajara

Guadalajara is the hub Improving and its writers call the "Silicon Valley of Mexico" — the country's densest technology ecosystem and a cornerstone of Improving's [nearshore outsourcing](/concepts/nearshore-outsourcing.md) delivery model. It entered Improving's footprint through the 2020 [iTexico](/entities/itexico.md) merger and recurs as a site of both technical delivery and community work.

## The Tech Ecosystem

- Hosts over 1,000 tech companies and roughly 150,000 tech jobs; Jalisco produces more than 10,000 tech specialists a year from ITESO, Universidad de Guadalajara, and Tec de Monterrey.
- In 2025 Silicon Valley companies announced $890 million of investment in Jalisco; electronics exports reached $12.9 billion in 2024.
- IBM, HP, Intel, and Bosch run major R&D centers in the city; events include Talent Land with 42,000+ attendees.
- Software developers earn about $36,833 a year, offering 40–60% savings versus the US.

## Improving's Presence

- Became a key nearshore delivery location when Improving merged with iTexico, which had offices in Guadalajara and Aguascalientes.
- Improving's leadership slate placed David Sandoval as President of Improving Mexico, positioning the country as a nearshore delivery engine; Guillermo Ortega extended the model to teams across LATAM, India, the USA, and Canada.
- The [CodeCup](/entities/code-cup.md) 2026 program paired 24 Improving engineers with 24 startups one-to-one ahead of the FIFA World Cup, with judges including Sandy Hermosilla, David Sandoval, and Mario Mendez.
- Improving covered travel for employees to attend Guadalajara Pride, a moment its writers describe as deliberately built [belonging](/concepts/belonging.md) and [community giving](/concepts/community-giving.md).
